# Awareness / Consciousness / Sentience — Terminology Discipline for FMT

**Ingested into aIware theory KB:** 2026-06-11 (Session 221). **Origin/full report:** `~/social/docs/awareness-vs-consciousness-terminology-report.md` (social session 2026-06-10; subagent-compiled field defs + interlinguistic map; FMT defs read directly from *Die Emergenz des Bewusstseins* 2015 Ch.3.4 pp.55–69 & Ch.5.4, and `paper/full/four-model-theory-full.md`). ## THE KEY FINDING (decision-relevant)
The feeling-floor Matthias informally calls **"awareness / level-0"** (feels & fears, locked in the now, no past/future, no symbols — fish/amphibian/reptile) is, in **his own 2015 book, in Chalmers, and across the field, "sentience"** (≈ Damasio *core* / Edelman *primary* consciousness). In the 2015 book and in Chalmers, **"awareness" means the *opposite*: content-directed access** ("a conscious being can be *unaware* of almost anything"). So "awareness is not consciousness," said to an expert audience, collides with their (and Matthias's own book's) usage.

**Recommendation for FMT:** use **"sentience"** for the felt floor; **qualify "consciousness"** (phenomenal/access/basic/extended) whenever it carries weight; treat **"awareness" as stipulated or avoid it** (weakest cross-linguistic anchor, most contradictory technical usages); lean on **"self-model"** as the language-independent discriminator — it sidesteps the whole lexical war by pointing at architecture.

## Two field-level fault lines (the words are swapped)
- **Chalmers 1995/96:** *awareness* = functional/access (the easy problems); *consciousness* = phenomenal (the hard problem).
- **Clinical neurology (Laureys, Plum & Posner):** *awareness* = phenomenal **content** axis; *consciousness* = arousal/wakefulness. **Exact inverse of Chalmers.**
- Cross-field consensus on only one thing: awareness/consciousness ≠ **attention** (keep insisting on this).

## Anchors FMT should lean on
- **Damasio core vs extended:** core = transient, non-linguistic, here-and-now, no autobiographical self (survives amnesia); extended = autobiographical, spans time, language-amplified. ≈ verbatim Matthias's "floor" and FMT's **basic vs extended consciousness**.
- **Edelman/Tononi primary vs higher-order**; **sentience vs sapience** (sentience = capacity for *felt* valenced states ≈ P-consciousness, the animal-ethics floor).

## Gruber 2015 → FMT 2026 lineage (the 2015 book IS the implicit/explicit × self/world architecture in prose)
- **Bewusstsein** = "ability of an entity to generate a model of itself ('Selbstmodell'), relate it to itself, and interact with it" (p.57). Information becomes conscious "when transferred from the self-model into the world-model" (p.61).
- Four 2015 models → FMT 2×2: **Selbstmodell → ISM** (implicit self), **Metamodell → IWM** (implicit world), **Ich-Modell → ESM** (explicit self), **Weltmodell → EWM** (explicit world). "Das Selbstmodell an sich ist nie bewusst" (p.63).
- Graded ladder **einfach → einfach/doppelt/dreifach erweitert** (p.61–63) = ancestor of FMT **basic → extended consciousness** (extended = can model its Ich-Modell and identify with it).

## Interlinguistic one-liners (why "self-model" wins)
- **Romance fusion:** ES *conciencia* / FR *conscience* = consciousness AND moral conscience in one word (Latin *conscientia* = "knowing-with"); disambiguate only by adjective.
- **Germanic gap:** German has *Bewusstsein* but **no clean native "awareness"** (*Aufmerksamkeit* = attention, false friend; *Selbstbewusstsein* = self-confidence) → proves the EN consc./aware. split is not linguistically universal.
- **Sinitic morphemic split:** CN/JP encode **awaken/feel (覚/觉) vs know/cognize (識/知)** at the morpheme level — arguably tracks phenomenal-vs-access better than the semantically-arbitrary EN Latin-vs-Germanic borrowing.
