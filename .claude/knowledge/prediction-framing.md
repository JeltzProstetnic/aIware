# Prediction Framing — Lessons Learned

Load when writing or revising predictions in any FMT publication or submission.

## History

The FMT paper's prediction count changed from 8 → 9 → 4 across revisions. The book published a numbered list that the author now regrets. Andrillon's NoC desk rejection specifically cited "predictions too general." AICE peer reviewers converged on the same weakness.

## What went wrong

1. **Mixed novelty**: Some predictions (psychedelic hierarchy) were shared with REBUS/PP. Listing them as "FMT predictions" invited the objection "that's not yours."
2. **Already confirmed**: Some (anesthesia/criticality) were post-hoc corroborations, not predictions. Listing them alongside untested predictions conflated evidence types.
3. **Untestable items**: AI consciousness predictions (P7, P10) were metaphysical, not empirical. Including them diluted the testable ones.
4. **Insufficient operationalization**: Predictions lacked sample sizes, effect sizes, measurement protocols, falsification criteria. "Too general" was accurate.
5. **Fixed counts become attack surfaces**: "Nine predictions" invites "your prediction #3 is wrong, so your theory is wrong." The count becomes the claim.

## What works

- Present testable consequences as **illustrations of architectural specificity** — "the architecture predicts, for instance..." not "here are N predictions."
- In the main paper (where space permits), operationalize fully: sample size, measures, effect size thresholds, falsification criteria, explicit comparison with competitor predictions.
- Separate post-hoc corroborations ("converging evidence") from novel untested predictions. Different evidential weight, different sections.
- For each prediction, state: (a) which architectural property generates it, (b) which competing theories make the same/different/no prediction, (c) what result would disconfirm it.
- Refer readers to the paper for the full catalogue rather than committing to a count in secondary materials (commentaries, pitches, abstracts).

## Theoretical corrections (Session 204, user)

1. **Criticality ≠ consciousness.** Criticality is a necessary substrate property for the computation, not for consciousness itself. An insect brain at criticality isn't human-conscious. The dual threshold is: criticality enables computation; architecture (richness of self-model) determines consciousness level. Don't conflate "criticality findings" with "FMT confirmations" — they confirm a shared necessary condition.

2. **No sharp developmental discontinuity.** The brain uses a continuous model space, not 4 discrete models. Even if self-referential closure has a theoretical threshold, in practice it would be washed out by the continuous substrate — practically unmeasurable as a sharp transition. Same applies to lucid dream onset. Don't impose digital thinking onto analog processes.

3. **Language under narcosis = FMT confirmation.** Katlowitz et al. (2026, *Nature*) showed hippocampal neurons perform semantic parsing, syntactic discrimination, word prediction, and representational plasticity under propofol — with zero conscious recall. This is exactly what FMT predicts: IWM continues operating (substrate intact) while EWM/ESM are suppressed (simulation offline). Stronger than generic "implicit learning persists."

## Context-dependent framing

**Paper (peer review)**: Humble. Only claim what survives hostile review. Carefully hedge convergence. Distinguish FMT-specific predictions from shared ones.

**Web / social / outreach**: Honest about actual explanatory scope. Almost all consciousness-related phenomena are consistent with FMT's architecture — anesthesia, sleep, dreams, psychedelics, ego dissolution, split-brain, anosognosia, blindsight, language under narcosis, developmental emergence, animal consciousness. The architecture generates the phenomena; the paper just can't claim all of them as "predictions" in peer-reviewed context.

## In-silico / crucible evidence framing (Session 267, 2026-07-24)

The crucible computational program is reported in the FMT paper as a self-contained, **droppable §8.9** ("In-Silico Tests of the Architecture") plus a **separate companion paper** (Gruber, 2026d). The full banked evidence lives at `docs/crucible-evidence-ledger-digest.md` — **NOT** the null-forward `docs/pending-cru36-prediction-revision.md` brief.

- **Capability-first, always.** The requirement is Class-4 *capability* (support universal/free computation), not proximity to any single criticality statistic. Avalanche-σ≈1 and edge-of-chaos-λ≈0 are interchangeable *instruments* — not co-occurring, not competing definitions (Kanders et al. 2017; the leaky-ESN finding that σ=1 ≠ the edge).
- **Foreground the banked positives, state the ceiling.** Closure has been shown (toy-scale, red-team-surviving) to (1) maintain a model recursion-specifically, (2) let a self-model-gated planner beat a full competent reactive bracket on depth, (3) confer a prospective-survival advantage, (4) carry a transfer capability its ablation abolishes (d=2.44; not unique to FMT — pre-concede). Lead with these, not the null.
- **Banked ≠ open.** The decisive scaled test (a closure-driven free-modeler whose capability *dies when the loop is cut*) is OPEN, and must be **graded / novelty-relative** — the stationary-closure impossibility theorem proves a clean binary loop-cut is impossible on deterministic stationary tasks. Never conflate banked mechanism demos with the open scaled test.
- **The CRU-36 null is FMT-consistent and minor.** One mis-wired spiking test on a homogeneous reservoir (no distinct self/world models); it sharpened the design. Report honestly; do not foreground.
- **The discriminating prediction is an INTERACTION** (criticality × closure; no main effect of criticality absent closure) — IIT/GNW predict a main effect. Interaction-framing survives "too general" better than a bare effect.
- **Process lesson:** before briefing a subagent (or drafting paper/companion content) on crucible results, use the aIware evidence-ledger DIGEST, never the null-forward CRU-36 brief. (Root cause of the S267 undersell — Fable drafted §8.9 + companion from the understated brief and had to be redone.)
