## A. Submission-level risks (failure modes that are NOT desk-reject)

### A1. BBS Seth commentary (AIW-49, deadline Jun 12)
- **Acceptance + heavy revisions Matthias can't service.** BBS commentaries are short and peer-reviewed, but the editorial board often returns 1–2 rounds of substantive revisions over 3–6 weeks. If R&R lands during AICE-26 (Jul 2) or in the middle of school break (mid-Jul through early Sep), Matthias's response window collapses against family/holiday capacity. F's audit shows Jul–Aug at 1–3 h/wk — a 2-week R&R window in this period gets ~3–6 hours of review attention. **Failure mode:** revision goes back partially-baked, second-round reviewer bounces it, commentary doesn't run.
- **Slot pre-empted.** BBS publishes a fixed number of commentaries per target article. If the deadline pulls higher-affiliation responses that overlap FMT's angle (Friston-Laukkonen "Beautiful Loop" group, anyone running a self-simulation framework), the editorial board cuts the unaffiliated commentary. Detection signal: BBS website lists accepted commentary authors before publication; check 2–3 weeks post-deadline.
- **Wrong target article framing.** The commentary frames FMT as "the architectural specification Seth lacks." If Seth's response paper concedes ground (say he extends biological naturalism to include computational architecture), the commentary's hook evaporates. Detection signal: pre-publication response from Seth or one of his lab members.

### A2. McFarnell Cortex registered report (AIW-48 / AIW-59)
- **Stage-1 accepted, Stage-2 data conflicts with FMT.** This is the *theoretically interesting* but strategically catastrophic case. The proposed paradigm is FMT vs ACU, varying uncertainty × affective significance × temporal pressure, measuring perceptual accuracy vs vividness vs agentive intensity. FMT predicts affect loss → agency loss only; ACU predicts affect loss → broad phenomenality attenuation. **If results show ACU-pattern (affect loss → broad attenuation), FMT publishes a paper that empirically *disconfirms* a core FMT prediction.** The paper still counts as a peer-reviewed FMT citation, but it is the wrong kind of citation — it gets cited as "Gruber & McFarnell 2026 found FMT's affect-agency dissociation prediction was not supported," and future FMT submissions arrive with that millstone. Probability is non-trivial: FMT predictions are derived from theory not from McFarnell's existing data, and McFarnell's prior framework (ACU) leans toward the broad-attenuation pattern.
- **3rd collaborator never recruited.** AIW-59 explicitly notes "need to recruit 3rd collaborator with participant pool." McFarnell is at Australian Catholic — small participant pool, no fMRI scanner. Without a 3rd collaborator with infrastructure, the registered report stalls at design stage indefinitely. Cortex IPA review takes 3–6 months even with full team; without recruitment, Stage-1 never submits. Detection signal: 4 weeks past McFarnell reply, no 3rd-author candidate identified.
- **McFarnell can't get IRB through ACU.** Independent of the design, single-PI ACU review boards have backed up severely in 2025–26. A 4–6 month IRB delay pushes Stage-1 submission past September, meaning no 2026 peer-reviewed FMT citation from this route.

### A3. JCS submission (AIW-46)
- **Accepted with R&R asking for empirical work Matthias can't run.** JCS is theoretical-friendly but the editorial culture (Imprint Academic, Horswell) sometimes returns "interesting, but please cite/respond to [3 papers Matthias hasn't read]." Each round = ~10 hours of reading + revision. F's audit budgets ~3–4 hours total for JCS as "fire-and-forget." If JCS comes back with substantive R&R, the bandwidth required violates the single-track May/June rule.
- **9000-word ceiling violation reintroduced in revision.** Reviewers commonly ask for additional content (more engagement with X, more discussion of Y). Matthias adds 1500 words to satisfy reviewers, paper goes from 9000 to 10500, JCS rejects on length grounds.

### A4. Salami-slice short report (AIW-47)
- **Reviewer asks "where's the original theory paper?" and finds five desk rejections.** A Cognition or NeuroImage:Reports reviewer doing due diligence on a "criticality-binding link" short report will search for the underlying theory and find a Zenodo preprint with no peer-reviewed home. The five-rejection trail is now a Google Scholar / Semantic Scholar artifact. Reviewers form opinions from search results before reading.
- **Effect-size prediction commits FMT to a falsifiable number that's wrong.** AIW-47 specifies "specific paradigm → specific brain measurement → effect-size prediction." If the prediction is calibrated from Tucker/Friston 2025 + Toker 2026 (both published in venues with selection effects), the actual replication effect size is smaller. The short report goes out with d=0.6, real effect is d=0.2, FMT loses credibility on the criticality claim that was supposed to be its strongest empirical anchor.

### A5. Entropy SI (if attempted)
- **Acceptance + APC pulls budget.** CHF 2,600 ≈ €2,750. Matthias is paying out of pocket. Once accepted, retracting over APC looks worse than not submitting. The financial commitment is the failure mode: the paper publishes in a JUFO Level-0 journal that senior consciousness researchers (Melloni, Seth, Chalmers) explicitly track as a quality signal. **The "first peer-reviewed citation" becomes a citation in a venue the target audience discounts**, which is worse than no citation because it's now load-bearing in CV but not in reputation.
- **Reviewers in MDPI's pool include FMT competitors.** Entropy's reviewer base for criticality-consciousness includes Tucker/Luu/Friston/Tagliazucchi/Chialvo. FMT criticality framing is partially derivative of their work; a reviewer who feels Gruber 2015 is a derivative of Friston 2010 has every incentive to reject in a venue with no editorial buffer.

### A6. Stage-2 conflict scenario (registered report dependency)
The general principle for any registered report: **Stage-1 acceptance creates an obligation to publish Stage-2 results.** The Open Science Framework registered-report contract means Matthias cannot withdraw if results disconfirm. This is the *whole point* of registered reports, and it's the right ethical commitment, but it means McFarnell's experiment is a **two-sided bet, not a one-sided opportunity.** Plan Architect must price this.

## E. Reputational / strategic risks

### E1. Entropy publication harms more than helps
**Yes.** A first peer-reviewed FMT citation in a JUFO Level-0 / CAS-removed journal does not function as the credibility-building asset the dissemination strategy requires. C-critique makes this explicit: "CHF 2,600 buys peer-review certification the target audience discounts." Senior consciousness researchers (Melloni, Seth, Chalmers, Friston) track venue tier — a paper in Entropy reads to them as "couldn't get into a Q1 venue." The Zenodo preprint already provides citability without the venue baggage.

**Worse:** if Entropy paper is published *while* BBS commentary is in review, the Entropy paper becomes the load-bearing publication on Matthias's CV at the moment Bochum/AICE-26 attendees Google him. First impression = MDPI venue.

**Recommendation:** Entropy should be Option E or F, not Option C/D. If used at all, only after BBS + McFarnell + JCS have all returned (positive or negative) and the preprint pipeline is empty.

### E2. Bach DM produces nothing
If the Bach DM channel goes silent without producing substantive content (a co-authored post, a public mention, an introduction), the *signal* sent to anyone watching (and 67.4K-follower accounts have watchers) is "Matthias got into Bach's DMs and Bach didn't engage further." This reads as more damaging than no DM at all because it implies Matthias was evaluated and dismissed. **Mitigation:** the DM doesn't have to "produce" anything visible — it just needs to remain warm. If Bach goes radio silent for 4+ weeks, do not re-DM. Accept it as ambient reputation maintenance and re-engage publicly on a *new* Bach post months later.

### E3. 7th desk rejection (if salami-slice attempted at the wrong venue)
The current count is 6 desk rejections (5 FMT + 1 RIM) across 5 journals. A 7th rejection — especially if it's on the salami-slice with new content in a venue Matthias picked specifically to avoid the pattern — is not just one more data point. It becomes the breaking-point signal that the rejection pattern is *content-related*, not packaging-related. The Session 184 strategy pivot was built on "packaging not content." A 7th rejection on a deliberately re-packaged short report breaks that hypothesis. **Mitigation:** salami-slice must go to a venue with structurally different acceptance dynamics (BBS-style commentary, McFarnell-co-authored RR, or a venue where a contact has signaled openness like JAIC via Kanai). Cold submission of salami-slice to Cognition/TICS Forum carries the same desk-reject risk as the full paper did.

### E4. Five-rejection-trail problem on Google Scholar
Independent of new submissions, the existing rejection trail is becoming a Google Scholar / Semantic Scholar discoverable artifact (Zenodo versions, conference abstract archives, the arXiv endorsement page). Reviewers doing diligence on any new FMT submission can find it. This is a *passive* reputational risk that grows with each new venue, regardless of whether new submissions land.

### E5. AICE-26 acceptance creates co-author obligation
If AICE-26 is accepted and Torrance offers post-conference collaboration (he already insisted on in-person attendance — that's a relationship investment), declining or dragging that collaboration damages a warm institutional contact. **Mitigation:** preview a likely Torrance ask (post-Brighton paper, chapter contribution, future workshop) and decide acceptance criteria *before* arriving in Brighton.

## Bottom line

The FMT effort May–Sep 2026 has more single-points-of-failure than capacity to recover from any individual failure. The dominant risks are:

- **Relationship-layer fragility** (5 of 7 warm contacts at or past freshness window — McFarnell is the most urgent)
- **Capacity collisions** that the 78-hour budget can't absorb (Ivoclar, Bochum follow-up, AICE expansion)
- **Strategic risk of low-tier venue acceptance** (Entropy in particular) producing reputation drag that exceeds citation gain
- **Stage-2 disconfirmation risk** in any registered report — the underweighted theoretical-interesting-but-strategically-bad case

The Plan Architect's plan should be evaluated not on whether it can succeed in the optimistic case, but on whether it survives any *one* of the top 5 failure modes above. If the plan requires more than 2 of the priority stack items to succeed for 2026 to count as a win, it is too brittle.
