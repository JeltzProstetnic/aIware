# Book Mining: Die Emergenz des Bewusstseins (2015) for AICE-26 Review Gaps

**Source:** Gruber, M. (2015). *Die Emergenz des Bewusstseins.* Lulu Press (self-published). 299 pp.
**Target:** AICE-26 reviewer feedback — 5 identified gaps in the English paper.
**Date:** 2026-05-11

---

## GAP 1 (HIGH): The Spreadsheet/Transistor Analogy

**Reviewer criticism:** The paper says "no transistor contains a sum" to argue qualia are incoherent at the substrate level. But the FULL COLLECTION of transistors CAN be said to implement the spreadsheet. Does the book address the part/whole vs. level/level distinction?

### Does the book address it?

**Partially, but through a different route.** The book does NOT use the spreadsheet/transistor analogy — that was developed for the English paper. The book's approach to the same issue is more nuanced and actually stronger in some respects.

### Key material found:

#### (a) The Computer Analogy critique (Ch. 5.2, pp. 104-106)
The book argues forcefully that the brain-as-computer analogy is fundamentally misleading, but then reclaims a LIMITED version of it for the cortical automaton:

> "Die Tätigkeit eines neuralen Netzes mit der Tätigkeit eines herkömmlichen Computers vergleicht, schnell disqualifiziert." (p. 286)
> [Anyone who directly compares the activity of a neural net with the activity of a conventional computer is quickly disqualified.]

But crucially:

> "Der kortikale Automat, also die Tätigkeit unseres Großhirnes, ist aber in der Lage, eine Art Computer zu 'simulieren' — einige Teile unseres Verstandes und unseres Bewusstseins SIND annähernd Turing vollständige Computer (allerdings mit einer recht hohen Fehlerrate, und sie existieren nur temporär und fallen nach Gebrauch stets wieder in sich zusammen)." (p. 286)
> [The cortical automaton, i.e. the activity of our cerebrum, IS capable of "simulating" a kind of computer — some parts of our understanding and our consciousness ARE approximately Turing-complete computers (albeit with a rather high error rate, and they exist only temporarily and collapse back into themselves after use).]

**This is key**: The book distinguishes between the SUBSTRATE (neural net) and the VIRTUAL SYSTEM (cortical automaton). The substrate is NOT a computer; the virtual system temporarily approximates one. This is a more careful formulation than "no transistor contains a sum."

#### (b) The cortical automaton and level distinction (Ch. 12, pp. 278-283)
The book's most powerful material for this gap comes from the cortical automaton concept:

> "Dieses scheinbar chaotische Muster der Feuervorgänge unserer Neuronen ist in Wirklichkeit ein sehr ausgeklügelter Apparat, der rechnet, denkt und intelligent durch ein Leben führt. Er ist aber nicht das Bewusstsein! Das Bewusstsein ist nur ein Effekt, der durch das Wechselspiel zwischen diesem kortikalen Automaten und dem Kortex selbst entsteht, wenn die Bedingungen dazu günstig sind." (p. 280)
> [This seemingly chaotic pattern of our neurons' firing is in reality a very sophisticated apparatus that computes, thinks, and intelligently navigates through a life. But it is NOT consciousness! Consciousness is only an effect that arises through the interplay between this cortical automaton and the cortex itself, when conditions are favorable.]

**This addresses the reviewer's objection directly**: The book explicitly says the WHOLE SYSTEM (all neurons firing together = the cortical automaton) is still not consciousness. Consciousness emerges from the INTERPLAY between the virtual system and the substrate. This is exactly the level/level distinction the reviewer wants — it's not about parts vs. whole, it's about the substrate level vs. the computational level, even when you consider the substrate as a whole.

#### (c) The five-system hierarchy as defense (Ch. 12, pp. 277-280)
The book lists eight fundamental principles and describes how they combine to produce the cortical automaton:

> "Die Feuervorgänge des Großhirnes zeichnen nämlich ein abstraktes dreidimensionales Muster, das sich ständig verändert, und sozusagen auf der Kortexfläche 'herumtanzt'. Das Muster ist aber eigentlich mehr als dreidimensional, denn es existiert nicht direkt im dreidimensionalen Raum, sondern in dem hochdimensionalen Raum, der von den Neuronen, Dendriten und Axonen aufgespannt wird." (p. 278)
> [The firing events of the cerebrum trace an abstract three-dimensional pattern that constantly changes and, so to speak, "dances around" on the cortical surface. But the pattern is actually more than three-dimensional, because it doesn't exist directly in three-dimensional space but in the high-dimensional space spanned by the neurons, dendrites, and axons.]

The analogy here is to looking at a hologram: close up you see only interference patterns (= neurons), but at the right distance/level you see the 3D image (= consciousness). **The whole collection of interference fringes doesn't "contain" the 3D image at the fringe level — the image is a property of the optical level.**

### Integration recommendation:
The paper should SUPPLEMENT (not replace) the spreadsheet analogy with:
1. **Explicit acknowledgment** that the full collection of transistors does implement the spreadsheet — at the computational level, not at the transistor level. The point is not part vs. whole but level vs. level.
2. **The cortical automaton argument**: Even considering ALL neurons firing together (the whole substrate), this is still not consciousness — consciousness is the interplay between the virtual pattern and the substrate that generates it.
3. **The hologram analogy from the book**: The whole holographic plate doesn't "contain" the 3D image at the plate level — the image is a property of the optical reconstruction level.

## GAP 2 (HIGH): Operational Definitions

**Reviewer criticism:** The paper uses "self-simulation," "self-referential closure," "model," "implicit," "explicit" without operational definitions. Does the book define these more precisely?

### Does the book address it?

**YES — extensively.** Chapter 3 (pp. 10-69) is entirely devoted to definitions, and Chapter 3.4 (pp. 55-69) provides careful, progressive definitions of consciousness and its constituent concepts. This is by far the richest vein of minable material.

### Key definitions found:

#### (a) "Consciousness" — formal definition (p. 57)

> **"Das Bewusstsein ist die Fähigkeit einer Entität (Lebewesen oder Maschine) ein Modell seiner selbst ('Selbstmodell') zu erzeugen, es auf sich selbst zu beziehen und damit zu interagieren."**
> [**Consciousness is the ability of an entity (living being or machine) to create a model of itself ("self-model"), to relate it to itself, and to interact with it.**]

And the reformulation (same page):

> **"Die Bewusstheit ist die fortlaufende Interaktion mit einem Modell, welches eine Entität von sich selbst erzeugt und auf sich selbst bezieht."**
> [**Consciousness (as ongoing state) is the ongoing interaction with a model that an entity generates from itself and relates to itself.**]

#### (b) "Model" — operational definition (p. 59)

> "Und mit 'Modell' ist an sich auch nur ein nicht-triviales Bezugssystem gemeint."
> [And by "model" what is simply meant is a non-trivial reference system.]

The book then specifies that a minimal model requires: (1) it must be placed in a reference system with the system itself; (2) the system must be able to recognize the boundary between itself and the rest of the world; (3) the boundary need not be conscious.

#### (c) The four models — operational definitions (p. 61)

> - "Das **Selbstmodell** ist ein Modell, mit welchem sich eine Entität selbst abbildet ('Schreibzugriff'), und welches die Entität interpretieren kann ('Lesezugriff'). (Achtung: unbewusstes Wissen!)"
> [The **Self-model** is a model with which an entity maps itself ("write access"), and which the entity can interpret ("read access"). (Note: unconscious knowledge!)]
>
> - "Das **Metamodell** ist eine Abbildung all dessen, was eine Entität abbilden und interpretieren kann."
> [The **Meta-model** is a mapping of everything that an entity can map and interpret.]
>
> - "Das **Ich-Modell** ist die Abbildung eines Selbstmodells, welches dem Bewusstsein zur Verfügung steht (also bewusst werden kann). [...] Das Ich-Modell ist eine explizite Abbildung des Selbstmodells."
> [The **I-model** is the mapping of a self-model that is available to consciousness (i.e., can become conscious). [...] The I-model is an explicit mapping of the self-model.]
>
> - "Das **Weltmodell** ist eine Abbildung des Metamodells, welche dem Bewusstsein zur Verfügung steht (also bewusst werden kann). [...] Das Weltmodell ist eine explizite Abbildung des Metamodells."
> [The **World-model** is a mapping of the meta-model that is available to consciousness (i.e., can become conscious). [...] The world-model is an explicit mapping of the meta-model.]

#### (d) "Implicit" and "Explicit" — operational definitions (p. 61)

> **"Bewusst wird eine Information dann, wenn sie vom Selbstmodell in das Weltmodell überführt wird."**
> [**An information becomes conscious when it is transferred from the self-model into the world-model.**]

And the key distinguishing criterion:

> The **Metamodell** (= implicit side) is "real, erlernt, implizit" [real, learned, implicit] — it consists of synaptic weights stored in the cortex, is robust, stable under short-term plasticity, and can only be significantly changed through long-term plasticity.
>
> The **Weltmodell** (= explicit side) is "virtuell, simuliert, explizit" [virtual, simulated, explicit] — it consists of transient electrochemical activity, has no permanent physical substrate, can be disrupted by magnetic fields or simple illusions.

These are operational: one is measured by structural imaging (connectivity, weights), the other by functional imaging (transient activity patterns).

#### (e) "Self-referential closure" — implicit in the progressive definition (pp. 59-62)

The book defines consciousness progressively:
1. **Simple consciousness**: Self-model + Meta-model + relationship between them
2. **Simply extended**: Meta-model observes the self-model
3. **Doubly extended**: The system observes its own observation of the self-model
4. **Triply extended**: The system observes itself observing itself observing...

Self-referential closure is achieved at "simply extended" level and above — it is operationally defined as "the meta-model's observation of the self-model is mapped within the self-model itself."

#### (f) "Conscious" as adjective — operational criterion (p. 57)

> "Nur Vorgänge, die mit diesem **Selbstmodell** interagieren, können laut dieser Definition auch 'bewusst' werden."
> [Only processes that interact with this **self-model** can, according to this definition, become "conscious."]

And more specifically (p. 61):
> "Es wird also etwas nur bewusst, wenn es vom Selbstmodell in explizites Wissen verwandelt wird, wobei das Bezugssystem ein Modell sein muss, welches dem Selbstmodell zugänglich ist."
> [Something only becomes conscious when it is transformed from the self-model into explicit knowledge, where the reference system must be a model accessible to the self-model.]

### Integration recommendation:
This is the most valuable material for the paper revision. The paper should add an "Operational Definitions" subsection (possibly as 3.1.1 or a table) that provides:
1. **Consciousness**: The ability to create a self-model, relate it to itself, and interact with it
2. **Model**: A non-trivial reference system (specify: requires write access + read access + boundary recognition)
3. **Implicit/Substrate-level**: Stored in physical connectivity (synaptic weights); measurable via structural imaging; robust to short-term perturbation; modifiable only through long-term plasticity
4. **Explicit/Simulation-level**: Transient electrochemical activity patterns; measurable via functional imaging (fMRI, EEG); disrupted by magnetic fields, anesthetics, simple illusions
5. **"Becomes conscious"**: Transfer from self-model to world-model (implicit → explicit)
6. **Self-referential closure**: The system's model of itself includes the modeling process — operationally, the meta-model's observation of the self-model is itself mapped within the models

## GAP 3 (MEDIUM): Implicit/Learned ↔ Explicit/Simulated Linkage

**Reviewer criticism:** Why must "implicit" mean "learned" and "explicit" mean "simulated"? Why is stored/structural knowledge necessarily non-conscious while generated/transient processes are phenomenal?

### Does the book address it?

**YES — with a neurological grounding argument.** The book doesn't just assert the pairing; it derives it from the physical properties of neural storage.

### Key material found:

#### (a) The physical basis of the pairing (Ch. 8.5, pp. 264-265)

> "Das Metamodell (mit dem Selbstmodell) ist ein reales, und relativ robustes Abbild der Welt, das vom Kortex antrainiert wird, also von den Informationen, die er von den darunterliegenden Kernen erhält. Das Metamodell hat somit ein physisches Substrat in den Gewichtungen der Neuronenverbindungen der Gehirnrinde. Man kann Teile davon selektiv beeinflussen oder zerstören [...] aber solange man den Kortex nicht physisch beschädigt, bleibt es großteils sehr stabil."
> [The meta-model (with the self-model) is a real, and relatively robust image of the world that is trained by the cortex, i.e., from the information it receives from the underlying nuclei. The meta-model thus has a physical substrate in the weightings of the neuronal connections of the cerebral cortex. One can selectively influence or destroy parts of it [...] but as long as one does not physically damage the cortex, it remains largely very stable.]

> "Das Weltmodell (mit dem Ich-Modell) ist ein virtuelles, und relativ instabiles Abbild der Welt, das vom Kortex erzeugt (simuliert) werden kann. Es wird von Projektionen [...] die sich aus dem Metamodell ergeben, erzeugt. Es hat somit selbst kein dauerhaftes physisches Substrat, denn es besteht nur aus transienter elektrochemischer Aktivität des Kortex. Es kann sogar durch ein starkes Magnetfeld beeinträchtigt werden, oder durch einfache Illusionen und Partytricks signifikant manipuliert werden."
> [The world-model (with the I-model) is a virtual, and relatively unstable image of the world that can be generated (simulated) by the cortex. It is generated from projections [...] that arise from the meta-model. It therefore has no permanent physical substrate itself, because it consists only of transient electrochemical activity of the cortex. It can even be impaired by a strong magnetic field, or significantly manipulated by simple illusions and party tricks.]

#### (b) Why learned = non-conscious (structural argument) (pp. 63, 260)

> **"Aber Achtung: das Selbstmodell an sich ist nie bewusst und kann nie bewusst sein!"** (p. 63)
> [**But attention: the self-model per se is never conscious and can never be conscious!**]

The argument: The self-model IS the thing doing the observing. It cannot observe itself directly — that would require an infinite regress. We only access it indirectly through the I-model (explicit self-model). This is not an arbitrary pairing but a logical necessity: the structural knowledge base cannot simultaneously BE the reference frame AND appear within the reference frame.

#### (c) The behavioral gradient argument (Ch. 8.3, p. 235)

The book provides a crucial diagram (p. 235) showing a gradient of behavioral complexity:
- Chemical gradients (no behavior in the proper sense)
- Conditioned behavior — modulated by "implicit memory"
- Goal-directed behavior — OVERLAP ZONE
- Template-based behavior — OVERLAP ZONE
- Rule-based behavior — modulated by "explicit memory"
- Conscious behavior

The implicit and explicit memory systems overlap precisely in the middle of the gradient. This is not a stipulation — it follows from the fact that behavior at intermediate levels can be driven by either system depending on attentional state.

#### (d) Cognitive learning requires consciousness (p. 242)

> "Beim **bewussten Lernen** muss der Mensch einen Weg finden, die Salienz, die zum Abspeichern von Bewusstseinsinhalten nötig ist, selbst zu erzeugen [...] Er muss sich also *bewusst* konzentrieren, Dinge *bewusst* im Kopf wiederholen [...] und sich *bewusst* bestimmter weiterer Tricks bedienen, die er kennt."
> [For **conscious learning**, a person must find a way to generate the salience needed for storing consciousness contents themselves [...] They must *consciously* concentrate, *consciously* repeat things in their head [...] and *consciously* use certain further tricks they know.]

This establishes that the implicit-explicit pairing maps onto different memory systems with different storage mechanisms and different learning requirements.

### Integration recommendation:
Add a paragraph in Section 3.3 (The Real/Virtual Split) explicitly arguing:
1. The pairing is not stipulated but follows from physical properties: structural (weights, connectivity) vs. dynamic (transient activity)
2. The structural side CANNOT be conscious because it is the reference frame from which consciousness is generated — appearing within consciousness would require infinite regress
3. The transient/dynamic side IS consciousness because conscious experience is itself a transient, generated process — this is empirically confirmed by the fact that anesthesia (which suppresses dynamic activity without destroying structural connectivity) eliminates consciousness
4. Cite the behavioral gradient showing the overlap zone as empirical grounding

## GAP 4 (MEDIUM): Animal Consciousness Evidence

**Reviewer criticism:** The paper claims animals have the four-model architecture but provides little evidence for software-like properties (forking, redirection, etc.) in non-human species.

### Does the book address it?

**PARTIALLY.** The book discusses animal consciousness in several places but does not provide the detailed evidence for software-like properties that the reviewer wants.

### Key material found:

#### (a) Animal consciousness assertion (Ch. 5.1, p. 103-104)

> "Was das Bewusstsein betrifft, so hat zumindest der Bonobo Zwergschimpanse namens Kanzi das Vorhandensein eines solchen meiner Meinung nach eindrucksvoll bewiesen."
> [As for consciousness, at least the Bonobo dwarf chimpanzee named Kanzi has impressively demonstrated its presence, in my opinion.]

The book cites Kanzi's mirror self-recognition, vocabulary of self-invented words, empathy with other primates, and complex social behavior. However, this is primarily presented as evidence FOR consciousness in primates, not as evidence for the specific four-model architecture.

#### (b) The consciousness-in-primates timeline (p. 104)

> "Wenn wir also darauf beharren wollen, dass nur Menschen ein Bewusstsein haben, sind wir auf der Suche nach dem Bewusstsein auf diejenigen Forscher angewiesen, die immer noch krampfhaft nach einem systematischen Unterschied zwischen unseren Gehirnen und denen der anderen Primaten suchen [...] Nach meiner Theorie wird man diesen frühestens am 36. August finden."
> [If we insist that only humans have consciousness, we are dependent on those researchers who still desperately seek a systematic difference between our brains and those of other primates [...] According to my theory, this will be found no earlier than August 36th.]

This is witty but not evidence.

#### (c) Evolutionary role of consciousness (Ch. 10, pp. 271-273)

> "Das, was wir als Intelligenz und Bewusstsein im Alltagssinn verstehen, ist ohne mehrjährige Erziehung durch Menschen nicht möglich."
> [What we understand as intelligence and consciousness in the everyday sense is not possible without years of education by humans.]

This discusses the role of upbringing in HUMAN consciousness but argues the basic architecture is shared with other mammals (via the six-layer cortex). The Baldwin Effect is discussed as the mechanism by which learned behaviors can become genetically predisposed.

#### (d) The qualia claim for animals (Ch. 5.4, p. 112)

> "Das Qualiaproblem ist aber meiner Ansicht nach keines. Ein Zombie hätte keine, wenn es einen gäbe. Eine Fledermaus hat sie vermutlich; ich vermute, dass zumindest die meisten Säuger und Vögel, und vielleicht sogar gewisse Reptilien ein Bewusstsein haben."
> [The qualia problem is, in my view, not a problem. A zombie would have none, if there were one. A bat probably has them; I suspect that at least most mammals and birds, and perhaps even certain reptiles, have consciousness.]

#### (e) Graduated consciousness in animals (Ch. 4.5, p. 92)

> "Man benötigt ein explizites Weltmodell und ein Ich-Modell. Allerdings heißt das nicht, dass höher entwickelte Tiere dies nicht leisten können. Dazu aber mehr in einem späteren Kapitel."
> [One needs an explicit world-model and an I-model. However, this does not mean that more highly developed animals cannot achieve this. But more on this in a later chapter.]

### What's missing:
The book does NOT provide:
- Evidence for dream-like forking in animals
- Evidence for ESM redirection in non-human species
- Specific empirical evidence mapping the four-model architecture to animal brains
- Discussion of split-brain experiments in animals as evidence for virtual cloning

### Integration recommendation:
This gap cannot be fully closed from the book alone. The paper revision should:
1. Cite the graduated consciousness argument more explicitly — the theory PREDICTS different species have different depths of self-modeling
2. Add references to empirical animal consciousness literature published since 2015 (e.g., the Cambridge Declaration on Consciousness 2012, corvid consciousness studies, octopus self-recognition)
3. Be more careful in the claims: the theory predicts WHAT PROPERTIES animals should have based on their cortical architecture, but software-like properties like forking require the full architecture and may be limited to species with certain cortical complexity

## GAP 5 (MEDIUM): Novelty Claim

**Reviewer criticism:** The paper self-identifies as "Dennett + Metzinger + neural networks." A reviewer called it an "unstable combination of familiar ideas." What does the book claim is genuinely new?

### Does the book address it?

**YES — in several places.** The book is more explicit about what is novel than the English paper.

### Key material found:

#### (a) The MDM + SMT synthesis claim (Ch. 8.5, pp. 258, 261)

> "Insgesamt liest sich die Arbeit zum SMT wie ein stark von Computeranalogien geprägtes Modell, obwohl Metzinger die Unterschiede zwischen Mensch und Maschine gezielt betont."
> [Overall, the work on SMT reads like a model strongly shaped by computer analogies, although Metzinger specifically emphasizes the differences between human and machine.]

> "Mein Modell des Bewusstseins könnte man bis hierher also vereinfacht dargestellt als eine Schnittmenge aus dem Multiple Drafts Model (MDM) und der Selbstmodelltheorie (SMT) bezeichnen, beruhend auf einer Permutation von sechsschichtigen neuronalen Netzen. Damit bleibt aber noch einiges ungeklärt."
> [My model of consciousness could be described up to this point, in simplified terms, as an intersection of the Multiple Drafts Model (MDM) and the Self-Model Theory (SMT), based on a permutation of six-layer neural networks. But this still leaves several things unexplained.]

**The key phrase is "damit bleibt aber noch einiges ungeklärt"** — the book explicitly says the MDM+SMT combination is only the starting point, and then adds what is genuinely novel.

#### (b) What the book claims is novel — the cortical automaton (Ch. 12, pp. 278-280)

The book identifies a specific emergent effect that "has not been mentioned before and is also not unknown per se":

> "Darüber hinaus gibt es aber noch einen emergenten Effekt, der bisher nicht erwähnt wurde, und in auch in keiner anderen mir bekannten Theorie so beschrieben wurde. Dieser Effekt kommt durch das Feuern der Kortexneuronen selbst zustande, und man kann ihn indirekt auf fMRI-Aufnahmen deutlich sehen."
> [Beyond this, there is yet another emergent effect that has not been mentioned before, and has not been described this way in any other theory known to me. This effect comes about through the firing of the cortex neurons themselves, and one can see it indirectly on fMRI images clearly.]

This is the cortical automaton — the high-dimensional pattern of neural firing that "dances" across the cortical surface and constitutes the virtual system. The book claims this specific concept — the identification of consciousness with the interplay between this virtual automaton and the physical substrate — is the genuinely novel contribution.

#### (c) The dissolution of the Hard Problem via levels (Ch. 8.5, pp. 261-263)

> "Man muss ein **explizites Bewusstsein** (Ich-Modell, und als Teil davon auch die Subjektivität) von einem **impliziten Bewusstsein** (Selbstmodell) unterscheiden, sowie zwischen einem tatsächlichen (**aktiven**) Bewusstsein (**Frames**) und einem **latenten** (**Modelle**)."
> [One must distinguish an **explicit consciousness** (I-model, and as part of it subjectivity) from an **implicit consciousness** (self-model), as well as between an actual (**active**) consciousness (**Frames**) and a **latent** one (**Models**).]

The real/virtual split — the claim that qualia exist at the virtual level and are categorically absent at the substrate level — is presented as the specific mechanism that dissolves the Hard Problem. Neither Dennett (who denies qualia are real) nor Metzinger (who doesn't claim to dissolve the Hard Problem) provides this.

#### (d) The five novel elements (synthesized from multiple chapters):

1. **The cortical automaton concept** (Ch. 12): A concrete physical interpretation of how consciousness arises — not in individual neurons, not in the whole brain, but in the high-dimensional pattern of activity that IS a cellular automaton operating at criticality. No other theory describes this specific mechanism.

2. **The real/virtual level distinction applied to qualia** (Ch. 8.5, p. 262): Qualia are virtual constructs — real at the computational level but incoherent at the substrate level. This is not Dennett (who says qualia are illusions) and not Metzinger (who says the self-model is transparent). It's a third position: qualia are real but level-specific.

3. **The criticality requirement derived from Wolfram** (Ch. 4.2, pp. 72-80; Ch. 12): The book derives the edge-of-chaos requirement from computational theory (Wolfram's Class 4), not from neuroscience. This was done before the large-scale empirical confirmation.

4. **The variable implicit-explicit permeability mechanism** (Ch. 8.5, pp. 262-263; Ch. 8.4, pp. 245-246): The book provides the mechanism for altered states (psychedelics, dreams, meditation) by varying the boundary between implicit and explicit processing — a unified mechanism for diverse phenomena that neither MDM nor SMT provides.

5. **The graduated consciousness hierarchy** (Ch. 3.4, pp. 57-62): The progressive definition from simple to triply extended consciousness via recursive self-modeling depth — providing a principled continuum rather than a binary.

### Integration recommendation:
The paper should revise Section 1.3 (Overview and Historical Context) to be more assertive about novelty:
1. **Acknowledge the ancestry explicitly** but frame it correctly: MDM + SMT are the STARTING POINT, not the destination
2. **State the five novel contributions** as a numbered list
3. **For each, state what it provides that the predecessors do not**: Dennett doesn't dissolve the Hard Problem (he dissolves qualia instead); Metzinger doesn't provide a mechanism for altered states or criticality; neural network theory alone doesn't address consciousness
4. **Emphasize the cortical automaton** as the genuinely original concept — the identification of consciousness with a specific physical phenomenon (high-dimensional activity pattern operating as a Class 4 cellular automaton)

## Cross-Cutting Observation

The 2015 book's greatest advantage over the English paper is that it has SPACE. The book devotes 60 pages to definitions before introducing the theory, builds the conceptual foundations across 120 pages before arriving at Chapter 12's synthesis, and lets arguments breathe. The English paper, compressed to ~16,000 words, necessarily lost much of this grounding.

The most efficient integration strategy is NOT to expand the paper massively, but to:
1. Add an operational definitions table (from GAP 2 material) — ~300 words
2. Add one paragraph on the level/level vs. part/whole distinction (from GAP 1 material) — ~150 words
3. Add one paragraph on the physical basis of implicit=non-conscious (from GAP 3 material) — ~200 words
4. Revise the novelty framing in the introduction (from GAP 5 material) — ~200 words
5. Add a note on animal consciousness limitations (from GAP 4) — ~100 words

Total: ~950 additional words, addressing all five gaps.
