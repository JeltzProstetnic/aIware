## The Architecture Seth Needs Is Substrate-Neutral

Seth (2025) names a real problem: substrate-independence claims about machine consciousness are vacuous without a principled specification of what an implementing system must do. His remedy — biological naturalism — is the wrong shape of answer, because the theoretical machinery he relies on is not biological. Predictive processing, active inference, and Markov blankets are mathematical formalisms defined over probability distributions. When Seth specifies the "right kind of causal architecture" — recurrence, integration, autopoietic closure, prediction-error minimisation — every property he names is computational. The qualifier "biological" rides along; the explanatory work is done by "the right architecture." The specification Seth correctly demands already exists in computational form. This commentary argues the Four-Model Theory (FMT; Gruber 2015, 2026) is one such candidate, and that recent convergence from the active-inference camp itself supports an architectural rather than substrate-specific answer.

**The architecture, briefly.** FMT identifies consciousness with a self-referential simulation generated by two kinds of models running in parallel — world-directed and self-directed — each with an implicit (learned, predictive, non-phenomenal) and an explicit (simulated, phenomenal) mode. The four-fold structure is a 2×2 of model *kinds*, not four discrete brain modules. The substrate must operate at criticality (the edge of chaos between ordered and disordered dynamics) for the explicit modes to sustain coherent self-simulation. The specification is substrate-neutral by construction: it says what a system must compute and what dynamical regime its substrate must occupy, not what the substrate must be made of.

**Convergence from inside Seth's own framework.** Laukkonen, Friston and Chandaria's *Beautiful Loop* (2025) derives a self-modelling, epistemically-deep architecture from active inference: a world-simulation that knows it is a model. This is independent convergence on FMT's core structural claim — self-referential simulation as constitutive of consciousness — from the very tradition Seth endorses. Friston is Seth's own theorist. If self-simulation is what active inference says consciousness is, the work is being done by the *loop*, not by the carbon.

**The criticality requirement.** FMT derived a theoretical requirement for substrate criticality in 2015, before the empirical consolidation. Recent work has independently confirmed it from multiple directions, none implicating biological specificity. Toker et al. (2026) identify circuit-level mechanisms of unconsciousness — interpretable as subcritical pushes — in adversarial AI trained on 680,000 electrophysiological recordings. Tucker, Luu and Friston (2025) ground consciousness in excitatory–inhibitory balance maintaining cortical networks at the critical transition. Algom and Shriki's ConCrit framework (2026) reviews 140+ datasets and shows criticality unifying leading consciousness theories. Bhatt et al. (2024) demonstrate that sleep restores cortical criticality on a continuous-recording timescale. The convergent property is dynamical, not material.

**Friston's von Neumann objection, levelled.** A natural rejoinder is Friston's argument that sentience is impossible on standard von Neumann architectures because memory and processing are separated. This is a Marr-3 (implementational) objection to a Marr-1 (computational) claim. Substrate-neutrality at the computational level is consistent with substrate constraints at the implementational level. Seth and FMT diverge on a different axis. Seth licenses a specific set of biological properties as necessary for the implementation; FMT licenses any substrate that can sustain the four-model architecture at criticality. If the empirical work above is right, the binding constraint is the dynamical regime, not the chemistry that produces it.

**The bootstrapping problem Seth inherits.** Beni (2026) identifies a methodological circularity in marker-based approaches to machine consciousness: markers are calibrated on human neurocognition and so detect the human implementation, not consciousness as such. Seth's "biological causal powers" are identified through these markers; the markers presuppose the substrate they are taken to vindicate. Breaking the circle requires specifying consciousness above both substrate and markers — at the architectural level. Biology cannot itself supply that exit.

**Biological counter-cases.** Corvids show flexible cognition, planning, and mirror self-recognition without a neocortex; their pallium is organised in nuclear clusters (Güntürkün and Bugnyar 2016). Cephalopods show problem-solving on a radically different nervous-system plan. If "biological" means "neocortical," these refute it. If "biological" means "alive," the position drifts toward vitalism. Locked-in patients exhibit full consciousness with zero embodied agency, blocking Seth's strongest version of the embodiment requirement. The architectural reading absorbs all three: the relevant property is the simulation at criticality, not the body that hosts it.

**What changes if Seth is right about the problem and wrong about the answer.** Seth's caution about premature attribution of consciousness to current AI systems is methodologically sound. Current LLMs lack the implicit/explicit distinction, lack a self-directed model coupled to a world-directed one, and do not run on substrates near a known critical regime. They are not architectures of the relevant kind, regardless of the substrate they run on. Conversely, ruling out artificial consciousness *in principle* on biological grounds is a stronger claim than the active-inference framework — by Friston's own collaborators — currently supports. The disciplined position is Seth's epistemic humility paired with an architectural specification. FMT supplies a candidate. Whether it is the right candidate is a question to be settled by the empirical work that is, encouragingly, already underway.

## Red-team checklist

1. **"Isn't FMT just IIT/GWT under a new name?"** No: FMT specifies the *content* structure (2×2 of model kinds, implicit/explicit, world/self) plus a dynamical regime (criticality). IIT specifies an information-integration measure; GWT specifies a broadcast architecture. FMT is orthogonal to both.

2. **"Where is the formal model?"** Acknowledged in Gruber (2026) as an open item; current paper specifies architecture and dynamical constraints. The commentary is not the venue for formalisation; it argues that *some* architectural specification is what Seth's framework needs.

3. **"Friston himself denies substrate independence."** Yes, in interviews; less so in the formal *Beautiful Loop* paper, which derives an active-inference architecture without committing to a specific substrate. Cited accurately.

4. **"Aren't corvid/cephalopod cases compatible with biological naturalism if 'biology' is read broadly enough?"** Yes — but only if "biological" is loosened to "any substrate that can run the relevant computation," at which point the position collapses into the architectural one.

5. **"The criticality requirement is post-hoc fitted to the 2024–2026 evidence."** No: the requirement is in Gruber (2015) and derived theoretically from Wolfram-class dynamics, *before* the empirical consolidation. The commentary avoids the stronger claim ("predictions made in 2015") in favour of the defensible one ("requirement derived in 2015, since confirmed").

6. **"Locked-in patients only refute *strong* embodiment claims."** Correct, and that is what is claimed here. Seth's strongest reading is what the case targets.

7. **"Why should reviewers prefer FMT over Beautiful Loop as the architectural specification?"** They needn't. The commentary argues that *some* architectural specification is what Seth needs, with FMT and Beautiful Loop both supplying convergent candidates. The substantive claim is that the answer is architectural, not biological — not that FMT specifically wins.
