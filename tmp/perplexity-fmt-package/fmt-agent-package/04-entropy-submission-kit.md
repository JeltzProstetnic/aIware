# *Entropy* Submission Kit (Unit 1)

## Why *Entropy* is "literally their thesis"

The journal's consciousness-research center of gravity is exactly what
Unit 1 contributes to:

- **Carhart-Harris's Entropic Brain Hypothesis** (most-cited paper in
  the consciousness corpus there) — entropy/criticality as marker of
  conscious states; psychedelics tune brain *toward* critical point.
- **Walter & Hinterberger 2022** — explicit review framing self-organized
  criticality as the unifying framework for consciousness. Calls for
  theories that synthesize criticality with GNW/IIT.
- **Algom & Shriki 2026 (ConCrit)** — 140-dataset consolidation showing
  consciousness tracks criticality across pharmacological, pathological,
  and physiological state changes.
- Tagliazucchi, Priesemann, Beggs & Plenz lineage — all routinely
  publish there.

Unit 1 provides a *theoretical derivation* of the criticality requirement
(from Wolfram's computational universality, 2015) that the journal's
empirical contributors have spent a decade confirming. It fills a missing
piece in the program rather than challenging it.

## Title patterns (pick one or remix)

Lead with the prediction-and-confirmation framing, not with FMT or the
Hard Problem:

1. *Criticality as a Theoretical Prerequisite for Consciousness: A 2015
   Derivation from Computational Universality, Confirmed by 2024–2026
   Empirical Synthesis*
2. *From Wolfram Class 4 to ConCrit: A Theoretical Bridge to the
   Empirical Criticality–Consciousness Convergence*
3. *Predicting the Criticality Requirement: Computational Derivation
   (2015) and Empirical Confirmation (2024–2026)*

**Avoid in title:** "Four-Model Theory," "Hard Problem," "dissolves,"
"unified theory," "self-model," "qualia." Reserve those for Unit 3.

## Abstract structure (≤250 words)

Strict template — agents should follow this skeleton when drafting:

1. **Sentence 1:** State the criticality–consciousness convergence as
   established (cite Algom & Shriki 2026, Hengen & Shew 2025, Walter &
   Hinterberger 2022).
2. **Sentence 2:** Note that this convergence has been almost entirely
   bottom-up (empirical), with limited prior derivation from
   computational first principles.
3. **Sentences 3–5:** Present the 2015 derivation: Wolfram's Class-4
   computational regime as the necessary substrate condition for
   real-time self-modeling; the argument that consciousness requires
   computation complex enough to sustain a self-simulation, ordered
   enough for that simulation to be coherent.
4. **Sentences 6–8:** Enumerate the five claims that follow from this
   derivation and have been independently confirmed since 2015. Cite
   each confirmation primary source.
5. **Sentences 9–10:** Note four further predictions that remain
   untested and the empirical paradigms under which they could be
   evaluated. Mention that one (psychedelics → anosognosia) is
   especially tractable given existing clinical infrastructure.
6. **Sentence 11:** State the contribution: a theoretical scaffold for
   the criticality–consciousness research program, derived independently
   of the empirical work it predicts.

**Do not:** mention dissolving the Hard Problem, the integrative
Four-Model Theory, or self-modeling architecture in the abstract. Those
belong in Unit 3.

## Special Issue selection

*Entropy* runs rolling SIs. At any time there are usually 2–4 active
calls relevant. Agents should:

1. Check https://www.mdpi.com/journal/entropy/special_issues for current
   open SIs.
2. Filter for any of: "consciousness," "criticality," "complexity,"
   "self-organization," "neural dynamics," "complex systems and
   cognition," "information theory of the brain," "entropic brain,"
   "altered states."
3. Prefer SIs with submission deadlines 2–6 months out (enough runway
   for review; not so short that the issue is closing).
4. Read the SI scope statement carefully. Cover letter must cite a
   specific phrase from it.

If no SI is currently open with a relevant scope, submit to the regular
journal track but flag in cover letter that the work is in scope for
the journal's continuing consciousness/criticality publication record
(citing 2–3 prior *Entropy* consciousness papers).

## Reputational mitigation

Yes, *Entropy* carries MDPI baggage. Three mitigations:

1. **Co-archive on PhilSci-Archive AND PsyArXiv.** The peer-reviewed
   citation comes from *Entropy*; the rigorous-academic citation comes
   from the PhilSci-Archive deposit.
2. **In all subsequent communication, cite the paper as "Gruber 2026,
   Entropy" without dwelling on the venue.** Most readers will not
   distinguish *Entropy* from higher-IF journals at first contact.
3. **Use Unit 1 as the foothold, not the destination.** Unit 3 will
   target a higher-tier venue once Unit 1 is in print and the track
   record asset is established.
