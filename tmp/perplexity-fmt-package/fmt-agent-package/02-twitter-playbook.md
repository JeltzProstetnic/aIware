## Reply heuristics (for any future thread)

1. **Lead with the argument, link second.** Single tweets that drop two
   PDF links read as self-promo. Threads that argue first and cite
   second read as substance.
2. **Engage inside the interlocutor's frame.** "Joscha is right, and..."
   is structurally better than "Actually, FMT shows..." Affirm-and-extend
   beats correct-and-supplant on Twitter every time.
3. **Never say "dissolves the Hard Problem" in the first tweet of a
   reply.** Use "category error," "level distinction," or "two-level
   ontology" — same content, lower allergy response.
4. **Use the criticality track record as a hook when relevant.** Phrasing
   pattern: "predicted in 2015, confirmed by [Algom & Shriki 2026 / Hengen
   & Shew 2025 / etc.]." This converts the "independent researcher" signal
   from negative to positive.
5. **Don't tag Bach or Kanai gratuitously.** They are already in-thread.
   Tagging adds nothing and reads as performative.
6. **Avoid:** illusionism comparisons, IIT criticism, GNW criticism in
   replies — these invite tribal pile-ons that obscure the FMT argument.
7. **Permitted dark humor** in replies to clearly bad-faith critics, but
   never in the substantive argument tweet.

## Anti-patterns (do not do)

- Quote-tweeting Bach/Kanai with FMT promotion → reads as parasitic.
- Replying to every consciousness thread → spam signal.
- Arguing about IIT in mentions → no upside, reputational downside.
- Posting the full abstract as a tweet thread → audience won't read it,
  and it scans as self-published-book-author energy.
- Engaging with anonymous "consciousness is woo" critics → wasted cycles.
