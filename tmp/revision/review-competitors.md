# Adversarial Review: Competitor Theory Advocates

Three systematic adversarial stances against the Four-Model Theory (FMT), each from the perspective of a committed advocate of a rival framework, followed by a synthesis of which objections are genuinely damaging.

---

## Stance 1: IIT Advocate (Tononi School)

### 1.1 Straw-manning and Misrepresentation

**The Phi intractability charge is overblown and selectively applied.** The paper states that "Phi is computationally intractable for realistic systems" (Section 7.2, citing Aaronson 2014 and Doerig 2019). While this is technically correct for exact Phi computation, IIT 4.0 (Albantakis et al. 2023) has developed approximation methods, and the intractability objection applies equally to FMT's own constructs. FMT's "self-referential closure" has no measurement protocol at all (the paper itself admits this in the text following Table 1). Computing approximate Phi for small systems is at least possible; measuring self-referential closure is currently impossible. The paper scores IIT lower on operationalizability while FMT's key construct is less operationalizable than Phi.

**The panpsychism charge is stated as if it were a settled objection.** The paper says IIT's axioms lead to "panpsychist commitments that many find problematic." This is a sociological observation, not an argument. IIT's proponents (Tononi, Koch, Albantakis) have defended panpsychist implications as a feature, not a bug, and the combination problem criticism (Chalmers 2016) applies to strong panpsychism, not to IIT's specific version where consciousness exists only in systems with non-zero Phi that satisfy the exclusion postulate. The paper does not engage with IIT's actual response to the combination problem.

**The exclusion postulate is dismissed too quickly.** The paper acknowledges IIT's boundary treatment as the strongest available, then moves on. It does not address IIT 4.0's postulates (intrinsicality, composition, information, integration, exclusion) in their current form. The 2023 formulation is substantially more developed than the 2004/2008 versions the paper primarily cites.

### 1.2 Does FMT's Superiority Claim Hold?

**The scoring matrix is rigged.** The paper acknowledges this partially (Section 7.1: "a rubric that included mathematical formalization, quantitative neural predictions, or specific mechanism identification would rank IIT, PP, and GNW considerably higher"). But then it proceeds to use the rigged rubric anyway and draws conclusions from it. An IIT advocate would argue that the eight requirements are pre-selected to maximize FMT's apparent superiority. The Hard Problem and Explanatory Gap are essentially the same requirement counted twice (the paper itself says "A theory that addresses the Hard Problem should simultaneously close the Explanatory Gap" in Section 2.2), inflating FMT's advantage. If you collapse them into one, FMT's lead shrinks.

**IIT's treatment of the Hard Problem is more rigorous than FMT's.** IIT identifies consciousness with a precisely defined mathematical quantity (integrated information) and derives the structure of experience from its axioms. FMT offers a philosophical argument (level confusion / category error) that is interesting but unfalsifiable in its core claim. The assertion that "qualia are constitutive properties of the computational level" is not a solution to the Hard Problem; it is a relabeling. Calling qualia "virtual" and declaring the Hard Problem a category error is no more (or less) satisfying than IIT's identification of consciousness with Phi. Both bottom out in foundational commitments.

**FMT's "dissolution" of the Hard Problem is actually weaker than it appears.** The spreadsheet analogy (Section 3.4) is doing enormous work. The claim that "the sum of column B" is incoherent at the transistor level is true but uninteresting: no one thinks spreadsheet sums are mysterious. The move from "spreadsheet properties are level-specific" to "qualia are level-specific in the same way" is the entire argument, and it is asserted rather than demonstrated. An IIT advocate would say: we know exactly what makes a spreadsheet sum a spreadsheet sum (the functional organization of the software). What makes a quale a quale? FMT says "self-referential closure at criticality." But this is a necessary-condition claim with no mechanism for why this particular computational architecture generates phenomenality while others do not. IIT at least provides axioms that connect the structure of experience to the structure of the system.

### 1.3 Does the Seth/Mediano Phi Critique Help FMT?

**If Phi is undefined for real systems, FMT's own constructs are in worse shape.** The paper cites the computational intractability of Phi as an IIT weakness. But Phi is at least defined mathematically and computable for small systems. FMT's "criticality at the virtual level" is neither precisely defined nor clearly computable. What exactly is the criticality state of the virtual system as opposed to the substrate? The paper uses substrate-level measures (branching ratio, DFA exponent, avalanche statistics) as proxies, but these are physical-substrate measures, not virtual-level measures. If FMT truly claims that criticality matters at Level 5 (the virtual system), it needs Level 5 measures, which do not exist.

**The "criticality is necessary but not sufficient" hedge weakens the theory.** FMT says criticality plus four-model architecture equals consciousness. But criticality is measured at the substrate level, and the four-model architecture has no independent measurement. The conjunction is unfalsifiable in practice: any failure can be attributed to the architecture requirement not being met, which cannot be independently verified.

### 1.4 Strongest IIT Objections (Summary)

1. FMT's Hard Problem "dissolution" is a relabeling, not a solution. The level-confusion argument is as much a foundational commitment as IIT's axioms.
2. The scoring matrix double-counts the Hard Problem and Explanatory Gap while excluding IIT's strengths (formalization, qualia space geometry).
3. FMT's core construct (self-referential closure) is less operationalizable than Phi.
4. FMT uses substrate-level criticality measures as proxies for a claimed virtual-level property, creating a measurement gap it does not acknowledge.
5. The "necessary but not sufficient" conjunction (criticality + architecture) makes the theory difficult to falsify, because the architecture requirement cannot be independently verified.

## Stance 2: Predictive Processing Advocate (Friston/Seth School)

### 2.1 PP's Actual Current State vs. the Paper's Characterization

**PP is not "explicitly silent on the Hard Problem."** The paper claims (Section 7.2) that "PP is explicitly silent on the Hard Problem (Seth, 2021, acknowledges this)." This is a selective reading. Seth (2021) argues that the "real problem" approach -- explaining the structure and contents of experience -- is more scientifically productive than Chalmers' Hard Problem formulation. This is not silence; it is a methodological rejection of the question's framing. Seth's position is that explaining the full structure of experience dissolves the Hard Problem by making it explanatorily idle, which is actually closer to FMT's own "category error" dissolution than the paper admits. The paper rates PP as "silent" on the Hard Problem while giving FMT full marks for essentially the same strategic move (declaring the problem misformulated).

**REBUS already accounts for altered states with more empirical detail than FMT's permeability mechanism.** The paper acknowledges REBUS (Carhart-Harris 2019) but claims FMT's permeability operates "at the structural boundary between substrate and simulation -- a level that is architecturally prior and from which the REBUS mechanism is derivable as one implementation" (Section 1.3, item 4). This is an extraordinary claim made without demonstration. REBUS has specific mechanisms: relaxed precision weighting on high-level priors, increased entropy in prediction error signals, hierarchical dysregulation. FMT's "permeability" is a single qualitative parameter with no formal model. Claiming that REBUS is "derivable" from FMT's permeability is backwards: REBUS is more mechanistically specified, not less.

**Active inference already provides accounts of self-models.** The paper treats FMT's ESM as distinctive, but active inference (Friston 2010; Hohwy 2013; Seth 2021) treats the self-model as an integral part of the generative model -- the organism's model of its own embodied states and their causes. Seth's interoceptive inference framework specifically models the experienced self as a controlled hallucination grounded in body-state prediction. The paper acknowledges this in passing (Section 1.3) but does not seriously engage with how much of FMT's self-model account is already captured by PP.

**PP has its own criticality account.** The paper presents criticality as FMT's distinctive contribution, but the free energy principle (Friston 2010) inherently predicts that biological systems operate near critical transitions. Friston's formulation of self-organized criticality as a consequence of free energy minimization predates FMT's use of Wolfram's classification. The paper cites Tucker, Luu, and Friston (2025) as convergent evidence for FMT but does not acknowledge that this is a PP paper that derives criticality from PP's own framework.

### 2.2 The ESM/EWM Double Dissociation

**A PP advocate would not accept this framing.** The paper argues (Section 9, Open Question 4) that FMT predicts ESM and EWM should be independently disruptable, while PP predicts they should covary because both are parts of a unified generative model. This is a mischaracterization of PP. The hierarchical generative model in PP is not monolithic -- it has distinct levels and modules with different precision weightings. PP absolutely predicts that self-related inference (interoceptive, proprioceptive) can be disrupted independently of exteroceptive inference. Seth's work on depersonalization (Seth et al. 2012; Seth 2013) explicitly models it as a disruption of interoceptive predictive processing while exteroceptive processing remains intact -- which is exactly the ESM/EWM dissociation FMT claims as distinctive.

**Depersonalization-derealization is already explained by PP.** The paper mentions depersonalization-derealization disorder as "suggestive" evidence for the ESM/EWM dissociation. But PP already has a detailed model of this: abnormal interoceptive prediction errors lead to feelings of unreality about the self (depersonalization) or the world (derealization), with the two dimensions being partially independent. This is not evidence for FMT over PP; it is evidence both theories can accommodate.

### 2.3 Where FMT Fails to Engage with PP's Actual State

**PP's account of dreams is more developed than acknowledged.** The paper presents FMT's dream account (Section 6.3) as if PP has nothing to say. Hobson and Friston (2012) proposed that REM dreaming serves to optimize the generative model by sampling from prior distributions without sensory constraint. This is a more mechanistically specified account than FMT's "the EWM runs on internally generated input," and it explains the function of dreaming (model optimization) rather than just its phenomenology.

**PP's account of psychedelic phenomenology is at least as good as FMT's.** REBUS (Carhart-Harris 2019) explains the dose-dependent visual hierarchy (simple to complex) through progressive relaxation of precision weighting from higher to lower levels of the predictive hierarchy. This generates the same prediction as FMT's permeability gradient but with a more specific mechanism. The paper admits this in Section 8.1 ("predictive processing frameworks generate a nearly identical prediction through relaxation of top-down priors") but does not draw the obvious conclusion: the permeability mechanism may simply be a less precise description of what REBUS already explains.

**PP already handles ego dissolution.** The paper claims (Section 8.3) that REBUS "predicts self-model relaxation but not the specific input-tracking pattern." This undersells PP. In the free energy framework, when self-model priors are relaxed (ego dissolution), the system must update its self-model from whatever sensory evidence is available -- which is precisely the input-tracking that FMT claims as unique. The difference is terminological, not substantive: PP says "the self-model updates from available sensory evidence when priors are weakened"; FMT says "the ESM latches onto dominant input when normal self-referential input is disrupted." These are the same prediction in different vocabulary.

### 2.4 Strongest PP Objections (Summary)

1. FMT's "dissolution" of the Hard Problem is functionally equivalent to Seth's "real problem" approach, yet FMT gets full marks while PP gets "silent." This is a double standard.
2. REBUS is more mechanistically specified than FMT's permeability, not less. The claim that REBUS is "derivable from" FMT is unsupported.
3. The ESM/EWM double dissociation is not distinctive to FMT -- PP already models self/world processing as partially independent via interoceptive vs. exteroceptive inference.
4. FMT's Prediction 2 (ego dissolution content tracks input) is derivable from PP's framework: weakened self-priors + available sensory evidence = input-dependent self-model updating.
5. FMT presents PP as having no criticality account, ignoring that free energy minimization inherently predicts near-critical dynamics.
6. Much of what FMT claims as novel (self-model, altered states, criticality) already exists in PP with better formalization and more empirical grounding.

## Stance 3: Global Workspace Advocate (Dehaene School)

### 3.1 Is GNW Accurately Characterized?

**"No deep processing without broadcast" is a caricature of current GNW.** The paper (Section 7.2) states that GNW predicts "all broadcast content is conscious and all conscious content is broadcast" and that "involuntary conscious experience (PTSD intrusions, unbidden memories, pain) shows that phenomenal experience can override attentional and workspace-access mechanisms." This mischaracterizes GNW. PTSD intrusions and unbidden memories are broadcast events -- they win the competition for workspace access precisely because of their high salience. GNW does not require voluntary attention for broadcasting; bottom-up salience drives broadcast as much as top-down attention. The paper confuses "broadcast" with "voluntary attention," which is not GNW's claim.

**The COGITATE results are more nuanced than presented.** The paper states that COGITATE "challenged GNW" because "posterior cortex, not the frontoparietal workspace, showed the strongest consciousness-related activity." This oversimplifies. The COGITATE results were equivocal for both IIT and GNW. Dehaene and colleagues have responded that the experimental paradigm (sustained stimulus maintenance) is not optimal for testing ignition, which is a transient event. The "ignition at offset" prediction was one specific prediction among many; its absence does not invalidate the entire framework.

**The "A radio broadcasts too" argument is facile.** The paper dismisses GNW's broadcasting mechanism by saying "A radio broadcasts too." This is a rhetorical move, not an argument. GNW's global broadcasting is a specific neural mechanism (long-range cortical connections allowing information sharing across distant brain regions) with specific signatures (P3b, late sustained negativity, frontoparietal activation), not an analogy to radio transmission. The same objection could be leveled at FMT: "A weather simulation self-refers too" (if you add a monitoring module). Both theories need to explain why their particular mechanism generates phenomenality; dismissing the competition with a one-liner is not engaging seriously.

**GNW has evolved since Baars (1988).** The paper cites Baars (1988) and Dehaene (2011, 2021) but does not engage with the latest developments. GNW now incorporates a rich account of hierarchical processing, attention as workspace gating, and the relationship between broadcasting and phenomenal experience. Dehaene's (2021) update explicitly addresses the relationship between workspace broadcasting and the richness of conscious experience.

### 3.2 Where Does FMT Actually Add Something?

**The Hard Problem is FMT's genuine value-add over GNW, but it is not unique.** GNW deliberately avoids the Hard Problem, treating it as outside the scope of a neuroscientific theory. FMT's attempt to address it is ambitious but, as the IIT advocate section argues, amounts to a relabeling rather than a solution. Still, the attempt is more than GNW offers. This is FMT's clearest advantage.

**The altered-states unification is genuine but overstated.** GNW can accommodate altered states (broadcasting under unusual conditions), but FMT's permeability mechanism does provide a more unified account. However, this advantage is shared with PP/REBUS, so it does not uniquely favor FMT over the entire field.

**The predictions are FMT's strongest card.** GNW does not generate predictions about psychedelic-anosognosia interactions, ego dissolution content, or DID alter-switching specificity. These are genuine contributions. However, a GNW advocate would note that GNW's predictions (ignition threshold, P3b as consciousness marker, masking paradigms) have actually been tested and largely confirmed, while FMT's predictions are untested.

### 3.3 GNW's Defense on Avian Consciousness

**The claim that GNW "requires fronto-parietal architecture" is contested.** The paper argues that GNW cannot account for corvid consciousness because corvids lack the specific fronto-parietal architecture. But GNW's core claim is about the computational principle (global broadcasting), not the specific anatomy. A nuclear-organized pallium can implement global broadcasting through different connectivity patterns. GNW advocates have argued (Dehaene et al. 2017) that the broadcasting principle is substrate-independent at the computational level -- the same move FMT makes for its own architecture. The paper denies GNW this flexibility while claiming it for FMT.

### 3.4 Strongest GNW Objections (Summary)

1. The paper caricatures GNW's broadcasting mechanism and confuses "broadcast" with "voluntary attention."
2. GNW's empirical predictions have been tested and largely confirmed; FMT's predictions are entirely untested.
3. The "radio broadcasts too" argument is a rhetorical dismissal, not a substantive critique.
4. GNW can be extended to non-mammalian substrates through the same computational-principle argument FMT uses.
5. FMT's genuine advantage (Hard Problem engagement) amounts to a philosophical relabeling, not a mechanistic explanation.

## Synthesis: Which Objections Are Genuinely Damaging?

### HIGH PRIORITY -- Must Be Addressed in Revision

1. **The Hard Problem "dissolution" is a foundational commitment, not a solution (IIT + GNW).** Both IIT and GNW advocates will (correctly) point out that declaring the Hard Problem a "category error" is as much a philosophical commitment as IIT's axioms or GNW's broadcasting-equals-consciousness thesis. The paper already acknowledges this ("Every theory of consciousness bottoms out in an analogous commitment," Section 3.4.3) but then proceeds to score FMT as "addressing" the Hard Problem while scoring others lower. The paper should either (a) explicitly own that FMT's treatment is a commitment rather than a solution, and adjust the scoring matrix accordingly, or (b) provide a clearer argument for why a "dissolution" is categorically better than a "solution" or "silence." Currently the paper wants it both ways.

2. **The scoring matrix double-counts related requirements and excludes rival strengths (IIT).** Collapsing Hard Problem and Explanatory Gap would reduce FMT's apparent lead. More importantly, the exclusion of formalization, quantitative prediction, and empirical track record as requirements is acknowledged but not addressed. The paper should add a second comparison table showing how theories rank on empirical/formal criteria, making the trade-offs explicit rather than relegating them to a footnote.

3. **PP's treatment of altered states, self-models, and criticality is undersold (PP).** This is the most damaging set of objections. The paper treats PP as if it were silent on self-modeling, altered states, and criticality, when in fact PP has well-developed accounts of all three. The REBUS framework is more mechanistically specified than FMT's permeability. The interoceptive inference account of the self-model substantially overlaps with the ESM. The claim that REBUS is "derivable from" FMT's permeability needs either demonstration or retraction. **This is the objection most likely to provoke a hostile review from any PP-aligned reviewer.**

4. **Prediction 2 (ego dissolution content) may not be unique to FMT (PP).** The claim that "no competing theory generates this prediction" needs to be tested against PP more carefully. PP's framework (weakened priors + sensory updating) generates a very similar prediction. The paper should either show why PP's version is meaningfully different or downgrade the uniqueness claim.

### MEDIUM PRIORITY -- Should Be Addressed But Not Fatal

5. **FMT uses substrate-level measures for a claimed virtual-level property (IIT).** The paper claims criticality matters at Level 5 (virtual) but uses Level 1-4 measures (branching ratio, DFA, avalanche statistics). This is a real conceptual gap. The paper should acknowledge it explicitly and discuss what virtual-level criticality measures would look like.

6. **The ESM/EWM dissociation is not distinctive from PP (PP).** Seth's interoceptive inference work already models self/world as partially independent inference streams. The paper should engage with this directly rather than claiming the dissociation as novel.

7. **GNW is caricatured on involuntary experience (GNW).** PTSD intrusions and unbidden memories are high-salience broadcast events, not counterexamples to GNW. This argument should be revised or removed.

8. **GNW's substrate-independence claim is denied unfairly (GNW).** The paper argues GNW requires specific fronto-parietal anatomy while granting FMT full substrate independence. GNW advocates make the same computational-principle argument FMT does. The paper should treat both theories consistently.

### LOWER PRIORITY -- Worth Noting But Not Urgent

9. **The "radio broadcasts too" dismissal is beneath the paper's otherwise serious tone (GNW).** Replace with a substantive argument about why broadcasting is insufficient for phenomenality.

10. **IIT 4.0 is not fully engaged (IIT).** The paper primarily engages with earlier IIT formulations. The 2023 Albantakis et al. paper introduces refinements that are not addressed.

11. **FMT's "necessary but not sufficient" conjunction makes falsification difficult (IIT).** This is a structural issue but not unique to FMT -- IIT's exclusion postulate and PP's Markov blankets face similar problems. Acknowledge the shared challenge rather than leaving it unaddressed.
