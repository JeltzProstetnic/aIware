### A. §4.3 Redundancy with §3.4 [~250 words saved]

**Analysis:** §4.3 "Emergence Without Strong Emergence" (315 words) substantially restates §3.4.4's argument about constitutively processual properties, supervenience, and the neither-strong-emergence-nor-functionalism position. Specifically:

- §4.3 line 426: "A complete substrate description contains all the *information* needed to reconstruct the conscious experience, but the experience itself is a property of the running process — it requires instantiation, not merely description" — this is a near-verbatim restatement of §3.4.4 line 262: "reading the code is not having the experience, any more than reading a musical score is hearing the music. Experience is a property of the running process, not of its description."
- §4.3 line 426: "consciousness is **ontologically weak** (no non-physical ingredients) but **epistemically processual**" — restates §3.4.4 line 264: "This is neither functionalism... nor property dualism... It is the recognition that some properties of physical processes are constitutively processual."
- §4.3 line 428: "Consciousness does not arise from combining micro-experiences (there are none to combine)" — the anti-panpsychism point already made in §3.4.2 and §3.4.5.

**What is unique to §4.3 (must be preserved):**
- The explicit positioning vis-a-vis Kim (1993) on strong emergence
- The "ontologically weak / epistemically processual" terminology pair (useful labels)
- The combination problem dismissal sentence

**Proposed cut:** Reduce §4.3 from 315 words to ~65 words. Keep the terminological contribution as a single paragraph: "The theory's position is therefore **ontologically weak** emergence (no non-physical ingredients; Kim, 1993) but **epistemically processual** (experiential properties accessible only through instantiation, not deduction). This avoids both strong emergence and the panpsychist combination problem (Chalmers, 2016): consciousness arises from the computational properties of self-simulation at criticality — the constitutively processual account developed in Section 3.4.4."

**Words saved: ~250**
