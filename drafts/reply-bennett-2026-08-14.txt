You're right about the hierarchy, and the reason is that in FMT the other-model isn't on it. The ladder counts self-recursion only. Modelling another agent is a world-model operation: the world model is a general modeller, and the self-model gives it a rich template to instantiate. There is no separate theory-of-mind mechanism to posit, so nothing to put on a rung.

That's also why there's no 2015 "listener model" section to send you. What's there is the constraint. Page 86, my translation: the sender must fit the serialisation to the receiver's short-term memory capacity, and "the receiver must be able to reconstruct, from the received information, the model the sender wanted to convey, otherwise we produce at best a misunderstanding." Decoder mismatch is in the same passage. The receiver "may have derived a mental model that completely contradicts ours but looks exactly the same in its serialisation."

Requirement and failure mode. Not a theorem. You proved the necessity, I didn't.

Which brings me to the thing I keep returning to in "Why Is Anything Conscious?". You write that when a and b are the same species, the efficient move is for each to use its own intent as an approximation of what the other thinks, and you call it a shortcut that makes second-order selves easier to realise. In FMT that isn't a shortcut, it's the mechanism. So where does the model of b come from otherwise? Probing needs a bounded decoder class and an exchange before the first utterance, and people model a stranger before they open their mouth.

Matthias
