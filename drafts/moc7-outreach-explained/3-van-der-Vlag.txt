VAN DER VLAG — "Locating the edge in reservoirs"   (still a draft)

WHO
Michiel van der Vlag, Forschungszentrum Jülich. Builds "reservoir computers" — big
random recurrent networks whose rich dynamics a simple readout learns to use — on The
Virtual Brain platform. Poster P2, Tue 13 Oct, 15:00.

WHAT HE CLAIMS
A "meta-reservoir": a second network that watches and regulates the first one, kept
near a critical regime. Self-reference and a self/world split are supposed to emerge
from the system regulating itself. His earlier chapter found the edge of chaos gives
the best memory and prediction.

WHERE FMT/YOUR WORK STANDS
Your companion paper found something that cuts against the usual story: in a leaky
echo-state network, the textbook setting "σ = 1" (supposedly critical) was actually in
the ORDERED regime, the real edge of chaos was much further out (σ ≈ 2.6), and the best
memory AND computation were in the ordered regime, not at the edge.

THE TWO QUESTIONS IN THE MAIL, IN PLAIN WORDS
 1. "How do you find the edge?" There are two different rulers: the Lyapunov exponent
    (does a tiny nudge grow or die out = true edge of chaos) and the branching ratio
    (does one spike trigger one spike on average). They can disagree — that is exactly
    what your result showed. If he measured with one and assumed the other, his "best at
    the edge" may really be "best in the ordered regime", like yours.
 2. "Does the meta-reservoir feed back into the lower one?" FMT's criterion is two-way:
    the self-model must change how the world-model updates, and vice versa. A watcher that
    only reads is a monitor, not closure. If his feeds back, his design is a candidate
    FMT-closure system; if not, it's the "read-only self-model" your poster says is not enough.
