MUDRIK — "Prediction maps: shared versus unique predictions"   (still a draft)

WHO
Liad Mudrik, professor at Tel Aviv University. One of the leads of COGITATE, the big
adversarial collaboration that pitted IIT against Global Workspace with pre-registered
predictions. Senior, very influential in "how do we test theories of consciousness".
Keynote Tue 13 Oct, 9:00.

WHAT HER TALK IS ABOUT
She draws a theory as a network: core claims in the middle, predictions at the edge,
arrows showing which prediction follows from which claim. Then she uses network maths
(centrality) to say which prediction is most "central" to the theory, i.e. which
experiment would hurt the theory most if it failed. Goal: stop testing theories on
peripheral details and test the claims that actually matter.

THE QUESTION IN THE MAIL, IN PLAIN WORDS
Two kinds of prediction:
 - SHARED: several theories predict it (e.g. "anaesthesia pushes the brain below
   criticality" — FMT says it, ConCrit says it too). If it comes true, no theory wins.
 - UNIQUE: only one theory predicts it (FMT's "psychedelics should ease anosognosia",
   or "lucid-dream onset is a phase transition"). If it comes true, that theory gains.
Does her map weight these differently? It should — a confirmed shared prediction is
cheap, a confirmed unique one is expensive. The question shows you understood her
method and points at a gap it may or may not handle.

WHY IT HELPS FMT
FMT has what her method needs and most theories lack: predictions written down in 2015,
with the derivation chain, tested later by others. That is a ready-made worked case for
her method. The mail offers it, without pushing. If she bites, FMT gets analysed by one
of the field's referees of theory-testing.
