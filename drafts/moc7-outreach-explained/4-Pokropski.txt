WHAT THEY FOUND
They computed IIT's Φ ("how integrated is this system", IIT's consciousness number) on a
realistic spiking network. Φ came out non-zero — but it tracked the membrane time
constant of the single neurons (how slowly each neuron leaks its charge), not anything
about how the network is wired or behaves. So Φ was measuring a property of the parts,
not of the whole. An artifact.

WHERE YOUR WORK STANDS
You hit the same kind of artifact with a different metric. The branching ratio σ ≈ 1 is
used as "this network is critical". But it tracks firing rate: a set of neurons that are
NOT connected at all, firing at the same rate, also gives σ ≈ 1. So σ ≈ 1 alone says
nothing about network criticality. (This is in your published companion paper.)

THE QUESTION IN THE MAIL, IN PLAIN WORDS
Both cases share one lesson: a metric that is supposed to measure the NETWORK can be
fooled by a property of single NEURONS. The fix is a "null": run the same measurement on
a fake system that keeps the single-neuron property (same time constant, or same firing
rate) but destroys the network structure. If the metric gives the same number, it was
measuring the neurons, not the network.
Question: should the field make that a standard rule for every proposed consciousness
metric? And does his philosophical framework ("de-idealization": make models more
realistic and see whether the measure survives) already imply it?

WHY IT HELPS
It's a methods point everyone at MoC7 cares about, it connects to the consensus-paper
track (a reporting standard is exactly the kind of thing that ends up in it), and it
shows your in-silico work is self-critical. Positions you as a careful methodologist,
not a theory salesman.

RISK
Very low. You agree with him and add a second example.

Your abstract reports that Φ in integrate-and-fire networks correlates with the neurons' membrane time constant rather than with network dynamics. I ran into the same class of artifact in my in-silico work on the Four-Model Theory of consciousness (FMT). In spiking reservoirs the branching ratio σ ≈ 1 tracks firing rate, and unconnected units at a matched rate pass it as well.

That made me wonder whether a proposed consciousness metric should routinely be reported against a single-neuron-matched null, such as a time-constant-matched or rate-matched surrogate. Does your de-idealization framework imply that, or would you draw the line elsewhere?
