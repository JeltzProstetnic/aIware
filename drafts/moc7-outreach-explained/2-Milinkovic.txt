MILINKOVIC — "Substrate or dynamical mode?"   (SENT 2026-10-08 17:34 with MG's edits — Gmail 1a11c269889155fb)

WHO
Borjan Milinkovic, postdoc at NeuroPSI (CNRS, Paris-Saclay), computational neuroscience.
With Jaan Aru he wrote "biological computationalism" (2025): consciousness needs the kind
of computation brains do, and current AI cannot have it. Talk Tue 13 Oct, 11:40.

WHAT HE CLAIMS
Brains are conscious because (a) their processing runs on many scales at once and the
scales cannot be separated (molecules, cells, circuits all matter together), and (b) they
compute with continuous values, not only discrete bits, because they are wet, fluid
systems. Digital computers do neither, so AI is unlikely to be conscious.

WHERE FMT STANDS
Opposite side. Your paper (§7.3) cites his paper by name and says: substrate independence
is an empirical prediction — build the four-model architecture with open-ended
computation in any substrate, and it should be conscious. Corvids are your example: no
cortex, different brain layout, yet conscious-looking.

THE QUESTION IN THE MAIL, IN PLAIN WORDS
His argument mixes two claims:
 1. "It must be BIOLOGICAL tissue."              (substrate claim)
 2. "It must COMPUTE in a certain way: multiscale, continuous."  (dynamics claim)
Claim 2 could in principle be built in hardware — e.g. an analogue neuromorphic chip,
which also computes with continuous values on several scales. So: would such a chip
count? If he says yes, his position is really about dynamics, and FMT can live with
that (it's a statement about which hardware supports free modelling). If he says no,
he is making the stronger substrate claim and has to say why.

NOTE — it fits your new hunch: you suspected criticality might be needed in efficient
spiking or biological implementations. His answer is useful evidence for that question.

RISK
Openly disagreeing with someone before his talk. The mail does it politely ("I'd rather
ask than assume"). Scientists usually like a clean question about their own argument.

Dear Borjan Milinkovic,

My preprint on the Four-Model Theory of consciousness (FMT) cites your 2025 paper with Jaan Aru by name in §7.3, and it takes the opposite side. FMT treats substrate independence as an empirical prediction: an artificial system that implements the architecture with open-ended computation should be conscious. Corvids, with their non-cortical pallium, are one reason I lean that way.

Since we disagree, I'd rather ask than assume. In your account, is the requirement the biological substrate itself, or the dynamical mode it supports, multiscale and continuous-valued? An analogue neuromorphic substrate with scale-inseparable continuous dynamics would separate the two readings. Would it meet your condition?
