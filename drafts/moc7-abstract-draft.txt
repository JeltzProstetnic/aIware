TITLE:
The Four-Model Theory: A Structural Taxonomy of Consciousness with Independent Empirical Confirmation

ABSTRACT (250 words):

The Four-Model Theory (FMT) proposes that consciousness arises from self-simulation across four nested computational models along two dimensions: scope (world vs. self) and mode (implicit vs. explicit). The implicit models (Implicit World Model, Implicit Self Model) are substrate-level, learned, and non-conscious. The explicit models (Explicit World Model, Explicit Self Model) are virtual, transient, and phenomenal — the simulation in which experience occurs.

The theory rests on five formal principles: (1) criticality — the substrate must operate at the edge of chaos; (2) virtual qualia — phenomenal properties constitutive of the computational level, incoherent at the substrate level, analogous to a spreadsheet value being meaningless at the transistor level; (3) a redirectable Explicit Self Model providing self-referential closure; (4) variable permeability between implicit and explicit processing; (5) simulation forking enabling parallel phenomenal threads.

Self-referential closure — the system's model includes a model of itself — distinguishes conscious from non-conscious computation and provides a formal criterion for the boundary problem. This requirement is substrate-neutral: any system meeting these structural conditions instantiates consciousness.

Five predictions derived from these principles prior to publication in 2015 have received independent empirical corroboration, including anesthetic-criticality convergence, sleep-dependent criticality restoration, sleep onset as dynamical bifurcation, and split-brain holographic degradation. Four novel predictions remain untested.

A category-theoretic formalization is in development, mapping model types as objects with natural transformations between implicit-to-explicit transitions, providing compositional semantics for inter-model relationships and criteria for substrate-independent consciousness detection.
