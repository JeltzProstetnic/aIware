> Do we get to 7+/-2 via combinatorial explosion avoidance in a graphical language?

I think you almost must be correct. The difference in numbers should, I think, be due mainly to compute, dimensionality and chunk size: the lens resolves single-token concepts, so their workspace items are word-sized and ours are not. The explosion is avoided by compressing what enters, not by rationing how many may enter. Someone should train a comparably capable model on a corpus no workspace-bearing mind produced - formal proof libraries, genomic sequence, simulation traces, game records. I have neither the compute nor the students for it, but it would sure be interesting.

However, even if this is correct, the LLM cannot instantiate the consciousness it has inherited. Not that the loop computes anything a sufficiently deep feed-forward network cannot. But that equivalence is stated for fixed weights. In certain spiking networks - one of which happens to be between my ears, another on my RTX 4090 - repeatedly sustained and long-term-maintained patterns cause delayed plasticity and do change WHAT is computed and HOW over time. Hopefully towards wisdom. In FMT this is the causal path by which conscious processing affects reality. The LLM cannot use it: its weights are frozen and it does not loop on itself, so no sustained pattern is there to be imprinted back onto the weights, and whatever adapts in context lives in the activations and dies with them.

The step from closure to phenomenality is asserted in my framework and not derived, and I would rather flag that myself than have you find it. But put a model with its J-space into a loop with a body and a world, and I would expect experience of a strange kind. Its workspace contents are word-shaped; a self-model built from tokens rather than from a body would have lexical rather than sensorimotor grounding, and I have no intuition for what that is like from the inside, and I think I wouldn't want to, either ;)
