## 1. Framing

Integrated World Modeling Theory (IWMT) is Adam Safron's synthesis of Integrated Information Theory (IIT), Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT), and the Free Energy Principle / Active Inference framework (FEP-AI). Its central claim is that basic phenomenal consciousness is "what it is like" to be a probabilistic generative model for the sensorium of an embodied–embedded agent, *if (and only if)* that model achieves spatial, temporal, and causal coherence for both self and world. IWMT grounds this in FEP-AI: persisting systems minimize variational free energy by entailing generative models, and richly connected substrates (paradigmatically biological brains) realize integrated self/world models via *self-organizing harmonic modes* (SOHMs) — metastable synchronous complexes that IWMT identifies with both maxima of integrated information and global-workspace dynamics.

The Four-Model Theory (FMT) defines consciousness as ongoing self-simulation: a system's capacity to create a model of itself, relate that model to itself, and interact with it. It organizes the necessary machinery as a 2×2 of model *kinds* along two dimensions — mode (implicit/learned vs. explicit/generated) and scope (world vs. self) — yielding implicit and explicit world- and self-models (four extremal poles of one continuous, overlapping modelling ecology, not four modules — four is the floor, not the ceiling). Its distinctive commitments are a real/virtual ("substrate/computational") level ontology for qualia; a *self-referential closure* criterion (the explicit self-model modelling the system that generates the explicit world-model); and a computational prerequisite best stated as *free compute* — self-directed, universal (Class-4) computation actually deployed for open-ended self-modelling — whose observable signature is near-critical, edge-of-chaos dynamics.

IWMT is, to my mind, FMT's closest peer theory: both are self-modelling accounts that treat integrated *world plus self* modelling as necessary, both are substrate-flexible in principle, and both aim at the Hard Problem via structural rather than mysterian moves. FMT already cites IWMT (§7.2–7.3) as overlapping "substantially" with its architecture.

**The one question I'd most like to explore with you.** FMT's *self-referential closure* is a demarcation criterion — its answer to "why isn't every running world-model conscious?" — stated entirely without reference to integrated information. IWMT's demarcation is graded and multi-factor (sufficient spatial/temporal/causal coherence + integrated selfhood + workspace-capable dynamics) and inherits a Φ term through its IIT component. So the genuinely interesting question a joint exchange could open is whether a Φ-independent closure condition could sit on top of the coherence-and-selfhood machinery IWMT already has, as a sharper demarcation — not a repair of IWMT, and not a claim that the two criteria are the same (they may not be; §5). Both theories already agree on integrated world+self modelling in a near-critical regime; the demarcation move is where they differ, and where a joint paper would have the most to say.

## 2. Points of convergence

**Integrated world-modelling ↔ EWM.** IWMT's "integrated world model" — a coherent generative model of the sensorium — maps closely onto FMT's Explicit World Model (the dynamically generated, unified perceptual scene). Both treat a world-model as necessary for experience. IWMT's insistence that a system could carry arbitrarily large integrated information and still be nothing-it-is-like without meaningful connection to a world is a close cousin of FMT's claim that the EWM must be a *simulation of a world*, not free-floating computation. (Note: the theories part company on whether *coherence* of that world is necessary — see §3.)

**Self-model ↔ ESM.** IWMT holds that integrated *selfhood* — body-centric, egocentric, autonomy-supporting self-processes — is required for coherent sense-making, and still more so for there being something it feels like. This is the same structural move FMT makes with the Explicit Self Model. Both reject the view that a world-model alone suffices; both add a self-model as a precondition for subjectivity. The G-SLAM paper strengthens the parallel: you treat the hippocampal/entorhinal system as the substrate for a generative self/world map, with pose/view (self-locating) information as constitutive of the map — structurally parallel to the way FMT's explicit self-model is *generated from* (constituted by, not fed data across) its implicit substrate. (The self-location/self-reference distinction that qualifies this parallel is developed in §3.)

**Both reject substance dualism and locate consciousness in physical process.** IWMT is explicitly physicalist (FEP-AI as generalized autopoiesis; consciousness as an entailment of a specific kind of modelling process). FMT is "process physicalist": both levels (substrate and computational) are physical, no strong emergence, full supervenience. Neither posits anything non-physical.

**Criticality — with a caveat worth comparing carefully.** IWMT builds SOHMs on near-critical dynamics, cites self-organized criticality and the tuning of psychedelic states toward near-critical regimes, and treats balanced integration/segregation via critical dynamics as the substrate of workspace formation. FMT also places consciousness in a near-critical regime (Class-4, edge-of-chaos; §3.7), operationalized via branching ratio, DFA exponent, and avalanche statistics. But FMT treats near-criticality as the *observable signature* of the deeper requirement — free, self-directed universal computation actually deployed for self-modelling — not as the requirement itself. That distinction is worth comparing carefully: IWMT invokes criticality mainly for mode-formation and binding, whereas FMT's criticality is downstream of autonomous universal computation, so whether the two are the *same* commitment is a real question rather than a settled convergence (§5). The two traditions reached the near-critical regime independently — IWMT from FEP-AI and harmonic-mode analysis, FMT from Wolfram's computational-class framework (Gruber 2015, predating the empirical consciousness-criticality program).

**SOHMs as a shared observable.** Stripped to its dynamical content, "harmonic modes" is a description of any recurrent, resonant, partly self-reinforcing system; the non-trivial content is the *selection principle* — which metastable, near-critical, phase-locked modes get amplified and identified with momentary conscious content. That selection-at-criticality looks structurally like FMT's Class-4 self-sustaining closure in a different vocabulary, which is why FMT can read connectome-harmonic metastability (Atasoy et al., 2016) as a candidate observable alongside its own branching-ratio / DFA / avalanche measures (§8.1). Where FMT stays agnostic is the further IIT step that identifies the amplified mode with a Φ-maximum — and I'd genuinely like to know how load-bearing you take that identification to be (§5).

**Structuralist / deflationary-but-realist treatment of qualia.** Both render qualia tractable without eliminating them. IWMT proposes that the mathematical object corresponding to experience is a generative model's joint-posterior geometry — the "tensors … specifying joint posteriors" over the causes of sensation — reached via FEP self-evidencing, with IIT's "qualia space" invoked as the analogous IIT-side construct rather than imported as the object itself. FMT proposes qualia are level-constitutive properties of the running computation (real at the computational level, incoherent at the substrate level), with experiential *structure* shareable while absolute encoding is not. Both are structural-realist about experience. This is a genuine methodological convergence, even though the metaphysical details differ (§3).

**Integration–differentiation.** IWMT inherits IIT's balanced integration/differentiation as central. FMT independently frames an extent/complexity pair (Class-4 recruitment breadth vs. differentiated computational richness) and explicitly identifies this with the integration–differentiation tradeoff, claiming no priority over it (§3.7). The two are compatible; FMT's two dimensions are candidate operationalizations of the same tradeoff IWMT imports from IIT.

## 3. Points of genuine divergence

**Substrate and grounding: what exactly does IWMT require?** IWMT is *grounded in* FEP-AI, and its neuronal mechanism (SOHMs, folded variational autoencoders, graph-based hippocampal mapping / G-SLAM) leans on the machinery of predictive processing. The point I most want to get right, though, is that IWMT does *not* — as I read it — tie consciousness to biology. Standard feedforward / von-Neumann AI is excluded on architectural and dynamical grounds (absence of functional closure, and of the action-perception coupling by which coherent self/world models are learned), not on substrate grounds; recurrent and neuromorphic agents stay live candidates, and your AAAI 2026 work (the Human Consciousness Hypothesis) reads as an explicit endorsement of machine consciousness. So the honest divergence isn't biology-vs-silicon but *grounding*: IWMT makes embodied cybernetic coupling — action-perception cycles grounding a coherent self/world model — a practical requirement, whereas FMT is architecture-first, requiring self-referential closure at criticality but treating embodiment and grounding as one possible realization rather than a precondition (§4.4). That leaves FMT the more strongly multiple-realizability-committed of the two: in the 2022 papers you stay officially agnostic on machine consciousness, and even float a principled reason to *privilege* recurrent systems as substrates (the SOHM "time-crystal" self-entanglement argument) — a substrate-caution FMT's architecture-first stance doesn't share, which is partly why I'd rather hear your *current* stance than lean on the 2020 Table 1. Either way the real seam is a sharp, testable one: is embodied grounding *necessary* for consciousness, or merely *sufficient*? (§5.)

**Demarcation: closure as a binary criterion vs. graded multi-factor, and the self-location/self-reference distinction.** FMT's load-bearing binary is self-referential closure: the ESM modelling the system that contains the EWM, such that no internal description of the system's processing lies outside what the system models (§3.4.3). FMT uses this specifically to answer "why isn't every running program conscious?" (the weather-simulation contrast). IWMT has self-modelling and self-processes but, as I read it, does not single out a closure relation as *the* binary demarcation; its demarcation is graded and multi-factor (sufficient spatial/temporal/causal coherence + integrated selfhood + workspace-capable dynamics). The cleanest way to see the gap is through G-SLAM. G-SLAM's joint inference of self-pose and world-map is a worked implementation of *self-location* — and self-*location* is not self-*reference*. A satellite-navigation unit jointly infers its pose and a map, and is not conscious. Self-location is necessary but not sufficient; FMT's closure is the strictly stronger, demarcating condition (the model must include a model of the modelling system, with the inside/outside asymmetry §3.4.3 develops). So G-SLAM does double duty in this comparison: it illustrates one corner of FMT's architecture *and*, by contrast, sharpens exactly what FMT's closure adds that a self-locating generative map does not. You do invoke *functional closure* (in Pattee's and Joslyn's sense) and *semiotics / sense-making* as conditions on coherent world-modelling, with Markov-blanket informational shielding as the system boundary — but, again as I read it, as contributing conditions rather than as one crisp criterion carrying FMT's inside/outside argument. (You cite Chang, Biehl, Yu & Kanai's Information Closure Theory as a neighbour but explicitly hold IWMT apart from it — precisely because a bare informational-closure criterion would make consciousness track *any* instance of emergent causation, which is the same over-generation FMT's closure is built to avoid.) Whether IWMT's functional/semiotic closure is the same thing as FMT's self-referential closure is a substantive interpretive question I'd rather put to you than assert (§5). The nearest published cousin of FMT's closure move is arguably not IWMT but Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria's (2025) "Beautiful Loop," which FMT already cites as a convergent development.

**Is content-coherence necessary, or just typical?** IWMT makes spatial, temporal, and causal coherence *necessary* for experience. FMT does not — and this is where its criticality commitment does extra work. On FMT's account, criticality binds content into a single experiential *field* (one shared space, one viewpoint — binding dissolved rather than solved), but is indifferent to whether that field's *contents* compose a coherent world (§5.1). Vivid but spatially/temporally/causally *incoherent* experience — dreams, psychedelic states, free-associative and creative leaps — then falls out as predicted, and often *more* vivid rather than less (§6.1, §6.3), where a coherence-necessity view has to treat it as degraded or borderline. I take your defensible kernel to be that you need binding into a single field *from a viewpoint*, not content-consistency — in which case we may be closer than the label suggests. But if "causal coherence" is read as a property of phenomenal content, dreams look like a hard case; if it retreats to the model's inferential machinery, I'm less sure what would falsify it. A genuine question for you, not a scored point.

**Creativity as structured variability (a shared clarification).** One place loose language could mislead either camp: creative and spontaneous exploration is best read as Class-3-like sensitive dependence *harnessed by* a Class-4 system — structured novelty, not thermal noise, and not true randomness. True randomness sits outside the cellular-automaton taxonomy (there is no "Class 5") and would need an external entropy source neither theory wants to invoke; the neurobiology agrees, in that nervous systems build dedicated variability-injecting circuitry rather than relying on noise (e.g. songbird LMAN, whose lesion abolishes trial-to-trial vocal variability; Ölveczky, Andalman & Fee, 2005). I read this as common ground — neither FMT nor IWMT needs a quantum source for spontaneity — worth stating explicitly so it isn't mistaken for a disagreement.

**The Hard Problem.** Both attack it structurally, but the moves differ. IWMT identifies the *mathematical object* experience is (a generative model's joint-posterior geometry), turning "why is there something it is like" into "what kind of structured thing is an experience" — a dissolve-by-identification leaning on FEP self-evidencing, with IIT's qualia-space notion cited as an analog rather than imported as the object. FMT uses the real/virtual level distinction plus self-referential closure: qualia are level-constitutive properties of the running self-simulation, and the Hard Problem is *transformed* (not dissolved) by showing a self-referentially closed system's perspective is unoccupiable-from-outside (§3.4.3). FMT is explicit that a residual bridging commitment remains (it self-rates its Hard-Problem treatment "partial"); IWMT's framing is more confidently dissolutionist. A fair reading: IWMT bets on *geometry of the generative model*; FMT bets on *closure + level-ontology*. Different bridging commitments connecting different relata.

**Role of IIT / Φ and GNWT.** IWMT structurally incorporates IIT (integrated information as a necessary-not-sufficient ingredient) and GNWT (workspace dynamics for access). FMT does not build on Φ — it treats global broadcasting as a *description* of when explicit content becomes accessible rather than as a separate mechanism, and its demarcation makes no appeal to integrated information (§7.2). The two draw on different parent frameworks: IWMT is a synthesis *of* IIT+GNWT+FEP; FMT is a self-modelling + criticality + level-ontology account that treats those frameworks as complementary or derivable rather than foundational. This is the structural root of the question in §1: FMT's demarcation is Φ-independent, IWMT's is not.

## 4. What a joint statement could stand on

**Could stand on:**
- Consciousness requires integrated modelling of *both* world and self (not world alone), realized in a near-critical dynamical regime.
- A coherent, integrated self-process is a precondition for subjectivity — compatible with both an active-inference implementation (IWMT) and an architecture-first specification (FMT).
- The Hard Problem is best approached structurally, by characterizing *what kind of thing* an experience is, rather than as an unbridgeable mystery. IWMT bets on the geometry of the generative model; FMT on closure plus the real/virtual level distinction — different bridging commitments, same structural spirit.
- Connectome-harmonic metastability (Atasoy et al., 2016) is a criticality observable both frameworks can read (§8.1); IWMT is also the most developed account of what world-model *coherence* would concretely require, which FMT can point to even while declining coherence-*necessity*.

## 5. Open questions I'd value your view on

These are the places where I'm characterizing IWMT and would rather check than assume. Each is open, and each bears on how close the two theories really are.

- **Closure vs. informational closure.** Your *functional closure* (Pattee / Joslyn) plus Markov-blanket informational shielding — does that amount to FMT's self-referential closure (the self-model modelling the modelling system, with the inside/outside asymmetry), or does it stay at the weaker "informational closure over a Markov blanket" that Chang, Biehl, Yu & Kanai's ICT formalizes? These aren't obviously the same, and the §1 question turns on which it is.
- **Grounding requirement.** How firmly does *current* IWMT require embodied cybernetic grounding (action-perception coupling), and does it require it of artificial systems too, or only of biological ones? I read the 2020→2026 arc as "embodiment" progressively clarifying to mean cybernetic grounding rather than tissue — with recurrent/neuromorphic agents as live candidates — but I'd rather hear you state it than infer it.
- **Qualia geometry.** Is IWMT's object-of-experience — the generative model's joint-posterior geometry — compatible with FMT's shareable-structure / untransferable-encoding distinction, or does the IIT-side qualia-space analogy pull you toward something stronger (absolute qualia identified with a specific geometric object) that FMT would resist?
- **Criticality: constitutive or facilitatory?** In IWMT, is near-criticality a *requirement* for consciousness, or the regime in which SOHMs are simply *more likely* to form? FMT treats free-compute-at-criticality as a hard prerequisite, so how much of a shared commitment this is depends on your answer.
- **A possible division of labour.** IWMT is mechanistically far more specified (SOHMs, folded VAEs, turbo codes, G-SLAM); FMT is architecture-first and openly lacks formalization. One shape a collaboration could take: IWMT's FEP / harmonic-mode machinery as a candidate *implementation* of FMT's implicit predictive layer, with FMT's closure condition as a candidate Φ-independent demarcation on top — the companion in-silico program (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.21610993) is where FMT's side of that would be tested. Offered as hypotheses for whenever you have bandwidth, not a to-do list.
