## A7: criticality premise (§3.7). New paragraph after "Concrete neural signatures", ~140 words

> Sipling, Zhang and Di Ventra (2026) extend this caveat. Avalanche power laws, their scaling relations and
> long-range temporal correlations can all arise across an extended phase of memory-induced long-range order,
> produced by coupling fast activity to slowly recovering metabolic resources, with no critical point; and no
> critical point has yet been located in cortex. Principle 1 requires a Class 4 regime and names
> near-criticality as its biological signature. An extended long-range-ordered phase, bounded by quiescence on
> one side and runaway activity on the other, is such a regime, so the necessity claim reads: without that
> regime, whether critical point or extended phase, no consciousness. Whether the operating point is tuned
> remains open; critical exponents have been found only at balanced excitation and inhibition (Janbon et al.,
> 2026) and to shift with arousal as a control parameter (Munn et al., 2026). The claim stays testable:
> manipulations that drive the markers out of the phase should abolish experience, and the metabolic titration
> Sipling et al. propose should not, as long as the markers persist.

## G1: J-space (Gurnee et al., 2026), three places

### (a) §7.2 GNW entry: replaces its last sentence ("No such manipulation currently exists, and its absence is a limitation the two frameworks share.")

> No such manipulation exists in brains. An engineered system now supplies half of it: in large language
> models, Gurnee et al. (2026) located a workspace of verbalisable representations whose contents are globally
> available, reportable, held across multi-step reasoning and switched in an ignition-like way, all within a
> single feedforward pass and without recurrent loops. On this theory that is broadcast without closure.
> The same system separates two claims the theory makes about language. The first follows from commitments
> already made: text produced by systems that run explicit models serialises those models (Gruber, 2015), so a
> corpus contains model structure beyond what the direct interpretation of its sentences yields, and a system
> trained on the corpus can recover that structure without closure. The distributional hypothesis (Harris,
> 1954; Firth, 1957) records the regularity; the theory supplies the mechanism that deposits it. The second
> claim is asserted: that the meanings deposited in this way write back into the language and change it, the
> linguistic analogue of the consolidation channel (Section 3.4.3). A frozen-weight model cannot do this. The
> first claim is testable: a comparable model trained only on corpora that no model-running system produced,
> such as genomic sequence, physical simulation traces or self-play game records, should develop no comparable
> workspace.

⚠ **One change from the S316 ruling for you to confirm:** the backlog listed *proof libraries* among the
falsifier corpora. I dropped them, because mathematicians write proofs, so a proof library *is* produced by
model-running minds and could not test the claim.

### (b) §3.x Block paragraph (was l.382): replaces "The operationalizations in Table 1 (PCI, P3b ignition, entropy measures) are therefore tracking the right target: they detect when the explicit models are running, which is when phenomenal consciousness is occurring."

> In brains, the operationalizations in Table 1 (PCI, P3b ignition, entropy measures) track this target:
> broadcast and ignition are the signature of content entering the closed explicit simulation. They are its
> signature and not its definition. The workspace Gurnee et al. (2026) located in language models shows
> ignition-like broadcast in a system without closure (Section 7.2), an instance of the marker drift discussed
> in Section 4.4.
