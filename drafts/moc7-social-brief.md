## Content angles — each is a self-contained post. Pick, do not stack them all into one.

### A. Having a self-model is not enough (the criterion)
- A system can carry a rich, decodable self-model that a controller merely *consults*, the way a car's dashboard reports its speed. FMT says that is not enough.
- The criterion is **closure**: the self-model and the world-model each enter the *other's update rule*. The system models the world through a model of itself, and itself through its model of the world.
- It is stated as an intervention, so it can be tested in any system you can intervene on: clamp the self-variables to a value, sweep the value, and check whether the world-model's updates change. Then do the same the other way round. One-way influence is ordinary causal coupling; only the two-way dependence counts.
- It must survive decoupling from current sensory input. That is what separates a self-*model* from a self-*monitor*, so a thermostat-like body-state controller does not qualify.
- It is graded (the weaker of the two directions sets the strength), and it is substrate-neutral.

### B. Predictions made in 2015, and how they are doing
The theory was first published in German in 2015. The poster splits its predictions in two:
- **Consistent with later independent work** (five): anaesthetics push the brain below criticality whatever their receptor mechanism; waking degrades criticality and sleep restores it; sleep onset is a bifurcation rather than a dimming (Li et al. 2025, n > 1,000, critical slowing about 4.5 minutes ahead); psychedelics expose processing stages in hierarchical order; split-brain surgery degrades both sides rather than splitting cleanly.
- **Honesty that must travel with B:** most of this corroboration is *shared* with other criticality-based frameworks, and some of the supporting work predates 2015. The posts may say "consistent with", never "confirmed" or "proved".
- **Still open** (five, all testable with existing methods): psychedelics should ease anosognosia (unawareness of one's own deficit) in disconnection-type cases; during ego dissolution, identity content should follow whatever sense dominates the input; switches between alters in dissociative identity disorder should show a self-referential → sensorimotor gradient; lucid-dream onset should be a phase transition with critical slowing beforehand, not a gradual ramp; the *structure* of qualia should align across people while the absolute neural encoding does not (group-level alignment already shown by Kawakita et al. 2025).
- The open predictions need people with clinical and imaging access. That is the call to action, and it is genuine: "come and argue with me" is on the poster.

### C. A result that changed the theory's own wording (the self-critical one — the strongest for this audience)
- Many theories, FMT included, linked consciousness to the brain operating "at criticality", the edge of chaos.
- In a leaky echo-state network (N = 1024), the standard criticality setpoint σ = 1 turned out *not* to be the edge of chaos: it sits in the ordered regime (Lyapunov exponent ≈ −0.12). The true edge was near σ ≈ 2.6. Memory and computation peaked together in the *ordered* regime.
- Consequence for FMT: the requirement is the *capability* for open-ended computation, used. Near-criticality is the signature that capability usually leaves in neural tissue, not the requirement itself.
- **MG ruling S329 (2026-10-02), verbatim:** *"consciousness requires free modeling and four models, not criticality!"*
- Source: the in-silico companion, DOI above. This result is numpy rate-model work and is unaffected by crucible's 2026-10-07 artifact audit.

### D. The two-axis picture (for a visual post)
- Two dimensions: **world vs self** and **implicit vs explicit**. The implicit side is the learned, non-conscious substrate; the explicit side is the virtual simulation in which experience occurs.
- They are four *kinds* of model, not four modules or brain regions. In a brain they are graded, overlapping, shifting partitions.
