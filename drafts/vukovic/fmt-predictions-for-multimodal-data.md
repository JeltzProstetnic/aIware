# Four-Model Theory: predictions that need time-aligned multimodal data

Matthias Gruber · ORCID 0009-0005-9697-1665 · 2026-10-20

The Four-Model Theory (FMT; Gruber, 2026, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18669891) makes several predictions about
*when* and *in what order* changes in conscious state appear across signals. Each one requires physiological
recordings and timestamped first-person reports on one time axis. The five below fall within the platform's pilot
areas (meditation, psychedelics). Identifiers refer to the FMT prediction map
(https://research.matthiasgruber.com/fmt-map/).

| ID | Prediction | Signals and reports | Domain |
|--|-----------------------|------------|-------|
| E7 | Permeability (how readily stored content enters experience) rises monotonically with 5-HT2A agonism across three or more dose levels. | EEG Lempel-Ziv complexity; 5D-ASC; reported content | psychedelics |
| F9 | As permeability rises, intermediate processing stages appear in a fixed order: phosphenes and form constants, then complex imagery, then dream-like scenes. | timestamped imagery reports aligned with EEG; eye tracking | psychedelics, hypnagogia |
| F1 | During ego dissolution, the reported identity content ("what I became") follows the dominant sensory modality, and controlling the input (visual, auditory, tactile) directs it. | speech reports; input modality log; EEG | psychedelics |
| E9 | Meditative absorption (e.g. jhāna) shifts cortical dynamics toward the near-critical signature. | EEG criticality measures; HRV; reported absorption depth | meditation |
| E3 | Lucid-dream onset shows phase-transition dynamics (critical slowing, discontinuous onset) that begin in self-model networks before spreading. | sleep EEG; eye-signal verification of lucidity; awakening reports | sleep, lucid dreaming |

What distinguishes these from single-signal tests is the ordering: F9 and E3 predict a *sequence*, E7 a dose
monotonicity, F1 a dependence of report content on the controlled input. Each requires the reports and the
physiology to be aligned in time.
