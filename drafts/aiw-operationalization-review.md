# Operationalization, Implementation, and What Φ Actually Got Wrong

## The question

> "isnt operationalization in this domain simply a mathematical statement expressing what most are too sloppy with words wanting to express with their theories? and isnt then the implementation the best possible operationalization ever because we can deliver precise mathematical framework AND numbers? because i do not want to just spit around formulas that dont do any good to any human nowadays imo, as long as they at least are reproducible, testable and mean something real. not like IITs phi."

Three claims, separable and separately adjudicable:

1. **Deflationary**: operationalization in consciousness science is nothing more than saying precisely what verbal theories say sloppily.
2. **Supremacy**: a running implementation is therefore the best possible operationalization — formal framework plus numbers.
3. **Value criterion**: formalism earns publication only if reproducible, testable, and about something real; Φ is the named failure.

## Verdicts

1. The deflationary claim is right about most of the field's formalism and wrong as a definition of operationalization. Transcription — notation bolted onto a verbal theory — is the floor, and much of consciousness science sits on it. But operationalization at its best does something no verbal statement can: it forces a choice between readings the words leave open, and sometimes the readings turn out to have different truth values. Your own programme contains the exhibits.

2. The supremacy claim over-reaches, and the proof is inside your own repo. An implementation is the best possible operationalization *of whatever it actually implements*. Whether that is the construct the theory means is a separate question — construct validity, in the measurement literature — and it is the one question the implementation cannot answer about itself. `closure_enabled=False` was precise, reproducible, and numeric for the entire programme, and it operationalized closure-*location*, not closure-*presence*. What survives of the claim is real and worth keeping: an implementation is the regime in which a bad operationalization is cheapest to convict. That is a different superlative, and it is the true one.

3. Keep the criterion, with one amendment and one uncomfortable consequence. The amendment: a number is only as real as the validation of the instrument that produced it — "we deliver numbers" is not yet value, because the knob delivered numbers for four kills. The consequence: measured against your own criterion today, FMT's formal apparatus is not better than Φ. It is *earlier* than Φ. What FMT has that IIT does not is a working error-correction loop between formalism and implementation, and the §1.3 deferral is compliance with your criterion, not a weakness — but only for as long as the loop keeps running. If Phase 1 never executes, the formalization paper becomes exactly the thing you despise, with a disclaimer attached.

The rest of this note defends these three verdicts and ends with the two things you can use: a decision rule for whether a piece of formalism earns its place in an FMT paper, and the concrete consequences for the three papers.

## 1. Four things, not two

The question runs together four distinct objects, and two of the conflations do damage.

- A **construct**: what the theory means — closure, permeability, criticality.
- A **formal statement**: a mathematical expression of the construct — O_ESM ⊆ S_EWM, T_{X→Y}, Φ(m*) = m*.
- An **operationalization**: a procedure that ties the construct to a measurement on data or on a system. This is Bridgman's (1927) coinage: the meaning of a concept, for measurement purposes, is the set of operations that determine it.
- An **implementation**: one particular executable realization of one particular operationalization.

The first conflation is formal statement with operationalization. Φ in IIT 4.0 is a fully formal statement with an operationalization that almost no physical system can undergo — the two can come apart completely, and in Φ's case they do. FMT's fixed-point condition Φ(m*) = m* is currently a formal statement with no operationalization at all; that is a legitimate state for roadmap work and an illegitimate one for a results claim.

The second conflation is operationalization with construct. This is the seam where CRU-58 lives, and it has a proper name: **construct validity** (Cronbach & Meehl, 1955). Reliability — that the procedure gives the same number every time — is what an implementation guarantees by construction. Validity — that the number tracks the construct the theory names — is exactly what it does not. The two are independent, and an implementation maximizes the first while leaving the second untouched.

Here is the sharpest way to state what implementation actually does to the sloppiness problem. A verbal theory spreads its imprecision across both links: word-to-formal and formal-to-measurement. An implementation eliminates the second link's slack entirely — the code *is* the operational definition, with no ambiguity of procedure — and thereby concentrates all remaining risk into the first link: the mapping from the theory's word to the variable's name. The sloppiness is not removed. It is relocated into an identifier. The variable was called `closure_enabled`, and that name was a verbal theory embedded in source code, wrong in the same way verbal theories are wrong, but now sitting on a single seam where it could be attacked and convicted in two lines of algebra. That relocation is the genuine advantage. It is not validity; it is *attackability*.

## 2. The deflationary claim: its strongest form, and where it breaks

The strongest version is defensible and worth stating without apology. Much of the formalism in consciousness science adds no constraint to the verbal theory it decorates. A formalism that cannot be wrong where the words could not be wrong is a costume: the precision of the notation gets mistaken for precision of the theory, and mathematical authority is borrowed rather than earned. The free-energy literature has faced exactly this charge (which of its claims could fail?), and the arbitrariness objections to Φ — why this integration measure among the many that satisfy the same axioms? — are the same charge in different dress. On this reading, "operationalize your theory" often means "transcribe your theory into symbols so it looks like physics," and your impatience with that is correct.

But transcription is only what formalization does when it fails. When it works, it does something structurally unavailable to natural language: it **selects**. A verbal claim is compatible with a family of precise readings; the formal statement must pick one; and picking one exposes facts about the family that no member of it announced. Four exhibits, all from inside the programme:

**Closure decomposed into waist and return.** For years "closure" was one word for one thing. The formal treatment forced the decomposition: a bottleneck is a narrow waist in a path A→k→B; closure is the case B = A. The waist buys efficiency (rank ≤ k, wiring 2·k·N instead of N²); the return buys self-consistency (a fixed point — a state consistent with its own re-entry; a relay has none). Retina→LGN→V1 is waist without return; thalamocortical loops are both. These are different properties doing different jobs, they dissociate in real anatomy, and the verbal theory had no way to notice they were separable — the word "closure" covered both, so no sentence using the word could pull them apart. The decomposition arrived the week an experiment forced it, after the theory had run without it for a decade.

**The two dials required a premise the words hid.** The verbal claim — extent and complexity of critical dynamics vary independently — is, taken against a single critical point, a category error: one critical point has one order parameter, and two independent dials cannot be built on it. The formalization (§4.7) had to *add* the spatial-heterogeneity premise (a field of locally critical regions) before the claim was even well-formed, and then had to place the dials on distinct mathematical objects (a percolation order parameter over space; a conditional Lempel-Ziv complexity over on-cluster dynamics) before independence was assertable as a conjecture rather than a confusion. The words did not merely lack precision; they concealed that a premise was missing.

**The derivation chain that was not one.** The verbal theory carried "closure ⇒ universality ⇒ Class 4 ⇒ criticality" as if it were a derivation. Formalization decomposed it into two lemmas, L1 and L2, and found both open — L2 resting on a principle that is itself a conjecture, with the standard results pointing the other way (§4.4). The formal act here did not express what the words meant. It exposed that what the words implied had never been established. That demotion — criticality from derived consequence to empirically motivated operating-regime hypothesis — is a *correction to the theory*, produced by the attempt to formalize, unreachable by more careful wording.

**Self-inclusion derived with no self-label.** The span law: a bottleneck must cover exactly the support of what it summarizes, and content that couples the world-model and the body forces the cost-optimal loop to span both — a loop over the system's own state, derived from wiring cost plus content support, with "self" appearing nowhere in the derivation. A verbal theory can assert that the self-model is special; only the formal treatment could show that self-inclusion falls out of cost alone.

The general point beneath the exhibits: **words cannot be wrong in a provable way, and that is their defect**. The value of formalization is not expressiveness — the verbal theory usually expresses more, not less — it is exposure. A formal statement stakes a position that a counterexample can kill, and in this programme the counterexamples have killed named claims on both sides of the ledger, sometimes within a day of their being made. The deflationary claim is therefore right as a diagnosis of transcription-grade formalism and wrong as an account of what the enterprise is. The test for which kind you are holding is retrospective and simple: has this formalism ever forced a correction its author did not want? Every exhibit above passes. Decoration never does.

## 3. The supremacy claim, adjudicated against CRU-58

The claim: an implementation is the best possible operationalization, because it delivers the framework and the numbers.

The counterexample is CRU-58, and it deserves to be stated at full strength because it is the single most instructive event in the programme's history. Every closure experiment to date operationalized "closure removed" as `closure_enabled=False`. The knob was mathematically exact — it zeroes the ESM columns in the rest rows, three lines of source. It was perfectly reproducible: byte-identical across arms, twelve seeds, scale-invariant from N=440 to N=22,000. It produced numbers, and the numbers fed four experiments. And it measured the wrong thing. The knob does not remove a loop; it decomposes one irreducible closed system into two closed systems and severs the coupling: spec(J_OFF) = spec(J_rest) ∪ spec(J_ESM) exactly, ON is one strongly connected component and OFF is two, and the loop-detecting instrument returns localization = 1.000 — a cleanly localized self-loop — on the *ablated* arm's ESM while returning VOID on the intact one. The ablated condition has two selves, not zero. Your own framing: a stroke, not an ablation — and the correction sharpened even that, because the rest's returning core was never touched at all; nothing regrew, the knob was an efferent disconnection of one region from the start. Four nulls were banked on an instrument whose defect was provable from the source in two lines, before any experiment ran.

So: an operationalization that was precise, reproducible, testable, and numeric was, for the entire programme to date, not an operationalization of the thing it named. Every axis of the supremacy claim was satisfied, and the construct-validity axis — the one the claim does not mention — failed. That is the precise sense in which the claim over-reaches: it enumerates the virtues implementations guarantee and omits the one virtue they cannot.

Two things stop this from being a refutation, and both matter.

First, look at how the failure was found and how long it lived. Φ's construct-validity disputes have run for two decades and remain unresolved, because Φ's canonical quantity cannot be computed on any system large enough to test the disputes. The knob's failure lived for a few weeks, was raised by a one-sentence intuition ("your 'closure off' just is like a stroke with a new self emerging elsewhere in such a simple brain"), and was settled — not argued, settled — the same night, by algebra on the artifact. Then the correction chain ran the other direction: the over-reaching theorem produced in response ("return-ness is not a manipulable variable") was itself killed within the day by building a modular counterexample — three SCCs to one at Δρ = 0.014. Neither the words, nor the math, nor the code held final authority; each convicted the others, and every conviction was cheap *because the artifact existed to run counterexamples against*. That is what an implementation uniquely buys, and neither prose nor pure mathematics buys it: errors of all three parties become mechanically decidable. The supremacy claim, correctly stated, is not "implementations are valid" — it is "implementations make invalidity cheap to prove." On that statement you are right, and the evidence for it is a repo full of convicted claims with dates on them.

Second, the implementation buys three further things worth listing, because they answer "what does it buy that words and math do not" concretely:

- **Forced totality.** Prose can leave anything unspecified indefinitely; an implementation cannot run with a hole in it. Every hand-wave becomes a design decision, and wrong design decisions become findable objects (the knob was findable; a verbal "imagine closure removed" never is).
- **Cost as a measurable.** The efficiency reframe's DV — cost at matched function — is not a quantity a verbal theory or an unimplemented formalism can produce. The OFF+τ result (a rival that ties with *less* structure — the wrong sign, honestly recorded) exists only because something was built.
- **Negative results with mechanisms.** The CRU-36 null, the delay-line tie at spiking scale, the fly-at-the-window correction — each null came with the reason for the null, because the substrate could be opened. A verbal theory's failed prediction returns one bit; an implementation's returns the failure mode.

One boundary on all of this, and it is one you set yourself. The implementation operationalizes the ML/formal lane only: fixed task, fixed horizon, function space, budget explicit. The S288 scope limit is exactly the statement that results in that lane do not transfer unmodified to the biological lane, where the licensed claim is budget-relative capability — there exists a capability C and a budget B such that no feedforward-plus-memory system within B realizes C while a closed system within B does, and biology never operates outside B; with the subject of that capability claim being consciousness as a whole architecture, not closure as a primitive. So "the implementation is the best operationalization of FMT" is lane-ambiguous as stated. It is the best available operationalization of FMT's efficiency claims. It is not, and at toy scale cannot be, an operationalization of the biological capability claim — for that lane the implementation supplies the matched pair biology never provides (same-N, differing only in wiring), which is motivation and mechanism, not measurement. Keeping the lanes separate is not pedantry; presenting crucible numbers as if they operationalized the biological claim would hand a reviewer the exact confusion the scope limit exists to prevent.

## 4. Φ, fairly — and FMT in the same mirror

What is actually wrong with Φ, on your three axes, stated so it would survive an IIT defender in the room:

**Reproducible.** In the small, fully: IIT 4.0 defines Φ exactly and PyPhi computes it as a reference implementation (Mayner et al., 2018). The defect is not sloppiness but reach: the computation is super-exponential in system size, exact Φ is feasible only for systems of the order of ten binary elements, and for any organism the canonical quantity is not computable even in principle-adjacent practice. Every empirical "Φ" is therefore a proxy, and the tether between proxy and canonical quantity is itself unvalidated. Reproducibility that cannot reach its referent breaks the next axis rather than its own.

**Testable.** The unfolding argument (Doerig et al., 2019): recurrent networks and their feedforward unrollings can be input-output identical while differing radically in Φ; since all empirical evidence is functional, a theory that assigns different consciousness to functionally identical systems is either false or shielded from evidence. IIT's response — accepting that a functionally identical unrolled system is not conscious — is a consistent position purchased at the price of empirical contact.

**Means something real.** The bullet IIT bites on simple systems: near-trivial lattice architectures score high Φ, which proponents accept and critics read as a reductio. Whichever way one reads it, the quantity's meaning is carried by axiomatic commitment, not by measurement contact.

Now the fact worth savoring, because it is the strongest single point in FMT's favor on this whole terrain: **crucible independently derived the unfolding argument's engine and FMT answered it differently.** The No-Free-Lunch unrolling result — a finite closed loop over a finite horizon equals feedforward plus memory — is the same mathematical fact Doerig et al. aimed at IIT. Where IIT met it by decoupling its quantity from function, FMT met it by moving the claim to where unrolling cannot follow: cost at matched function. The unrolled rival is not denied — it is priced (7.9× synapses in the matched-realism comparison; O(N²) against 2·k·n_ESM as the analytic shape, crossover still unswept and honestly flagged as such). And where a DV must escape the raster-functional class entirely, the programme engineered one that does: the observable-mediation asymmetry is a property of the pair (raster, W), outside the reduction theorem's quantification class by construction. This is what surviving your own unfolding theorem looks like, and it happened by design, not luck: the theory demoted a categorical claim to an efficiency claim *because the implementation forced the issue*. IIT never had the forcing mechanism. That, and not any present superiority of apparatus, is the real asymmetry between the two programmes.

Because the present state of apparatus, held to your own criterion, reads as follows. The formalization paper is a specification: §1.3 defers verification of the mathematics to domain experts; L1 and L2 are open; ν_crit has no measurement; ρ(s, ν, t) has no estimator; the θ_k are free parameters; the category-theoretic modules have no falsifier yet. The one Phase-1-ready piece — the permeability profile as transfer entropy, Table 1 — is testable precisely because it borrows established instruments, and its discriminating power against rivals is unshown (predictive processing also predicts directed-information changes under psychedelics; the LZ/PCI convergence is already credited as consistent-with, correctly, which concedes it is not a discriminator). So: on "reproducible," Φ currently leads — it has a reference implementation and FMT's model density has none. On "testable," FMT's Phase 1 leads conditionally — the predictions are reachable by existing instruments, which Φ's canonical quantity never will be — but the condition is that someone runs them. On "means something real," CRU-58 showed FMT's first operationalization of its central construct failing construct validity — and being caught in weeks, which is the entire difference.

The honest summary you asked for: **FMT's formalism is not currently better than Φ on your criterion; it is younger than Φ, with a functioning correction loop that Φ's structure forecloses.** The §1.3 concession puts FMT in a *stronger* position than IIT with respect to the criterion's purpose — it prevents the claims from outrunning the instruments, which is the exact failure that made Φ your negative example — and a *weaker* position with respect to the criterion's letter, today. The gap between those two readings is closed by exactly one thing: executing Phase 1. A specification plus a disclaimer, left unexecuted, is formula-spitting with better manners.

One tension inside your own paper should be named while we are here. §1.1 frames the formalization gap as: IIT has Φ, PP has free energy, GNW has broadcasting models, FMT has verbal descriptions — "this is its single largest vulnerability." If Φ is the negative example, possession of a formalism cannot be the standard, and §1.1 partially argues from the badge rather than the constraint. The paper's own better statement is §9.1 (constraint, quantitative predictions, functional forms that can be empirically wrong). At the next version, the gap should be measured in constraints the theory accepts, not in notation the rivals own. This is a framing repair, not a claims repair.

## 5. The decision rule

What you asked for: a checkable test for whether a given piece of formalism earns its place in an FMT paper, usable by a future session without me present. Four questions, asked of the formal object in the order given.

**Q1 — Selection.** What choice does this formalism force that the words leave open? Name the readings it separates. If every verbal reading maps to the same formal statement, it is transcription: permissible as compression, never creditable as contribution, and cut when it costs space.

**Q2 — Falsifier with an instrument.** What measured number would refute it — and has the instrument been shown *able* to produce that number? (An estimator never shown to move is not an instrument; a null from a rig never shown capable of a positive is not a null. The branching-sigma saturation and the S4 rig are the standing exhibits.) No answer: the object is roadmap-grade. It may appear in a specification paper flagged open; it may not carry weight in a results paper or be cited as if operationalized.

**Q3 — Construct check.** What else would produce the same number? State the confound, and state what would show the quantity is tracking the wrong construct. Concretely: does the manipulation manipulate the construct, or something merely co-resident with it? This is the CRU-58 question, and it is asked *before* the experiment, in writing. A control is only as good as the variable it moves.

**Q4 — Unrolling survival.** If a functionally matched rival reproduces the number, what happens to the claim? Acceptable answers: the claim is cost-indexed and the rival's price is the DV; or the quantity is provably outside the raster-functional class. Unacceptable: the tie refutes the theory (then the claim was mis-posed as necessity), or the tie is ignored (then the quantity has Φ's disease).

Genre gate on top: a **specification paper** may carry objects passing only Q1, labeled open — the formalization paper is this genre and mostly complies. A **results paper** requires all four for every quantity that carries a conclusion. The **master paper** may state its asserted-not-derived identities (§3.4.3 and its kin) as commitments, never as derivations — which it already does.

Worked, for calibration: permeability-as-transfer-entropy passes Q1 (it selects directed flow over correlation, and channel-family over scalar), Q2 (Table 1's profile, with established estimators), Q3 partially (the confound — TE changes from arousal or gain changes rather than boundary permeability — should be written down before Phase 1 runs), Q4 (a profile claim, cost-free, no unrolling exposure). Φ(m*) = m* passes Q1 (it selects the constitutive reading and gives the "no outside" argument a definite mathematical shape) and nothing else yet — roadmap-grade, correctly housed in Phase 3, and the rule's function is to keep it from ever being cited as though it had passed Q2. `closure_enabled=False` passed Q1 and Q2 and was never asked Q3; asking it would have cost one SCC decomposition — milliseconds — and would have saved four experiments.

The rule's one-line form, for the file header of any future formal section: *what does it select, what would kill it, what else makes that number, what does the tie cost the rival.*

## 6. What changes in the papers

**`paper/fmt_formal/fmt-formalization.md`** — published; changes target the next Zenodo version, but item (a) binds the programme now.

- (a) **P1 — Phase 4's ablation predictions inherit the CRU-58 exposure, and any future run must carry an ablation-validity audit.** §8 Phase 4 rests on "architectural ablation as the critical test," and prediction 1 ("ESM ablation produces categorical behavioral difference") operationalizes ablation the way the knob did. CRU-58 proved that on a recurrent substrate an ablation can decompose rather than remove — the ablated arm carried a purified self-loop. Before any Phase-4 result is read, the ablated arm must be audited: SCC decomposition of the effective connectivity, dominant-loop-mode with enrichment correction, verification that the removed mechanism has not reconstituted in the remaining tissue.
