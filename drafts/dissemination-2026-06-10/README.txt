TITLE:
The Four-Model Theory of Consciousness: A Simulation-Based Framework Unifying
the Hard Problem, Binding, and Altered States

AUTHOR: Matthias Gruber  ·  ORCID 0009-0005-9697-1665  ·  matthias@matthiasgruber.com

ABSTRACT (paste into ResearchGate / Zenodo abstract field):
This paper presents the Four-Model Theory, in which consciousness is constituted by ongoing self-simulation across four nested models arranged along two axes — scope (world vs. self) and mode (implicit vs. explicit). The implicit models are substrate-level, learned, and non-conscious; the explicit models are virtual, transient, and phenomenal. Qualia are constitutive properties of the computational level — virtual constructs incoherent at the substrate level, as a spreadsheet cell's value is incoherent at the transistor level. The theory addresses the Hard Problem by revealing a category error that seeks phenomenal properties where they categorically do not exist, and explains why self-simulation specifically generates experience through self-referential closure. Combined with a criticality requirement, the theory derives diverse phenomena from five principles: criticality, virtual qualia, a redirectable self-model, variable permeability, and simulation forking — unifying psychedelic phenomenology, anesthetic mechanisms, dream states, split-brain phenomena, dissociative identity disorder, and animal consciousness. Several claims derived from the theory's 2015 axioms have received converging independent support (Hengen & Shew, 2025; Algom & Shriki, 2026; Xu et al., 2024), though this convergence is shared with competing criticality-based frameworks. Four novel predictions remain untested — including that psychedelics should alleviate anosognosia and that ego dissolution content is controllable via sensory input — predictions no competing theory generates.

KEYWORDS: consciousness; hard problem; self-model; simulation; qualia; criticality;
binding problem; altered states; psychedelics; self-referential closure
