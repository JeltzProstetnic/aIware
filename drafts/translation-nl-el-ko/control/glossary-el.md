corpus — book, papers, figures — uses IWM/EWM/ISM/ESM. **Keep the abbreviations exactly: IWM, EWM, ISM, ESM.** Latin

- First use in a chapter: full EL name + (IWM). Thereafter the abbreviation alone («Το IWM…»), which reads naturally in
  Greek science prose. The abbreviations are neuter and take the neuter article: **το IWM, το EWM, το ISM, το ESM**
  (τα μοντέλα → τα IWM/EWM… in the plural).
- **implicit/explicit = υπόρρητο/ρητό — LOCKED.** Do NOT drift to έμμεσο/άμεσο (=indirect/direct — imports the wrong

- Generic "a self-model" (NOT one of the four) → **μοντέλο εαυτού**. Generic "world-model" → **μοντέλο κόσμου**.

## 2. Consciousness-terminology discipline (Greek FUSES consciousness with moral conscience — this is the central trap)

Unlike Japanese, Greek **συνείδηση carries BOTH meanings** — consciousness AND moral conscience (like IT *coscienza*,
ES *conciencia*, FR *conscience*). The whole book is about the first sense; a single slip into the moral reading
corrupts the argument. The discipline:

- **consciousness = συνείδηση, always** (the awareness/experience sense). Qualify when EN qualifies: φαινομενική
  συνείδηση, συνείδηση πρόσβασης, βασική συνείδηση, εκτεταμένη συνείδηση.
- **Moral "conscience" (if it appears) = ηθική συνείδηση / η φωνή της συνείδησης** — always disambiguated with ηθική
  or context. Never leave a bare συνείδηση where the moral sense is meant; never let the moral halo bleed into the
  theory's συνείδηση.
- **awareness (the content-directed access sense) = επίγνωση — LOCKED.** In this book "awareness" means *being aware
  OF a content* (the access sense). Render as **επίγνωση** / «επίγνωση του Χ». This preserves the EN
  awareness-vs-consciousness contrast cleanly (επίγνωση vs συνείδηση), and επίγνωση is the natural, established EL
  rendering in cognitive-science and mindfulness prose.
  - Do **NOT** use **αυτεπίγνωση / αυτογνωσία** for plain awareness — those are self-directed and would silently
    collapse world-directed awareness into self-awareness. Permitted ONLY where the EN itself says *self-aware(ness)*.
  - Do **NOT** default to **αντίληψη** (=perception — a different faculty) or **γνώση/αναγνώριση** as renderings of
    "awareness". Do not improvise synonyms (συνειδητοποίηση is allowed only for the punctual EN "to realize/become
    aware", never as the standing term for "awareness").
- **The felt floor = αισθαντικότητα (sentience)** — not επίγνωση, not bare συνείδηση. Gloss on first use (§1).
- **conscious / unconscious (adjective) = συνειδητός / ασυνείδητος**; "the unconscious processing" = η ασυνείδητη
  επεξεργασία. Keep ασυνείδητο (Freudian noun "the unconscious") out of it unless the EN invokes psychoanalysis.
- **self-model is the escape hatch** — when the EN leans on architecture, keep it architectural: μοντέλο εαυτού. It
  sidesteps the συνείδηση/conscience collision entirely.

## 4. Do NOT translate (leave verbatim)

- Proper names, author name, place names (Innsbruck stays Ίνσμπρουκ in prose per §researcher-name rule; the author's
  name **Matthias Gruber** stays Latin on the cover/title). Bibliographic citations, journal titles, DOIs, reference
  entries — all verbatim.
- The abbreviations IWM/EWM/ISM/ESM and the acronym FMT.
- Technical acronyms (fMRI, MEG, ECoG, EEG, DMT, NDE, IIT, Φ, LZ, PCI, DOC) — keep as-is in Latin (Φ is already Greek —
  keep it); may gloss on first use, e.g. fMRI (λειτουργική μαγνητική τομογραφία).
- Researcher names in running prose take standard Greek transliteration on first mention with the Latin original in
  parentheses — e.g. Τσάλμερς (Chalmers), Μέτζινγκερ (Metzinger), Ντένετ (Dennett), Λίμπετ (Libet) — thereafter Greek
  alone. Inside citations and reference entries the Latin form stays verbatim (no transliteration).
