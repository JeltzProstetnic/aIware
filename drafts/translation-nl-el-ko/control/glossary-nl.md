- **implicit/explicit = impliciet/expliciet — LOCKED.** Do NOT switch to latent/manifest or onbewust/bewust. ## 2. Consciousness-terminology discipline (Dutch is CLEAN on the base term — the traps are on the awareness side)

Dutch does not fuse consciousness with moral conscience (unlike IT/ES/FR coscienza/conciencia/conscience): **bewustzijn** = consciousness, **geweten** = moral conscience — two entirely separate words. So *bewustzijn* is stable. The discipline:

- **consciousness = bewustzijn, always.** Qualify when EN qualifies: fenomenaal bewustzijn, toegangsbewustzijn, basaal bewustzijn, uitgebreid bewustzijn.
- **awareness (the content-directed access sense) = gewaarzijn — LOCKED.** In this book "awareness" means *being aware OF a content* (the access sense). Render as **gewaarzijn** / "het gewaar zijn van X". This preserves the EN awareness-vs-consciousness contrast cleanly (gewaarzijn vs bewustzijn), and gewaarzijn is the natural, established rendering in NL cognitive-science and mindfulness prose.
  - Do **NOT** collapse "awareness" into **bewustzijn** — that erases the contrast the whole argument runs on (this is the primary Dutch trap).
  - Do **NOT** use **zelfbewustzijn** for this — that is self-directed and would silently collapse world-directed awareness into self-awareness, corrupting the theory. "zelfbewustzijn" is permitted ONLY where the EN itself says *self-aware(ness)* in the everyday sense.
  - **besef, gewaarwording, notie, gevoel** and bare English **awareness** are all **FORBIDDEN** as renderings of "awareness" in this book — do not improvise synonyms.
- **The felt floor = sentientie (sentience)** — not gewaarzijn, not bare bewustzijn. Gloss on first use (§1).
- **Moral "conscience" (if it appears) = geweten.** Never "bewustzijn".
- **self-model is the escape hatch** — when the EN leans on architecture, keep it architectural: **zelfmodel**. It sidesteps the lexical wars in every language, including Dutch.
