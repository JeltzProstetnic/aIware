# RIM Related-Work / Convergence — Prior-Art Citation Verification

**Purpose:** Verify exact bibliographic details and characterize how strongly each predecessor
claims motivation is part of cognitive architecture, so the RIM "convergence" passage can be
framed honestly ("consistent with" / "independently converges with" — never "they proved our thesis").

**RIM's own differentiator (do NOT attribute to any source below):** the *multiplicative recursive*
formalization K × P × M (motivation as a scalar factor whose absence zeroes out effective
intelligence) plus *falsifiable predictions*. The sources below make **qualitative architectural**
claims that motivation/drives are integral to cognition; none formalize intelligence as a
multiplicative product, and none derive falsifiable quantitative predictions from it. So the
correct verb is **"converges with"** on the constitutive claim and **"goes beyond"** on the formalism.

Verified via web search/fetch on 2026-07-01. All bibliographic details cross-checked against
publisher/journal/DOI sources.

## 1. Dietrich Dörner — Psi-theory (cognition + motivation + emotion integrated)

### 1a. German monograph — CONFIRMED
> **Dörner, D. (1999). *Bauplan für eine Seele.* Reinbek bei Hamburg: Rowohlt.**
> Hardcover, 1st edition, 831 pp. ISBN 3-498-01288-6 (= 9783498012885).
> (Later paperback: Rowohlt Taschenbuch / rororo, 2001, ISBN 3-499-61193-7 / 9783499611933.)

What it argues: A book-length attempt to model the human psyche — including motivation and feeling,
the areas Dörner explicitly says psychology and AI have neglected — as a single "hyper-complex
machine." The whole project is that a soul/mind cannot be modeled by cognition alone: needs,
drives, and emotions are built into the design, not appended. This is the foundational statement
of Psi-theory.

### 1b. Best English peer-reviewed statement — CONFIRMED
> **Dörner, D., & Güss, C. D. (2013). PSI: A Computational Architecture of Cognition, Motivation,
> and Emotion. *Review of General Psychology*, 17(3), 297–317.**
> DOI: 10.1037/a0032947

What it argues: Presents PSI theory as a *formalized computational architecture* that, unlike most
architectures, models not only cognition but also motivational and emotional processes **and their
interactions**. Core constructs: five basic human needs, need-satisfaction driving perception,
memory schemas, planning and action; emotion is defined and its role in cognitive/motivational
processes elaborated.

**Constitutive vs. modulator:** **CONSTITUTIVE (strong)** — for motivation. Psi-theory explicitly
rejects treating motivation/emotion as a bolt-on: "Emotion is not understood as an independent
sub-system, a module or a parameter set, but an intrinsic aspect of cognition." Drives are
*foundational* — without need-satisfaction pressure the cognitive system "would lack direction and
purpose." **Nuance to be honest about:** in Psi-theory *emotion* is treated as an **emergent
property of modulation** of cognition (arousal, focus depth, sampling rate), whereas *motivation
(the needs/drives)* is the constitutive driver. So the strong constitutive convergence is on
**motivation/drives**, not on emotion-as-a-factor. It is an architecture-level claim about a
situated agent — it does not cast motivation as a scalar multiplier of an intelligence quantity.

## 2. Joscha Bach — MicroPsi / Psi (architecture of *motivated* cognition)

### 2a. Book — CONFIRMED
> **Bach, J. (2009). *Principles of Synthetic Intelligence — PSI: An Architecture of Motivated
> Cognition.* Oxford University Press. Oxford Series on Cognitive Models and Architectures (vol. 4).**
> ISBN 978-0-19-537067-6 (hardcover 0195370678).

Subtitle, year, and publisher all confirmed exactly as the prompt stated. Based on Bach's doctoral
work at the Institute for Cognitive Science, Universität Osnabrück; introduces the MicroPsi
implementation of Dörner's PSI.

What it argues: The very title stakes the claim — cognition is **motivated cognition**. Bach
integrates motivation and emotion with perception and reasoning over grounded neuro-symbolic
(spreading-activation) representations, arguing an integrated understanding of mind *requires* the
motivational layer, not merely benefits from it.

### 2b. MicroPsi conference/technical papers — CONFIRMED (two available; the 2015 one is more on-point)
> **Bach, J. (2012). MicroPsi 2: The Next Generation of the MicroPsi Framework. In *Artificial
> General Intelligence — AGI 2012* (LNAI/LNCS 7716, pp. 11–20). Berlin/Heidelberg: Springer.**
> DOI: 10.1007/978-3-642-35506-6_2
>
> **More directly on motivation:** **Bach, J. (2015). Modeling Motivation in MicroPsi 2. In
> *Artificial General Intelligence — AGI 2015* (LNCS 9205, ch. 1, pp. 3–13). Springer.**
> DOI: 10.1007/978-3-319-21365-1_1

What they argue: The 2012 paper documents the re-implementation of MicroPsi as neuro-symbolic
spreading-activation networks (the substrate). The 2015 paper is the strongest single-paper
statement that motivation is *central* to the architecture — modeling drives/needs as what
selects goals and steers cognition. **Recommendation:** if citing one MicroPsi paper for the
*motivation* point, cite the 2015 "Modeling Motivation in MicroPsi 2"; use the 2012 paper only if
you want the architecture-substrate reference.

**Constitutive vs. modulator:** **CONSTITUTIVE (strong).** Bach's entire framing ("motivated
cognition") is that cognition is organized around and driven by needs; motivation is architectural
bedrock. Same caveat as Dörner: it is an agent-architecture claim, not a multiplicative-factor
model of an intelligence score.

## 3. Ron Sun — CLARION motivational subsystem (drives → goals)

### CONFIRMED (recommended primary citation)
> **Sun, R. (2009). Motivational Representations within a Computational Cognitive Architecture.
> *Cognitive Computation*, 1(1), 91–103.**
> DOI: 10.1007/s12559-009-9005-z

Alternative book-length source (also real, use if you want a monograph):
> **Sun, R. (2016). *Anatomy of the Mind: Exploring Psychological Mechanisms and Processes with
> the Clarion Cognitive Architecture.* Oxford University Press.**

What it argues: Sun argues for the **essential motivational representations necessary for a
comprehensive computational cognitive architecture** — a dual structure of *implicit drive
representations* (low-level primary drives = physiological; high-level primary drives = social;
plus derived/secondary drives) and *explicit goal representations*. Drives establish motivational
context; goals are formulated from them; the action-centered subsystem then executes. In CLARION,
the Motivational Subsystem (MS) is one of the architecture's core subsystems (alongside ACS, NACS,
and the Meta-Cognitive Subsystem) — i.e., it is **built into** the architecture, not optional.

**Constitutive vs. modulator:** **CONSTITUTIVE (strong).** Sun's language — motivation as
"essential ... necessary for a comprehensive computational cognitive architecture" — is an explicit
necessity claim: you cannot have a complete cognitive architecture without the motivational
subsystem. **Honest nuance:** CLARION treats motivation as a *necessary distinct subsystem* within
a modular architecture (motivation is one of four integral pillars), rather than as a term that
multiplies through every cognitive operation. Constitutive at the architecture level; not a
multiplicative-scalar claim.

## 4. Aaron Sloman & Monica Croucher — necessity of emotion/motive mechanisms

### CONFIRMED
> **Sloman, A., & Croucher, M. (1981). Why Robots Will Have Emotions. In *Proceedings of the 7th
> International Joint Conference on Artificial Intelligence (IJCAI-81)*, Vancouver, Vol. 1,
> pp. 197–202.**
> Also issued as Cognitive Science Research Paper, University of Sussex.
> Stable copy: https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/81-1/Papers/039.pdf
> ACM DL: https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.5555/1623156.1623194

What it argues: Emotions are not add-ons but **unavoidable consequences of the design of any
intelligent, resource-bounded agent with multiple motives.** Emotions arise from interactions
between *motives*, beliefs, and percepts: fulfilment or violation of one motive disturbs processes
driven by other motives. Understanding emotion therefore requires understanding motives and the
processes they generate. The paper sets out an interdisciplinary methodology for minds of animals,
humans, and machines and examines prerequisites for intelligent decision-making.

**Constitutive vs. modulator:** **CONSTITUTIVE by necessity** — but of *emotion*, with *motives*
as the underlying substrate. The claim is that motive-processing is central to the architecture of
any intelligent agent and that emotions necessarily emerge from it. This supports the broader
"affect/motivation is architecturally necessary" lineage. **Honest nuance:** it is closest to
"intelligence entails motive-driven processing" but frames the payoff as *emotions*, and makes no
claim resembling motivation-as-a-multiplicative-factor-of-intelligence. Best cited as the earliest
statement of the necessity lineage.

## Framing guidance for the RIM convergence passage (honest summary)

- **All four** independently treat motivation/drives as **constitutive** of cognitive architecture
  (not mere performance modulators) — a genuine, citable convergence for the general lineage claim
  "motivation is integral to cognitive architecture." Safe language: *"RIM's constitutive treatment
  of motivation independently converges with a lineage of motivated-cognition architectures —
  Dörner's Psi-theory (Dörner 1999; Dörner & Güss 2013), Bach's MicroPsi (Bach 2009, 2015), and
  Sun's CLARION (Sun 2009) — as well as early necessity arguments (Sloman & Croucher 1981)."*

- **Two honest boundaries** to preserve RIM's differentiator:
  1. **Scope.** These are *agent-architecture* claims (motivation steers a situated agent's
     cognition). RIM's claim is about *models/measures of intelligence* requiring a motivation term.
     Related but not identical scope — say "converges with" not "identical to."
  2. **Formalism.** None cast motivation as a **multiplicative, recursive scalar** (K × P × M) whose
     absence zeroes out effective intelligence, and none derive **falsifiable quantitative
     predictions** from that structure. This is RIM's unique contribution — the lineage supplies the
     *constitutive intuition*; RIM supplies the *formalization + falsifiability*.

- **Verb discipline:** "consistent with," "independently converges with," "anticipated
  qualitatively by" — never "proves," "confirms," or "establishes" RIM's thesis.
