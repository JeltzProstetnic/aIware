TITLE:
An Operational Detector for Machine Consciousness: Self-Referential Closure and Open-Ended Computation as an Intrinsic, Substrate-Neutral Test

ARTICLE TYPE: Research Article
SECTION: Assessing AI Consciousness

ABSTRACT (148 words):
Whether a machine is conscious cannot be settled by behavior. Intrinsic Computational Functionalism specifies the realization relation a conscious system must satisfy, yet deliberately leaves three questions open: which intrinsic structure is consciousness-relevant, how to extract it, and at what grain. We propose an answer to each. Drawing on the Four-Model Theory of consciousness, we name the structure — self-referential closure, in which a system's self-model and world-model each enter the other's update, sustained by open-ended computation deployed for autonomous self-modeling. We give an extraction procedure: three operational, substrate-neutral checks yielding a verdict with graded confidence plus a unit-individuation edge case. We answer grain with two intrinsic dials, extent and complexity. A minimal open-ended spiking substrate specifies how the checks would be built; toy-scale in-silico dissociations motivate them; a worked embodied example illustrates the detector on a generic architecture class, converting a realization relation into a decision procedure.

KEYWORDS:
machine consciousness; artificial consciousness; Four-Model Theory; intrinsic computational functionalism; self-referential closure; open-ended computation
