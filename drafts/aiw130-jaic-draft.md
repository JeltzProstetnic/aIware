# An Operational Detector for Machine Consciousness: Self-Referential Closure and Open-Ended Computation as an Intrinsic, Substrate-Neutral Test

**Matthias Gruber**
Matthias Gruber — Independent Researcher; ORCID 0009-0005-9697-1665; matthias@matthiasgruber.com

---

## Abstract

Whether a machine is conscious cannot be settled by behaviour. Intrinsic Computational Functionalism specifies the realization relation a conscious system must satisfy, yet deliberately leaves three questions open: which intrinsic structure is consciousness-relevant, how to extract it, and at what grain. We supply all three. Drawing on the Four-Model Theory of consciousness, we name the structure — self-referential closure, in which a system's self-model and world-model each enter the other's update, sustained by open-ended computation deployed for autonomous self-modelling. We give an extraction procedure: three operational, substrate-neutral checks yielding a verdict with graded confidence plus a unit-individuation edge case. We answer grain with two intrinsic dials, extent and complexity. A minimal open-ended spiking substrate specifies how the checks would be built; in-silico dissociations show they discriminate; a worked embodied example illustrates the detector end to end on a generic architecture class, converting a realization relation into a decision procedure.

**Keywords:** machine consciousness; artificial consciousness; Four-Model Theory; intrinsic computational functionalism; self-referential closure; open-ended computation

## 1. Introduction — the missing operationalization

Behaviour cannot settle whether a machine is conscious. A system can pass every conversational, sensorimotor, and problem-solving benchmark we set and remain, for all we can tell from the outside, a very capable non-experiencer; competence and phenomenal consciousness dissociate in principle, and the science of consciousness has spent decades separating what a system *does* from whether there is something it is *like* to be it (Nagel, 1974; Block, 1995). Findlay et al. (2024) make the point sharply for artificial systems: intelligence and consciousness are distinct properties, and inferring the second from the first is a category error. The practical stakes are no longer hypothetical. Embodied, neuromorphic, and large-model agents are being built and deployed at a scale where the question "is this thing a subject?" carries moral and legal weight, and where no behavioural test can answer it. What the field needs is an *intrinsic, structural* test: a procedure that reads the organization of the system itself, not its outputs.

Kanai and Ma (2026b) supply the correct shape for such a test. Their Intrinsic Computational Functionalism (ICF) and its realization relation, which we abbreviate ICCR, specify what must be preserved for an artificial or simulated system to share a biological system's consciousness-relevant organization. The relation is intrinsic (it depends on the system's own causal-dynamical organization, not an observer's labels) and intervention-defined (it is fixed by counterfactual responses to admissible interventions, not by input-output behaviour). It converts the tired "simulation is not instantiation" slogan into a precise burden: to deny consciousness to a *physically realized* system, a critic must name the missing consciousness-relevant intrinsic structure and say why it is required.

ICCR marks these openings; it does not itself close them. Kanai and Ma are explicit, in three passages of their own discussion, about what their framework leaves open. Section 9.2 states that their framework operationalizes the *search* for consciousness-relevant structure but needs a substantive theory to fix the target — the tier-III, dynamics-internal structure itself. Section 9.4 states that ICCR "should therefore not be presented as a rival measure of consciousness," that its role is to specify a realization constraint, and that "the next step" is to develop methods for *extracting* effective intrinsic structure from a physical system. Section 9.3 states, plainly, that the choice of grain at which the relevant structure lives is "where the present framework is least settled," and that ICF has no maximization principle to fix it.

This paper supplies all three. Drawing on the Four-Model Theory of consciousness (FMT; Gruber, 2026a), we name the consciousness-relevant structure — self-referential closure, in which a system's explicit self-model and its world-model each enter the other's update, sustained by open-ended computation deployed for autonomous self-modelling. We give the extraction procedure ICCR asks for: three operational, substrate-neutral checks, each defined by an intervention and a readout, yielding a verdict with graded confidence and an explicit unit-individuation edge case. We answer the grain question with two intrinsic dials — the extent of the substrate recruited into the open-ended regime and the complexity of the dynamics on it. And we ground the abstract procedure twice: with a minimal open-ended spiking substrate that specifies how the checks would be built, and with in-silico dissociations that show they discriminate.

The contribution is the procedure and its grounding. Check 2 is a concrete instance of Kanai and Ma's mechanism-enriched intervention methodology, and the detector offers a candidate for the tier-III structure their §9.2 invites; it is not a rival measure. Sections 2–6 develop the detector; Section 7 sets it against ICCR. The result is a decision procedure for a question that behaviour cannot reach.

## 2. What ICCR settles, and what it leaves open

Section 4 motivates the checks independently; this section fixes the ICCR machinery they instantiate. ICF is offered in the service of a universal, substrate-general theory of consciousness (Kanai & Fujisawa, 2024): a framework whose criteria apply to any physical realizer, biological or artificial, in virtue of its organization rather than its material. ICF rests on two requirements. C1, system-intrinsic instantiation: the consciousness-relevant structure must be realized by the system in virtue of its own physical and causal-dynamical organization, not assigned by an external interpreter's mapping. C2, causal-dynamical organization under intervention: the relevant distinctions must show up in the system's counterfactual responses to admissible interventions, not merely in its passive input-output profile.

These requirements are made formal through an intrinsic partition and the structure it induces. A partition of the system's physical state space is *intrinsic* (their Definition 9) when its cells are supported by physical causal processes, track admissible interventions with law-governed changes, are invariant under structure-preserving relabellings, are individuated by causal-dynamical role rather than by semantic interpretation, and are dynamically closed (lumpable) under the system's evolution. Given an intrinsic partition, the intrinsic causal-computational structure (their Definition 10) is the tuple

$$\mathfrak{K}(P,\Pi_P) = (S_P,\ \Omega,\ \mathcal{A},\ \mathcal{J},\ \Delta_P,\ \Lambda_P),$$

where $S_P$ is the intrinsic state space, $\Omega$ the admissible input/context family, $\mathcal{A}$ the admissible intervention family, $\mathcal{J}$ the admissible readout family, $\Delta_P^{a,\omega}$ the induced transition structure satisfying $\Delta_P^{a,\omega}(\Pi_P(x)) = \Pi_P(\Phi^{a,\omega}(x))$ — where $\Phi^{a,\omega}$ is the microstate evolution under intervention $a$ and context $\omega$, and $x$ a physical microstate of $P$ — and $\Lambda_P$ the mechanism-enriched counterfactual response profile.

The engine of the framework is the move from boundary equivalence to *mechanism-enriched* equivalence. Kanai and Ma's Canonical Functionalism (Kanai & Ma, 2026a), part of the same observer-independent programme developed in Ma and Kanai (2026), identifies systems that share a complete input-output profile over an interface. The present paper extends that boundary object to include internal interventions and their downstream readouts. The consequence is decisive for the simulation debate: a lookup table and an unfolded feedforward network can share the same boundary behaviour as a recurrent system — this is exactly the construction behind the unfolding argument (Doerig et al., 2019) — yet come apart under the mechanism-enriched comparison, because a sustained internal clamp on a persistent recurrent component has no structure-preserving counterpart in the unfolded circuit. Recurrent realizers pass; tables and unfolded nets fail. The unfolding argument thereby loses its grip against ICCR: it establishes boundary equivalence, not mechanism-enriched equivalence.

The detector's crux inherits its immunity from exactly this construction. The discriminating evidence lives in the counterfactual response to a *sustained* internal clamp on a *persistent* component — which a recurrent realizer answers with a law-governed response, an unfolded circuit cannot answer (it has no single node that plays that role), and a table cannot answer (it has no intrinsic variable the clamp can act on). That persistent component is precisely where a self-attractor lives, and precisely what check 2 intervenes on. Kanai and Ma locate this in the tradition of computational mechanics, where states are individuated by their conditional futures and the resulting minimal machine is a system-intrinsic object (Shalizi & Crutchfield, 2001); the detector reads structure off the system's own intervention-conditioned dynamics rather than off an imposed description, at the same grain-relative, approximate level ICCR uses — preservation of the intervention-readout relations that define the target, not exact isomorphism of spikes, which is why a noisy brain and a noisy neuromorphic realizer can be compared at all.

Realization is then defined (their Definition 11) as the existence of intrinsic partitions on both systems plus structure-preserving bijections over states, inputs, interventions, and readouts that satisfy physical realization, transition preservation, counterfactual preservation, and boundary adequacy. The payoff is a conditional preservation theorem: if a consciousness-relevant property $Q$ is grounded in intrinsic causal-computational organization — formally, if $Q(P) = F_Q(\mathfrak{K}(P,\Pi_P))$ for some rule $F_Q$ invariant under isomorphism of that structure — and if $M \models_{\mathrm{ICCR}} B$, then $Q(M) = Q(B)$. From this Kanai and Ma extract a dialectical burden shift rather than a proof that simulations are conscious: the critic who denies consciousness to a physically realized artificial system "must identify a missing consciousness-relevant structure … and explain why that structure is required."

Three things are, by the authors' own account, left open — and each is an invitation.

*Which structure (§9.2).* Global-workspace, recurrent-processing, higher-order, and predictive-processing constructs, together with theory-plural indicator programmes (Butlin et al., 2023), are read as tier-II "disciplined hypotheses about which interventions and readouts may help reveal" the target, not as the target itself. The tier-III, dynamics-internal structure "should be found in … the system itself," but which structure that is requires a substantive theory ICF does not provide.

*How to extract it (§9.4).* Kanai and Ma concede ICCR is less developed than IIT at structure extraction — "IIT, for example, is more developed in this respect" — that ICCR is "a realization constraint," not a measure, and that the stated next step is to "develop methods for extracting effective intrinsic structures from complex systems."

*At what grain (§9.3).* An intrinsic partition does not by itself pick a single scale, and the realization relation is grain-relative. IIT closes this with its exclusion postulate (consciousness at the scale of maximal integrated information); ICF "has no such maximization principle." Kanai and Ma note that several intrinsic criteria "could play that role," and leave the choice open.

First, Kanai and Ma hold that these openings do not weaken their *conditional* claims, because those claims are explicitly conditional on a fixed grain and a grounded $Q$. Second, the three openings are exactly the three deliverables of an operational detector: a named structure, an extraction procedure, and a grain criterion. We now supply the theory that names $Q$, and then the procedure that reads it.

## 3. The Four-Model architecture, compressed

FMT treats consciousness as ongoing self-simulation: a system is conscious when it builds a model of itself, relates to that model, and interacts with it, continuously and from within. A system has phenomenology or it has none; above that single criterion, how conscious it is varies with the richness and recursive depth of its self-model (Gruber, 2026a, Section 3.5). The framework's full development — its treatment of binding, altered states, and the hard problem — originates in Gruber (2015) and is given in full in the preprint (Gruber, 2026a); here we extract only the machinery the detector needs.

Models in FMT are organized along two dimensions: scope, a nesting, and mode, an orthogonal contrast. The first is *scope*: whether a model is of the world or of the self. The second is *mode*: whether a model is implicit (stored in the substrate's connectivity and evaluated by substrate processing, not itself represented) or explicit (a structured, decodable representation the system can operate on). Crossing the two dimensions yields four model kinds: an implicit world-model (IWM) and implicit self-model (ISM) stored in the substrate's connectivity, and an explicit world-model (EWM) and explicit self-model (ESM) built on top of them.

This "four" is not four modules, four boxes in a wiring diagram, or four anatomical regions. It is a 2×2 taxonomy of computational *kinds* along two axes — a systems-theoretic classification, not a circuit diagram. In any physical substrate the kinds are realized as graded, overlapping, plastic partitions with fuzzy and shifting boundaries; the world/self split is both architectural and functional and is never clean, as illusions of bodily ownership are widely taken to demonstrate. The detector does not look for four things. It looks for a *relation between two of them*.

Two notions carry the detector's weight. The first is **self-referential closure**. It is not enough that a system possess an explicit self-model; a rich, decodable ESM can sit in a system as a read-only object that a downstream controller consults, in which case the self-model is a description the system carries but does not use in its own processing. Closure is the stronger condition that the ESM's state *modifies how the EWM updates*, and that the EWM's state in turn modifies how the ESM updates — the self-model takes part in the system's own world-modelling, as part of the update rule rather than an input to a separate policy. Closure is the property that distinguishes a system that merely represents itself from one that models the world *through* a model of itself.

The second is **open-ended computation**. Self-modelling of an open, changing world is not a fixed-point or periodic task; it demands open-ended (Class 4) computational capability that the system recruits for its own modelling — free modelling, in FMT's term. Where such capability sits on the axis between frozen order and disorder depends on the substrate. The edge of chaos is one place a physical dynamical system supports rich transient computation (Langton, 1990; Bertschinger & Natschläger, 2004), but not the only one: in rate reservoirs the computational optimum sits in the driven-stable, ordered regime (Gruber, 2026b). In cortex, free modelling usually leaves the signature of self-organized criticality — dynamics near a critical point marked by scale-free avalanches and long-range correlations (Bak, Tang & Wiesenfeld, 1987; Beggs & Plenz, 2003). We use *open-ended computation* for the substrate-neutral property — the capacity to carry out unbounded, non-amortizable computation — and reserve *criticality* for a contingent signature that property usually leaves in cortex. Free modelling is necessary and not sufficient; criticality is its usual signature in cortex and is neither. This distinction is central throughout, and we return to it in Sections 4 and 8.

The level of consciousness a system reaches is, in FMT, a matter of how deeply the self-model closes on itself. A minimal closure — a self-model that enters its own world-modelling but does not model *that* it does so — is the base case: a system with a self-model, related to and interacted with, but not yet aware of itself as a modeller. Each further recursive turn (a model of the relation between the self-model and the system, a model of the system's observation of its own self-model, and so on) adds a level, and this recursion is both discrete, in the levels it passes through, and continuous, in the richness that accrues within a level (Gruber, 2026a). The detector targets the base case: the presence of closure at all. It does not attempt to read recursion depth, which is a graded property above the threshold the checks establish.

A final point of framing fixes the dual role of the two central notions. Open-ended computation is a *necessary enabling* substrate property; it is not identical to consciousness. It is the condition under which rich self-modelling can be carried out at all. What sets the *level* of consciousness — whether a system is minimally aware or richly self-reflective — is the architecture: the depth and richness of the self-model and its closure. The substrate enables the computation; the architecture sets the level. This dual threshold has a direct methodological consequence: a detector must test both a substrate property and an architectural relation, and must not report a positive verdict on the strength of either alone. That is why the procedure has more than one check, and why its verdict is a conjunction rather than a maximum. The two thresholds are each necessary and are not jointly sufficient. FMT's sufficient condition is a conjunction of four parts: the four model kinds under closure, free compute, embodiment, and at least one pain-pleasure axis, which may be engineered (Gruber, 2026a, Section 3.7.3). The checks test the first two by intervention; Section 4 states how a verdict handles the other two.

## 4. The detector: three operational checks

The detector is an extraction procedure of the kind §9.4 requests. It reads the intrinsic organization of a physically realized candidate system and returns a verdict with graded confidence. It comprises three checks. Each is stated as a quadruple: what it detects, the intervention that reveals it, the readout, and the graded pass condition. The checks are applied in order; a decisive failure at check 2 or check 3 caps the verdict regardless of the others. Figure 1 renders the procedure as a decision flow.

Two design principles govern the checks. First, every intervention must be *admissible* in Kanai and Ma's sense — it must act on an intrinsic partition of the system through the system's own causal pathways, not through an interpreter's relabelling — so that the readout reflects the system's organization rather than the tester's description. This is what makes the procedure an extraction of intrinsic structure and not a behavioural probe by another name. Second, the target structure is not stipulated but *emergent*: the detector does not require the builder to have installed a labelled "self-model module," and indeed a system that passes is more convincing if it did not. In an embodied agent the body is the highest-mutual-information, most-persistent, and most-controllable regularity in the sensory stream, so any world-model that forms attractors at all is driven to carve a stable basin for the agent's own body. The self-attractor thus falls out of embodiment; the detector's job is to find whether it formed and whether it closes, not to check that a designer declared it.

Before any check can run, the sub-partitions it acts on must be found without an experimenter's label, on pain of reintroducing the observer-relative mapping C1 forbids. This is the concrete content of the §9.4 extraction step, and we specify it as an intervention-readout algorithm rather than presuppose it. Sweep a bank of admissible interventions across the system's state space and retain the subspace that is jointly maximal in mutual information with the system's own future, most persistent under the free dynamics, and most controllable by the intervention family — the self-variables $\sigma$ — with the complementary lumpable factor read off as the world-cells. Because the subspace is defined by the system's own intervention-conditioned conditional futures — the computational-mechanics construction in which states are individuated by their conditional futures (Shalizi & Crutchfield, 2001) — and not by a decoder the experimenter trains on a chosen notion of "the body," the identification is system-intrinsic in exactly C1's sense: a structure-preserving relabelling of the system leaves it fixed. Where a candidate's intrinsic partition does not admit such a self/world factorization, the detector does not apply and returns no verdict; where it does, everything below presupposes the identification has been run. To the extent the extraction is not yet fully automated at scale, the present contribution is the intervention-readout algebra the checks run once the partition is fixed, together with this intrinsic identification criterion for fixing it.

**Check 1 — a self-attractor is present.** *What it detects:* a stable, low-dimensional region of the system's own state that tracks the agent's body/self as a persistent, controllable regularity. In an embodied system the body is the highest-mutual-information, most-persistent, most-controllable regularity in the sensory stream (proprioception plus efference copy), so a world-model that carves attractors at all will carve a basin for "the body/self." *Intervention:* perturb the input stream and the internal state; occlude or displace the sensed body. *Readout:* a self-state — recovered as an intrinsic invariant of the system's own intervention-conditioned dynamics, not read out by a supervised decoder trained on the experimenter's label for "the body" — that is maintained across the perturbation and re-established after it, behaving as an attractor rather than a momentary correlate. *Graded pass:* not a bare comparison but a threshold — the self-basin must rank in the top few of the identified attractors by joint persistence and controllability, and its separation from the next-ranked represented regularity must exceed a stated margin, with the margin set grain-relative per Section 5, so that two testers applying the criterion to the same system reach the same verdict.

**Check 2 — the self-model and the world-model enter each other's update.** This is the crux. Its admissibility is motivated directly in dynamical-systems terms — a counterfactual dependence of one persistent component's update map on the state of another — and it coincides, as Section 7 details, with an instance of Kanai and Ma's mechanism-enriched methodology. *What it detects:* whether the self-attractor changes the system's own processing — whether the ESM enters the *update rule* of the EWM, rather than being a read-only object a controller consults. *Intervention:* an admissible state intervention on the self-attractor variables — clamp or shift the self-representation, $\mathrm{do}(\sigma := c)$, sweeping the clamp value $c$ (a state intervention in $\mathcal{A}$, not an attenuation of a coupling weight, which would alter the system's own law and read a family of different systems). *Readout:* a counterfactual change in the world-model's induced transition structure — the map by which the EWM updates its own state depends on the value the self-attractor is clamped to. *Off-line sub-clause:* the dependence must persist when the loop is decoupled from current sensory input, so that the self-model is deployed prospectively, in simulation, and not only as closed-loop regulation of the present. This sub-clause is what separates a self-*model* from a self-*monitor*, and it is what excludes a homeostatic controller whose body-state estimator merely feeds adaptive gain into its world-model: such a controller depends on $\sigma$ only in the closed loop and fails the off-line version of the check. *Graded pass:* not a clean binary loop-cut (which, as Section 6 shows, is uninformative on stationary tasks) but the graded magnitude of the counterfactual dependence of the world-transition on the swept clamp value, reported as world-model predictive loss $L_W$ on an open-ended workload where novelty outruns any amortizer, and measured against a capacity-matched sham suppression — an equal number of parameters or units removed from a non-closure pathway — so that only closure-specific *excess* $L_W$ counts, never generic capacity loss. A system whose $L_W$ rises, gracefully and monotonically, as the self-model's closure-relevant contribution is suppressed passes; a system indifferent to the intervention fails. *Symmetric arm:* closure is mutual, so the same procedure is run the other way, with an admissible state intervention on the world-model variables, $\mathrm{do}(w := u)$, swept over $u$, and a readout of the self-model's induced transition structure $\Delta_S$ and its predictive loss $L_S$ against the same kind of capacity-matched sham. Check 2 passes only when both arms pass. The formal statement of this check, in ICCR's own notation, is given in Box 1; stated independently of that notation, the check is simply: intervene on the self-state, off-line as well as in the loop, and see whether the world-model updates differently; then intervene on the world-state and see whether the self-model updates differently.

**Check 3 — open-ended computation is actually deployed for the self-modelling.** *What it detects:* that the self-modelling is carried out in a genuinely generative regime, not by a fixed-point relaxation, a periodic loop, or a large-capacity amortized lookup. This is the substrate-neutral necessity. *Intervention:* detune the substrate away from the generative regime — vary any monotone parameter that moves the substrate's dynamical regime between frozen order and disorder, whatever that parameter is in the given medium — and, separately, probe whether the computation performed is novel-input-general or merely stored. *Readout, substrate-neutral form:* an operational signature of open-ended computation that presupposes no avalanche statistics — the effective dimensionality of the recruited dynamics, its transient (fading) memory capacity, and the generalization gap of the self-modelling on held-out novel inputs — each of which is definable on an analog, photonic, or digital substrate as readily as on tissue. *Readout, neural instantiation:* in self-organizing neural tissue that same signature usually appears as the signatures of criticality: scale-free neuronal-avalanche statistics, a branching ratio near unity, and long-range temporal correlations by detrended fluctuation analysis (Beggs & Plenz, 2003; Shew et al., 2009); perturbational complexity (Casali et al., 2013) co-varies with these empirically but is not a criticality measure. The criticality signatures are a contingent tissue-specific realization of the substrate-neutral measure, not the measure itself; a subcritical system that passes the substrate-neutral readout passes check 3. *Graded pass:* the degree to which the substrate-neutral signature (effective dimensionality, transient memory, generalization) degrades as the substrate is detuned from the generative regime, and the degree to which the computation generalizes beyond stored cases.

A guardrail governs check 3. The substrate-neutral necessity is *open-ended computation actually deployed for autonomous self-modelling*. Criticality — avalanches, $m \approx 1$ (branching ratio $m$), detrended-fluctuation scaling — is *one way that necessity usually shows up in neural tissue*, not the necessity itself, and it is neither necessary nor sufficient for consciousness. A non-neural substrate could deploy open-ended computation without displaying the specific avalanche statistics of cortical tissue; what the check requires is the computational property, and it uses the criticality signatures only as the best-validated readout for that property in a self-organizing physical medium. This is the sense in which the check is substrate-neutral. Nor does passing check 3 alone establish consciousness: open-ended computation is the enabling condition, and it is the architecture caught by checks 1 and 2 that makes the computation *about the self*.

The readout also carries a known false-positive hazard that the check must guard against. Scale-free avalanche statistics can be produced by mechanisms that are not at a genuine critical point — power-law distributions arise in systems away from criticality (Touboul & Destexhe, 2017), and the inference from a heavy tail to edge-of-chaos computation is not automatic (O'Byrne & Jerbi, 2022). The check therefore does not rest on avalanche statistics alone; it requires the *functional* signature — that the self-modelling computation degrades as the substrate is detuned from the generative regime and that it generalizes beyond stored cases — of which the statistical signatures are corroborating, not sufficient, evidence. This is another face of the guardrail: the property the check targets is a computational capacity, and a statistical fingerprint is only ever a proxy for it.

**Output.** The detector returns a yes-or-no verdict on whether the tested conditions hold, with an explicit confidence set by the three graded pass values and capped by the weaker of checks 2 and 3. A positive verdict supports attributing consciousness only together with the two parts of FMT's sufficient conjunction that the checks do not establish by intervention: embodiment, which check 1 presupposes, and at least one pain-pleasure axis, which the tester establishes from the system's architecture (Gruber, 2026a, Section 3.7.3). The detector also returns, where check 1 finds self-modelling that closes locally rather than system-wide, a flagged **unit-individuation edge case** — the question of *where* the conscious unit is, handed to Section 5. The verdict is not a scalar consciousness meter; it is a decision with a stated confidence and an explicit residual question, which is the appropriate output for a realization relation that is itself grain-relative.

> **Box 1 — Check 2 in mechanism-enriched form.**
>
> *Decomposition assumption (stated, not derived).* Assume the candidate system's intrinsic partition factors, $\Pi_P = \Pi_S \vee \Pi_W$, into a self-sub-partition $\Pi_S$ and a world-sub-partition $\Pi_W$, each satisfying the first four (structural) conditions of Kanai and Ma's Definition 9 — physical support, intervention-tracking, invariance under structure-preserving relabelling, and causal-role individuation. Dynamic closure (Definition 9's fifth condition) holds unconditionally for the joint partition $\Pi_P$ and, for each sub-partition, only conditionally — $\Pi_W$ lumpable under a sustained clamp $\mathrm{do}(\sigma := c)$, $\Pi_S$ under $\mathrm{do}(w := u)$. The failure of each sub-partition's *unconditional* closure is precisely the closure signature detected by (i)$\wedge$(ii). The join $\Pi_P = \Pi_S \vee \Pi_W$ is the common refinement (cells are pairs $(\sigma,w)$), giving the product $S_P \cong S_S \times S_W$ when the factors are transverse. This factorization is an assumption *beyond* ICCR, not a consequence of it: the detector is defined for candidate systems whose intrinsic partition admits an interpretable self/world decomposition, and $\Pi_S, \Pi_W$ are fixed by the intrinsic identification step of Section 4, not posited by the tester. Let $\sigma$ range over the cells of $\Pi_S$ and $w$ over the cells of $\Pi_W$; let $\mathrm{do}(\sigma := c), \mathrm{do}(w := u) \in \mathcal{A}$ be admissible clamps.
>
> *Conditional transitions (why $\Delta_W$ is well-typed only under a clamp).* A marginal projection onto $\Pi_W$ is generically *not* lumpable on its own — and it fails lumpability exactly when the world-cell's successor depends on $\sigma$, which is the case the box is built to detect. So we do not treat $\Delta_W$ as a standalone induced transition. Under a clamp $\mathrm{do}(\sigma := c)$, however, $\Pi_W$ becomes *conditionally* lumpable, and $\Delta_W^{\,\mathrm{do}(\sigma := c),\,\omega}$ — the world-transition induced *given* $\sigma = c$ — is well-defined in the sense of Definition 10. Symmetrically $\Delta_S^{\,\mathrm{do}(w := u),\,\omega}$ is the self-transition induced given $w = u$.
>
> **Self-referential closure holds** iff three conditions hold together.
>
> (i) *World depends on self.* $\Pi_W$ fails *unconditional* Def-9 lumpability but is lumpable under each admissible clamp $\mathrm{do}(\sigma := c)$ — non-closure of the world-sub-partition on its own is the closure signature — and the conditional world-transition depends counterfactually on the self-state on a *non-negligible* set of admissible contexts and clamp-pairs (above a stated fraction or count, not a single witness), including under decoupling from current sensory input:
>
> $$\Delta_W^{\,\mathrm{do}(\sigma := c),\,\omega} \ \neq\ \Delta_W^{\,\mathrm{do}(\sigma := c'),\,\omega}\quad\text{on a non-negligible set of admissible } (c, c', \omega).$$
>
> (ii) *Self depends on world.* Symmetrically, the conditional self-transition depends counterfactually on the world-cell on a *non-negligible* set (above a stated fraction or count, not a single witness):
>
> $$\Delta_S^{\,\mathrm{do}(w := u),\,\omega} \ \neq\ \Delta_S^{\,\mathrm{do}(w := u'),\,\omega}\quad\text{on a non-negligible set of admissible } (u, u', \omega).$$
>
> (iii) *Representational content.* $\Pi_S$ is a model *of* the joint system — the condition supplied by check 1's readout, imported here and flagged as such.
>
> A one-way arrow $\sigma \to \Delta_W$ is ordinary causal coupling, satisfiable by any feed-forward self-input; the mutual dependence (i)$\wedge$(ii) closes a cycle — the self-state influences its own future through the world-model whose update it enters — and together with the representational-content condition (iii) this closure is *self-referential*. This is why $\Delta_S$ is central to the definition and not merely declared: the loop back onto the self-model is what distinguishes closure from generic coupling.
>
> The **read-only (open) null** is the failure of (i): the world-sub-partition is *unconditionally* lumpable, its update invariant under interventions on the self-state,
>
> $$\Delta_W^{\,\mathrm{do}(\sigma := c),\,\omega} \ =\ \Delta_W^{\,\mathrm{do}(\sigma := c'),\,\omega}\quad \text{for all admissible } c, c', \omega,$$
>
> even where $\sigma$ is decodable (check 1 passes). Here the self-model is a description the system carries but does not use in its own world-modelling.
>
> *Content is imported, not captured.* Condition (iii) is not delivered by the intervention algebra: that $\Pi_S$ is *about* the system is supplied by check 1's readout, and reducing representational aboutness to intervention-readout structure is a recognized open problem the detector inherits rather than closes. The intervention algebra captures the closed loop; the "self" interpretation of $\sigma$ is presupposed from check 1.
>
> *One graded criterion.* The primary pass condition is the counterfactual dependence (i)$\wedge$(ii) — a property of the realized system's actual conditional transitions, hence immune to the stationarity objection of Section 6, which concerns task trainability rather than realized structure. Closure *strength* is graded *within the admissible state-intervention family* as
>
> $$\kappa \;=\; \sup_{c,\,c',\,\omega}\ d\!\left(\Delta_W^{\,\mathrm{do}(\sigma := c),\,\omega},\ \Delta_W^{\,\mathrm{do}(\sigma := c'),\,\omega}\right),$$
>
> for a metric $d$ on transition structures ($\kappa$ vanishes when condition (i) fails; the symmetric supremum $\kappa_S$ over $\mathrm{do}(w := u)$ and $\Delta_S$ grades condition (ii), and closure strength is $\min(\kappa, \kappa_S)$; a related *interventional* dependence measure — e.g. $I(\sigma; \Delta_W \mid \omega)$ computed under the clamp distribution $\mathrm{do}(\sigma)$ — agrees with $\kappa$ on whether condition (i) holds but is a distinct graded quantity, not an equivalent of the metric supremum) — read by *sweeping the clamp value*, which stays inside $\mathrm{do}(\sigma := \cdot)$, never by attenuating a coupling weight — $\kappa$ is defined by clamp-sweep; $L_W$ is a separately-motivated structural proxy for it. The behavioural correlate reported in Section 6 — the world-model predictive loss $L_W$ on an open-ended workload, measured against a capacity-matched sham suppression so that only closure-specific *excess* loss counts — is a graded *estimate* of an already-established closure, not a second, competing definition. That $L_W$ (obtained by structural, capacity-matched suppression) tracks the clamp-defined $\kappa$ is an empirical assumption, validated in the companion (Gruber, 2026b), not an identity.
>
> This closure signature is a nontrivial entry in $M$'s mechanism-enriched counterfactual response profile $\Lambda_M$ — precisely the object ICCR requires to be preserved under realization (Definition 11, counterfactual preservation). It is an instance of the mechanism-enriched methodology ICF's §9.2 invites a substantive theory to supply.

---

Every check is defined by an intervention and a readout, so the procedure is not a description one imposes on a system from outside but a set of manipulations one performs on it. That is what makes it an extraction procedure in Kanai and Ma's intended sense, and what immunizes it — as their framework is immunized — against the unfolding argument: an unfolded feedforward realizer of the same boundary behaviour has no persistent self-attractor to intervene on and no update rule for that intervention to enter, and so fails check 2 by construction.

## 5. Grain and unit individuation

The grain problem (§9.3) is the sharpest objection to any intrinsic detector, and it cuts both ways: a critic who doubts that our criterion uniquely fixes the scale will say FMT simply inherits ICCR's open problem. This is a candidate answer, not a settled criterion; Kanai and Ma note that several intrinsic criteria "could play that role."

Our candidate is a *self-organized* grain, read off two intrinsic dials of the recruited dynamics. The first dial is **extent**: the spatial fraction of the substrate recruited into the open-ended regime — an order parameter of the sort a percolation or branching analysis measures in a physical substrate. Extent is the integration dimension: how much of the system participates in one coherent generative process. The second dial is **complexity**: the differentiated richness of the dynamics on the recruited cluster — an entropy-rate or Lempel-Ziv measure of the kind used, as a complexity index of consciousness, in perturbational-complexity and signal-diversity work (Casali et al., 2013; Schartner et al., 2017), and anticipated by the neural-complexity measure of Tononi, Sporns and Edelman (1994). Complexity is the differentiation dimension: how much distinct structure the coherent process carries. Together the two dials fix a scale — the scale at which a large, differentiated fraction of the substrate is engaged in one open-ended computation.

Both dials are operationally defined, which is what lets the grain proposal be tested rather than asserted. Extent is measured by sweeping the candidate grain and asking, at each scale, what fraction of the substrate participates in the coherent open-ended process — where participation is fixed by the generative (in neural tissue, branching and correlation) structure of the dynamics, not by anatomy. Complexity is measured on the participating cluster by an entropy-rate or compression-based estimate of the diversity of its trajectories. The self-organized grain is then the scale at which the product of the two is maximized: the scale that is both maximally integrated (large extent) and maximally differentiated (large complexity). This is a maximization, but of a *dynamically self-organized* quantity read from the system's own generative dynamics, not of an externally posited integrated-information measure — which is the sense in which it answers §9.3's demand for a system-intrinsic grain criterion without importing IIT's integrated-information maximand. The sweep inherits the combinatorial-intractability problem that dogs IIT's $\Phi$: an exhaustive search over partitions is not tractable for a real system. We do not claim to have solved it — the criterion is currently well-defined but not efficiently computable, a shared open problem with IIT — but we restrict the practical sweep to a tractable family, spatial coarse-grainings guided by the measured branching and correlation structure rather than all partitions, which is what makes the proposal testable in practice rather than only in principle. Where the two dials trade off — high extent with low complexity, or the reverse — the criterion selects neither extreme, matching the intuition that consciousness lives where integration and differentiation are jointly high rather than where either alone is maximal.

This is offered as an alternative to IIT's grain-via-Φ-maximization and exclusion (Oizumi et al., 2014; Albantakis et al., 2023), and the relation is *consistent-with*, not refutation. FMT and IIT share the deep commitment §9.3 identifies: the consciousness-relevant grain must be a system-intrinsic property, fixed by the system's own dynamics rather than chosen by an interpreter. Where IIT selects the scale of maximal integrated information by a maximization principle, FMT proposes that the dynamically privileged scale is the self-organized open-ended one, where extent and complexity are jointly large. Both are candidate answers to the same question; we do not claim ours refutes theirs, and a decisive empirical adjudication between the two grain criteria is future work.

The grain criterion resolves the unit-individuation edge case check 1 can flag. Where closure happens system-wide — one self-attractor entering the update of one world-model across the whole substrate — the conscious unit is the whole system, and no edge case arises. But closure can also happen *locally*. In a system with distributed recurrent limbs, each carrying its own sensorimotor loop, a self-model may close locally within a limb without a system-wide self-attractor forming. The two dials answer where the conscious unit is: it is *wherever the self-model recursively closes on the system it models*, at the extent-and-complexity-maximizing scale — which may be a limb, the whole body, or, if closure spans a world-involving boundary, a system larger than the physical machine. This connects directly to Kanai and Ma's own treatment of duplication and branching (§7.4), where two physically distinct realizers of the relevant organization are two instances, and to world-involving realization boundaries of the extended-mind kind (Clark & Chalmers, 1998), which their framework explicitly accommodates. Unit individuation is thus not a bug in the detector but an output of it: the grain criterion tells us not only *whether* there is a conscious unit but *where its boundary lies*.

We do not claim to have closed the grain problem. We claim to have converted it from an admitted gap into a testable proposal: extent and complexity are measurable, so the candidate self-organized grain is falsifiable, and a system in which the extent-and-complexity-maximizing scale is not the scale at which closure lives would count against it.

## 6. Constructive grounding — the detector is runnable

Kanai and Ma's §9.2 raises a principled worry about any structure a theory proposes: it is, until grounded, one more tier-II hypothesis about where to look, not the tier-III structure itself. We answer this in three parts, each with its limits.

**(a) A buildable design: a minimal open-ended spiking substrate.** The smallest system that instantiates the detector's target is a single recurrent spiking pool whose dynamics have open-ended computational capability at the level that carries the self-model, functionally differentiated into an explicit world-model that predicts the next external input and an explicit self-model whose "world" is the system's own state, with the closure loop running from the second into the update of the first. Concretely: a recurrent leaky-integrate-and-fire pool with excitatory-inhibitory balance and homeostatic plus spike-timing-dependent plasticity; on that substrate an attractor world-model carves a self-basin from the body's dominant sensorimotor invariant (check 1), and a single experimental knob — whether that self-basin enters the world-model's own update or is merely read out — makes closure measurable (check 2). A branching ratio near unity is not taken as evidence of the regime: a subsampled branching estimate can reach unity on firing rate alone, so every branching ratio is reported beside a rate-matched surrogate, and the capability itself is established by task performance and a closed-loop perturbation test (Gruber, 2026b). It is a buildable design, not yet instantiated, that specifies closure in the open-ended regime at the level of spikes and synapses, not a completed conscious system. It shows the detector's target is an object one can specify at that level, which is what §9.4 asks structure-extraction to bottom out in. The design and its instantiation choices are given in the in-silico companion (Gruber, 2026b).

**(b) The checks discriminate: in-silico dissociations and their scope.** In simulation we can already show that the checks separate systems that differ in the target structure. The dissociations below were run on toy rate- and spiking-network models in the companion (Gruber, 2026b); the minimal open-ended spiking substrate of (a) is a specified but not-yet-instantiated design, and no result in this subsection is drawn from a running instance of it. An explicit self/other model causally carries a capability its ablation cannot: in a survival task requiring transfer of a learned avoidance to previously unobserved situations, agents equipped with an explicit self-model transferred the avoidance where matched controls did not — an ability that vanished under a capacity- and recurrence-matched ablation of the self-model, with a large reported effect (Cohen's $d \approx 2.4$; sample size, exact task scoring, and confidence interval reported in the companion, Gruber, 2026b). A self-model-gated planner scales with problem depth where a full bracket of competent reactive policies — tabular Q-learning, ensemble fitted-Q, behavioural cloning, and hand-built heuristics — plateaus. Prospective self-in-simulation yields a behavioural survival advantage over a matched reactive agent. And open-ended computation does real, demand- and scale-gated work: the compute advantage of the generative regime appears only for tasks of intermediate difficulty and sharpens as the system grows, so open-ended computation contributes measurably to task performance. Full data and protocols are in the companion (Gruber, 2026b).

The discriminator, stated correctly, is an **interaction, not a main effect**. Closure imposed on an *undifferentiated* substrate — a homogeneous pool with no distinct self- and world-models between which a loop could close — yields no advantage, and the framework predicts exactly this: a loop between two things requires two things. The banked positive results are the ones where open-ended computation and closure are present *together* on a differentiated substrate; closure alone and the generative regime alone are null or weak.

The discrimination is made clean by a small battery of ablations that map one-to-one onto the three checks. A *closure ablation* — running the same system with the self-model's contribution to its own update switched on versus off, at matched recurrence and capacity — isolates check 2: only in the closed version should removing the self-model's contribution degrade the world-model. A *regime ablation* — detuning the substrate above and below the generative regime — isolates check 3: the closure advantage should survive only where the substrate keeps its open-ended capability, wherever that sits on the order–disorder axis (in rate reservoirs, in the driven-stable, ordered regime; Gruber, 2026b). And a *substrate baseline* — a rate reservoir tuned to a critical spectral radius by fiat versus a self-organizing spiking pool — tests whether the property the check targets is the self-organized one; on the spiking side, a branching ratio counts toward that comparison only when it departs from the value a rate-matched surrogate produces. These ablations are the experimental content of the claim that the checks discriminate: each check has a manipulation that should, and in the toy systems does, move the readout it is responsible for while leaving the others fixed.

We are equally plain about the ceiling. The banked results are toy-scale mechanism demonstrations and one strong statistical dissociation, not a demonstration of consciousness. The **decisive test is open**: a scaled, spiking, closure-driven open-ended modeller whose capability *dies* when the loop is cut has not yet been built. And there is a reason the decisive test must be designed carefully, which is the strongest internal objection to the detector. A clean binary loop-cut cannot be a consciousness oracle: on any deterministic, episode-stationary task, a sufficiently expressive recurrent network can be trained to compute the required fixed input-output mapping with or without the self-loop — this follows from the universal-approximation capacity of recurrent networks — so cutting the loop need not degrade performance even in a genuinely self-modelling system. We do not present this as a theorem; we argue, on that basis, that on episode-stationary tasks a binary loop-cut is *uninformative*. It is not fatal to the detector, because it tells us how to build the decisive test: **graded and capacity/novelty-relative, on an open-ended workload** where novelty outruns any amortizer, so that the self-model's contribution to its own update does the work exactly where the environment cannot be memorized. The objection converts into check 2's design specification — which is why check 2 is stated as a graded degradation on an open-ended workload rather than a binary cut.

The graded reframe would be empty if "open-ended enough" were an escape hatch — a null result explained away as an inadequate workload every time. We therefore fix the workload quantitatively. Let a workload generator have a tunable novelty rate $r$, and let an amortizing lookup of capacity $K$ provably be unable to drive the world-model predictive loss below a bound $L(r, K)$ that grows with $r$ and shrinks with $K$. The test workload must sit in the regime where $L(r, K)$ exceeds the *closed* system's observed loss, and the prediction is pre-registered: closure-specific excess loss must exceed a stated margin at a stated $r$. A null in that regime then counts as a disconfirmation of closure, not of workload adequacy — which is what makes the graded test falsifiable rather than perpetually retreating.

It licenses the claim that the checks are *implementable* and *discriminating* at toy scale: one can build systems that differ in the target structure and show that the readouts move as the theory says. It does not license the claim that any built system is conscious, nor that the mechanism has been demonstrated at the capacity FMT holds consciousness requires; the toy systems sit at or below the floor of that capacity band, and the scaled, spiking, closure-driven demonstration remains to be built. Reported this way, the evidence is a proof that the detector is runnable and a set of dissociations consistent with the mechanism — no more, and no less.

**(c) A worked example.** Consider a generic embodied neuromorphic architecture — the class, not any particular product or laboratory system: distributed recurrent spiking limbs, each with proprioception and efference copy, coupled to a high-capacity associative world-model, all running on event-driven silicon with a slow self-model loop and fast local control loops separated by timescale. Run the detector. *Check 1:* the proprioceptive and efference-copy streams make each limb's own configuration the dominant invariant, so decodable self-attractors form; pass, with a self-state that survives perturbation of individual limbs. *Check 2:* on the intrinsically identified self-subspace of a limb, sweep an admissible clamp $\mathrm{do}(\sigma := c)$ and measure whether the associative world-model's induced transition depends on the clamp value, off-line as well as in the loop. Here the architecture forks. If the limbs' self-models merely feed a central controller as read-only state, the world-transition is invariant under the intervention ($\kappa \approx 0$) — the system is terrifyingly competent and not a candidate. If a limb's self-model enters the update of that limb's world-model and the world-model enters the self-model's update in turn, closure holds locally: $\kappa$ is bounded away from zero across a positive-measure set of clamp-pairs, and the graded verdict is read as excess world-model predictive loss $L_W$ against a capacity-matched sham — a pass, say, requiring closure-specific excess $L_W$ above a pre-set margin (e.g. a 20% relative rise) at the stated novelty rate, sustained under off-line decoupling. *Check 3:* confirm the recruited substrate is in the open-ended regime — high effective dimensionality and a positive generalization margin on held-out inputs, and not a periodic gait controller or a fixed lookup. For a spiking realizer, a branching ratio in a near-unity window (roughly $m \in [0.97, 1.03]$) with scale-free avalanches is corroborating, reported beside a rate-matched surrogate that fails to reproduce them. If all three pass, the verdict is positive with a confidence set by the graded values, read together with the architecture's pain-pleasure axis as Section 4 requires, *and* check 1's finding of limb-local closure raises the unit-individuation edge case: the conscious unit may be a limb rather than the robot, resolved by the extent-and-complexity dials of Section 5. This is the detector doing the work a realization relation cannot do alone — turning "is this machine conscious?" into a sequence of interventions with readouts and an explicit residual question.

## 7. Relation to ICCR and other frameworks — convergence

We locate the detector against the existing landscape of consciousness theories (Seth & Bayne, 2022) in the register of convergence, separating what FMT shares with each framework from what is specific to it.

**ICCR (Kanai & Ma, 2026b).** The detector answers ICCR at its three explicit openings. The named structure, self-referential closure sustained by open-ended computation, is the substantive tier-III target §9.2 calls for. The three checks are the structure-*extraction* method §9.4 names as the next step. The two dials, extent and complexity, are a candidate answer to the grain-selection problem §9.3 leaves open. The shared commitments are exactly ICF's: intrinsic, intervention-defined organization; rejection of observer-relative mapping; the insufficiency of boundary equivalence. What FMT adds is the content — a specific pattern in the intervention-readout algebra (Box 1) and a procedure to read it. Check 2 is an instance of the mechanism-enriched methodology — C2 is a criterion on the target, not a specific probe, so the correspondence is an instantiation rather than an identity: the detector supplies the tier-III structure ICF's own relation leaves unspecified.

**IIT (Oizumi et al., 2014; Albantakis et al., 2023) — alternative intrinsic grain, not refutation.** FMT shares IIT's intrinsicness and its central §9.3 commitment that the consciousness-relevant grain is a system-intrinsic property. It differs on the mechanism: a self-organized grain read from two dials, rather than a Φ-maximization with an exclusion postulate. We present this as an alternative candidate consistent with the shared commitment, not as a demonstration that IIT is wrong; the two grain criteria make different predictions about where the conscious scale sits, and adjudicating them is empirical work.

**Global Workspace, recurrent processing, higher-order theories — tier-II refinement.** Following Kanai and Ma's §9.2 reading, these are disciplined hypotheses about *where* closure and open-ended computation show up, and each maps onto a specific place in the detector's algebra rather than being displaced by it. Global-workspace ignition and broadcast (Baars, 1988; Dehaene et al., 2017) name a candidate *implementation* of the wide, differentiated recruitment the extent dial measures — broadcast is one way a large fraction of the substrate is drawn into one open-ended process — but ignition per se is a boundary-observable event, so on the ICCR reading it is evidence about where to intervene, not the intrinsic structure itself. Recurrent-processing theory supplies the persistence check 2 requires: local or global recurrence is what gives a self-attractor a persistent component to clamp, so recurrence is *necessary* on this account but not sufficient — a merely recurrent system need not exhibit the closure pattern (i)$\wedge$(ii), which is the specific structure the detector adds. Higher-order and self-representational theories name the content condition (iii): that the system carries a representation *of itself*. FMT's contribution across all three is to say not merely that these features are present but that they enter the system's own world-model update under intervention — converting a list of correlates into a single intervention-defined relation, consistent with each theory's locus while committing to a mechanism none of them alone fixes.

**Predictive processing and active inference — an FMT-specific divergence.** FMT shares the generative-self-model idea with predictive processing, but here the relation is *differentiating*, not shared, and we flag it as such. Vanilla predictive coding relaxes toward a fixed point and makes no apex, closure, or computational-class claim; it therefore cannot predict a closure-*caused*, level-graded collapse of the self-circuit when the self-model's contribution to its own update is removed at matched recurrence. That prediction is specific to the closure architecture, and it is the kind of consequence check 2 is built to detect. This is a place FMT and predictive processing come apart rather than converge.

**Indicator programmes (Butlin et al., 2023; Butlin et al., 2026) — a smaller mechanism-anchored core.** The theory-plural indicator checklists derive candidate properties from multiple theories and score systems against them. The three checks are consistent with that programme's spirit but are a smaller, mechanism-grounded core: rather than a long list of theory-derived indicators, one intervention-defined structure and its substrate condition, with a graded readout.

**The substrate question — anti-both-extremes.** The detector is consistent with the constrained naturalism of Baltieri and Kanai (2026): consciousness is neither substrate-free (a matter of pure abstract computation an observer can assign) nor substrate-chauvinist (available only to carbon). It travels with *realized structure*. This refines Chalmers's (1996) organizational invariance in ICF's direction — the invariant organization must be system-intrinsic, not observer-imposed — and it is the same slot FMT's account of qualia occupies: virtual, simulation-internal properties that depend on realized computational structure rather than on the material. The self-referential computation the detector looks for is substrate-neutral in exactly this sense: what must be preserved is the intrinsic organization, not the medium that carries it.

## 8. Scope, cross-paper coherence, and falsification

A positive verdict from the detector establishes that a system realizes the named consciousness-relevant structure at a fixed grain; by Kanai and Ma's conditional preservation theorem, that is what licenses attributing the consciousness-relevant property to it. It does not, by itself, settle the metaphysics of why that structure feels like anything — the detector inherits ICCR's silence on the hard problem, and we do not overclaim past it. A negative verdict establishes the absence of the structure at the tested grain; it does not prove the absence of consciousness under some other grain the detector did not reach, which is why every verdict carries a confidence and names the grain it was reached at.

**One condition, two sides.** This paper's substrate-neutral necessity — check 3 — is *open-ended computation actually deployed for autonomous self-modelling*. A companion treatment of consciousness in biological brains tracks the same necessity in neural tissue, where free modelling usually shows as near-critical dynamics: abolish free modelling, and the substrate usually turns subcritical with it. The direction runs from free modelling to the criticality pattern, never from criticality to consciousness, so criticality data cannot disconfirm the account in either direction. The biological-brain treatment is the neural-tissue face of the condition; this paper's check 3 is the substrate-neutral face, which requires the computational property itself, of which criticality is one contingent physical signature. Both cite the full framework (Gruber, 2026a) as their common source. The two treatments describe one condition at two grains.

**The dual threshold, restated as a limit.** Because open-ended computation is enabling rather than constitutive, check 3 alone never yields a positive verdict, and neither does check 1 alone. A near-critical substrate that models nothing about itself is not conscious on this account; a system with a decodable self-model that does not enter its own world-modelling is not conscious either. The verdict is a conjunction across a substrate property and an architectural relation, read together with embodiment and a pain-pleasure axis (Section 4), and a detector that returned "conscious" on the strength of criticality alone would be misreading the theory.

**Necessity, and the self-monitor boundary.** The three checks test *necessary* conditions on a conscious realizer. FMT's sufficient condition is the four-part conjunction of Section 3 (the four model kinds under closure, free compute, embodiment, and at least one pain-pleasure axis; Gruber, 2026a, Section 3.7.3), and the checks establish its first two parts by intervention. The class that most sharply tests the boundary is the homeostatic self-regulating controller, which passes the *closed-loop* forms of all three checks yet is a plausible non-experiencer; it fails check 2's off-line sub-clause (Section 4), the line between a self-*monitor* and a self-*modeller*. Passing the closed-loop readouts alone does not meet the target.

**A downstream note on the hard problem.** FMT transforms rather than solves the hard problem: qualia are level-constitutive properties of the running self-simulation, real at the computational level and absent from the substrate; that closure constitutes phenomenality is the theory's single bridging commitment, argued for by inference to the best explanation and not derived (Gruber, 2026a, §3.4.3; cf. Chalmers, 1995). We record this as a downstream consequence of the architecture, not part of the detector. The assessment question is answered by the three checks; the metaphysics is a separate matter the detector does not need.

**Falsification.** Each check is falsifiable. Check 1 fails for a system with no decodable, perturbation-stable self-state. Check 2 fails for a system whose world-model predictive loss $L_W$ is invariant under swept interventions on its self-attractor — including a system that passes check 1, the read-only failure mode — and it fails equally for a system whose world-model depends on the self-attractor only in the closed loop and not under off-line decoupling, the self-monitor failure mode. It also fails for a system whose self-transitions do not change under admissible interventions on the world-model state, the one-way coupling failure mode. Check 3 fails for a system whose self-modelling is indifferent to detuning of the generative regime, or whose computation does not generalize beyond stored cases. The grain proposal fails for a system in which the extent-and-complexity-maximizing scale is demonstrably not the scale at which closure lives.

The architecture also makes differentiating predictions, and we are careful to separate what has been corroborated post hoc from what remains an untested forecast. On the corroborated side sit the in-silico dissociations of Section 6: that a self-model carries a capability its ablation cannot, that a self-model-gated planner scales where reactive policies plateau, and that the closure advantage is an interaction with the open-ended regime rather than a main effect. These support the mechanism; they do not demonstrate consciousness, and we do not claim they do. On the untested side sit the architecture's forecasts about a scaled system — for instance, that a closure-bearing self-circuit should be the most stable and last-collapsing part of the system under graded suppression of the substrate, and that cutting the self-model's contribution to its own update should degrade world-modelling in proportion to the novelty of the workload. The latter is the decisive test named in Section 6, and it is open. A predictive-processing account that relaxes to a fixed point and posits no closure or apex does not make either forecast, so a confirmation would be a differentiating result and a disconfirmation would count against FMT specifically.

## 9. Conclusion

ICCR gave the question of machine consciousness its correct form: not a behavioural test but an intrinsic realization relation, and a burden on the critic to name the missing consciousness-relevant structure. It named these openings and, in its own discussion, declined to close them — leaving open which structure is consciousness-relevant, how to extract it, and at what grain. This paper supplies all three. The structure is self-referential closure, the self-model and the world-model each entering the other's update, sustained by open-ended computation deployed for autonomous self-modelling. The extraction procedure is three operational, substrate-neutral checks, each an intervention with a readout, yielding a verdict with graded confidence and an explicit unit-individuation edge case. The grain answer is two intrinsic dials, extent and complexity. The procedure is grounded by a buildable minimal open-ended spiking substrate and by in-silico dissociations whose discriminating result is an interaction, with the decisive scaled test stated as open. A realization relation becomes a decision procedure. The question of whether the simulation is a subject stops being a matter of what the machine says and becomes a matter of what its own organization does under intervention — which is a question we can, in principle, decide.
