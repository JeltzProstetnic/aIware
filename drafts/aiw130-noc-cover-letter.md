# Cover letter — Neuroscience of Consciousness special issue

**To:** The Guest Editors, *"Is There More to Consciousness Than Computation?"* — Yaïr Pinto, Adrien Doerig, Krzysztof Dołęga
**Re:** Research Article submission — *"More Than Computation, Made of Computation: Free Computation as a Necessary Condition, and a Self-Model That Takes Itself for Its Bearer"*
**Author:** Matthias Gruber (independent researcher; ORCID 0009-0005-9697-1665)

Dear Editors,

I am submitting the enclosed Research Article for consideration in the special issue *"Is There More to Consciousness Than Computation?"*

The paper answers the special issue's question directly and on its own axis. It argues **yes** — there is more to consciousness than base computation — while remaining squarely **computationalist**: the "more" is itself computation, a second-order regime in which computation operates on computation (a self-model modelling its own modelling), realized as self-referential closure sustained at criticality. Criticality enters not as an implementationist demand on the substrate but as the *signature* this computation leaves in neural tissue. The paper therefore sits precisely on the computationalism-versus-implementationism axis the issue is built around, and stakes out a position that is neither "computation suffices as usually construed" nor "a special substrate is required."

Its lead rests on two claims. First, a **necessity**: consciousness requires *free*, open-ended computation, and in biological brains that requirement appears as near-criticality — **no criticality, no consciousness**. This necessity is one-directional and enabling, not sufficient: a near-critical cortex can run heavy *unconscious* modelling, so criticality does not by itself entail consciousness. The paper gives the first principled explanation of *why* the cortex must be near-critical for consciousness — not because criticality is generically optimal (the paper grants that account its full strength) but because it is the necessary substrate for the second-order computation that constitutes experience. Second, a **constitutive** claim: experience is made when information from the brain's implicit models is integrated into its explicit models and fed to a self-model that confuses itself with the being it occupies — not by the criticality that merely enables the computation. The necessity claim is operationalised to a stated disconfirmation design (Section 5), and the convergence section situates the theory as consistent-with, not competing against, the integration, world-modelling, and recursive-self-modelling traditions.

**Format note.** This is a theoretical Research Article; its "Methods" are the argument and the disconfirmation design of Section 5, and its "Results" are the lead claim and its test (Sections 3 and 5). If the editorial office requires literal empirical section headings, I will gladly convert the manuscript to a Brief Perspective.

**Originality and exclusivity.** The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration at any other journal.

**Shared-framework disclosure.** The complete framework this paper compresses is available as a preprint (Gruber, 2026; concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.18669891), and a substrate-neutral companion treatment (referenced in Section 8) is likewise available (Gruber, 2026; DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21610993) and is being prepared for a different venue. The two are related but distinct: the present paper's central claim (that free, open-ended computation — appearing as near-criticality in neural tissue — is a necessary condition for consciousness, and that experience is constituted by the integration of implicit-model information into a self-conflating explicit self-model) and its primary evidence (the neural state-transition literature and the Section 5 necessity/disconfirmation design) do not duplicate the companion's claim or evidence. I disclose both for full transparency.
