## Abstract

First-person methods, micro-phenomenology included, cannot establish the structure of the minimal self. The minimal self is defined as pre-reflective: it is lived without being taken as an object. A report about it is a behavioural output, and producing that output requires the reporting system to represent its own state as an object, which is the reflective operation the minimal self is defined by lacking. The report is then reconstructed from memory, re-simulated during evocation and serialised into language. What reaches the transcript is a description of a reflective model of the minimal self, however skilled the interview. This argument is architectural and is stated within the Four-Model Theory of consciousness (FMT), on which the minimal self corresponds to the basic, non-recursive explicit self-model, possibly resting on a floor of basal sentience, while reporting requires the recursive rungs of self-modelling above it. The empirical literature on unreliable introspection reaches a convergent conclusion by a different route. Micro-phenomenological reports do carry accurate information about experiential content and can predict neural data. The claim concerns the pre-reflective self-structure: ubiquity of for-me-ness, agentive versus passive selfhood, selfless states. On these questions reports can neither confirm nor refute a position. The paper sets out the third-person methods that reach the minimal self without routing through report, and proposes a test, "minimal-self blindness", that adapts the choice-blindness design of Petitmengin et al. (2013) to covert manipulations of bodily self-location, ownership and agency. The thesis fails if, at matched manipulation strength, trained interviews gain as much in tracking the ownership or agency manipulation as in tracking a content manipulation.

**Keywords:** minimal self, pre-reflective self-consciousness, micro-phenomenology, introspection, self-model, first-person methods, no-report paradigms, for-me-ness

## 1. Introduction

Every first-person report about the minimal self is produced by a system operating one level above it. The minimal self, in the phenomenological tradition, is the pre-reflective, non-positional self-presence that accompanies experience without being an object of it (Gallagher 2000; Zahavi 2005). To report on one's own experience, a system must represent that experience as something it has, which is to take it as an object. The report is then assembled from memory and put into words. It follows that first-person methods, however refined, describe the explicit self-model's reconstruction of experience, and so they are the wrong instrument for the structure of the minimal self. They remain a good instrument for much else, including the content of experience.

At the seventh Models of Consciousness conference (MoC7, Copenhagen, October 2026), micro-phenomenology appeared as a growth area: combined with 7T imaging (Allen 2026), with clinical trials and with transformer-based analysis of interview corpora. The same programme carried a session on the minimal self and the regress problem of self-awareness. The two lines of work now meet, since micro-phenomenology is increasingly offered as the method by which the structure of pre-reflective experience can be described with rigour (Petitmengin 2006; Petitmengin et al. 2019).

A report is produced in four stages: objectification, reconstruction, re-simulation and serialisation. The first is architectural and suffices for the thesis; the other three add distortion by degree. The familiar critiques of introspection are empirical (Nisbett & Wilson 1977; Schwitzgebel 2008): they show that reports often turn out wrong. Micro-phenomenologists reply that those critiques target naive, untrained introspection (Petitmengin & Bitbol 2009). The objectification step holds for trained introspection as well, because it concerns what a report is, whatever the reporter's skill.

Experience is not thereby illusory: FMT is realist about phenomenal properties at the level of the running simulation (Gruber 2026, §3.4.5), which separates the position from Dennett's heterophenomenology (Dennett 2003). Nor are reports worthless or micro-phenomenology bad science. And the minimal self is not unknowable. It can be studied by inference from data that do not pass through the explicit self-model; Section 6 lists such methods.

## 2. The minimal self

Gallagher (2000) drew the distinction now standard in cognitive science between a minimal self, "consciousness of oneself as an immediate subject of experience, unextended in time", and a narrative self that is extended in time and constituted partly by the stories one tells. The minimal self carries a sense of ownership (this experience is mine) and a sense of agency (I am causing this movement), and the two can come apart, as in schizophrenic thought insertion, where a thought is experienced as one's own in location but not in authorship.

Zahavi (2005, 2014) develops the phenomenological version. Pre-reflective self-awareness is the "for-me-ness" or first-personal givenness of experience: every experience is given to someone, and that givenness is not one more item among the contents of experience. It is the subjective character of experience as such (Zahavi & Kriegel 2016). Zahavi also defends a structural claim, inherited from the Heidelberg School, that reflective self-awareness presupposes pre-reflective self-acquaintance. If self-awareness required reflection, the reflecting act would need its own reflection to be self-aware, and so on without end; the regress stops only if the self is already given before it is reflected upon.

Legrand (2006, 2007) roots pre-reflective self-consciousness in sensorimotor integration, distinguishing self-as-subject from self-as-object. Christoff, Cosmelli, Legrand and Thompson (2011) draw a methodological consequence that is close to the present argument. They observe that much of the cognitive neuroscience of the self, including the cortical midline literature (Northoff & Bermpohl 2004), studies self-as-object, using tasks in which participants judge whether trait adjectives describe them. Such tasks miss the pre-reflective self-as-subject, which Christoff and colleagues propose to locate in sensorimotor self-specifying processes. Their point concerns task paradigms; Section 4 argues that it holds for reports as well.

Blanke and Metzinger (2009) define minimal phenomenal selfhood through three components: self-identification with a body, self-location in space and a first-person perspective. These components can be manipulated experimentally. In full-body illusions, synchronous visuotactile stimulation makes participants identify with a virtual body seen at a distance, and the effect is measured implicitly, as drift in self-location toward the virtual body and as skin-conductance responses to threats directed at it (Lenggenhager et al. 2007; Ehrsson 2007). Park and Blanke (2019) extend the approach to the coupling of interoceptive and exteroceptive signals. The minimal self can thus be manipulated and measured without asking anyone about it.

Below this level, several authors posit a more basic affective core. Damasio (1999) distinguishes a non-conscious protoself, a transient core self that survives severe amnesia, and an autobiographical self. Panksepp (1998) locates a primary-process "SELF" in subcortical emotional circuits, and Panksepp and Northoff (2009) argue for a trans-species core self. Their evidence comes largely from animal and lesion studies.

Whether the minimal self is ubiquitous in experience is disputed. Guillot (2017) separates three notions often run together: for-me-ness (experience is given to a subject), me-ness (the subject is aware of herself) and mineness (experience is given as belonging to the self). Letheby (2020), Letheby and Gerrans (2017) and Millière (2017) argue that psychedelic ego dissolution may be a case of experience without minimal selfhood. Metzinger (2020) and Gamma and Metzinger (2021) study "minimal phenomenal experience", a reportedly contentless and selfless wakefulness reached in meditation, through a questionnaire with 1,403 usable responses. At MoC7, Lépingle (2026) argued that the minimal self is embodied but passive and affective, with the "I can" of agency a founded layer above it, and argued from dissociation cases where bodily self-presence survives a collapse of agency.

Most of the evidence in this dispute, on both sides, is retrospective report.

## 3. Micro-phenomenology at its best

Varela (1996) proposed neurophenomenology as a research programme in which disciplined first-person descriptions and third-person neural data constrain each other. Vermersch (1999) treated introspection as a practice that can be trained and improved without first possessing a complete theory of it. Depraz, Varela and Vermersch (2003) described "becoming aware" as three gestures: suspension of habitual attitudes, redirection of attention from the object of experience to the way it is given, and letting-come of what was lived but not yet noticed. They describe becoming aware as a transformation of experience. The method was not built on the assumption that reflection leaves the reflected experience untouched.

Petitmengin (2006) set out the micro-phenomenological interview in its current form. The interviewer helps the participant stabilise attention on a single, singular past episode; guides them into "evocation", a re-living of the episode that is checked through linguistic and non-verbal markers; asks about the how of the experience and avoids questions of what and why; and uses "content-empty" questions that do not suggest an answer. The aim is the pre-reflective, procedural dimension of experience: what a person was doing inwardly while performing a task, which they could not have said unprompted. Petitmengin, Remillieux and Valenzuela-Moguillansky (2019) describe the analysis by which generic diachronic and synchronic structures are extracted from sets of such interviews.

The method also has a theory of its own validity. Petitmengin and Bitbol (2009) argue that the classical critiques of introspection, from Comte to Nisbett and Wilson, rest on a poor understanding of the introspective process and a naive picture of objectivity. They propose that the validity of a first-person description be assessed by its authenticity, meaning that it issues from evocation of a singular experience, and its coherence, meaning its internal consistency and its consistency with other descriptions, in place of correspondence with an independent fact (see also Bitbol & Petitmengin 2013, 2017).

Descriptive Experience Sampling (DES) is a separate method with a different timing. A random beep interrupts daily life; the participant notes the "pristine" experience ongoing at the moment of the beep, and an expositional interview follows within a day. Iterative training is used to bracket presuppositions (Hurlburt & Akhter 2006; Hurlburt & Heavey 2006; Hurlburt 2011). Hurlburt and Schwitzgebel (2007) worked through real samples as proponent and sceptic, and their exchange remains the most detailed public test of the method.

The methods have empirical successes. Lutz, Lachaux, Martinerie and Varela (2002) used trained first-person reports of preparation before a stereoscopic task to sort trials into clusters, and the clusters had distinct patterns of EEG synchrony that would otherwise have been averaged away. Petitmengin, Remillieux, Cahour and Carter-Thomas (2013) repeated the choice-blindness protocol of Johansson et al. (2005), in which most participants fail to notice that the photograph they are asked to justify is not the one they chose, and showed that participants guided by a micro-phenomenological interview detected the manipulation in 80% of cases. Their conclusion was that people are usually unaware of their decision processes but can reach them through specific mental acts. At MoC7, Allen (2026) reported a study in which micro-phenomenological interviews on a simple somatosensory and motor timing task revealed experiential dimensions of sound and speech that the design did not contain; pre-registered hypotheses derived from the interviews predicted elevated activity in auditory and verbal areas in the participants and trials where such experience was reported, with preliminary support in 49 participants. This follows Gallagher's (2003) proposal of "front-loading" phenomenology into experimental design (see also Gallagher & Sørensen 2006).

These results show that trained interviews recover information about experiential content and task-time processing that untrained reports miss, and that this information is accurate enough to predict neural data. Training also improves introspective accuracy on bodily sensitivity tasks (Fox et al. 2012), and finer report scales reveal graded experience that binary scales hide (Ramsøy & Overgaard 2004).

## 4. The architecture of a report

### 4.1 The Four-Model Theory in brief

FMT (Gruber 2026; first formulated in Gruber 2015) holds that consciousness is constituted by ongoing self-simulation. It distinguishes four model kinds along two axes: scope (world or self) and mode (implicit or explicit). The implicit world model and implicit self model are learned knowledge stored in the substrate's connectivity; they are never directly conscious. The implicit self model holds body schema, proprioceptive calibration, motor skills and autobiographical memory structures. The explicit world model and explicit self model (ESM) are generated from the implicit models and current input as transient activity patterns, and their content is phenomenal when it enters the self-model's closed loop. The four are extremal points of one continuous modelling ecology in a single substrate. They are not four modules.

FMT grades consciousness by depth of recursive self-modelling (Gruber 2026, §3.5). Above the floor of minimal consciousness, at the basic level, the system generates an explicit world model and a rudimentary, non-recursive ESM: it experiences a world and has a minimal sense of being a subject in it. At the next level, simply extended consciousness, the ESM includes a model of the system's own states and processes; the system experiences and also represents that it experiences. Doubly and triply extended consciousness add further orders of self-modelling. The levels are points on a continuum, and an individual moves among them with state. FMT describes the onset of a lucid dream as exactly this crossing: the dreaming ESM goes from experiencing as an agent to experiencing that it is an agent (§6.3).

FMT also states what converts content that is present in experience into content the system can interrogate (§3.4.6). At the basic level, self-content can be present in the explicit models without the system being able to grasp, examine or manipulate it. Reportability begins when the explicit models become rich enough to interact with themselves, so that the self-model maps and operates on its own content and the system can take that content as an object. Each rung of the recursive ladder adds one degree of such self-interaction. FMT does not treat reportability as a broadcast stage separate from phenomenality; reportability is added recursive depth. FMT further distinguishes this depth from the capacity of the narrow channel through which the explicit models read and write the high-dimensional implicit models (§3.6.1): rank sets how much the simulation holds at once, while depth sets whether the simulation can take its own contents as an object.

### 4.2 Where the minimal self sits

On this architecture the minimal self of the phenomenological tradition corresponds to the basic, non-recursive ESM. It is phenomenal, it is a sense of being a subject in a world, and it does not take itself as an object. Its source material is the implicit self model, which is never conscious and so cannot itself be the minimal self. Beneath the basic ESM, FMT places a floor of minimal consciousness (Gruber 2026, §3.5): a rudimentary closure, phenomenal and so consciousness at its minimum, whose content is the now plus primordial pain or nothingness, with no reflection, no modelled past or future and no discernible qualia. It is roughly what the animal-welfare literature calls sentience. Reportability grows with recursion depth, since a report needs content taken as object, memory and language; the floor is conscious content with no recursion above it, so it is unreportable by definition. Deep sleep is probably unconsciousness; non-deep sleep is the reportable state closest to the floor. This floor corresponds to Panksepp's primary-process core and plausibly to the passive, affective layer Lépingle identifies. The argument below needs only that the minimal self sits below the first reflective rung, which both the basic ESM and the floor of minimal consciousness satisfy.

The mapping is summarised as follows.

| Phenomenological term | FMT locus |
|---|---|
| Subject-less floor (if any) | Minimal consciousness: rudimentary closure, below the basic ESM |
| Minimal, pre-reflective self; for-me-ness | Basic consciousness: rudimentary, non-recursive ESM |
| Reflective self-awareness; the self that reports | Extended consciousness: ESM modelling the system's own states |
| Narrative self | ESM self-narrative, extended in time |
| Source material for all of the above | Implicit self model, never conscious |

### 4.3 Step one: reporting requires objectification

Every first-person datum, whether an interview transcript, a DES description, a questionnaire item or a confidence rating, is a behavioural output of the reporting system. Micro-phenomenologists accept this. A report about one's own experience requires that the system represent its own state as a state it is in. In FMT's terms, the ESM must include a model of the system's own states, which is the definition of simply extended consciousness. A system running only the basic ESM has a self-perspective but has nothing that represents the self-perspective; it can experience as a subject and cannot represent that it is doing so. It therefore has no representation from which a report about the self-perspective could be generated.

The minimal self is defined by not being taken as an object (Zahavi 2005; Zahavi & Kriegel 2016). The two definitions together give the first conclusion. Any report about the minimal self is generated from an extended-level model of it. Producing the report changes the level at which the self is running, and the report describes the higher level's model of the lower one.

Guillot's (2017) taxonomy gives this a precise form. For-me-ness is a structural feature of how experience is given. Me-ness is awareness of the self, which places the self among the contents of experience. Reporting converts the first into the second, because what the reporting system describes is a self that has been made into content. A report saying "there was a sense that the experience was mine" is a report of me-ness or mineness, produced by a model in which the self figures as an object, about an earlier state in which, on the phenomenologist's own definition, it did not.

The introspection literature discusses a version of this under the name of the refrigerator-light problem (see Schwitzgebel 2007): opening the door to check whether the light is off turns it on. In the case of the minimal self, the looking is done by the same system that is being looked at, and the act of looking is the change of level.

### 4.4 Step two: memory reconstructs

Micro-phenomenological interviews are retrospective. The episode under study is usually minutes to hours old, sometimes older. The report is therefore built from a memory trace, and recall is reconstructive: it fills gaps with schemas (Bartlett 1932) and is sensitive to the wording of the question (Loftus & Palmer 1974). The interview's content-empty questions reduce suggestion, and evocation is designed to reinstate the episode in its singularity. Neither makes retrieval non-reconstructive.

FMT adds a specific point about what a memory of the minimal self could contain. Consolidation writes into the implicit models what passed through the explicit models. If the episode was lived at the basic level, the trace holds the episode's contents together with whatever self-specifying information the implicit self model encodes (body schema, posture, interoceptive state). It holds no reflective representation of the minimal self, because none was formed. Whatever reflective structure the report attributes to the self in that episode must be generated at retrieval.

DES reduces this problem by sampling at a beep and describing within a day. It shortens the delay. It does not remove the first step, since describing the pristine moment still requires taking it as an object.

### 4.5 Step three: evocation re-simulates

Evocation, the core technique of the micro-phenomenological interview, asks the participant to re-live the episode: to return to the place, the posture, the sensations, until the episode is present again (Petitmengin 2006). On FMT this is a new run of the simulation, generated from the implicit models with the past episode as the target. It is not a playback of a stored recording, because the explicit models are not stored anywhere; they are generated each time from what the substrate holds (Gruber 2026, §3.2). The re-simulation runs in a different context from the original. The participant is now in an interview, attending to their own experience at the invitation of an interviewer, and so is running the ESM at an extended level while the episode is re-generated.

The re-lived episode can be vivid and detailed and can carry accurate content: what was seen, the sequence of inner actions, the location of a sensation. What it cannot carry is the original's self-structure, because the self-structure of the re-simulation is that of a reflecting subject attending to its own past. The micro-phenomenologist's evocation markers (slowed speech, unfocused gaze, present tense) certify that re-simulation is taking place. They cannot certify that the self-structure of the re-simulation matches the self-structure of the original.

### 4.6 Step four: language serialises

A report is a sequence of words produced through a narrow channel from a high-dimensional state. Serialisation compresses: the reader of a report reconstructs a model of what the speaker intended (Gruber 2015), and two very different states can yield the same sentence. Serialisation can also write back. Describing a face in words impairs later recognition of that face (Schooler & Engstler-Schooler 1990), so that the act of verbal description changes the memory it describes. In a micro-phenomenological interview, which typically runs for an hour or more on a single episode, the participant describes the same moments repeatedly in increasingly fine vocabulary. Every round of description is an opportunity for the description to overwrite the trace.

The ESM also fills gaps fluently. The left-hemisphere interpreter of split-brain patients produces confident explanations for actions initiated by the disconnected hemisphere (Gazzaniga 2000). In choice blindness, participants justify choices they did not make, and their justifications for manipulated and non-manipulated trials are linguistically almost indistinguishable (Johansson et al. 2006; Hall, Johansson & Strandberg 2012). FMT treats this as the ESM's normal mode of operation: the ESM maintains a continuous self-narrative by confabulating across gaps, on waking, after anaesthesia and wherever its sources are incomplete (Gruber 2026, §4.2.2). The confabulating ESM and the reporting ESM are one process.

### 4.7 The conclusion of the architectural argument

A first-person method samples the extended-level ESM's re-simulated, verbally serialised model of an earlier frame. At its best, with a trained participant, a skilled interviewer, authentic evocation and coherent descriptions, it yields an accurate description of a reflective model of the minimal self. These are data about the reporting system: how the reflective self-model represents its own pre-reflective layer. It cannot settle claims about the structure of the pre-reflective layer itself, such as whether for-me-ness accompanies all experience, whether minimal selfhood is agentive or passive, or whether selfless experience occurs, unless it is anchored to evidence that does not pass through the explicit self-model.

The empirical critiques of introspection converge with this conclusion and reach it by a different route. Nisbett and Wilson (1977) showed that people explain their behaviour by applying causal theories, with no special access to their own processes; Schwitzgebel (2008) argued that naive introspection is unreliable even about current conscious experience in favourable conditions; Irvine (2012) argued that subjective measures of consciousness inherit these problems. Bayne and Spener (2010) and Spener (2015) hold that introspection can be trusted only where it can be calibrated against independent measures. The present argument gives a reason why one domain resists that calibration from the inside. Calibration through report would require a report that reaches the pre-reflective layer, and that is the thing every report lacks.

The architectural argument also predicts which features of experience reports will distort. Features that consist in not being objectified, such as for-me-ness, perspectival origin, the absence of a subject-object split and the temporal thickness of the lived present, should fare worst. Contents of the explicit world model, and self-content that is already object-like (the felt location of a sensation, the posture of the body, the sequence of inner actions), should fare best. Training should help the second group substantially and the first group little. Section 7 turns this into an experiment.

## 5. Six replies from micro-phenomenology

**R1. Micro-phenomenology is not naive introspection.** Training, second-person guidance, evocation, the focus on how, and the bracketing of theory answer the critiques of Nisbett and Wilson and of Schwitzgebel, which concern untrained reports. This reply is decisive against the empirical critiques. It does not touch the architectural argument, which concerns what a report is and holds for a perfectly skilled reporter. Training improves the model produced at the extended level. Producing a report still requires that level.

**R2. The method exists to reach the pre-reflective, and pre-reflective means lived but unnoticed, which is still conscious.** This is the core claim of the method, and much of it is correct. FMT distinguishes content that is present in experience from content that is reportable (§3.4.6), and the micro-phenomenological interview is a technique for bringing present content into report. It works for contents that were lived pre-reflectively: an inner strategy, the location of a sensation, a fleeting image. Such contents become objects of the reflective model and can then be described accurately, because being an object does not change what they are. Depraz, Varela and Vermersch (2003) themselves describe becoming aware as a transformation. FMT supplies the mechanism of the transformation: a step up the recursive ladder. For the pre-reflective self-structure, whose defining property is that it is not an object, the step up changes what it is.

**R3. Validity is authenticity and coherence, not correspondence.** Petitmengin and Bitbol (2009) redefine the validity of first-person descriptions in a way that sets aside the demand that descriptions match an independent fact. Accepting the redefinition has a consequence. Claims about the structure of the minimal self are correspondence claims: that for-me-ness is present in every experience, that some experiences lack it, that minimal selfhood includes a sense of agency. If validity is authenticity and coherence, an authentic and coherent description can still fail to correspond, and the method's own theory of validity does not license it to settle these claims. A coherent body of reflective models of the minimal self is a coherent body of reflective models.

**R4. Micro-phenomenology defeats choice blindness.** Petitmengin et al. (2013) found that guided participants detected the manipulation in 80% of cases. This is the strongest empirical reply. The manipulated feature in choice blindness is content of the explicit world model: which face was chosen, which features it had. Detecting the swap means detecting a mismatch between two explicit models, the remembered choice and the presented photograph, which the reflective self-model can do once the interview directs attention to the choice; unguided participants mostly fail at it. The result shows that guided interviews improve access to decision content and process. It does not show access to the pre-reflective self-structure. Section 7 proposes the experiment that would.

**R5. Micro-phenomenological reports predict brain data.** Lutz et al. (2002) and Allen (2026) show that reports carry information about experience that has neural correlates. In Allen's study, what the interviews revealed in a non-verbal task were experiential dimensions of sound and speech: content of the explicit models, and language-adjacent content at that. Neither study is evidence about the structure of minimal selfhood, and neither was designed to be.

**R6. The argument contradicts itself.** The Heidelberg argument, as Zahavi (2005) develops it, holds that reflection presupposes pre-reflective self-acquaintance. Any reflection that denies access to the pre-reflective self depends on that self in the very act of denying. The reply is that presupposing a layer is a different relation from describing it. FMT agrees that reflection presupposes the minimal self: the extended ESM is generated on top of a running basic ESM and could not exist without it. That is the Heidelberg founding relation, stated in architectural terms. Founding does not confer descriptive access. A higher layer can depend on a lower one in every operation and still represent it only as an object, as the extended layer represents everything it represents. Formal treatments presented at MoC7 resolve the regress without giving reflection that access. Williford (2026) models pre-reflective and reflective self-consciousness with non-well-founded sets. Kawakami (2026) formalises Zahavi's retentional solution by guarding each self-reference with a one-step delay, so that reflection operates on the retained, just-past phase of the stream. That is the strongest version of the reply: reflection grasps the lived phase through retention, not through memory. On the present account, the retained phase is still received by the extended ESM as an object, so retention shortens the delay, as DES does, without removing the change of level.

A further consequence applies to both sides of the dispute over whether minimal selfhood is ubiquitous. The arguments that psychedelic ego dissolution or minimal phenomenal experience are selfless rest on retrospective reports (Letheby 2020; Millière 2017; Gamma & Metzinger 2021). So does much of the phenomenological case that for-me-ness accompanies all experience. If reports cannot reach the pre-reflective self-structure, they can neither establish ubiquitous for-me-ness nor refute it. A report that "there was no self" is produced by a reflective self-model describing, after the fact, a state it represents as selfless. Whether the earlier state lacked minimal selfhood, or lacked the conditions for a reflective model of it, is a question the report cannot decide. Third-person evidence can bear on it, such as the raised neural entropy reported for minimal phenomenal experience, which Lopez-Sola (2026) reinterprets through complexity measures.

Clinical phenomenology presents a harder case. Interviews such as the Examination of Anomalous Self-Experience detect disturbances of ipseity in schizophrenia-spectrum disorders, and these disturbances have diagnostic value (Sass & Parnas 2003). On the present account, such interviews detect that the reflective self-model's representation of the self has changed. A change can be visible from a level that cannot recover what changed, as one can see that a photograph is blurred without being able to recover the scene. That answer is partial (Section 8).

## 6. Methods that do not pass through the report

The methods that reach the minimal self share one principle: manipulate or measure it along a channel that does not require the participant to model themselves as an object, and treat any report as one more channel whose generator, the extended ESM working on memory through language, is known. The following are available now.

**Implicit bodily-self measures under covert manipulation.** Full-body and rubber-hand illusions alter self-identification and self-location, and the alteration can be measured as drift in self-location, as skin-conductance responses to threats directed at the illusory body and as changes in heartbeat-evoked potentials (Lenggenhager et al. 2007; Ehrsson 2007; Park & Blanke 2019). These measures index components of minimal phenomenal selfhood (Blanke & Metzinger 2009) without asking.

**No-report and no-cognition paradigms.** Optokinetic nystagmus and pupil size track perceptual alternations in binocular rivalry without report, and when report is removed, frontal activity associated with introspection and action drops out (Frässle et al. 2014; Tsuchiya et al. 2015). Related designs separate the correlates of perception from the correlates of reporting (Pitts, Metzler & Hillyard 2014) and decode transitions of conscious content from prefrontal activity in the absence of report (Kapoor et al. 2022). Overgaard and Fazekas (2016) questioned whether no-report designs isolate the true correlates of consciousness, and Block (2019) argued that removing report does not stop participants thinking about their experience, so that a no-cognition condition is needed. Block's point supports the present argument. Silent reflection may engage the extended ESM as fully as spoken report, so for the minimal self the strict version of the design is one in which the readout is implicit or physiological and the participant has no task directed at their own experience.

**Perturbational complexity.** The perturbational complexity index measures, without report, the capacity of cortex to sustain complex responses to direct stimulation, and it separates conscious from unconscious states (Massimini et al. 2005; Casali et al. 2013). It can be applied in contrasts that remove the extended ESM while sparing the basic level: lucid against non-lucid REM sleep, ketamine against propofol, deep meditative absorption against rest.

**Serial awakening scored against physiology.** Serial-awakening designs distinguish three outcomes: experience with recall of content, experience without recall of content, and no experience (Siclari et al. 2017; Windt, Nielsen & Thompson 2016). The middle category already separates having experienced from being able to say what was experienced. If non-deep sleep is the reportable state closest to the floor and deep sleep is unconsciousness, awakenings from deep NREM sleep should yield no experience reports beyond chance, while in non-deep sleep "experience without recall" should rise with arousal threshold. Both variables are standard sleep-laboratory measures, so this is a re-analysis of existing data.

**Lesion and dissociation logic.** Anosognosia for hemiplegia is the clearest clinical case in which the reporting self is wrong about the subject's own body. On FMT, the implicit self model registers the deficit and the ESM does not receive it (Gruber 2026, §3.6). Fotopoulou et al. (2010) found that anosognosic patients who explicitly denied that sentences about their paralysis applied to them were nonetheless slowed in suppressing completions of those sentences, a dissociation between implicit and explicit awareness of the deficit. The sincere first-person report and the implicit measure disagree, and the implicit measure tracks the fact. Split-brain confabulation (Gazzaniga 2000), thought insertion (Gallagher 2000) and depersonalisation supply further dissociations of the same kind, and Lépingle's case for a passive, affective minimal self was built from dissociations of this sort.

**Physiological measurement of altered self-states.** Psychedelic ego dissolution and minimal phenomenal experience can be studied through complexity and entropy markers, with reports treated as one dependent variable whose relation to physiology is modelled.

**In-silico ablation.** In an artificial system with an explicit self-model and a separable component that generates reports, the state of the basic self-model can be read directly and compared with what the report component produces. This is the one setting in which the ground truth about the lower self-model is available, so the distortion introduced by reporting can be measured as a function of recursion depth and channel bandwidth. This would extend the in-silico program of Gruber (2026, §8.11), which does not yet include a report component. The point concerns the reporting channel; nothing about the method assumes that such a system is conscious.

First-person reports keep a role in all of these. They supply hypotheses, as in front-loaded designs (Gallagher 2003), and they are data about the reporting system whose relation to the other measures can be modelled. What they lose is the role of ground truth for the pre-reflective self-structure.

## 7. A test: minimal-self blindness

The architectural argument makes a differential prediction. If reporting converts for-me-ness into me-ness, then training in first-person methods should improve the accuracy of reports about experiential content much more than it improves the accuracy of reports about the pre-reflective self-structure. The design below adapts the protocol of Petitmengin et al. (2013).

**Design.** A within-participant study with two manipulation arms and two or three report conditions.

The content arm repeats a choice-blindness manipulation: a covert change to a feature of a stimulus the participant has chosen, presented for justification.

The minimal-self arm manipulates three features covertly. Self-location and self-identification are manipulated with the cardio-visual variant of the full-body illusion, in which a virtual body flashes in or out of time with the participant's heartbeat and the heartbeat signal is supplied surreptitiously (Aspell et al. 2013; Park & Blanke 2019). The cardio-visual variant is preferred to visuotactile stroking because asynchronous stroking is visible, and detecting it would be detecting content. Sense of agency is manipulated by inserting a variable delay between a voluntary key press and its visual effect, kept below each participant's piloted threshold for detecting the delay itself, with intentional binding, the compression of perceived time between action and effect, as the implicit marker (Haggard, Clark & Kalogeras 2002). Implicit markers are recorded throughout: self-location drift, skin-conductance response to threat, heartbeat-evoked potential, intentional binding.

The report conditions are an untrained retrospective report and a full micro-phenomenological interview conducted by a certified interviewer. An immediate DES-style probe can be added as a third condition if resources allow.

**Prediction.** The gain from the micro-phenomenological interview over untrained report will be ordered. It will be largest for the content manipulation, replicating Petitmengin et al. (2013); intermediate for self-location, the feature closest to the object-like self-content of Section 4.7; and smallest for self-identification and agency, which are features of the pre-reflective self-structure. In the minimal-self arm, interview reports will track the implicit markers trial by trial less closely than they track the change in the content arm. Their content will converge on the participant's prior beliefs about selfhood and on the descriptive vocabulary of the interview itself, which can be scored with topic-modelling tools of the kind now applied to first-person reports (Beauté 2026). The implicit markers will show that the manipulation took effect in both arms.

**Refutation.** Two quantities are fixed in the pre-registration: the interview's gain in detection sensitivity (d′) over untrained report, and the trial-wise correlation between report and implicit marker, each with an equivalence margin. If, with manipulation strength matched on the implicit markers, the gain for self-identification or agency falls within the margin of the gain for content, and the report-marker correlation is not lower, the thesis is false for that feature. Prior beliefs about selfhood are measured by questionnaire before the session, so that a match produced by belief can be separated from a match produced by tracking. An ordering in which self-location fares worse than self-identification also counts against the account, since it reverses the predicted order.

**Design risk.** The interpretation depends on matching the strength of the manipulations. If the implicit markers show a weaker effect in the minimal-self arm, a weaker report effect is uninterpretable. Pilot work must calibrate the arms on the implicit markers before the report comparison is run, and the list of which features count as content, which as object-like self-content and which as self-structure must be fixed in the pre-registration. Without that list, any reportable feature could be reclassified as content after the fact. A residual asymmetry cannot be fully removed: in the content arm the swapped photograph is in front of the participant at the time of report, while in the minimal-self arm the changed feature is a past felt state. Withdrawing the content stimulus before the interview reduces the asymmetry, at the cost of departing from the protocol of Petitmengin et al. (2013).

The study uses established paradigms and the best version of the method under test. It requires a laboratory with both micro-phenomenological and bodily-illusion expertise, a combination that MoC7 showed to exist.

## 8. Limits

**Performative contradiction.** FMT was itself developed partly from introspection (Gruber 2015), and the floor of minimal consciousness invoked above is in origin the author's own intuition. Serial-awakening data are reports as well. Reports are outputs of a known generator. Like any instrument whose response function is understood, they can be modelled and corrected for, and inferences can be drawn through them when they are combined with measures that do not share their generator. The claims made here about the minimal self rest on architecture, implicit and physiological data and dissociations. A residue remains: the existence of a minimal self, as distinct from its structure, is motivated partly by reflective intuition.

**Falsifiability.** Taken alone, the objectification step is conceptual: that a report is generated at the extended level follows from the two definitions, and no datum bears on it. The empirical claim is that this change of level costs accuracy for self-structure and not for content. That claim forbids an outcome, namely trained reports tracking a covert ownership or agency manipulation as well as a matched content manipulation, and Section 7 states the margins by which that outcome would be measured.

**The content-structure line.** The argument depends on separating content, which reports can reach, from pre-reflective self-structure, which they cannot. Some self-related features, such as felt self-location and body posture, may be partly reportable as content of the explicit models. If the line is drawn after the data, it can absorb any result. Section 7 therefore fixes it in advance and predicts an ordering across it.

**Phenomenology is not introspection.** Zahavi (2005) and others hold that claims about for-me-ness are claims about the invariant structure of experience, reached by phenomenological analysis, and not reports of particular episodes. The present argument concerns first-person reports used as data, and leaves such analysis untouched as philosophy. It applies once the ubiquity of for-me-ness is put to empirical test, as in the dispute over psychedelic and meditative states, because there the evidence offered on both sides is reports.

**Clinical phenomenology.** The detection of ipseity disturbance through interview (Sass & Parnas 2003) is answered only partly by the claim that interviews detect a change in the reflective model. If changes in the pre-reflective layer can be read off reliably from changes in the reflective model of it, the reflective model carries some structural information about the layer below, and the gap is narrower than argued here. How much narrower is an empirical question.

**Relation to Dennett.** Heterophenomenology also treats reports as texts to be interpreted (Dennett 2003, 2007). The present position differs in two respects. It is realist about experience at the level of the running simulation, so the reports describe something real. And it says where the distortion enters (the change of level, the reconstruction, the re-simulation, the serialisation), which is what allows it to predict which features of experience will be distorted.

**Dependence on FMT.** The mapping of the minimal self onto the basic ESM is FMT's. A reader who rejects FMT can still accept the first step of the argument if they accept two premises: that reporting on one's own state requires representing it as an object, and that the minimal self is defined by not being represented as an object. The second is standard in the phenomenological literature. The first is contested: Zahavi (2005) holds that reflection can disclose the pre-reflective structure it thematises without distorting it, and a reader who accepts that view will reject the first step. FMT adds the levels at which the two operations occur and the mechanism that relates them.

**Method diversity.** Micro-phenomenology, DES, explicitation interviewing and questionnaire studies of minimal phenomenal experience differ in timing and procedure. The memory step bears most heavily on retrospective interviews and least on DES. The objectification step applies to all of them equally.

## 9. Conclusion

A report about the minimal self is produced by the reflective self-model, from a reconstructed memory, during a re-simulation, through language. Each stage is well documented on its own; together they mean that the report describes a reflective model of the minimal self. Training improves that model and can make it accurate about experiential content. It cannot make a reflective model pre-reflective. The questions that divide the literature on the minimal self, including whether for-me-ness is ubiquitous, whether minimal selfhood is agentive and whether selfless experience occurs, therefore need third-person evidence: covert bodily-self manipulations measured implicitly, no-report and no-cognition designs, perturbational complexity, serial awakening scored against physiology, dissociations, and artificial systems in which the lower self-model can be read directly. First-person methods contribute hypotheses and data about the reporting system. The experiment in Section 7 can show whether the gap between content and self-structure exists in the form the architecture predicts.
