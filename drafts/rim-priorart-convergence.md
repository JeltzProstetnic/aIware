## Proposed insertion — new paragraph in §3.4 "Relation to Existing Work"

**Placement:** after the van Geert / Balboni dynamic-systems paragraph (current line ~158), before the closing "What is missing … the integration" synthesis paragraph (current line ~160).

> A parallel lineage in computational cognitive science reached the constitutive claim from the direction of agent architecture rather than psychometrics. Dörner's Psi-theory (Dörner, 1999; Dörner & Güss, 2013) models the psyche as an integrated system in which needs and drives are not a bolt-on module but an intrinsic aspect of cognition — a system stripped of need-pressure "would lack direction and purpose." Bach's MicroPsi, whose defining description is *an architecture of motivated cognition* (Bach, 2009, 2015), builds motivation into the substrate as the mechanism that selects goals and steers perception and reasoning. Sun's CLARION (Sun, 2009) makes the motivational subsystem one of four core pillars of the architecture, holding that motivational representations are necessary for a comprehensive computational account of cognition; and the tradition descends from an early necessity argument — Sloman and Croucher's (1981) claim that motive-driven processing is an unavoidable feature of any resource-bounded intelligent agent. The recursive model converges with this tradition on the core point that motivation is constitutive of, not merely correlated with, intelligent cognition, and diverges from it in two respects that define its contribution. First, in scope: these are architectures for situated *agents*, whereas the recursive model brings the constitutive-motivation claim into the theory and measurement of *intelligence* itself — the domain where, as Section 2 documents, motivation has been treated as an external predictor at best. Second, in formalism: none of these architectures casts motivation as a multiplicative term whose absence drives the product toward zero, nor derives quantitative, falsifiable consequences (the Brunswik-symmetry aggregation prediction of Section 3.1, the Gf–Gc divergence, the Flynn-effect reversal) from that structure. The lineage supplies the qualitative intuition; the recursive model supplies the formalization and its falsifiable consequences. This same tradition also anticipates the paper's central claim about artificial intelligence (Section 5): a research program that spent decades building *motivated* cognitive architectures implies, by its own emphasis, that systems lacking a motivational layer — today's large language models — are missing something architecturally essential rather than merely undertrained.
