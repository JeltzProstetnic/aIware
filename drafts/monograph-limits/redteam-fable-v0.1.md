# Red-team review — *No Outside* v0.1

**Verdict in one sentence:** the book's spine (embedding, resource deficit, absent generator) holds, but the draft contradicts its own corrected principle in four chapters, asserts and denies the description/instance claim across Chapters 5 and 14, and Chapter 6 states its positive thesis in a form the same chapter refutes twenty lines later — all fixable, none cosmetic.

**Counts:** 4 BLOCKER · 17 MAJOR · 45 MINOR (66 findings; verified-correct items listed separately in §3 and not counted).

Severity: [BLOCKER] wrong or self-refuting · [MAJOR] argument or consistency fails as written · [MINOR] local fix.

---

## 1. Cross-chapter contradictions

**1.1 [BLOCKER] The unifying principle is stated in its refuted strong form in four chapters.**
Preface (l. 13), Ch 5 (l. 307) and Ch 15 (l. 985) carry the corrected form: *the generator never appears completely in what it generates*, with the arithmetization caveat (writer B's fix). The strong form, which the Preface itself says is false, survives at:
- l. 273: "This is the principle that the generator does not appear in what it generates"
- l. 429: "The implicit models that generate the simulation are not part of the simulation."
- l. 473: "In both, the generator cannot appear in what it generates."
- l. 619: "The generator of a description does not appear in the description"
- l. 713: "It does not appear in any configuration; every configuration is its output."
- l. 865: "generated by implicit models that do not themselves appear in what they generate … it is structurally excluded"
Problem: a reader who has just been told (l. 307) that a formal system represents its own proof relation exactly meets, six chapters later, "the generator cannot appear". Self-refuting as a book. Ch 8 (l. 509, 541) already has the precise form: *the generator as a whole cannot appear; any of its contents can.*
Fix: at each location insert "completely"/"as a whole" and, where the sentence turns on it, the clause "represented as the cause of what it is generating at that moment" (Ch 7's own scoping at l. 431). For l. 713 (time): "The update as such does not appear in any configuration as content; what appears is its trace, the order of configurations."

**1.2 [BLOCKER] Ch 5 §5.6 asserts what Ch 14 denies, and the forward reference does not neutralize the assertion.**
l. 329: "turning it into an instance would be a further operation performed on it — instantiation, which is not reading. The limit therefore survives…"
l. 933: "Instantiation in a host is not needed." l. 953: "at the limit of completeness description and instance stop being two things."
Problem: Ch 5 states, flatly and as a limit that "survives" everything, that an instance requires an operation beyond the description. Ch 14 denies exactly this. l. 331's "Chapter 14 returns to this limit and argues…" announces a re-scoping, not a reversal; what arrives is a reversal. Also l. 327 adopts FMT's score/music analogy, which is the host-execution picture Ch 14 rejects.
Fix: rewrite l. 327–329 reader-relatively from the start: "A description of a process, however complete, does not give its reader an instance of it. The reader may run it, and running is a further operation; what the structure is for anything inside it is a different question (Chapter 14)." Drop "It is the only one of the four that would" or restate it as "the only one of the four that would survive for the reader."

**1.3 [MAJOR] Ch 4 identifies the never-assigned base layer with FMT's implicit layer; Ch 4 §4.3 and Ch 7 say the implicit layer contains consolidated, once-assigned content.**
l. 233: "a layer that was never assigned by interpretation at all … In the Four-Model Theory that layer is the implicit one"; l. 235: "It is not knowledge in the defined sense, since it was not assigned."
l. 251: "What is learned explicitly becomes implicit through consolidation"; l. 449: "the trained capacity, consolidated over a lifetime" is "part of the apparatus."
Problem: the implicit layer is a superset of the base layer. Consolidated content *was* assigned, so by the Ch 4 definition it is knowledge, and the sentence "the implicit layer is not knowledge" is false by the book's own definitions. This also matters for Ch 8, whose resolution ("the real side is whatever is not being read at the moment", l. 509) requires exactly that the implicit side is not a fixed never-assigned stratum.
Fix: l. 233: "In the Four-Model Theory that layer lies in the implicit models — the part of them that was grown rather than consolidated." l. 235: "The grown part is not knowledge in the defined sense…" Add one sentence: "Consolidated content joins it as a second kind of implicit material, once assigned and no longer held as content; Chapter 7 uses that distinction."

**1.4 [MAJOR] Ch 7 and Ch 13 use the implicit/explicit boundary as a fixed two-level ontology; Ch 8 has shown that reading cannot stand alone.**
l. 433: "the implicit models are the reference system … and so they are on the wrong side of the relation to appear in it." l. 865: "The apparatus is not hidden somewhere in the scene; it is structurally excluded from it."
l. 509: "It is not the two-level ontology as the paper's headline states it, because on it the real side is not a fixed set of models." l. 541: "particular contents can cross."
Problem: Ch 8's first discipline ("no derivation may import both strengths at once") is broken by Ch 7 and Ch 13, which import the level reading at full strength ("structurally excluded", "on the wrong side") for the apparatus argument. Ch 8 says that argument needs only the whole-at-once constraint.
Fix: in Ch 7 (l. 429, 433) and Ch 13 (l. 865, 867) replace "the implicit models are not part of the simulation" with "the generating process, taken as a whole, cannot be content of the simulation it is generating; its parts can leak in (Chapter 8), and leaks are not the generator." Ch 13 l. 867 then reads: what the four hypotheses disagree about is the generator as a whole, which is what no leak delivers.

**1.5 [MAJOR] Ch 9 equates reversibility with time-symmetric laws; Ch 11 says they are different properties.**
l. 599: "The microscopic dynamics … can be run backwards — unitarity in the quantum formalism, time-symmetric laws in the classical one"
l. 751: "Reversibility and time-reversal symmetry are different properties, and doubting one does not license doubting the other."
Fix l. 599: "— unitarity in the quantum formalism, invertible equations of motion in the classical one —" (time-reversal symmetry is a stronger, separate regularity, which the weak interaction lacks).

**1.6 [MAJOR] The arrow needs a compressed *initial* state (Ch 11); the substrate has no first state and sits on a cycle (Ch 12); a bijective finite cycle must return to the compressed state.**
l. 719: "a highly compressed initial state from which the interior decompresses"; l. 791: "Every trajectory ends in a cycle. On the cycle, each state has exactly one successor and one predecessor"; l. 803: "a finite, edgeless substrate on a cycle, with no first state".
Problem: on a bijective cycle through a low-entropy state, coarse-grained entropy cannot be monotonic around the cycle (Poincaré recurrence): the arrow must reverse somewhere, or the compressed state must recur as a phase (SB-HC4A's §5.4 cyclicity with possible CPT alternation is one answer). The draft never says which, and Ch 13 (l. 883) only notes the observer-measure debt. A reversible finite cycle is also, exactly, the Boltzmann-fluctuation setting of Ch 13 — the book should say so where it adopts the cycle (l. 793).
Fix: add to l. 793 one paragraph: the cycle returns to the compressed phase; between visits the interior arrow runs one way; the model owes a statement of what happens at the turn (CPT flip, singularity transition), and the recurrence is the Boltzmann regime whose observer measure Chapter 13 demands. Replace "initial state" at l. 719 with "the compressed phase from which each cycle decompresses".

**1.7 [MAJOR] The book says measurement independence is assumed in every Bell argument and certified in none; SB-HC4A §6.5 presents it as *derived* from the entanglement structure, and Ch 10 reports the paper as merely "retaining" it.**
l. 655: "No observer can certify, from inside, its independence from the system it is part of." l. 999: "assumed in every Bell-type argument and certified in none." l. 665: "My cosmology paper retains measurement independence and rejects factorization."
SB-HC4A §6.5: "The apparatus settings … are not entangled with the pair before the measurement, and therefore do not participate in the locus. Measurement independence is preserved."
Problem: the paper's argument claims from inside what the book says cannot be had from inside (and the argument is a non sequitur anyway — tracker 4c). The book should not describe the paper's position as a result.
Fix l. 665: "My cosmology paper assumes measurement independence — as every Bell-type argument must — and rejects factorization." See §7, SB-HC4A item (c).

**1.8 [MAJOR] Ch 14 leans on perspective-free mathematical truth that Ch 6 and Ch 7 withhold.**
l. 941: "It does not bring the creatures' consciousness into existence, as a printed proof does not make the theorem true."
l. 1025: "Truth from no perspective does not travel, because there is no such perspective"; l. 463: "Whether logical truths hold independently of every perspective is not decided…"
Problem: the analogy presupposes theorems are true independently of any proof or perspective — Platonism the book declines. The climax should not borrow it.
Fix: "as a second copy of the file does not add a second world."

**1.9 [MINOR] "First limit" names two different things.**

## 2. Logical flaws

**2.1 [BLOCKER] Ch 6 states its thesis in the form it then refutes.**
l. 361: "A claim can be finished off for good without anyone holding the claim that replaces it as final"
l. 381: "A stronger version of the principle says that a refutation, once made, survives every change of framework. History refutes that version."
Also l. 367 "Showing it takes one contradiction" vs l. 377 "Absolute falsehood in the sense used here is earned by accumulated refutation."
Problem: "for good" is the refuted version. And "one contradiction suffices" and "earned by accumulation" are two different claims (the local verdict vs. its epistemic security) presented as one. Ch 15 (l. 1013) repeats the slogan.
Fix l. 361: "A claim can be refuted relative to input that every successor keeps, without anyone holding the claim that replaces it as final." l. 377: separate the two: one contradiction establishes the verdict relative to the input; what accumulates is security against the input itself being withdrawn.

**2.2 [MAJOR] Ch 6 makes falsity relative to admitted input and then asserts a contradiction that was "there before anyone looked".**
l. 377: "the contradiction with the round shadow was there before anyone looked"; l. 407: "What a system admits is up to the system, and a refutation is relative to what it admits."
Problem: before the observation is admitted there is no system–input relation in the defined sense; "there before anyone looked" is a claim about the world, which the chapter says no perspective compares its model against.
Fix: "the contradiction was available to anyone who admitted the observation, before anyone did; what consensus adds is only who has admitted it."

**2.3 [MAJOR] The claimed independence from Popper does not hold for the half of the asymmetry that does the work.**
l. 405: "The asymmetry argued for here has a different origin. It is derived from the reference-system architecture … It does not depend on the claims being universal."
l. 383: "A refutation is pinned to one specific contradiction … a positive claim … is displaced the moment a better-fitting model arrives."
Problem: the architecture yields one asymmetry — model-vs-*world* (unavailable) against model-vs-*input* (available). But "this model fits the admitted input here and now" is a positive, singular claim and is as checkable as its negation; so the architecture does not make positive verdicts uncheckable, it makes *truth* uncheckable. The gradient of l. 383 ("one contradiction pins; every competitor exposes") is the universal/existential asymmetry — Popper's — relocated to input. l. 405's singular-claim example ("this model matches the world here and now") is uncheckable because of "world", not because it is positive.
Fix: split the chapter's claims: (i) architecture: no standpoint for truth-as-match; (ii) logic: one derivation refutes, no finite set confirms — concede this is Popper's and state what is added (it applies to any reference system, and the social and consolidation mechanisms are new). Delete "It does not depend on the claims being universal."

**2.4 [MAJOR] Ch 5 §5.3's new argument equivocates between a shortcut for *all* trajectories and a shortcut for *this* one, and its last clause proves too much.**
l. 299: "if the whole it belongs to had a closed form, every part's state would be a function of the whole's and would inherit the closed form — so a whole that contains an irreducible part is irreducible."
l. 301: "a whole that defeats neither contains no describer."
Problem: Turing excludes a *general* procedure over all programs. A universal machine running a trivial program has a reducible trajectory; its presence does not make the actual whole irreducible. The contrapositive needs the describer's actual trajectory to be irreducible, which is a fact about what it computes, not about its universality. "Contains no describer" then follows only because "describer" was defined as universal at l. 299 — circular — and in a finite world every trajectory is a lookup table (l. 297 concedes this), so the conclusion is false in exactly the finite setting §5.1 adopted. Writer B flagged this; as written it should not stand.
Fix: weaken to what holds: "a whole whose describer actually performs irreducible computation has no closed form for its actual trajectory; and no general shortcut exists for a world that contains universal describers, because such a shortcut would decide what they compute." Delete the last clause of l. 301.

**2.5 [MAJOR] Ch 7's reason why the apparatus has no stand-in proves too much.**
l. 431: "The apparatus has no stand-in, because any stand-in would be another product of the apparatus."
Problem: the hunger stand-in (same paragraph) is also a product of the apparatus and works fine. The reason given does not distinguish the two cases. The real reason is Ch 4/Ch 8's: a stand-in for the *current generation as a whole* would have to be generated by it while standing for all of it — the regress.
Fix: "The apparatus has no adequate stand-in, because a stand-in for the whole of what is generating this scene would have to be generated inside the scene by the very process it stands for, and would then be one more item in the scene, not the process."

**2.6 [MAJOR] Ch 14's "generating side" equivocates, and the converse of the principle is asserted, not derived.**
l. 949: "An observer who holds the rule and the states as content … stands on the generating side"
l. 951: "what is generated cannot be had, as an inside, by whoever holds the generator."
Problem: the reader of the disk is not the generator (the rule is); holding the rule as content puts the reader *outside*, not on the generating side. And "the generator cannot appear in what it generates" does not entail "what is generated cannot be had as an inside by whoever holds the generator"; the second is the constitutive limit restated as if it followed. The book's own thesis forbids reading it off a "side".
Fix: replace "the generating side" with "the side that holds the structure as content"; state the converse as what it is: "The constitutive limit is not derived from the absent-generator principle; it is its mirror image, and it rests on the same fact — no perspective has another perspective's frame as content."

**2.7 [MAJOR] The description-length criterion is defined over a *file* and its decoding, after the chapter has said the file is unnecessary.**
l. 941: "If the relational structure is what suffices, the disk is unnecessary too." l. 961: "An inside belongs to a structure only if … the decoding that exhibits its internal relations is short relative to the structure."
Problem: once the medium is dispensed with, "the structure" is the abstract trajectory and "decoding" has no referent; the random-file example (l. 963) is about a reading of a medium. Two levels are mixed. Stated over abstract structures, the criterion says: a trajectory has an inside only if it is compressible to a rule — and then rule-plus-first-state, unexecuted, has an inside, which is Tegmark's position (l. 929) that the chapter says it stops short of.
Fix: choose one level. Either (a) the criterion ranks *encodings*: a medium carries an inside only if the inside's organization is in the medium rather than in the reader's map — then the disk is not unnecessary, it is one admissible carrier among others; or (b) the criterion ranks *structures*, in which case say plainly that an unexecuted rule-plus-seed specifies a world with an inside, and defend the distance from Tegmark on the FMT-closure condition (l. 969), not on compressibility.

**2.8 [MAJOR] The signature singles out a cone, not "the one coordinate along which the update runs"; and Ch 8's two statements of the time/space cut are different cuts.**
l. 711: "It records that one of the four coordinates is unlike the others, because it is the one along which the others are generated." l. 517: "the dimension the geometry treats like the others is the one along which the update runs."
l. 495: "Physics cuts state from change"; l. 515: "The separation of the configuration that is from the step that turns it into the next is a cut… Time is one dimension of four."
Problem: Lorentz invariance means no coordinate axis is "the" time axis; the signature distinguishes timelike *directions* (a class) from spacelike ones. A lattice update picks one axis, and Ch 10 (l. 671) correctly says that preferred frame is invisible at our level. So "the one along which the update runs" is a frame-dependent claim the signature does not support. Separately, Minkowski unifies the time coordinate with the space coordinates; he does not unify state with update — a 4D block formulation still has the hyperbolic initial-value structure (Ch 11, l. 703). The cut that is "erased and kept" is coordinate time vs. coordinate space; the state/update cut is kept intact.
Fix l. 711: "It records that one class of directions is unlike the others: the directions along which configurations can generate configurations. The substrate's update picks one axis in that class; our level sees only the class." Fix l. 495/515: name the cut as time-coordinate vs. space-coordinates and keep state/update as the computational form of it, not as the same cut.

**2.9 [MINOR] l. 245 "a valid rule never takes input the system admits into a contradiction, and that can be checked from inside" — the "never" cannot be checked from inside; Ch 7 l. 461 has it right ("shown not to fail").** Fix: "and its failures can be checked from inside".

**2.10 [MINOR] l. 647 "their joint distribution factorizes to any precision one cares to test" — λ is unobservable, so P(λ, x, y) is not testable; only settings-vs-observables are.** Fix: "nothing in the dynamics correlates the digits of π with the state of the crystal, and a superdeterminist owes the mechanism that would."

**2.11 [MINOR] l. 881 "instantiated uniformly by a mechanism that produces brief, isolated thoughts more cheaply" — uniform over observers makes cost irrelevant; cost-weighted is not uniform.** Fix: "instantiated with no observer privileged, by a mechanism whose cost per observer is what fixes the weighting…"

**2.12 [MINOR] l. 195 "A world that exceeded the ceiling would … appear as Class 4" is unargued: a Class 5 (ontically random) world appears as noise.** SB-HC4A §9.2 has the needed form ("A Class 5 universe could contain Class 4 pockets"). Fix: "A world above the ceiling, if it had Class 4 regions, would appear to observers in those regions as Class 4 throughout."

**2.13 [MINOR] l. 441 "A path not taken stays available in the simulation: somebody else took it, so it is no fantasy" — non sequitur; nobody need have taken it.** Fix: "A path not taken stays representable in the simulation, as paths others took are."

**2.14 [MINOR] "Outside" equivocates.** l. 445 "From outside, nothing is missing" means another embedded perspective, not the outside the thesis denies. Fix: "From another perspective, nothing is missing".

**2.15 [MINOR] l. 985 "or the generator losing its place" (and l. 13 "the generator's place outside its product") is not explained anywhere.** Either give the regress in one clause ("because a frame that appears whole as content needs a further frame to hold it") or cut the disjunct.

**2.16 [MINOR] l. 815 "many-worlds reifies the wavefunction more completely than its rivals" — Bohmian mechanics and objective-collapse theories reify it too; Ch 2 already made the continuum objection theory-neutral.** Fix: "and it applies to every reading that reifies the wavefunction, many-worlds included."

**2.17 [MINOR] l. 1053 method litany ("Every … Every … Every … Every … And every …") is a five-fold imperative the chapter does not argue for one by one.** Each clause is earned elsewhere; here it reads as a credo. Cut to Ch 1's one-sentence rule (l. 83) plus the observer-measure clause, which is the one new obligation.

## 4. Redundancy (both locations; which to keep)

**4.1 [MAJOR] The cognitive ceiling, including the sentence "I do not know how to resolve this objection from within, and I am not sure it can be resolved from within", appears verbatim three times:** l. 199 (Ch 3), l. 479 (Ch 7), l. 1037 (Ch 15).
