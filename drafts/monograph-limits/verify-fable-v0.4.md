## 2. New logical defects introduced by the revision

**M1 [MAJOR] — The "outside view" gloss contradicts Chapter 13's own treatment of the thermal model, and the bit-string sentence is true only of free readings.**
l. 895: "Every such count is made from outside the universe, and the view from outside the universe is not a perspective. From there, every long enough bit string contains every mind (Chapter 14); the view can draw no distinction between one structure and another, so it is no standpoint."
l. 905, ten lines later: "Where a theory does count from outside, the measure ... has to be supplied by the theory of the generator, as the thermal model supplies it, and the theory is then tested by the distribution of what experience contains."
Problem, in three parts. (a) If the outside count is "no standpoint", the canonical Boltzmann count is no standpoint either, yet l. 897 and l. 905 treat it as tested and refutable. The chapter says of one object that it is nothing and that it is tested. (b) The count is not *made* from outside: a cosmologist counting Boltzmann brains stands inside, with a frame, and distinguishes brains from fluctuations perfectly well. What is true is that the count ranges over a space no inhabitant samples, so no refutation can land on it unless the theory supplies a measure — which is exactly what l. 893 and l. 905 already say. The "no standpoint" rhetoric adds nothing to that and misdescribes the theorist. (c) "Every long enough bit string contains every mind" follows from *free readings* (Putnam, l. 979), not from counting from outside: Tegmark's structures are individuated by their relations and a random string does not contain the worm under its intrinsic structure. At l. 981 the sentence is in its right place, because the triviality objection assumes free readings; at l. 895, l. 1001 and l. 1083 it is applied to all outside counts, where it is false of the very theories named at l. 893. The sound criterion the book already owns is: specified measure, derivable from the theory's stated dynamics and so exposed to refutation (thermal model) versus no measure (uniformity clause read as a weighting, "everything occurs somewhere"); l. 863 says this in so many words.
**Fix.** l. 895, replace the first two sentences: "No such count is checked by anyone inside: the space it ranges over is one no inhabitant samples, so a lower probability on it contradicts no input anyone admits. A theory applied from inside owes no observer measure. It is tested the way Chapter 6 tests any reference system..." and delete "From there, every long enough bit string contains every mind (Chapter 14); the view can draw no distinction between one structure and another, so it is no standpoint." (keep the from-within sentence that follows). l. 1001: "The count runs over the space of structures, and no inhabitant of any structure samples that space; nothing I admit as input is a draw from it, so the count refutes nothing and owes nothing until a measure is supplied (Chapter 13)." l. 1083: see m6 for the frame argument, which needs no bit strings. l. 817 (Ch 12) can keep "no standpoint" if it is read as "nothing to check against", but "taken from outside the universe" should go the same way. The ruling's conclusion — the debt belongs to outside-counting theories and falls where no measure is supplied — survives intact; only its justification changes.

**M2 [MAJOR] — D1's condition "available and readable" excludes the running brain and imports a reader into the condition for an inside.**
l. 985: "An inner world needs the trajectory's states present in some medium, available and readable."; l. 987: "FMT's architecture in a realized, readable trajectory"; l. 961: "that the trajectory exist somewhere as realized states that can be read."
Problem. A running brain realizes its trajectory one state at a time; past states are gone except as traces. Its trajectory is never "present in some medium, available and readable" as a whole to any third person — only the current state is. On the condition as worded, the paradigm case fails it, while the disk passes. Second, "readable" makes the existence of an inside depend on a reader's capacity, which l. 959 ("For them nothing on it is about anything; it is what there is") and l. 961 ("whether anyone reads the disk") deny. MG's phrase was fixed for the disk case, where "available and readable" and "realized" coincide; the book has to state the condition so that successive realization counts too. l. 991 already has the right word ("each state present and fixed by its predecessors") and l. 997 the right contrast (scan = one state; file = whole trajectory).
**Fix.** l. 985: "An inner world needs the trajectory's states realized — present in some medium at some time, all at once on a disk or one after another in a running brain. They need not have been computed there: the states an executed rule would produce, printed out, have an inner world even if they were found by chance and nothing ever ran the rule, while the rule and the first state with nothing realized have none." l. 987: "FMT's architecture in a realized trajectory". l. 961: "What cannot be dropped is that the trajectory be realized somewhere, as states that exist and not as states a rule would produce."

**M3 [MAJOR] — Core (ii) is justified by a principle that is false as stated: certification does not require holding the remainder.**
l. 285: "no part can certify its own description of the whole complete. The description may in fact be complete, and for a whole generated by a short rule it can be. But checking that nothing remains outside it would require holding the remainder"; l. 1081: "A perspective that claims to have certified its description complete claims to have held the remainder it checked for, which is more than its description."
Problem. Certification by proof needs no enumeration: Euclid certified that no largest prime exists without holding the primes; Presburger arithmetic is provably complete without anyone listing its truths. A logician reads l. 285 and stops there. The claim is sound, but on a different ground, and the book has it: a proof of exhaustiveness drawn from inside is a derivation within the part's own reference system from the input it admits, so it certifies consistency with what has reached the part, never that nothing has failed to reach it (Chapter 6, l. 367–369); and a formal system strong enough to *state* its own completeness is one Gödel's theorems govern (l. 311). The "remainder" image also makes core (ii) look like capacity in disguise, which l. 285 then has to deny.
**Fix.** l. 285, replace "But checking that nothing remains outside it would require holding the remainder, and the part has nothing to check against except what it already holds and what reaches it." with: "But a check that nothing remains outside it is made from inside it: either by holding the remainder, which the part cannot do, or by a proof, and a proof from inside is drawn from the input the part admits and shows at most that the description agrees with that input — never that no input is missing (Chapter 6). A formal system strong enough to state its own completeness is one the theorems of 5.4 govern." l. 1081: "A perspective that claims to have certified its description complete claims either to have held the remainder it checked for, which is more than its description, or to have proved from its own input that there is none, which its input cannot show (Chapters 5, 6)."

**M4 [MAJOR] — Chapter 15 derives the reading limit and the "world is mathematical" limit from the core; neither chapter that states them does.**
l. 1075: "The other two are failures of certification, and certifying a reading or a world would need the model and what it models held side by side as content (Chapter 6). That need meets the core, which holds for every perspective without condition, because no perspective is outside."
Problem. Chapter 1 (l. 77) grounds the reading limit in "the system contains only itself"; Chapter 6 (l. 367) grounds truth-as-match being unavailable in "every comparison of a model with the world is a comparison of one model with another." Neither is core (i) (own current generation) or core (ii) (certifying completeness). Certifying that the electromagnetic potential is surplus needs the world's electromagnetic facts beside the formalism, not the certifier's own generation and not the whole; the core is not what blocks it. What blocks it is the premise Chapter 6 states — no standpoint outside all reference systems — which is the *ground* of the core, not the core. The agent that wrote this flagged it as its own reading; it is not a ruling, and it is not derived.
**Fix.** l. 1075, last sentence: "That need meets the condition Chapter 6 states, which no perspective escapes: every comparison of a model with the world is a comparison of one model with another, because no perspective is outside. The core limits rest on the same fact; the reading limit is not derived from them." Also l. 1079 is unaffected (it speaks of "the core" and "the conditional limits" only).

**M5 [MAJOR] — D2's precondition does no work against the Boltzmann brain, and if read so that it does, it contradicts Chapter 13.**
l. 1003: "consciousness can only experience a contrast between order and disorder, and it cannot arise surrounded by chaos alone. Order is a precondition of observers"; l. 1005: "A system cut off from ordered input from before any ordered sensory processing would build no world-model, and would not be conscious."
Problem. The canonical Boltzmann brain satisfies the precondition with its own few kilograms of order; it is not "surrounded by chaos alone" in any sense that bears on it, since what it experiences is its own ordered content. So the precondition explains why *every* observer finds order and nothing about how much; l. 1009 concedes the latter, so this is consistent but the paragraph reads as if it answered more. The developmental conjecture at l. 1005 excludes only systems that must *build* a world-model without input. A fluctuation does not build; it arrives assembled with implicit content in place — which is exactly what Chapter 13 l. 885 says FMT permits ("A momentary instantiation equipped with the right implicit content would therefore present to itself exactly as I present to myself on waking"). If the conjecture is meant to exclude assembled minds as well — MG's words "never can consciousness arise if surrounded only by chaos, except of prior consciousness" suggest it is — then it refutes the canonical Boltzmann brain and Chapter 13's fourth hypothesis on architectural grounds, Chapter 13 nowhere uses that, and l. 885 contradicts it. Either way the two chapters do not agree on whether FMT admits an assembled mind.
**Fix.** State the conjecture's scope and say what it reaches: l. 1005 add, after "would not be conscious.": "This reaches minds that come to be by building a model; whether a mind could arrive assembled, with the models of a life it never lived already in place, is what the Boltzmann hypothesis asserts and what Chapter 13 leaves undecided, and the conjecture does not decide it." Then l. 1003: "consciousness can only experience a contrast between order and disorder, so every observer finds order wherever it looks; that is what selection on the observer delivers, and all it delivers." And in Chapter 13 l. 885 add the cross-reference: "(whether a model can arrive assembled rather than built is a question Chapter 14 states and does not settle)".

**M6 [MAJOR] — The edge-of-order asymmetry treats as a rival hypothesis a reading the book's own method classifies as bookkeeping.**
l. 1007: "The patch reading says that order may end anywhere beyond what has been observed; the lawful-whole reading says it will not. Further observed order never refutes the patch reading"; l. 1009: "on the patch reading the far order we see ... is neither explained nor anomalous."
Problem. A reading that forbids no content is, by l. 1091 ("either predicts something new or is labelled bookkeeping") and l. 915 ("content it forbids, so that what I find can refute it"), not a hypothesis. The lawful reading is the only hypothesis on the table; the patch reading is the absence of one — "everything occurs somewhere" in another costume (l. 893). Presenting the two symmetrically and then letting the patch reading set the cost inverts the book's own rule. The cost that survives is Chapter 6's general one: the far order fits the lawful reading and fitting establishes nothing.
**Fix.** l. 1007, add after "Of the two, the lawful reading is the one exposed to refutation.": "It is also the only one that is a hypothesis. The patch reading forbids nothing, so by the rule of Chapter 1 it is bookkeeping: a record that order has been found as far as anyone has looked." l. 1009: "The cost is that the far order we see, the galaxies and the smoothness of the cosmic microwave background, is what the lawful reading predicts and no evidence for it over the record (Chapter 6): it is how far this order has been found to reach. The argument from excess order keeps its force..." (rest unchanged).

**M7 [MAJOR] — Chapter 8 equivocates on "substrate": the stored/running argument establishes that stored structure at rest has no experience, not that the physical level has none.**
l. 515: "it keeps the claim that the substrate cannot feel, because that claim can be argued without levels."; l. 517: "the claim that the substrate cannot feel inherits that status."; l. 509 reports FMT: "a real side, the substrate, which has no experience" (§3.4.6).
Problem. FMT's "substrate" in §3.4.6 is the physical level as such; running activity is as physical as weights. The argument of l. 517 — closure is a property of running activity, weights at rest enter no update, therefore weights at rest have no experience — is valid given P3 and establishes "stored structure has no experience". It does not establish "the physical level has no experience", which is the claim that relocates the hard problem and which l. 515 rightly says goes with the level reading. Using "the substrate cannot feel" for both lets a reader think the level reading's conclusion has been rescued without it. A second, smaller point: FMT's P3 as written is level-relative ("the second computational level's modeling closed on itself", §3.4 head); the book uses it stripped of that clause, which is legitimate but should be said, since the paragraph's point is that no level ontology is used.
**Fix.** l. 515: "it keeps the claim that stored structure cannot feel, because that claim can be argued without levels; the stronger claim, that the physical level as such has no experience, is the level reading's and goes with it." l. 517, first sentence: "The argument runs on a distinction inside one level, between structure that is stored and activity that is running, and on P3 read without its level clause: phenomenality is the modelling closed on itself, wherever that closure runs." Last sentence: "and the claim that stored structure cannot feel inherits that status." Record §3.4.6's wording as a candidate in `docs/monograph-paper-correction-candidates.md` (the paper's "substrate" would need the same disambiguation).

**M8 [MAJOR] — Chapter 8 grounds all cuts in the core; the core forces one cut, capacity forces the rest.**
l. 547: "Any embedded perspective makes cuts: it cannot hold its own current generation as content and cannot certify any description of the whole complete (Chapter 5), so it works with separated, partial descriptions, and where the whole is incompressible, capacity forces the separation as well."; l. 505 and l. 543 still ground cuts in capacity alone ("Chapter 5 showed that a proper part of an incompressible whole cannot hold a complete description of the whole, so a mind that is a part sees nothing without devices of this kind").
Problem. "Partial" is not "separated": a map that leaves off a region is incomplete and has no seam. The step from the core to "separated" at l. 547 is unargued. What the core does force is one seam — between the describer's own generation, which it cannot hold, and what it describes — and that seam *is* the implicit/explicit cut and the description/instance cut (l. 557 says so). Time/space and boundary/projection are compressions, and Chapter 8's own account (l. 543) grounds them in capacity, which is world-conditional. So the honest statement is: every embedded perspective makes at least one cut, by the core; where the whole is incompressible it makes more, by capacity. l. 505 and l. 543 should carry the same two-part grounding so the chapter does not say in three places that cuts come from capacity and in one that they come from the core.
**Fix.** l. 547: "Any embedded perspective makes at least one cut. It cannot hold its own current generation as content (Chapter 5), so any theory it builds of a whole that contains it describes the describer only as a part among parts, under rules of its own, with a seam where that part meets what it generates — which is the implicit/explicit cut and the description/instance cut of this chapter. Where the whole is incompressible, capacity forces further separations, and those are the cuts of time from space and boundary from projection." l. 505: "...so a mind that is a part of an incompressible whole sees nothing without devices of this kind, and a mind that is a part of any whole makes at least the one cut between itself as describer and what it describes (Chapter 5)."

## 3. Weakest remaining arguments, in the order a hostile reader reaches them

The five the brief named: the outside-view move is M1; the two-core limits are M3 (core ii) with core (i) sound as a regress argument but stated as architecture, not theorem — a reader who wants a theorem will not get one and the book does not promise one; the edge-of-order asymmetry is M6; stored-vs-running is M7 and its dependence on P3 is stated correctly at l. 517 (the FMT §3.4 head says "argued for and not derived (Section 3.4.3)" verbatim); the felt-necessity proposal against fluency is handled as the brief required (l. 465 concedes identical signs and names the test), and the residual weakness — that until the scaffolded-producer test runs the proposal is a redescription of fluency findings in FMT vocabulary plus one explanandum fluency does not address (perspective-freedom, l. 457) — is stated by the chapter itself. Two further points worth a sentence each:

- **l. 981 "A view that draws no distinctions is no standpoint" and l. 1083 "Any perspective that distinguishes anything, this and not that, has a frame."** The second is the clean argument and the first is the vulnerable one (M1). D3 should rest on frames only: distinguishing is what a frame does, a frame is part of what there is, so a perspective that distinguishes is embedded. No bit strings needed.
- **l. 971 "for any one perspective a structure is either its frame or its content, never both at once."** This is now the one fact both the absent generator and the constitutive limit rest on, and it is asserted. A reader will ask why not both: a self-model is part of the frame and is content. The answer the book has is "as a whole and at the same time" (l. 509, 519): parts of the frame can be content; the frame as a whole cannot be, on pain of regress. l. 971 should carry the qualifier: "a structure is, taken as a whole, either its frame or its content, never both at once."

## 5. MINOR findings

- **m1 [MINOR] Ch 13 l. 903** "A finite-but-unbounded substrate on a cycle ... which the paper adopts as a declared architectural choice (Chapter 11)". The paper declares reversibility (§8.4) and offers cyclic renewal as a conjecture (§5.4); the finite-but-unbounded cyclic grid is the author's picture "beyond the cosmology paper" (Ch 12 l. 801). Fix: "A finite-but-unbounded substrate on a cycle, which my own picture adopts (Chapter 12) and which the paper's declared reversibility yields on any finite substrate (Chapter 11), or a cyclic cosmology...".
- **m2 [MINOR] Ch 11 l. 763**, three precisions. (a) "Reversibility of the whole state follows from two other properties ... Each of the two is undecidable from inside" invites the inference that a conclusion is undecidable because its sufficient conditions are; the paragraph does block the direct route in its next sentence, so put that sentence first and make the reduction supporting: "Reversibility cannot be read off directly ... Nor does it follow from anything the inside can establish: it follows from determinism and finiteness together, and each of those is undecidable from inside". (b) The reduction yields bijectivity *on the cycle*; the paper's declared choice at l. 725 is injectivity everywhere ("no two distinct substrate states evolve to the same state"). (c) "Finiteness cannot be told apart from quasi-infinity" — quasi-infinity is not the contrary of finiteness; Ch 12 l. 805 makes the coherent version finite. Fix: "Finiteness cannot be told apart from infinity, because both present as quasi-infinite from inside (Chapter 12)".
- **m3 [MINOR] Ch 8 l. 515** "The level reading buys the relocation of the hard problem, and the meta-problem, since the machinery that generates the simulation is not in the simulation" — l. 551 and Ch 7 l. 433 ground the meta-problem in the whole-at-once constraint, which needs no levels. - **m4 [MINOR] Ch 8 l. 517** "cortical activity is suppressed, synaptic structure is spared, and experience goes" — not every anaesthetic suppresses activity (ketamine raises some; propofol produces slow oscillations); what is disrupted is the integration that closure needs. - **m5 [MINOR] Ch 14 l. 961** "A second copy of the file does not add a second world" — a type-identity thesis about minds, asserted in one clause, which applied to two identical running brains says there is one mind. Either defend it or weaken: "Whether a second copy of the file adds a second world or a second realization of one, I leave open; nothing here needs it."
- **m6 [MINOR] Ch 15 l. 1083** "By its own standard a contradiction counts against nothing, so it refutes nothing either, and it makes no claim that competes with any other." False of paraconsistent reasoners: a dialetheist asserts claims that compete, and holds that *some* contradictions are true, not that none counts. Fix: "A perspective that does not is no second exception: it stands outside the reach of every refutation, these included, which is Chapter 6's condition and no exemption peculiar to the limits; what it has in place of refutation is a question for its own logic." Also, per M1, replace "and the outside view draws no distinctions: from it every long enough bit string contains every mind" with "and a perspective with no frame distinguishes nothing, since distinguishing is what a frame does".
- **m7 [MINOR] Ch 7 l. 461** "The literature takes as given that felt rightness follows ease of production. The proposal supplies a mechanism" — the literature finds and models it (fluency is a mechanism). Fix: "The literature's own account is that felt rightness follows ease of production. The proposal supplies a second mechanism beneath it:".
- **m8 [MINOR] Ch 14 l. 1005** "which I state as a conjecture derived from the theory" — self-narration of the author's move (register file, S320 entry). Fix: "FMT suggests a developmental form of the precondition. It is a conjecture the theory suggests and does not establish."
- **m9 [MINOR] Ch 5 l. 303** "A describer taken as unbounded admits no general shortcut to a world that contains it, because such a shortcut would decide what it computes." Ambiguous between "no shortcut exists" and "the describer can hold none". Only the second follows (a describer holding the shortcut could compute its own next output and produce another), and only if the describer actually runs the computations in question. Fix: "A describer taken as unbounded can hold no general shortcut to a world that contains it: holding one, it would read off its own next output and could then produce another, so the shortcut would be wrong about the describer."
