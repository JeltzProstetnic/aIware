## 2. The two new sections

### 2.1 "The order that needs explaining" (Ch 14, l. 981–991)

**What holds.** The generalization at l. 983 is valid given the position (nothing singled out the worm but two properties, so every structure with them has an inside). The Boltzmann transposition at l. 985 is the right consequence to draw and is drawn honestly. The three-part mapping onto Ch 13's debt at l. 987 is exact (closure answers "what implements"; "every structure meeting the two conditions" answers "which histories occur"; the weighting is unpaid). The description-length direction at l. 989 is offered as a direction and its three obstacles at l. 991 are the right three, including the one that returns Ch 13's within-structure question unchanged. Consistency with Ch 6: "neither confirms nor disconfirms" (l. 987) is what the falsity criterion says of a theory that makes no prediction. Consistency with Ch 8 / Ch 15 l. 1047: l. 991 concedes that description length is a reader's measure, i.e. the residue of the description/instance cut, in the criterion's own weighting.

**N3 [MAJOR] — the criterion is applied to the whole structure at l. 959 and to a region at l. 985, and to the whole again at l. 989, without saying so.** l. 959: "An inside belongs to a structure only if… the shortest description that exhibits its internal relations is short relative to the structure." An island of order in incompressible noise, taken as the structure, has a shortest description about as long as itself and *fails* that criterion; on l. 959 as written, the noisy wholes counted at l. 985 never had insides and the problem does not arise. l. 985 patches this in passing — "because it applies to the region that carries the inside" — which is a rescoping of the criterion not made where the criterion is stated, and l. 989 then applies description length "to the whole structure" for the weighting. The two uses are legitimate; they must be two named uses of one quantity, which l. 989's "One quantity would then do two jobs" already half-says.
**Fix.** l. 959: "An inside belongs to a region of a structure only if that region carries its own organization: only if the shortest description that exhibits the region's internal relations is short relative to the region. For an observer the region is its world, as far as its own states depend on it." l. 985, replace the sentence beginning "The description-length criterion does not exclude" with: "The criterion of the previous section admits them, because it is met by the island itself: an island of order is as compressible as its observers require, and what surrounds it enters no relation the island's states depend on." l. 989: "Description length suggests one direction, applied this time not to the region that carries the inside but to the whole structure in which it sits."

**N18 [MINOR] — l. 985 "Counted so… Nothing supplies one here."** The count "among structures up to any given size" is a uniform measure over finite structures, i.e. a measure has been supplied, by choice. Say so: "Nothing but the choice to count uniformly supplies one here, and that choice is what the thermal version did not have to make."

**N14 [MINOR] — l. 983 "The position returns Chapter 13's debt in a larger form."** "Returns" reads as "repays". Use "incurs".

### 2.2 "The limits as refutations" (Ch 15, l. 1059–1067)

**What holds.** The self-application problem is stated correctly at l. 1061 and the resolution — impossibility claims travel as refutations checkable from the claimant's own admitted self-description, not as positive truths — is the right one and matches Ch 6's mechanism (l. 1065). The regress refutation of "I hold my own generator as content" (l. 1063, second case) is valid and is Ch 4's argument. The exemption for a perspective that denies being embedded (l. 1065) is honest and consistent with Ch 12 (the bound cannot be found from inside). The split of "no outside" into refutation and positive picture (l. 1067) is correct and is the strongest sentence in the section.

**N1 [MAJOR] — l. 1061 contradicts §What travels.** "Yet on the account of what travels, no positive claim passes between perspectives intact." §What travels (l. 1029–1031) says relational structure and compression within scope do pass intact: "Its validity within the scope travels: anyone at that level can test it and obtain the same result." The chapter denies at l. 1061 what it asserted at l. 1031. **Fix:** "Yet on the account of what travels, a positive claim passes between perspectives only as structure and as validity within a scope, never as truth from no perspective. If the limit claims were positive truths about all perspectives, held from none, they would be exactly what that account says no one holds."

**N2 [MAJOR] — l. 1063's first refutation overclaims and contradicts Ch 5 and Ch 15 l. 1003.** "No perspective that is a proper part of what it describes holds a complete account of the whole… A perspective that claims to hold a complete account of the whole contradicts what it admits about itself: that it is a proper part with fewer resources than the whole." Ch 5 l. 283 defines completeness so that a generative description can be complete, and l. 291 says a part *can* hold the complete generative description of a compressible whole ("the rule plus the initial condition fit where the state does not"); SB-HC4A's universe is compressible by construction. Ch 15 l. 1003 states the limit correctly scoped ("a complete description of an *incompressible* whole"). So the contradiction claimed at l. 1063 is not available for the case the book itself regards as actual. What is available is the disjunction Ch 5 actually proves. **Fix for l. 1063, first and third sentences:** "Each limit is an impossibility: no perspective that is a proper part of what it describes holds the whole as content — not its state where the whole is incompressible, not its future where the whole is irreducible, and not its own current generation ever — and none holds its own generator, as a whole, as content." and "A perspective that claims to hold the whole as content contradicts what it admits about itself. If it claims the whole's state, it claims more degrees of freedom than a proper part has, and the claim fails by counting wherever the whole is incompressible (Chapter 5). If it claims the whole's generator, holding the generator gives it the whole's future only by running it, behind the whole, and a shortcut through a whole that contains the shortcut would decide its own output ahead of itself (Chapter 5)."

**N5 [MINOR] — the limits inherit Ch 6's gradient and its transfer condition, and the section does not say so.** Ch 6 l. 359 makes refutations resistant, not immune; l. 401 makes transfer conditional on treating contradiction as failure. l. 1071 "the refutations hold for all of them" reads as absolute. **Fix:** append to l. 1065: "They carry the resistance Chapter 6 gives a refutation and not immunity: the input they rest on — being embedded, having a frame — is what a perspective would have to withdraw to escape them, and a perspective that does not treat contradiction as failure is outside their reach as it is outside every refutation's." l. 1071: "and the refutations hold for all of them that keep the input."

**N4 [MINOR] — l. 1067 misuses the defined term *cut*.** "FMT's levels, SB-HC4A's automaton, the description-length criterion of Chapter 14 and the reading of becoming as irreducibility are positive claims… They are didactic cuts." Ch 8 defines a cut as a separation; the automaton and the criterion are models, not separations (Ch 15 l. 1047 itself treats the criterion as something the description/instance cut leaves a residue *in*, not as a cut). The clause "they make phenomena understandable that are not understandable without them" is also an unargued universal. **Fix:** "They are offered for their explanatory power. Some of them are cuts in the sense of Chapter 8; none makes a claim to absolute truth. Nothing about them travels the way the limits do."
