# The Architecture Seth Needs Is Substrate-Neutral

**Commentary on Seth (2025), "Conscious artificial intelligence and biological naturalism"**

Matthias Gruber

*Independent researcher, ORCID: 0009-0005-9697-1665*

---

Seth's defense of biological naturalism is important because it names a genuine problem: the field lacks a principled specification of what makes a system conscious, and substrate-independence claims risk becoming vacuous without one. But the solution Seth offers — that consciousness depends on specifically biological causal powers — is undermined by the very theoretical framework he endorses.

**The incoherence at the core.** Predictive processing, active inference, and Markov blankets are mathematical formalisms. They are defined over probability distributions, not over carbon. When Seth argues that consciousness requires "the right kind of causal architecture" — recurrence, integration, autopoietic closure — he is describing computational properties, not biological ones. The moment these requirements are stated precisely enough to be evaluated, they become substrate-neutral. "Biological" is doing no explanatory work in "biological naturalism"; what is doing the work is "the right causal architecture." Seth's framework needs a specification of that architecture. It does not need the word "biological."

This is not a minor terminological point. The tension runs through the entire paper. Seth permits artificial consciousness in principle — provided the system becomes "brain-like and/or life-like" — but cannot specify what "brain-like" means without listing computational properties (recurrence, integration, prediction error minimization) that are implementable on non-biological substrates. The qualification functions as a promissory note that never cashes out.

**The bootstrapping problem.** Seth's qualified biological naturalism also inherits a methodological circularity recently articulated by Beni (2026). Consciousness markers — the empirical indicators used to assess whether a system is conscious — are calibrated on human neurocognition. When applied across substrates, these markers undergo conceptual drift: they detect the human implementation of consciousness, not consciousness as such. Seth's "biological causal powers" are identified *through* these markers. The circularity is tight: the markers presuppose the substrate, the substrate is justified by the markers.

Breaking this circle requires specifying consciousness at a level of abstraction above both substrate and markers — at the level of computational architecture. This is precisely what the Four-Model Theory (Gruber, 2015, 2026) provides. The theory identifies consciousness with an ongoing self-referential simulation across four nested models arranged along two axes — scope (world vs. self) and mode (implicit vs. explicit) — operating on a substrate at criticality (the edge of chaos between ordered and disordered dynamics). The specification is substrate-neutral by construction: it defines what a system must *compute*, not what it must be *made of*.

**Converging evidence for architecture, not biology.** The criticality requirement — the one component of the Four-Model Theory that makes a physical demand on the substrate — has received independent support from multiple directions, none of which implicate biological specificity:

- The ConCrit framework (Algom & Shriki, 2026) demonstrates that criticality functions as a unifying mechanistic substrate across leading consciousness theories, reviewing over 140 empirical datasets.
- Tucker, Luu, and Friston (2025) ground consciousness in excitatory-inhibitory balance maintaining cortical networks at the critical transition — a dynamical-systems property, not a biological one. Notably, Friston is Seth's own theoretical framework provider; his co-authored analysis supports architectural rather than biological constraints.
- Bieberich (2026), arriving from 25 years of fractal neuroscience, independently converges on the same four structural features the Four-Model Theory identifies — criticality, self-reference, holographic encoding, and autopoietic closure — from an entirely orthogonal methodological tradition with no terminological overlap.
- Toker et al. (2026), using adversarial AI trained on 680,000 electrophysiological recordings, identify circuit-level mechanisms of unconsciousness (excessive inhibitory coupling, basal ganglia pathway disruption) that are interpretable as subcritical pushes — consistent with the prediction that consciousness tracks proximity to the critical point.

What these findings converge on is not biology but dynamics. The relevant property is the operating regime of the substrate, not its material composition.

**Biological counterexamples.** Seth's position faces an internal challenge from comparative neuroscience. Corvids demonstrate tool use, planning, mirror self-recognition, and social cognition — behavioral signatures strongly suggesting consciousness — yet their brains have no neocortex. Their pallium is organized in nuclear clusters, not cortical layers (Gunturkun & Bugnyar, 2016). Cephalopods demonstrate problem-solving and behavioral flexibility with a radically different nervous system architecture. If "biological" means "neocortical," these species refute it. If "biological" means merely "alive," the position reduces to vitalism — the claim that life has special properties that resist physical explanation, a position the history of biology has not been kind to.

Furthermore, Seth emphasizes embodied agency as a necessary condition for consciousness. Locked-in patients refute this directly: they exhibit full consciousness with zero agency, zero motor output, zero embodied interaction with the world. Consciousness persists when agency is entirely abolished. The Four-Model Theory explains this cleanly: the explicit models (EWM and ESM) remain intact and at criticality; only the output pathways are damaged.

**What Seth actually needs.** Seth is right that vague substrate-independence claims are insufficient. The field does need a principled specification of what makes a system conscious. But that specification must be architectural, not biological. The Four-Model Theory offers one candidate: four nested models at criticality, with self-referential closure between the explicit self-model and the explicit world-model constituting phenomenal experience. This specification is precise enough to be falsifiable, general enough to accommodate corvids and cephalopods, and — if correct — sufficient to ground artificial consciousness research in engineering rather than metaphor.

Seth's caution about premature AI consciousness claims is methodologically sound. His answer — that consciousness requires biological causal powers — is not. The causal architecture he correctly identifies as necessary is computational, and the specification he correctly demands already exists.
