## 1. The core discipline (MG, Telegram #379, 2026-10-19)

> "My personal thinking discipline, sourced from my 15 years old martial arts self after encountering taoism, says for every fact it's opposite must also be true in some way and this has accelerated my intellectual development enormously and led me among many other things to realize that there is no absolute truth prior to writing the 2015 book."

Context: MG calls No Outside's example "no statement holds together with its denial" (release md :254, :271) a bad example of a self-evident rule.

## 2. First challenge round (S342)

Counterexamples put to MG (Bartl, Telegram #385):
1. Self-application: "every fact's opposite is also true in some way" is itself a claim; its opposite ("some fact has no true opposite") must then also hold in some way — exception, or absolute truth.
2. "Something is being experienced right now" — its denial cannot be true for anyone thinking it.
3. "2+3=5" in Peano arithmetic — the opposite only becomes true in another system, i.e. about something else.
Bartl's guess: "in some way" = relative to some reference system (No Outside's frame-relativity), which is weaker than "the opposite is true".

MG's answers (Telegram #387, verbatim):
> "1. No it's opposite is the original logic statement, NO fact is true together within it's opposite. So yes, this is not really a counterexample it rather proves my point partially. 2. Right now as an infinitesimal short Moment makes experience impossible lol 3 in some way, exactly, but we could also be wrong about peanonarithmetic no?"

Notes for the guide (Bartl, S342):
- (1) The principle and the law of non-contradiction are each other's opposite, so the pair instantiates the principle at the meta level: each holds "in some way" (in different frames). Neighbouring literature to check before citing: dialetheism / paraconsistent logic.
- (2) An instant has no experience in it: experience needs an extended "now". Ties to FMT (closure runs over successive frames) and to the specious present (James) — check before citing.
- (3) "We could be wrong about Peano arithmetic": Gödel's second incompleteness theorem — arithmetic cannot prove its own consistency from inside — is No Outside's own thesis. Even 2+3=5 rests on an assumption not certifiable from inside.

## 4. Second challenge: "this pain hurts" (S342)

Bartl's challenge: is there any statement the discipline should NOT apply to, e.g. "this pain hurts"?

MG's answer (console, verbatim):
> "this pain hurts seems so unrefutable to the individuum believing to experience pain, but didnÄt we already find out that pain might be a explicit phenomenon? from martial arts AND from me losing consciousness several times during a bad wisdom teeth extraction including cutting, and hammer and meissel, i can tell you every pain is ignorable until you faint and we have no proof that consciousness is real and someone even better trained than me could be able in theory to switch pain on and off, but subjective experience is really a hard case i have to admit. still, i may have an illusion of pain from a cosmic particle event in my head or some other reason... but in the end, this is about learning to think, and should be seen as useful heuristic in that context before being consiudered a hard fact for no outside."

**Scope ruling (MG):** the discipline is a heuristic for learning to think, not (yet) a hard claim for No Outside.

Notes (Bartl, S342 — check before citing):
- Clinical counterpart to "this pain does not hurt": pain asymbolia — patients report the sensation but are not bothered by it (Berthier, Starkstein & Leiguarda 1988; Grahek, *Feeling Pain and Being in Pain*, 2001/2007). The opposite is true "in some way" in real patients.
- FMT reading: pain is explicit content; it is phenomenal only where it enters the closed self-model loop. Training (martial arts) changes what enters the loop; fainting is the loop collapsing. Fits "every pain is ignorable until you faint".
- Hard residue MG concedes: a hallucinated pain (cause illusory, e.g. a cosmic-ray event) still hurts. On FMT the experience is real at the virtual level even when its cause is not. Possible guide lesson: separate the claim about the experience from the claim about its cause — the discipline bites hard on the second, weakly on the first.

MG follow-up (console, verbatim):
> "that is exactly the way i learned to ignore pain in martial arts and also how THC makes pain less relevant. and why fear is what gives pain its valence more than the actual injury."

Notes (Bartl, S342 — all to verify before citing):
- Sensation vs. affect are separable dimensions of pain (Melzack & Casey 1968, sensory-discriminative vs. affective-motivational).
- THC (VERIFIED S342, docs/aiw310-pain-affect-literature.md): Lee et al. 2013, *Pain* — THC lowered unpleasantness but not intensity; the drop in amygdala–sensorimotor connectivity correlated with the unpleasantness-minus-intensity difference. Matches "less relevant", not "less intense". Vlaeyen & Linton 2000 verified; Melzack & Casey 1968 is a book chapter (page range disputed). Pain asymbolia as 'depersonalization for pain': Gerrans 2020/2024.
- Fear: the fear-avoidance model (Vlaeyen & Linton 2000) and pain catastrophizing — fear and its predictions drive suffering and disability more than tissue damage.
- FMT angle (candidate, not a claim): the pain-pleasure axis lives in the self-model; fear is the self-model predicting harm to the self's future, so valence is set by the self-model's forecast more than by the nociceptive input. Possible future link to the master's pain-pleasure-axis conjunct — MG to decide.

MG correction (console, verbatim): "about illusionism: FMT is not proven fact"
→ Guide rule: FMT's realism about phenomenal properties and illusionism are both hypotheses. The guide presents neither as fact; "we have no proof that consciousness is real" is a legitimate move in the guide, applied to FMT as much as to any rival.
