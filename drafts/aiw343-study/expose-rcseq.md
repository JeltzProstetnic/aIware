# Exposé: Everyday perceptual "leaks" and the sense that the mind has hidden depth

**Applicants:** Matthias Gruber, Dipl.-Ing. (UMIT alumnus, biomedical informatics; Alpine Institute for Advanced Research), principal investigator · Ass.-Prof. Dr. Michael Netzer, Institute of Medical Informatics, UMIT TIROL, co-investigator [to be confirmed].
**Type:** anonymous online questionnaire, non-interventional, adults, no vulnerable groups. **Requested track:** shortened procedure.

## Question
Many people feel that the mind is deeper than any explanation can reach. One computational theory of consciousness (the Four-Model Theory, Gruber 2026) traces part of this intuition to a concrete source: occasional "leaks" of the brain's own processing into experience — closed-eye flicker, visual "static", hypnagogic images, perceptual filling-in. People differ in how often they notice such leaks. The study tests whether people who notice more of them hold the hidden-depth intuition more strongly, independent of their religious, paranormal or dualist beliefs. Competing theories (Integrated Information Theory, Global Workspace Theory, Predictive Processing) make no such prediction; Attention Schema Theory and illusionism predict no relation; a sociocognitive account predicts the relation runs entirely through belief.

## Method
- **Participants:** adult volunteers in two arms, total N ≤ 1,000. (1) About 220 research scientists, recruited personally by the PI through a neutral invitation. (2) Later, up to about 800 members of the public, recruited via the Institute website, with an identical but separate survey copy. Participation is voluntary, unrewarded and invisible to anyone, including the PI.
- **Procedure:** one online questionnaire, ~25 min, self-hosted in the EU (no IP addresses, only two technical cookies — session and anti-forgery token —, no analytics). Contents: age band (3 categories) and gender; frequency of 14 everyday perceptual experiences; agreement with statements about the mind; standard short scales for personality (BFI-10), unusual experiences (O-LIFE short), dissociation (DES-B), paranormal belief (RPBS) and dualism; a funnel debrief. All scales are used under their published licences.
- **Part two:** at the end participants can open a separate form to leave an email address for a later invitation. The form is stored separately and cannot be linked to the answers. Part two will be submitted separately.
- **Analysis:** pre-registered on OSF before data collection; primary test a one-tailed partial correlation in the colleague arm (n = 200 gives power .87 for r = .20), replicated in the public arm (n = 800 gives power .88 for r = .10); mediation analysis corrected for measurement error. Analysis code is fixed in advance and tested on simulated data.

## Ethical and data-protection aspects
- **Anonymity:** no identifiers; demographics restricted to prevent re-identification in a single-employer sample; free-text answers never published. Answers are treated as anonymous; because some items concern religious/philosophical beliefs and health (GDPR Art. 9), collection rests on explicit consent (Art. 6(1)(a) + 9(2)(a)); the public dataset is coarsened to k ≥ 5 on quasi-identifiers. The other personal data is the optional email in the separate form (consent, deleted after recruitment or after 12 months at the latest).
- **Burden and risk:** minimal. Some items concern unusual perceptual experiences and dissociation; every item can be skipped, and a support contact is given.
- **No deception:** a neutral title; the hypothesis is withheld until data collection closes and then published on the Institute website.
- **Consent:** informed consent on the first page (attached).
- **Retention:** anonymous data 10 years (good scientific practice); de-identified data published with the paper.
