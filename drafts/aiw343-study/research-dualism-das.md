# AIW-343: licence research for the death-anxiety and dualism measures

Researched 2026-10-24 (S358 subagent). Scope: an anonymous, non-commercial online questionnaire study of adults in Austria, including research scientists. All DOIs below were checked against Crossref on 2026-10-24. This is not legal advice. "Licence-clean" means a written licence or the rights-holder's own statement covers the use.

## Task 1: Templer Death Anxiety Scale (DAS)

**Citation:** Templer, D. I. (1970). The construction and validation of a Death Anxiety Scale. *Journal of General Psychology, 82*(2), 165–177. doi:10.1080/00221309.1970.9920634 (verified). 15 true/false items: 9 keyed true, 6 keyed false. PsycTESTS record: doi:10.1037/t04680-000. The DOI exists in Crossref. Its terms could not be read (see below).

### Conclusion on the DAS

**Usable without permission: UNCERTAIN, leaning no.** No licence and no author waiver can be found. The practical risk for an anonymous, non-commercial study is low, but it is not licence-clean. Three options, from cleanest to least clean:
1. Swap in a measure with a clean licence (see below). **Recommended.**
2. Check the PsycTESTS "Permissions" field via an institutional login (University library, or the institute). If it carries the standard APA non-commercial-research wording, the DAS becomes usable on that basis.
3. Ask T&F through RightsLink for the 1970 article (the "reuse in a questionnaire/survey" type, if it is offered). There is no estate contact to ask.

## Task 2: replacing the Stanovich Dualism Scale

The Stanovich scale: Stanovich, K. E. (1989). Implicit philosophies of mind: The Dualism Scale and its relation to religiosity and belief in extrasensory perception. *Journal of Psychology, 123*(1), 5–23. doi:10.1080/00223980.1989.10542958. Publisher T&F; 27 items; no open licence.

### (a) Riekki, Lindeman & Lipsanen (2013)

- **Citation:** *Advances in Cognitive Psychology, 9*(3), 112–120. Conceptions about the mind-body problem and their relations to afterlife beliefs, paranormal beliefs, religiosity, and ontological confusions. PMC4158462, PMID 25247011.
  - ⚠ **Two DOIs exist.** The one in the brief, 10.5709/acp-0138-5, resolves through Crossref to the journal (Vizja Press). PMC carries **10.2478/v10053-008-0138-5**, the older De Gruyter/Versita DOI. Both point to the same paper. Cite 10.5709/acp-0138-5.
- **Licence:** the PMC copy says *"This is an open-access article distributed under the terms of the Creative Commons Attribution License, which permits unrestricted use, distribution, and reproduction in any medium, provided the original work is properly cited."* That is **CC BY**. ⚠ OpenAlex lists the record as **cc-by-nc-nd**, which is probably the journal's current default. The article's own licence statement (CC BY) governs. Either licence allows non-commercial reuse; only CC BY allows translation.
- **Items printed?** **Yes**, in full, in Appendix A ("Loadings of the 26 Items in the Mind-Body Relationship Scale on Three Factors"). Response format: **5-point, 1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree.** Factor analysis gave 3 factors: reflective dualism, emergentism and monism.
- ⚠ **This does NOT get rid of Stanovich.** The scale is described as "a modification of Stanovich's 27-item Dualism scale". Items marked ᵃ are "from the original Dualism scale". That covers **6 of the 12 dualism-factor items** and **6 of the 7 monism items**. CC BY on Riekki's article cannot relicense Stanovich's text, so the ᵃ items carry the same unresolved permission question as the original.

**Factor 1, Reflective dualism**, verbatim with loadings. ᵃ = Stanovich original. (R) = loads negatively, so reverse-score it.
1. Minds are in principle independent of bodies, to which they are only temporarily attached.ᵃ (.72)
2. The mind is a special form of energy, currently unknown to man, that is in contact with the brain and affects it.ᵃ (.72)
3. Thought processes cannot be just brain processes.ᵃ (.68)
4. The mind is immaterial and it works with the brain to generate our behavior. (.68)
5. The consciousness of myself does not die with my physical body. (.66)
6. The body belongs to the world of material and natural laws. The mind is a different kind of existence a spiritual way of being. (.65)
7. The body is material and the mind is immaterial. (.64)
8. Some mental processes have no connection to brain processes.ᵃ (.64)
9. The mind is not part of the brain, but it affects the brain.ᵃ (.60)
10. Mental states are activities of my nervous system. (−.58, R)
11. The mind as a whole is made up of substance and material processes. (−.58, R; also loads .47 on monism)
12. The mind and the brain are totally different things.ᵃ (.57)

**Riekki-original (non-ᵃ) dualism items, which are licence-clean under CC BY:** items 4, 5, 6 and 7, plus reverse-keyed items 10 and 11. Item 6 is printed without punctuation between "existence" and "a spiritual way of being"; that is how it appears in the source.

**Factor 2, Emergentism** (all 6 are Riekki-original, so clean): "The activity of the mind is based on the brain, but it is also something more than just the outcome of brain activity." / "The mind is based on brain activity, but the mind as a whole is more than only the activity of the brain." / "The operation of the mind is generated from the activity of the brain, but the operation of the mind is qualitatively different from the operation of the brain." / "The mind is based on the brain, but the mind also has attributes that the chemical and physiological events of the brain do not have." / "Although the mind is based on the brain, the mind is more than a biological process that takes place in the brain." / "The mind is based on the activity of the brain, but one cannot perceive the attributes of the mind as such in the brain."

**Factor 3, Monism:** 6 of the 7 items are Stanovich originals (ᵃ). The only Riekki item is "The mind is a physiological state of the brain."

### Recommendation for a sample of research scientists

**Primary choice: the five Navarro-Peña et al. (2026, PNAS) mind–brain statements, 0–4 scale, with the dualism statement as the key item.**
- Licence-clean: CC BY 4.0, attribution only, translation allowed.
- Built for and fielded on scientists (2,657 neuroscientists), so a direct comparison norm exists. It avoids the lay and religious register ("souls", "spiritual energy") that scientists rate as naive.
- Very short (5 items), and it separates dualism from non-reductive physicalism and dual-aspect monism. For FMT that split matters more than a single dualism score.
- Weakness: single items per position, so no internal-consistency estimate. The paper reports a PCA (5 components) instead of a validated scale.

**If a multi-item dualism score is needed:** add the **Riekki-original reflective-dualism items** (4, 5, 6, 7 plus reverse-keyed 10 and 11, on a 5-point scale). They are clean under CC BY. Leave out the six ᵃ items. Note that the subset was never validated on its own; compute α in the sample.

**Does MG still need Stanovich's permission?** **No**, provided the study uses neither the original Stanovich items nor Riekki's ᵃ-marked items. It **does** need permission if the full Riekki 25/26-item scale is used, because 12 of its items are Stanovich's. As a courtesy and a safety net, a short email costs nothing:

- **Keith E. Stanovich**, Professor Emeritus of Applied Psychology and Human Development, University of Toronto. **keith.stanovich@utoronto.ca**, verified 2026-10-24 as the mailto link on his own site (keithstanovich.com/Site/Home.html). The 1989 copyright is likely held by T&F (*J Psychol*), not by Stanovich. Even his OK would leave the publisher's rights formally open, which is one more reason to avoid those items.
