## Proposal (~450 words)

Seth's defense of biological naturalism identifies a genuine problem — vague substrate-independence claims lack principled specification — but his solution is self-undermining. Predictive processing, active inference, and Markov blankets are mathematical formalisms defined over probability distributions, not biology. When Seth specifies the "right kind of causal architecture" (recurrence, integration, autopoietic closure), he describes computational properties implementable on non-biological substrates. "Biological" does no explanatory work; "the right causal architecture" does all of it.

This commentary argues that the specification Seth correctly demands already exists: the Four-Model Theory (Gruber, 2015, 2026) identifies consciousness with ongoing self-referential simulation across four nested computational models — Implicit World Model, Implicit Self Model, Explicit World Model, Explicit Self Model — operating at criticality. The specification is substrate-neutral by construction, falsifiable, and addresses the bootstrapping circularity that Beni (2026) identifies in marker-based approaches: consciousness markers calibrated on human neurocognition cannot generalize across substrates without an architecture-level specification.

Three lines of converging evidence support architectural over biological constraints: (1) The ConCrit framework (Algom & Shriki, 2026) demonstrates that criticality — a dynamical-systems property, not a biological one — unifies leading consciousness theories across 140+ datasets. Tucker, Luu, and Friston (2025) ground consciousness in excitatory-inhibitory criticality balance — notably, Friston is Seth's own theoretical framework provider, yet his analysis supports architectural rather than biological constraints. (2) Bieberich (2026), from 25 years of independent fractal neuroscience research, converges on the same four structural features (criticality, self-reference, holographic encoding, autopoietic closure) from an entirely orthogonal tradition. (3) Comparative neuroscience undermines biological specificity: corvids demonstrate consciousness-indicating cognition without neocortex (nuclear pallium; Gunturkun & Bugnyar, 2016), and locked-in patients exhibit full consciousness without the embodied agency Seth identifies as necessary.

The commentary concludes that Seth's methodological caution is sound but his answer is not. The field needs an architectural specification of consciousness, not a biological one. The Four-Model Theory provides a candidate that is precise enough to be falsifiable, general enough to accommodate non-mammalian substrates, and — if correct — sufficient to ground artificial consciousness research in engineering rather than metaphor.
