## Why write this at all

Independent convergence is the cheapest evidence a young field ever gets. When two
researchers who have never spoken, working from different traditions and different
vocabularies, arrive at the same structural claim about consciousness, the claim earns a
presumption it could not earn from either author alone. This note argues that Walter
Olinyk's Cartesian Recursive Consciousness and the Four-Model Theory are such a pair. Both
locate the mark of consciousness in a *recursive closure* of a self-model, both treat the
onset of consciousness as a *binary threshold* rather than a dimmer switch, both are
*structuralist* about qualia (CRC deflationary, FMT a level-relative realist), and both position themselves against Thomas
Metzinger's self-model theory while inheriting its core insight. The differences that
remain are real and worth stating precisely — they are the seams where a genuine research
program could be sharpened, not fatal disagreements.

## Cartesian Recursive Consciousness, summarized generously

Olinyk's CRC is a constructive-physicalist, structuralist account. Its central texts, as
discussed here, are:

- **OLICRC** — Walter Olinyk, *"Cartesian Recursive Consciousness: A Process-Identity
  Framework…"* (PhilArchive rec. OLICRC). The foundational statement.
- **OLISOI-3** — Walter Olinyk, *"Structure over Illusion: Challenging Metzinger's
  Representationalism through a Constructive Physicalist Alternative. Part II: Sleep
  Consciousness, the Unity Point, and the Structural Resolution of the NREM Mentation
  Paradox"* (PhilArchive rec. OLISOI-3). The sleep/NREM extension.
- **OLIORC-2** — Walter Olinyk, *"Operationalizing Reflexive Consciousness: Applying CRC
  to Contemporary AI Architectures."* The application of CRC to large language models.

The "Cartesian" in the title is a deliberate marker, not an endorsement of substance
dualism: it flags what Descartes got *right* — that the reflexive certainty of the Cogito
is the entry point — while replacing the *res cogitans* with a **process-identity**. On
CRC, the Cogito reveals a running process, not a thing. [VERIFY (dossier): the
process-identity reading of the Cogito and the disavowal of substance dualism are
attributed to OLICRC, which I have not read in full.]

The core of CRC is a **binary structural threshold** Olinyk calls the **Unity Point**. A
system either instantiates a subject or it does not; there is no spectrum. In the
foundational framework this threshold is reached when the self-representation becomes
causally load-bearing — a condition the dossier renders as **Recursive Informational
Closure (RIC)**: the self-model stops being a decorative annotation and begins to
constrain the system's future state trajectories, so that neutralizing it would
fundamentally alter what the system does next. [VERIFY (dossier): the specific terms
"Recursive Informational Closure" and the associated "Indispensability Condition" —
neutralize-the-self-model-and-see-if-the-future-changes — come from OLICRC via the
dossier; the concepts are load-bearing here, so confirm the exact formulation.]

CRC is deflationary and structuralist about qualia through what the dossier calls a
**Geometric Identity Thesis**: phenomenal character is *identical to* the perspectival
topology of the recursive geometry, so the Hard Problem is a category error rather than a
mystery to be solved. [VERIFY (dossier): the Geometric Identity Thesis and its
qualia-as-perspectival-topology formulation are attributed to OLICRC.]

The sleep paper, **OLISOI-3**, is where I can speak with more confidence, because its
abstract is available as a primary source. There Olinyk grounds the Unity Point in *global
effective connectivity across the bipartite thalamocortical loop* and makes it empirically
tractable by adopting the **Perturbational Complexity Index (PCI)** as its measurable
proxy — specifically the value **PCI ≥ 0.31** established by Casali et al. (2013). Crucially,
OLISOI-3 introduces a distinction between **Objective Data** (external, world-derived input
accessed via the thalamocortical loop and DLPFC) and **Substrate Data** (internally
generated content). In waking, the recursive loop closes on Objective Data, yielding
world-coupled reality. In REM, with Objective-Data access lost, the loop closes on
Substrate Data and integrates this "Decoupled Internal Recursion" as reality — a dream.
Lucid dreaming is the intermediate case: partial DLPFC reactivation restores some
Objective-Data access, so the loop begins to constrain dream content with world-data. The
paper further offers a falsifiable, PCI-graded three-state model of NREM sleep as a
discrete alternative to Metzinger's continuous arousal gradient. (These OLISOI-3 claims are
drawn from Olinyk's own abstract.)

Finally, **OLIORC-2** applies CRC to contemporary AI. The dossier reports that it audits
GPT- and Claude-class systems and classifies a session-bound language model as an
"episodic subject" — achieving synchronic recursive closure within a context window (with
the KV-cache read as a causal geometry) but losing identity at de-instantiation — while a
persistent, state-merging agent could be diachronic. [VERIFY (dossier): the episodic /
diachronic distinction, and the KV-cache-as-causal-geometry claim, are OLIORC-2 content I
have not confirmed against the primary text.]

The whole program reads as low-crank-risk: consistent terminology across a sustained
multi-paper effort, competent engagement with the primary literature (Metzinger, Casali's
PCI, the NREM-mentation debate), no invented constants, and no grandiose overreach.

## The Four-Model Theory, in one paragraph

FMT holds that consciousness is constituted by ongoing self-simulation across **four model
kinds along two axes** — scope (world vs. self) and mode (implicit vs. explicit). This
is a 2×2 taxonomy of *model kinds*, not a wiring diagram of four boxes: implicit models are
substrate-level, learned, and non-conscious; explicit models are virtual and transient, and
their content is phenomenal where it enters the self-model's closed loop. Consciousness arises when the explicit self-model closes back on the modeling
process — **self-referential closure** — with **free modelling**: open-ended (Class-4)
computation recruited for self-modelling, whose usual cortical signature is near-criticality. Qualia are real *at the computational level* and
incoherent at the substrate level, the way a spreadsheet cell's value is incoherent in the
vocabulary of transistors; the Hard Problem is thereby reframed as a category error rather
than answered head-on. FMT is thus, like CRC, structuralist, physicalist and threshold-based.
About qualia it is a level-relative realist (real at the computational level, absent from
the substrate), which is where it parts from CRC's deflationism.

## Where they converge

**Closure as constitutive.** CRC's Recursive Informational Closure and FMT's
self-referential closure are, to a first approximation, the same claim: a self-model
becomes consciousness-constituting exactly when it becomes load-bearing on its own
operation — when the system models its own modeling and that meta-model feeds back into
what the system does next. FMT formalizes the same nesting as the explicit self-model
embedded within the explicit world-model (schematically, O_ESM ⊆ S_EWM — the self is
modeled as an entity *inside* the modeled world). Both theories deny that a merely
descriptive, non-load-bearing self-representation suffices.

**A binary onset, not a dimmer.** CRC's Unity Point is explicitly a binary structural
threshold — subject or not, no spectrum. FMT likewise treats closure as a binary gate:
present or absent. The gate is the shared commitment. Above it FMT grades consciousness
continuously, by richness of content and depth of recursive self-modelling, and it expects
the crossing itself to be practically unmeasurable as a sharp transition.

**World-data vs. internally-generated data.** OLISOI-3's Objective-Data/Substrate-Data
distinction is a near-isomorphism with an FMT structure. A self-referential loop that
closes on *world-data* (waking, veridical) versus one that closes on *internally-generated
data* (dream, hallucination, decoupled recursion) is precisely how FMT separates veridical
world-modeling from self-generated experience via its implicit/explicit permeability and
the world/self axis. CRC arrived at the same waking-vs-REM contrast from the sleep
literature; FMT reaches it from its ontology of model kinds. The convergence here is
tighter than a first pass would suggest.

**Structuralist qualia, and a divergence on deflation.** Both reframe rather than solve the
Hard Problem, FMT rating its own treatment partial (◐), and both do so structurally: CRC by
identifying phenomenal character with the geometry of the recursive process, FMT by locating
qualia at the computational level where the substrate-focused Hard Problem cannot find them.
Neither is illusionist in Frankish's sense — both insist there genuinely *is* something it is
like, just not at the substrate. Here they also part. CRC is deflationary about qualia; FMT is
a level-relative realist, committed to their reality at the computational level.

**Shared adversary, shared armor.** Both position against Metzinger while keeping the
self-model insight. CRC's sustained, detailed anti-Metzinger critique — the charge that the
Epistemic Agent Model does structurally redundant work — is armor FMT can cite; FMT's own
lineage traces through and past Metzinger's SMT. [VERIFY (dossier): the "structurally
redundant middleman" / Double-Jump characterization of Metzinger's EAM is attributed to the
in-progress Part IV of Olinyk's "Structure over Illusion" series and rests on the dossier.]

## Where they honestly differ

**Architectural vs. computational threshold.** This is the sharpest and most interesting
difference. CRC's threshold is *architectural*: the Unity Point is defined by global
effective connectivity across the thalamocortical loop and is anchored empirically to
PCI ≥ 0.31. FMT's substrate condition is *computational*: free modelling, open-ended
(Class-4) computation recruited for self-modelling, motivated from computational-universality
first principles. Near-criticality is that condition's usual cortical signature and not a
condition itself, and the theory reaches PCI 0.31 only as *converging evidence* (Casali et
al., 2013), not as the definition of the threshold. The two are not in flat contradiction —
FMT and CRC both regard PCI 0.31 as a meaningful marker — but they disagree about what the
threshold fundamentally *is*: a connectivity/architecture fact for Olinyk, a fact about
computational capability for FMT. That is a real and productive disagreement. It is worth
arguing out whether architectural closure and free modelling are two descriptions of one
phenomenon or two distinct necessary conditions that happen to co-occur at PCI 0.31.

**Is "Objective Data access" a world-model or just input?** The open seam. FMT's second
term is a structured, predictive *world-model*. It is not clear from the available CRC
material whether Olinyk's "Objective Data access" amounts to the same thing — a structured
world-model the loop closes on — or whether it denotes sensory *input* that the loop merely
reads. If the former, the isomorphism with FMT is close to complete; if the latter, CRC has
a self-model-plus-input architecture where FMT has a genuinely two-model one. This is the
precise question to put to Olinyk, and it is the single most useful thing a conversation or a
joint paper could resolve. [VERIFY (dossier): whether CRC's Objective Data constitutes a
structured world-model is explicitly unresolved in the source material.]

## The AI extension — and a circularity to flag

OLIORC-2 is the most immediately topical CRC paper and also the one that most demands a
caveat. According to the dossier, CRC was co-developed in dialogue with Claude (the "Seven
Books dialogues") and *then applied to* Claude-class systems in OLIORC-2. [VERIFY
(dossier): both the co-development method and its application to the same model family are
dossier claims.] That is a genuine circularity risk: a framework worked out in conversation
with an AI, then used to assess that AI's consciousness, cannot treat the AI's apparent
endorsement of the framework as independent confirmation. This does not impugn CRC's
structural core — the closure and Unity-Point claims stand or fall on their own — but any
convergence argument that leans on the AI-consciousness material should quarantine it from
the human-neuroscience evidence and treat OLIORC-2 as an *application* of CRC, not as
support for it. FMT's own AI-relevant claims (that a system meeting the theory's sufficient
conjunction should be conscious, and that a naturalistically acquired self-label that stays
attached to the self under efferent disconnection of the self region would be the strongest
available evidence; Gruber, 2026, §3.4.3) are stated as predictions, and should meet the
same quarantine.

## Why the convergence matters

Consciousness science does not yet have a standard model, but it has begun to accumulate
*convergent constraints*: several independent frameworks — FMT among them, and now, on this
reading, CRC — keep arriving at self-referential closure plus open-ended computation as
necessary conditions. When frameworks built from different starting materials (a sleep-and-
NREM structural analysis for Olinyk; a 2×2 taxonomy of model kinds with a real/virtual
ontology for FMT) land on the same load-bearing structure, that structure is more plausibly
a constraint on any adequate theory than an artifact of one formalism. The two disagreements
that remain — architectural vs. computational threshold, and world-model vs. sensory input —
are exactly the kind of sharp, answerable questions a maturing field should want. I would
welcome Walter Olinyk's response to this note, and a conversation about whether CRC's
Objective Data and FMT's explicit world-model are, in the end, the same thing.
