## A2 — §7.2 unfolding + §4.4 substrate independence (Basri, BarAvi & Hemmo 2026)

`10.1016/j.concog.2026.104121`, *Conscious Cogn*, online 2026-09-08. **Read: abstract, highlights,
introduction, section openings and reference list** (pasted by MG S323). Sections 3 and 5, where the
arguments are carried out in detail, are unread, so anything below that depends on their detailed proof is
marked.

**What they argue (their words where quoted):**
(i) The unfolding argument is unsound: Premise 2 rests on the universal approximation theorems, which hold
on compact domains and *"fall short of establishing that the approximations in question are relevant for our
cognitive functions"*.
(ii) The substitution argument cannot be escaped by *"looking inside the brain"* for **any functionalist**
theory, because by the Putnam / Hemmo–Shenker theorem *"any (complex enough) physical system … implements at
once infinitely many formal structures"*; only a reductive identity theory ("Flat Physicalism") escapes.
(iii) They survey two classes of reply to Putnam — added constraints that single out one computation, or all
computations on a par — and cite the constraint accounts (Anderson & Piccinini 2024, "robust mapping";
Hemmo & Shenker 2022, "singling out a computation"). **So the constraint reply FMT would give is one they
have already engaged; how they rebut it is in the unread part.**

**Proposed paragraph, §7.2, after l.869:**
> Basri, BarAvi and Hemmo (2026) reject the unfolding argument's second premise: the approximation theorems
> guarantee a feedforward match only on compact domains, which does not show that the approximation holds
> for a system whose outputs depend on an open-ended input history. On their reading the argument does not
> reach the Four-Model Theory, and the resource-bound reply above becomes a second, independent answer. They
> also raise a harder objection against every functionalist theory: by Putnam's theorem as generalised by
> Hemmo and Shenker, a sufficiently complex physical system implements indefinitely many formal structures
> at once, so no measurement of the brain fixes which function it realises. The theory answers with a
> constraint on which structure counts. The function it identifies with consciousness is the one the system
> itself runs on its own states: the self-model is read, and its output changes the system's subsequent
> dynamics and behaviour (Section 3.4.3). A mapping under which that feedback makes no counterfactual
> difference to the system is not an implementation of the architecture, whatever formal structure it
> assigns (Chalmers, 1996b). This places the theory among the constraint-based responses to Putnam's
> theorem, which Basri et al. dispute, and it rests on that dispute's outcome.

**§4.4:** one pointer sentence after the multiple-realizability footnote: *"For the objection that physics
underdetermines which function a system realises, see Section 7.2."*

**New reference (verified S323):** Chalmers, D.J. (1996). Does a rock implement every finite-state
automaton? *Synthese*, 108(3), 309–333. `10.1007/BF00413692` — would be *Chalmers 1996b*, since the paper
already cites *The Conscious Mind* (1996).

⚠ **Before this goes in:** the last two sentences are only as strong as their unread rebuttal of the
constraint class. If you buy the PDF, I check Section 5 against this paragraph first.

computation and optimal representations"*. Per the sweep agent (full text read): a geometric reservoir
property (neighbourhood separation) is proven necessary and sufficient for **universal approximation**;
numerically it peaks at input-conditioned λ_max ≈ 0⁻. No theorem ties it to marginal stability, and the

**Proposed addition to L2 item (v), `fmt-formalization.md:307`, after the Zenil sentence:**
> The nearest proven result of L2's shape concerns approximation universality in driven reservoirs: a
> geometric separation property is necessary and sufficient for universal approximation, and numerically
> it peaks at the edge of stability (Roig, Muñoz, & Morales, 2026). What remains open is a theorem that the
> property forces the maximal Lyapunov exponent to zero, and an extension from approximation to
> computational universality.

**Proposed:**
