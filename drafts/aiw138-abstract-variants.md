# FMT v14 abstract — two versions to compare

Both encode the settled three-principle structure. Both obey the standing rules: two *kinds* never four
modules; one-directional criticality; honest convergence; closure→phenomenality flagged as asserted,
not derived; no fixed prediction count presented as the claim.

Current v13 abstract for reference: `paper/full/four-model-theory-full.md` line 15 (~330 words).

**The choice is strategic, not stylistic.** A signals *formal theory* and is aimed at the readers who
decide whether FMT is rigorous — Kleiner, Blum, the AMCS/formal wing. B signals *theory of
consciousness* and is aimed at readers who want to know what it is about before what it assumes —
which is most neuroscience and philosophy reviewers, and both NoC guest editors.

## Version A — principles lead (≈305 words)

Three principles generate the Four-Model Theory. First, open-ended computation allows free modelling;
in neural tissue this appears as near-criticality, which is the signature the property leaves in a
substrate rather than the requirement itself. Second, recurrent non-linear dynamics of sufficient
complexity host a second computational level — persistent activity patterns that survive their own
inputs, interact, and compute over one another, individuated by their mutual effects rather than by
the cells that carry them. Third, phenomenology is a real, physical effect on that level, arising
where the modelling closes on itself: where the self-model enters the update rule of the world-model.

The architecture follows. Any system that models both world and self, at both the substrate and the
simulation level, instantiates four model kinds along two axes — scope (world vs. self) and mode
(implicit vs. explicit). Four is the floor rather than the ceiling: a minimum the principles force,
not a posited set of modules. Qualia are constitutive properties of the computational level,
incoherent at the substrate level as a spreadsheet cell's value is incoherent at the transistor level,
which reframes the Hard Problem as a category error that seeks phenomenal properties where they do not
exist. That closure constitutes phenomenality is argued for and not derived (Section 3.4.3).

Redirection of the modelling resource, variable implicit–explicit permeability and forking of the
self-model are consequences rather than axioms, and through them the architecture accounts for
psychedelic phenomenology, anaesthetic mechanisms, dream and lucid-dream states, split-brain
phenomena, dissociative identity disorder and animal consciousness. Several claims derived from the
theory's 2015 axioms have since received converging independent support (Xu et al., 2024; Hengen &
Shew, 2025; Algom & Shriki, 2026), though that convergence concerns the computational requirement and
is shared with other criticality-based frameworks. The theory's novel predictions — among them that
psychedelics should alleviate anosognosia, and that ego-dissolution content tracks the dominant
sensory input — remain largely untested.

## Version B — the claim leads, principles as the compression (≈310 words)

Consciousness, on the Four-Model Theory, is constituted by ongoing self-simulation: a system builds a
model of itself, relates to that model, and interacts with it. Models divide along two axes — scope
(world vs. self) and mode (implicit vs. explicit) — yielding four model kinds rather than four
modules. The implicit models are substrate-level, learned and non-conscious; the explicit models are
virtual, transient and phenomenal. Qualia are constitutive properties of the computational level,
incoherent at the substrate level as a spreadsheet cell's value is incoherent at the transistor level,
which reframes the Hard Problem as a category error that seeks phenomenal properties where they do not
exist.

The framework rests on three principles. Open-ended computation allows free modelling, and
near-criticality is the signature that property leaves in neural tissue rather than the requirement
itself. Recurrent non-linear dynamics of sufficient complexity host a second computational level:
persistent activity patterns individuated by their mutual effects rather than by the cells that carry
them. Phenomenology is a real, physical effect on that level, arising where the modelling closes on
itself — where the self-model enters the update rule of the world-model. That this closure constitutes
phenomenality is argued for and not derived (Section 3.4.3). The four kinds are then a minimum the
architecture forces rather than a posit: four is the floor, not the ceiling.

Redirection of the modelling resource, variable permeability and forking of the self-model follow as
consequences, and through them the theory accounts for psychedelic phenomenology, anaesthetic
mechanisms, dream and lucid-dream states, split-brain phenomena, dissociative identity disorder and
animal consciousness. Several claims derived from the theory's 2015 axioms have since received
converging independent support (Xu et al., 2024; Hengen & Shew, 2025; Algom & Shriki, 2026), though
that convergence concerns the computational requirement and is shared with other criticality-based
frameworks. Novel predictions — among them that psychedelics should alleviate anosognosia — remain
largely untested.

## What changed from v13 in both

- "five principles: criticality, virtual qualia, a redirectable self-model, variable permeability, and
  simulation forking" → three principles, with the other three named as consequences.
- "Combined with a criticality requirement" → criticality demoted to signature throughout.
- The four kinds are explicitly derived rather than announced — §3.2's "principled minimum" surfaced
  into the abstract for the first time.
- The convergence caveat now says *what* the convergence is about (the computational requirement),
  which is more specific and more honest than v13's "shared with competing criticality-based
  frameworks".
- v13's "The theory yields five novel predictions … four remain untested and the fifth …" is gone.
  Committing to a count in the abstract makes the count the claim; `prediction-framing.md` forbids it.

## Recommendation

**B.** A reads as though the theory is primarily a formal system, and FMT's five desk rejections were
not for lack of formalism — they were, per AIW-106, format and affiliation. An abstract that opens on
three abstract principles gives a hostile editor a faster exit than one that opens on consciousness.
B keeps the three principles fully visible in paragraph two, where they do the same work without
being the first thing an editor decides whether to care about.

Use A only if the venue is explicitly formal — the JAIC slice or a mathematics-of-consciousness outlet.
