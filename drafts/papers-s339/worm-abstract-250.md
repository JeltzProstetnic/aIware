Computational theories of conscious experience face a dilemma. Without a counterfactual condition on implementation, triviality arguments in the style of Putnam and Searle make every open system implement every computation. With such a condition, Maudlin's Olympia makes idle machinery matter to what is experienced. This paper defends a third exit for a conditional claim: if a simulated organism has experience, a static record of its whole trajectory has the same experience. Three conditions are kept apart. A trajectory is *realized* when its states are present in some medium and can be recovered one by one by a decoder that never computes one state from another. A realized trajectory has *internal dependency structure* when a rule much shorter than the trajectory relates its successive states; this structural relation is defended against Barnes's causal-history condition. Whether such a structure has an inside is decided by a candidate architecture, here the closure of self-model and world-model proposed by the Four-Model Theory. Triviality is answered without counterfactuals: a mapping that does any work is itself an object in the world, and algorithmic information records where a trajectory is stored, so the pixie a Putnam mapping finds in a rock is stored in the mapping. The conditional has a definite denial. A record and a run hold the same states and relations, so if the record lacks experience, what it lacks is a physical property of actual unfolding, and which horn holds depends on whether physical time involves genuine becoming.
