**v2 (2026-10-19)**

Supersedes v1 (2026-10-19).

*Corrections from a cross-check of all FMT publications.* A cross-check of the FMT corpus against the master paper and against itself led to these corrections:
- Definition of consciousness (Section 2). The paper said that FMT defines consciousness as a capacity. It now gives the master's definition: consciousness is the ongoing process in which a system runs a model of itself, relates that model to itself and interacts with it, and the capacity for that process is named separately. On the old wording a dormant robot that had the capacity would have counted as conscious.
- Model kinds (Section 2). "Two kinds of models, of the world and of the self" now reads "two scopes of model, the world and the self", the master's term for that dimension.
- Sufficient, not necessary (Section 8). The conditions for consciousness are now stated as a sufficient conjunction, with suffering needing one condition more, and each condition is described as a place where the conjunction could be left unmet. The embodiment paragraph adds that FMT does not make embodiment necessary for consciousness to exist.
- Cost window (Section 3). Closure is an efficiency advantage "within a cost window", where the paper said "fit window", a term the master does not define.
- References. The Four-Model Theory is cited by its concept DOI (10.5281/zenodo.18669891) instead of a pinned version, and the entries for Hu, Lin, & Lipson (2025) and Kuehn & Haddadin (2017) now carry volume, issue and page numbers.

No ask, argument or conclusion changed. The abstract is unchanged.
