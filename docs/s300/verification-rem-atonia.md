## (b) What each citation actually establishes

### Mechanism

- **Valencia Garcia et al. 2018 (Nat Commun).** Causal, not correlational. Viral knockdown of vGAT in
  GABA/glycine neurons of the **ventromedial medulla** in rats produced REM sleep without atonia plus
  abnormal, vigorous motor activity — head, fore/hindlimb, tail and snout movements, "seeking food
  with the snout", "trying to run or jump". Crucially the rats **kept their eyes closed**, i.e. they
  remained asleep. This is the cleanest "atonia is a separable, targetable mechanism" evidence
  available. Cite this if you need one modern reference for the circuit.
- **Valencia Garcia et al. 2017 (Brain).** The upstream half: genetic inactivation of glutamate
  neurons in the **sublaterodorsal tegmental nucleus (SLD)** recapitulates RBD. Together with the
  2018 paper this establishes the SLD(glutamate) → ventromedial medulla(glycine/GABA) → motoneuron
  chain.
- **Brooks & Peever 2012 (J Neurosci).** The receptor-level answer, and it corrects a common
  oversimplification: atonia is **not** glycine alone. Blocking glycine receptors is insufficient.
  REM paralysis is reversed only when motoneurons are cut off from **GABA_B *and* GABA_A *and*
  glycine** receptor-mediated inhibition. If the paper says "glycinergic inhibition", that is
  incomplete — say "glycinergic and GABAergic".
- **Arrigoni, Chen & Fuller 2016 (J Physiol).** Best single dedicated review of atonia specifically
  (anatomy, cells, synapses). Preferred over the general REM reviews if the citation is about atonia
  rather than REM at large.
- **Peever & Fuller 2017 (Curr Biol).** General, highly readable REM review; good for a broad
  "modern account" citation. Read directly. p. R1240 states the atonia generator was localised by
  Jouvet to dorsal pontine brainstem, later refined to the SLD (also termed subcoeruleus), whose
  ventral portion contains spinally projecting glutamatergic neurons that produce atonia by
  activating inhibitory interneurons in the ventral medulla and spinal cord.
- **Luppi 2025 (J Sleep Res).** Most recent network-level review. Paywalled — cited from metadata,
  see UNVERIFIED.

**Classic vs modern, stated cleanly for the paper:** the *classic descriptive* work is Jouvet's
1960s lesion series (atonia exists, and a pontine lesion abolishes it). The *modern circuit-level*
account is the SLD→ventromedial-medulla→motoneuron pathway with combined glycinergic/GABAergic
inhibition, established by targeted genetic/pharmacological manipulation (Brooks & Peever 2012;
Valencia Garcia et al. 2017, 2018; reviewed by Arrigoni et al. 2016). Do not cite Jouvet for the
mechanism — cite him for the phenomenon and the dissociation.

### Jouvet

- **Jouvet & Delorme 1965.** Bilateral symmetrical mediodorsal pontine tegmental ("peri-locus
  coeruleus alpha") electrolytic lesions in cats → permanent loss of REM atonia, with complex motor
  behaviour appearing during REM. The term *oneiric behaviour* originates here.
- **Sastre & Jouvet 1979.** The behavioural catalogue. Repertoire consists of **species-typical**
  behaviours: orienting/head-raising, exploration, predatory-attack behaviour, rage and fear
  displays, and grooming. **Attack was the most frequent complex behaviour. Sexual behaviour was
  never observed.** Note the cats explored *blindly* and, in grooming sequences, licked the cage or
  the floor rather than themselves.
- **⚠ What was actually observed — the overstatement guard.** What is observed is: species-typical
  motor patterns emitted during REM sleep, with eyes closed and without appropriate engagement of the
  real environment. What is **not** observed is any dream content. "The cats acted out their dreams"
  is an *interpretation*; the data are motor patterns released by disinhibition. The paper must state
  only the former. Note also that Peever & Fuller (2017) attach the "act out their dreams" sentence
  to a citation of **Jouvet & Michel 1960** (*C R Soc Biol* 154, 636–641), not to the 1965 lesion
  paper — an example of exactly this citation slippage in the secondary literature.
- **Hendricks, Morrison & Mann 1982 — the decisive corrective, and it matters to the argument.**
  Across 25 cats, the behaviour released depends on **lesion site**, not on dream content: small
  lesions gave only unorganised limb/head movements; lesions near the superior colliculus gave
  orienting behaviour; rostroventral midbrain or amygdalar damage gave violent attack-like behaviour;
  larger ventral lesions gave coordinated locomotion. Their own conclusion is quotable and directly
  on point: *"PS without atonia is not simply a state during which neural activity of normal PS can
  be expressed behaviorally."* In other words, the classic cat preparation is **not** a clean readout
  of an otherwise-untouched simulation — the lesion damages more than the atonia switch. If the paper
  leans on Jouvet's cats for separability, this citation must appear alongside, or a reviewer who
  knows the literature will use it against the argument.

### RBD

- **Schenck et al. 1986.** The founding description. Five patients (four men aged 67–72, one woman
  aged 60). PSG documented variable loss of chin atonia, greatly increased limb twitching, increased
  REM ocular density; no seizure activity. Videotaped behaviours: stereotyped hand motions, reaching
  and searching gestures, punches, kicks. Clonazepam and desipramine suppressed the behaviours, with
  relapse on withdrawal. Establishes RBD as a distinct parasomnia category.
- **ICSD-3 / ICSD-3-TR.** Diagnostic criteria: repeated episodes of sleep-related vocalisation and/or
  complex motor behaviour, documented by PSG to occur during REM sleep (or, by history, dream
  enactment) **plus** PSG evidence of REM sleep without atonia. ICSD-3 = 2014; text revision = 2023.
  For an academic paper prefer Dauvilliers et al. 2018 as the citable source, with ICSD as secondary.
- **Postuma et al. 2019 (Brain).** The prodromal headline number. 1280 polysomnographically confirmed
  iRBD patients, 24 centres, mean follow-up 4.6 y. **Conversion to an overt neurodegenerative
  syndrome: 6.3% per year; 73.5% converted by 12 years.** Predictors (hazard ratios) include
  quantitative motor testing 3.16, motor exam 3.03, olfactory deficit 2.62, MCI 1.91–2.37, abnormal
  DAT scan 1.98, REM atonia loss 1.54. This is the strongest single citation for "RBD is a genuine
  brainstem-circuit failure, not a behavioural curiosity."
- **Iranzo et al. 2013 (Lancet Neurol).** Post-mortem pathology in iRBD — the histological
  confirmation of synucleinopathy. Use alongside Postuma when the claim is pathological rather than
  epidemiological.
- **Schenck et al. 1996 / 2013.** The original longitudinal signal (38% at ~13 years) and its 16-year
  update (**81%**, 21/26). Historically important; superseded in statistical power by Postuma 2019.
- **Oudiette et al. 2009.** Corrective to the violence stereotype: non-violent, elaborate behaviours
  also occur in RBD. Relevant because the clinical picture is shaped by which episodes get reported.

## (c) Content vs expression — the answer

**Question:** when atonia fails, is the dream content itself altered, or only its motor expression?

**Short answer:** The literature does **not** cleanly support "content is unchanged." It splits, and
the split tracks *how the dreams were sampled*. Retrospective/clinical-interview sampling finds large
content differences; prospective laboratory awakening and diary sampling mostly finds none. The
project's separability claim survives, but only in its weaker form, and it must be stated with the
sampling caveat rather than asserted.

### Evidence that content differs

- **Fantini et al. 2005** (49 iRBD vs 71 controls). Dreams with at least one aggressive episode:
  **66% vs 15%** (p < 0.00001). Aggression/friendliness ratio **86% vs 44%** (p < 0.0001). Animal
  characters **19% vs 4%** (p = 0.0001). Sexual content **0% vs 9%** (p < 0.0001). Critically, the
  same patients were **not** more aggressive awake — they scored *lower* on the physical
  aggressiveness subscale (16.5 ± 6.4 vs 20.4 ± 8.3, p = 0.034). This is the most-cited pro-difference
  finding.
- **Uguccioni et al. 2013.** RBD dreams more complex, less bizarre, more aggressive than
  sleepwalking/sleep-terror dreams; 75% of RBD subjects counterattacked when assaulted in the dream.
  ⚠ The comparison group is **sleepwalkers, not healthy controls** — this does not answer the
  atonia question and should not be cited as if it did.
- **Jigalin & Schredl 2025.** Prospective lab dreams: aggression **19.44% (RBD) vs 5.58% (controls)**.
  But: 36 RBD dreams vs 124 control dreams, and the authors themselves label it an exploratory
  finding possibly reflecting small sample size. They also found retrospective dream recall higher in
  RBD but **no prospective difference in recall** — their own interpretation is that RBD patients
  preferentially recall the dreams tied to enacted episodes and awakenings.

### Dissent — evidence that content does NOT differ

- **D'Agostino et al. 2012**, titled *"Challenging the myth of REM sleep behavior disorder."*
  129 dream reports (77 from 12 iRBD patients, 52 from 12 controls), analysed for both form and
  content. Result, verbatim: **"No statistically significant differences were found between the
  dreams of iRBD patients and those of normal controls in any of the applied measures."** The only
  difference was in *wakefulness* (higher passivity in iRBD). ⚠ Caveat the authors state: over 80%
  of patients were on treatment during collection.
- **Valli et al. 2015.** Sleep-laboratory dream reports, PD with RBD (9 patients, 37 reports) vs PD
  without RBD (6 patients, 32 reports); 563 codable dream elements. Verbatim: **"There were no
  significant between-group differences in the number or distribution of elements reflecting
  action-filledness or vividness, emotional valence or threats."** Within-group only, the RBD group
  had more negative than positive dreams (p = 0.012) and a non-significant trend toward more intense
  actions (p = 0.066). This is the cleanest design in the set for the atonia question, because both
  groups have PD — the RBD/no-RBD contrast isolates atonia loss rather than neurodegeneration.
- **Oudiette et al. 2009.** Non-violent elaborate behaviours (eating, smoking-type sequences,
  everyday acts) also occur in RBD. The "violent dreams" picture is partly a selection artifact:
  violent episodes are the ones that injure a bed partner and reach the clinic.

### Synthesis for the project

1. **The apparent content difference is best explained as a recall and selection artifact, not as
   altered content.** Retrospective methods (Fantini 2005) yield large differences; prospective
   methods (D'Agostino 2012; Valli 2015) yield none. Enactment produces awakenings, awakenings
   produce recall, and violent enactment produces clinical referral. Jigalin & Schredl's 2025
   retrospective/prospective dissociation on *recall frequency* is direct evidence for this
   mechanism operating in this population.
2. **Therefore write the weaker claim, not the stronger one.** Defensible: *"There is no consistent
   evidence that dream content differs when atonia fails; the reported differences appear when dreams
   are sampled retrospectively and largely disappear under prospective laboratory sampling."*
   NOT defensible: *"Dream content is unchanged; only motor expression differs."* The 2025 prospective
   finding, weak as it is, blocks the absolute version.
3. **⚠ A separate confound the project should know about — this is the bigger threat to the
   argument than the dream-content data.** RBD patients are **not** a clean "atonia removed,
   simulation intact" preparation. RBD is a prodromal synucleinopathy: 6.3%/year phenoconversion,
   73.5% at 12 years (Postuma 2019), with post-mortem synuclein pathology (Iranzo 2013). These
   patients have progressive brainstem and, in many, cortical and cognitive degeneration. Any content
   difference could be caused by the neurodegeneration rather than by the atonia loss. The same
   applies to the cat lesion model, where Hendricks et al. 1982 showed the released behaviour tracks
   lesion site. **The clean separability evidence is the targeted-manipulation animal work**
   (Valencia Garcia 2018: knock down one inhibitory population, atonia goes, animal stays asleep) —
   which is clean precisely because no one can ask a rat what it dreamt. Separability of the
   *mechanism* is well established; separability of *mechanism from content* is supported but not
   directly demonstrated in any single preparation. Say so.
4. **Valli 2015 is the best single citation for the separability claim**, because the PD-with-RBD vs
   PD-without-RBD design controls for neurodegeneration. Pair it with D'Agostino 2012, and cite
   Fantini 2005 as the contrary finding rather than omitting it.
