# AIW-280 literature check: "Too Late to Report"

Prepared 2026-10-20 for the first draft of paper 4 (`drafts/papers-moc7/minimal-self.md`, brief in `docs/papers-moc7/minimal-self-brief.md`).

**Claim under test (FMT, MG S334).** The minimal self is the basic, non-recursive explicit self-model (ESM) resting on a sentience floor. A first-person report is produced through the ESM plus memory plus language, so every report is a post-reflective reconstruction and never a readout of the minimal self. The paper must also say what reports can still deliver.

## 1. Zahavi: minimal self, pre-reflective self-awareness, for-me-ness

**Zahavi, D. (1999).** *Self-Awareness and Alterity: A Phenomenological Investigation.* Evanston, IL: Northwestern University Press. (No DOI; record confirmed in OpenAlex and by reviews in *Husserl Studies* 2001, doi:10.1023/a:1010661206111.)
Zahavi's first systematic defence of pre-reflective self-awareness against reflection models of self-consciousness, drawing on Husserl, Sartre and the Heidelberg School. It sets up the regress argument: if self-awareness required a reflecting act, that act would need its own reflection. *Bears on the claim:* supplies the definition the paper's first step relies on. **Supports** (definitional premise).

**Zahavi, D. (2005).** *Subjectivity and Selfhood: Investigating the First-Person Perspective.* Cambridge, MA: MIT Press. doi:10.7551/mitpress/6541.001.0001
Defends a minimal, experiential self identified with the first-personal givenness ("for-me-ness") of experience, which is not an extra content but the way experience is given. The book also discusses the classical objection, going back to Natorp, that reflection objectifies and so cannot disclose lived experience as it was lived, and answers it on Husserl's behalf: reflection modifies, but it discloses something that was already there, and the modification can itself be noticed **(summary not checked against text this session; locate the chapter and pages before citing)**. *Bears on the claim:* gives the definition (pre-reflective = not taken as object) and also the best phenomenological reply to the paper. The paper's own move, "reflection presupposes the minimal self but cannot describe it faithfully", has to be set against Zahavi's "reflection discloses with modification". **Supports** the premise; **threatens** the conclusion.

**Zahavi, D. (2014).** *Self and Other: Exploring Subjectivity, Empathy, and Shame.* Oxford: Oxford University Press. doi:10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199590681.001.0001
Part I restates the experiential minimal self and answers critics who deny that for-me-ness is ubiquitous (including the absorbed-coping objection associated with Dreyfus and Schear). *Bears on the claim:* the critics he answers argue partly from what reflection adds; the paper should cite his reply alongside them. **Constrains**.

**Zahavi, D. (2017).** Thin, thinner, thinnest: defining the minimal self. In C. Durt, T. Fuchs & C. Tewes (eds.), *Embodiment, Enaction, and Culture*. MIT Press. doi:10.7551/mitpress/9780262035552.003.0010
A short reply to Ratcliffe and to Ciaunica and Fotopoulou, defending the experiential minimal self and rejecting the proposal that it is interpersonally constituted. *Bears on the claim:* fixes what Zahavi means by "minimal" at its thinnest, which is the target the paper must hit. **Supports** (scoping).

**Zahavi, D. (2018).** Consciousness, self-consciousness, selfhood: a reply to some critics. *Review of Philosophy and Psychology* 9, 703–718. doi:10.1007/s13164-018-0403-6
Reply in the *RPP* exchange to Guillot, Howell & Thompson and others who doubt that for-me-ness is a phenomenal feature **(summary not checked against text this session)**. *Bears on the claim:* the paper's "reports cut both ways" position (reports can neither establish nor refute ubiquitous for-me-ness) needs this reply cited, since both sides of that exchange argue largely from what reflection finds. **Constrains**.

**Zahavi, D., & Kriegel, U. (2016).** For-me-ness: what it is and what it is not. In D. Dahlstrom, A. Elpidorou & W. Hopp (eds.), *Philosophy of Mind and Phenomenology*, Routledge, 36–53. (Verified in the brief; PhilArchive ZAHFWI.)
Already in the brief's table; listed here because it is the cleanest statement that for-me-ness is the subjective character of experience, not an item in it. **Supports** (definitional premise).

**Frank, M. (2022).** In defence of pre-reflective self-consciousness: the Heidelberg view. *Review of Philosophy and Psychology* 13, 277–293. doi:10.1007/s13164-022-00619-z
Separates egological self-consciousness, which involves concepts and the awareness of oneself as the "I" of a state, from anonymous pre-reflective self-consciousness, which has no subject–object structure and cannot be described as a representation or acquaintance relation. The pre-reflective variety founds the egological one. *Bears on the claim:* Frank's two varieties line up with the paper's two levels (floor/basic ESM versus reflective ESM), and his claim that the founding layer has no subject–object structure is a direct statement of why a report, which needs that structure, must convert it. **Supports**; useful as a Heidelberg ally for the "presupposes but cannot describe" reply (R6 in the brief).

## 2. Gallagher: minimal versus narrative self, ownership and agency

**Gallagher, S. (2000).** Philosophical conceptions of the self: implications for cognitive science. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 4(1), 14–21. doi:10.1016/s1364-6613(99)01417-5
Introduces the minimal self (immediate, pre-reflective, unextended in time) against the narrative self (extended, partly constituted by stories), and splits the minimal self into sense of ownership and sense of agency, which dissociate in schizophrenic thought insertion. *Bears on the claim:* the narrative self is by definition a product of memory and language, which is the machinery the paper says every report runs through. A report thus has the format of the narrative self even when its topic is the minimal self. **Supports**.

**Gallagher, S. (2012).** Multiple aspects in the sense of agency. *New Ideas in Psychology* 30(1), 15–31. doi:10.1016/j.newideapsych.2010.03.003
Distinguishes a pre-reflective, first-order sense of agency from reflective, higher-order attributions of agency, and argues that experimental measures often capture the second while claiming the first **(summary not checked against text this session)**. *Bears on the claim:* an author on the phenomenological side already concedes that measured agency reports are frequently reflective attributions. **Supports**.

**Synofzik, M., Vosgerau, G., & Newen, A. (2008).** Beyond the comparator model: a multifactorial two-step account of agency. *Consciousness and Cognition* 17(1), 219–239. doi:10.1016/j.concog.2007.03.010 (online 2007)
Splits the sense of agency into a non-conceptual feeling of agency and a conceptual judgement of agency, which is interpretive and draws on beliefs, context and post-hoc cues. *Bears on the claim:* gives the agency case of the paper's thesis an established empirical vocabulary: any verbal or rating report of agency is a judgement, and the feeling is reached only through implicit measures (e.g. intentional binding). **Supports**, and fixes terminology the draft should adopt.

**Baggio, G., & Gallagher, S. (2026).** Minimal self-sentience. *Synthese* 208. doi:10.1007/s11229-026-05742-3
Extends minimal pre-reflective self-awareness to non-human animals as "minimal self-sentience": a reflexive, automatic, affectively valenced bodily responsiveness through which an organism experiences its own interactions before any higher-order cognition or reflective act. Criticises Humphrey's and Seth's accounts for placing the threshold too high or too neurocentrically. *Bears on the claim:* this is the nearest published counterpart to FMT's sentience floor beneath the basic ESM, and it is new (2026). The draft must cite it and say where FMT's floor differs (FMT ties the floor to a rudimentary closure; Baggio and Gallagher tie it to bodily responsiveness). **Supports** the two-layer picture; a priority question for the "minimal self = ESM + floor" formulation.

## 3. Petitmengin and micro-phenomenology

**Petitmengin, C. (2006).** Describing one's subjective experience in the second person: an interview method for the science of consciousness. *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 5, 229–269. doi:10.1007/s11097-006-9022-2
The micro-phenomenological interview: stabilise attention on one singular past episode, guide evocation (re-living) checked by linguistic and non-verbal markers, ask about the how rather than the what or why, use content-empty questions. Aims at the pre-reflective, procedural layer of experience. *Bears on the claim:* the method's core step, evocation, is a re-simulation of the episode under reflective attention, which is the paper's step three. **Threatens** (best method) and **supports** (its own description concedes re-living).

**Petitmengin, C., & Bitbol, M. (2009).** The validity of first-person descriptions as authenticity and coherence. *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 16(10–12). (HAL hal-00434456; page range still unresolved as recorded in the brief.)
Replies to the introspection critiques from Comte to Nisbett and Wilson and recasts validity as authenticity and coherence rather than correspondence with an independent fact. *Bears on the claim:* if validity is not correspondence, micro-phenomenology cannot settle correspondence claims about the structure of the minimal self. **Constrains** the method in the paper's favour.

**Petitmengin, C., Remillieux, A., Cahour, B., & Carter-Thomas, S. (2013).** A gap in Nisbett and Wilson's findings? A first-person access to our cognitive processes. *Consciousness and Cognition* 22(2), 654–669. doi:10.1016/j.concog.2013.02.004
Repeats the choice-blindness protocol; participants guided by a micro-phenomenological interview detect the swap in most cases where unassisted participants do not. The authors conclude that people can reach their decision processes through specific mental acts. *Bears on the claim:* the strongest single result for trained report. What it shows is detection of a mismatch between two pieces of world content (which face), which the reflective ESM handles well. **Threatens**; answered only by the content/self-structure distinction and the proposed minimal-self-blindness test.

**Petitmengin, C., & Lachaux, J.-P. (2013).** Microcognitive science: bridging experiential and neuronal microdynamics. *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 7, 617. doi:10.3389/fnhum.2013.00617
Proposes pairing disciplined elicitation of sub-second experiential dynamics with intracranial EEG, so that experiential and neural micro-dynamics can be matched at comparable grain. *Bears on the claim:* a model of how reports earn their keep, by being anchored to an independent neural measure. **Supports** the "what reports can deliver" section (reports as hypothesis generators, calibrated externally).

**Petitmengin, C., Remillieux, A., & Valenzuela-Moguillansky, C. (2019).** Discovering the structures of lived experience: towards a micro-phenomenological analysis method. *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 18, 691–730. doi:10.1007/s11097-018-9597-4 (verified in the brief)
The analysis method for extracting generic diachronic and synchronic structures from interview sets. *Bears on the claim:* the structures it extracts are structures of the reported episodes; whether they are structures of the minimal self is the question at issue. **Constrains**.

**Tewes, C. (2023).** Micro-phenomenology as experientially based access to consciousness. *Journal für Psychologie* 31(1), 239–263. doi:10.30820/0942-2285-2023-1-239
Asks whether verbalising experience in a micro-phenomenological interview is only construction or a methodological transformation that still gives intersubjective access to the experience, and whether the method reaches phenomenal experience given the mediation by interview, categorisation and evaluation. Proposes mixed-method integration. *Bears on the claim:* a sympathetic author posing the paper's question from inside the method. **Constrains**; cite as evidence that the question is live within the community.

**Alcaraz-Sánchez, A. (2021/2023).** Awareness in the void: a micro-phenomenological exploration of conscious dreamless sleep. *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 22, 867–905. doi:10.1007/s11097-021-09743-0
Five participants interviewed with the micro-phenomenological interview about objectless awareness during sleep; analysis yields dimensions including "self-perception" and "perception of absence". *Bears on the claim:* a direct case of the method applied to a state near FMT's floor, with self-perception reported from a state that, on FMT, held no reflective self-model. **Threatens** if taken at face value; the paper can use it as the clearest worked example of a post-reflective reconstruction being read as a description of the floor.

## 4. Reportability and the unreliability of introspection

**Nisbett, R. E., & Wilson, T. D. (1977).** Telling more than we can know: verbal reports on mental processes. *Psychological Review* 84(3), 231–259. doi:10.1037/0033-295x.84.3.231
People report the causes of their own responses by applying implicit causal theories, not by introspective access; reports are accurate when the real cause happens to be salient and plausible. *Bears on the claim:* empirical precedent that reports are theory-driven reconstructions. Its scope is cognitive processes, not the self-structure of experience. **Supports**, with limited scope.

**Schwitzgebel, E. (2008).** The unreliability of naive introspection. *Philosophical Review* 117(2), 245–273. doi:10.1215/00318108-2007-037
Argues that people are broadly inept, not just fallible at the margins, about their own current phenomenology, even under favourable conditions of extended reflection; examples include emotion, peripheral vision and the phenomenology of thought. *Bears on the claim:* supports the general point but targets naive introspection, which micro-phenomenologists set aside. **Supports**; does not by itself answer the trained-report reply.

**Schwitzgebel, E. (2011).** *Perplexities of Consciousness.* Cambridge, MA: MIT Press. doi:10.7551/mitpress/8243.001.0001
Book-length case studies (dream colour, echolocation, visual perspective, inner speech) showing that introspective reports track cultural metaphors and theoretical expectation. *Bears on the claim:* evidence that the vocabulary available to the reporter shapes the report, which the paper attributes to the serialisation stage. **Supports**.

**Hurlburt, R. T., & Heavey, C. L. (2001).** Telling what we know: describing inner experience. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 5(9), 400–403. doi:10.1016/s1364-6613(00)01724-1
Argues against wholesale pessimism: with careful method (random sampling, brief delay, iterative interviews that bracket presuppositions), people can give trustworthy descriptions of momentary inner experience **(summary not checked against text this session)**. *Bears on the claim:* DES shortens the memory stage but still requires taking the moment as an object. **Constrains** (concede DES's gains on content and timing).

**Hurlburt, R. T., & Akhter, S. A. (2006).** The Descriptive Experience Sampling method. *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 5, 271–301. doi:10.1007/s11097-006-9024-0 (verified in the brief)
The method paper for DES. Listed for completeness; same bearing as above.

**Johansson, P., Hall, L., Sikström, S., & Olsson, A. (2005).** Failure to detect mismatches between intention and outcome in a simple decision task. *Science* 310(5745), 116–119. doi:10.1126/science.1111709
Participants choosing the more attractive of two faces mostly fail to notice when handed the other one, and give introspectively derived reasons for a choice they did not make. *Bears on the claim:* the ESM produces fluent reasons for states it did not have, and those reasons read like reports of the real choice. **Supports** (serialisation and confabulation stages).

**Schooler, J. W. (2002).** Re-representing consciousness: dissociations between experience and meta-consciousness. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 6(8), 339–344. doi:10.1016/s1364-6613(02)01949-6
Distinguishes experience from meta-consciousness, the intermittent re-representation of experience that makes it explicitly known. Two dissociations follow: temporal (one can be in a state, e.g. mind-wandering, without meta-awareness of it) and translation (re-representing experience, especially verbally, can distort it, as in verbal overshadowing) **(summary not checked against text this session; abstract not available through OpenAlex)**. *Bears on the claim:* the closest psychological statement of the paper's mechanism. Meta-consciousness is a re-representation, and reports come from the re-representation. **Supports**; also prior art (see the final section).

**Lambie, J. A., & Marcel, A. J. (2002).** Consciousness and the varieties of emotion experience: a theoretical framework. *Psychological Review* 109(2), 219–259. doi:10.1037/0033-295x.109.2.219
Proposes a two-level view in which first-order phenomenology is distinguished from second-order awareness, and explains "unconscious" emotion as phenomenology without awareness. Attention mode (detached versus immersed) and direction (self versus world) shape what is experienced. *Bears on the claim:* gives the paper an established psychological precedent for phenomenology that is present without being reflectively accessed, and for the claim that the attentional stance of reporting changes the experience. **Supports**.

**Overgaard, M., & Sandberg, K. (2012).** Kinds of access: different methods for report reveal different kinds of metacognitive access. *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 367(1594), 1287–1296. doi:10.1098/rstb.2011.0425
Different report formats (confidence ratings, direct experience descriptions) give different results on the same task, which suggests they draw on different kinds of access. *Bears on the claim:* the report is shaped by the reporting route, not just by the experience. **Supports**.

**Irvine, E. (2019/2021).** Developing dark pessimism towards the justificatory role of introspective reports. *Erkenntnis* 86, 1319–1344. doi:10.1007/s10670-019-00156-9
Treats introspective reports as measurements and argues they fail minimal conditions of measurement, so they cannot play a strong justificatory role in settling disputes in consciousness science, now or later. *Bears on the claim:* a measurement-theoretic route to the paper's "reports cannot settle structural disputes" conclusion. **Supports**.

**Morris, A. (2025).** Invisible gorillas in the mind: internal inattentional blindness and the prospect of introspection training. *Open Mind* 9, 606–634. doi:10.1162/opmi_a_00204
Argues that much apparently unconscious high-level cognition may be preconscious, missed through internal inattentional blindness, and would become reportable with attention; proposes a science of introspection training. *Bears on the claim:* the strongest recent case that training can expand what reports reach. The paper's reply is that attending to the minimal self is the level change, so it cannot be an instance of reaching a preconscious item. **Threatens**; cite and answer.

**Sperling, G. (1960).** The information available in brief visual presentations. *Psychological Monographs: General and Applied* 74(11), 1–29. doi:10.1037/h0093759
Whole report of a briefly flashed letter array yields about four items, but a cue after the display lets subjects report any row, showing that more was available briefly than full report shows. *Bears on the claim:* the report captures what survives selection and decay at report time, not the state that was there. **Supports** (and is the empirical basis of the overflow debate below).

**Block, N. (2007).** Consciousness, accessibility, and the mesh between psychology and neuroscience. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 30(5–6), 481–499. doi:10.1017/s0140525x07002786 (verified in the brief)
**Block, N. (2011).** Perceptual consciousness overflows cognitive access. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 15(12), 567–575. doi:10.1016/j.tics.2011.11.001
Block argues from Sperling-type and related data that phenomenal consciousness has more capacity than cognitive access, so reportable content underdetermines experience. *Bears on the claim:* overflow is the content-side analogue of the paper's claim; FMT's distinction between rank (capacity of the channel) and depth (recursion) lets the paper take a side. **Supports** the gap between experience and report; whether FMT endorses overflow must be decided before citing.

**Cohen, M. A., & Dennett, D. C. (2011).** Consciousness cannot be separated from function. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 15(8), 358–364. doi:10.1016/j.tics.2011.06.008
Argues that experiences in principle unreportable and functionally inert are scientifically unknowable, so a theory positing them is unfalsifiable. *Bears on the claim:* the floor is defined as unreportable, so this is the direct objection. FMT's answer is that the floor is not functionally inert and can be reached by third-person measures; the paper must say so explicitly. **Threatens**.

**Eagleman, D. M., & Sejnowski, T. J. (2000).** Motion integration and postdiction in visual awareness. *Science* 287(5460), 2036–2038. doi:10.1126/science.287.5460.2036
From flash-lag experiments, argues that the percept attributed to the moment of the flash depends on events in roughly the following 80 ms: awareness is postdictive. *Bears on the claim:* even at the sub-second scale, what is experienced as "then" is assembled after the fact. **Supports** (reconstruction begins before memory is involved).

**Herzog, M. H., Kammer, T., & Scharnowski, F. (2016).** Time slices: what is the duration of a percept? *PLOS Biology* 14(4), e1002433. doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.1002433
**Herzog, M. H., Drissi-Daoudi, L., & Doerig, A. (2020).** All in good time: long-lasting postdictive effects reveal discrete perception. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 24(10), 826–837. doi:10.1016/j.tics.2020.07.001
Review long-lasting postdictive effects (feature integration over hundreds of milliseconds) and argue for a two-stage model in which long periods of unconscious processing precede discrete conscious percepts. *Bears on the claim:* strengthens the postdiction point and supplies a time scale. **Supports**. Caution: if conscious percepts are discrete outputs, a reviewer may ask where a continuous minimal self sits in such a model.

**Tsuchiya, N., Wilke, M., Frässle, S., & Lamme, V. A. F. (2015).** No-report paradigms: extracting the true neural correlates of consciousness. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 19(12), 757–770. doi:10.1016/j.tics.2015.10.002
Argues that report confounds the search for neural correlates of consciousness, and that paradigms inferring conscious state from involuntary markers (eye movements, pupil) can separate the correlates of experience from those of report. *Bears on the claim:* the methodological template for the paper's "what to use instead" section. **Supports**. Pair with Overgaard & Fazekas (2016) and Block (2019), already verified in the brief, for the critiques.

## 5. Closest prior art (details in the final section)

**Schear, J. K. (2009).** Experience and self-consciousness. *Philosophical Studies* 144, 95–105. doi:10.1007/s11098-009-9381-y
Argues against the thesis that all experience involves pre-reflective self-consciousness, using absorbed, skilled activity; the sense that experience was "for me" may be supplied by the reflection that looks for it **(summary not checked against text this session)**. *Bears on the claim:* same mechanism as the paper's step one, used to doubt ubiquity. **Prior art**; the paper differs by refusing the sceptical conclusion as well.

**Howell, R. J., & Thompson, B. (2017).** Phenomenally mine: in search of the subjective character of consciousness. *Review of Philosophy and Psychology* 8, 103–127. doi:10.1007/s13164-016-0309-0
Argue that there is no distinctive phenomenal "mineness" detectable in experience and that subjective character is better treated non-phenomenally **(summary not checked against text this session)**. *Bears on the claim:* a deflationist position argued partly from what introspection does not find. **Constrains**; the paper's even-handed line applies to them as much as to Zahavi.

**Lane, T. (2012).** Toward an explanatory framework for mental ownership. *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 11, 251–286. doi:10.1007/s11097-012-9252-4
Argues from clinical cases (thought insertion, somatoparaphrenia) that the felt ownership of mental states depends on further processing rather than being intrinsic to the state **(summary not checked against text this session)**. *Bears on the claim:* if ownership is added by processing, ownership reports describe the added layer. **Supports**.

**Lane, T. J. (2020).** The minimal self hypothesis. *Consciousness and Cognition* 85, 103029. doi:10.1016/j.concog.2020.103029
Notes that the minimal self ("sheer for-me-ness") has been explicated by conceptual analysis and hermeneutic psychiatry, and proposes to make it an experimental concept anchored in intrinsic brain activity such as the default mode network. *Bears on the claim:* an independent call to study the minimal self by third-person means. **Supports** the "what to use instead" section.

**Deane, G., & Demekas, D. (2025).** Recursive self-models and minimal phenomenal experience. PsyArXiv preprint. doi:10.31234/osf.io/u8y6f_v1 (not peer reviewed)
Proposes a computational architecture in which a behavioural policy is recursively coupled to a model that synthesises explanatory programs about that behaviour; minimal phenomenal experience is what remains when narrative elaboration is minimal. *Bears on the claim:* a recursive self-model account of minimal experience close in spirit to FMT's recursion ladder. It does not make the report argument. **Neighbour**; cite as convergent, flag preprint status.

**Windt, J. M. (2015).** Just in time: dreamless sleep experience as pure subjective temporality. In T. Metzinger & J. M. Windt (eds.), *Open MIND*. Frankfurt am Main: MIND Group. doi:10.15502/9783958571174
Argues that methods for trusting dream reports extend to reports of dreamless sleep experience, and models such experience as pure subjective temporality, a candidate for minimal phenomenal experience. *Bears on the claim:* her floor (phenomenal now plus duration, no further content) is close to FMT's floor (the now plus primordial pain or nothingness). She is more optimistic about report than the paper. **Constrains/neighbour**.

## What first-person reports can still deliver

The literature supports a substantial positive list. The paper should state it plainly, since a reviewer from the micro-phenomenology community will check whether the concessions are real.

1. **Content of experience.** Trained reports recover what was seen, heard, felt and in what sequence, with enough accuracy to predict neural data (Lutz et al. 2002; Petitmengin & Lachaux 2013; Allen, MoC7 2026) and to detect content manipulations that untrained participants miss (Petitmengin et al. 2013).
2. **Procedural micro-dynamics.** The inner steps of a task, which participants cannot state unprompted, can be elicited (Petitmengin 2006), and the resulting categories can sort trials for neural analysis (Lutz et al. 2002).
3. **Hypotheses for third-person tests.** Front-loaded phenomenology generates hypotheses that are then tested by measures that do not depend on report (Gallagher 2003; Gallagher & Sørensen 2006; Allen's pre-registered design).
4. **Trial classification and contrast conditions.** Reports and ratings split trials into seen/unseen or present/absent so that neural or behavioural measures can be compared, provided the report format is chosen with its access route in mind (Overgaard & Sandberg 2012).
5. **Data about the reporting system itself.** How the reflective self-model represents its own lower layer, what vocabulary and theory it brings, and how that changes with training (Schwitzgebel 2011; Fox et al. 2012). This is a legitimate object of study in its own right, and on FMT it is the extended ESM's self-model of the basic ESM.
6. **Calibrated domains.** Where an independent measure exists, report reliability can be quantified and trusted to that degree (Bayne & Spener 2010; Spener 2015).
7. **Detection of change and mismatch at the content level.** Reports are good at noticing that something differs between two explicit models (choice-blindness detection under interview).

What the literature does not support is any of these extending to structural claims about the pre-reflective layer: whether for-me-ness is ubiquitous, whether the minimal self is agentive or passive, whether selfless experience occurs. On those, reports from both sides of each dispute (Zahavi versus Schear, Howell & Thompson and Guillot; Letheby and Millière on ego dissolution; Metzinger's MPE survey; Alcaraz-Sánchez on dreamless sleep) pass through the same reflective route, and none is calibrated against a measure that bypasses it. Irvine (2019) reaches the same verdict on general measurement grounds.

## Closest prior art and the gap

The core claim, that reflection changes what it reflects on and so cannot give the pre-reflective layer as it was, is old. The paper should say so in its first section rather than let a reviewer say it.

- **Natorp's reflection objection** (Natorp, *Allgemeine Psychologie nach kritischer Methode*, 1912; primary source not verified this session) holds that reflection objectifies lived experience and so cannot disclose it as lived. Heidegger took it up in his early lectures. Zahavi (1999, 2005) reconstructs the objection and gives the Husserlian reply that reflection modifies but discloses, and that reflection presupposes pre-reflective self-acquaintance. **This is the closest prior art for the claim itself.** The paper's step one is a naturalised Natorp objection.
- **Schooler (2002)** gives the psychological version: reports come from meta-consciousness, a re-representation of experience that can lag it (temporal dissociation) or distort it (translation dissociation). Lambie & Marcel (2002) give a matching two-level account for emotion.
- **Schear (2009), Howell & Thompson (2017) and Guillot (2017)** use the reflection point to doubt that for-me-ness is ubiquitous or phenomenal.
- **Christoff, Cosmelli, Legrand & Thompson (2011)** argue that self-reference tasks study self-as-object and miss the pre-reflective self-as-subject. **Gallagher (2012)** and **Synofzik, Vosgerau & Newen (2008)** separate the pre-reflective feeling of agency from reflective judgement and note that experiments mostly measure the judgement.
- **Dennett's heterophenomenology** (Dennett 2003, 2007) treats reports as texts to be interpreted, not readouts, but is anti-realist about the experience behind them, which FMT is not.

**What none of these does, and what the paper adds:**

1. **A specified architecture with a level assignment.** The prior art says "reflection" without saying what reflection is. FMT locates the minimal self at the basic, non-recursive ESM over a sentience floor, and reportability one recursive rung above. The claim then follows from where the report is generated, not from a phenomenological observation about reflecting. Baggio & Gallagher (2026) independently propose a sentience-level minimal self, which should be cited as convergence on the floor, but they make no report argument.
2. **A staged pipeline.** Objectification, reconstruction from memory, re-simulation in evocation, serialisation in language. The prior art covers one stage each: Natorp and Schooler the first, Bartlett and Loftus the second, Petitmengin's own description of evocation the third, verbal overshadowing and choice blindness the fourth. No source assembles them and shows that the first stage alone defeats trained report, while the other three add distortion by degree.
3. **Even-handedness.** Natorp's line was used by Heidegger against Husserl and by Schear, Howell & Thompson against Zahavi, that is, in favour of one side. The paper's position is that the same argument removes report-based evidence from both sides of the ubiquity, agency and selflessness disputes. No source found makes that symmetrical claim.
4. **A concession on content with a stated boundary.** The skeptical literature (Nisbett & Wilson, Schwitzgebel) is general; the phenomenological literature defends reports in general. The paper grants trained reports their success on content and denies it only for pre-reflective self-structure, which is narrower than either side and testable.
5. **A differential, falsifiable prediction.** "Minimal-self blindness" adapts Petitmengin et al. (2013) and predicts that interview training raises tracking of a content manipulation much more than of a covert ownership or agency manipulation, with implicit markers (self-location drift, SCR, intentional binding) as the ground truth. No prior source proposes such a test. The proposal's weak point, already flagged in the brief, is matching manipulation strength across arms.

**Risks a reviewer will raise from this literature.** (a) Zahavi's reply to Natorp: reflection modifies but discloses, and some forms of reflection ("accompanying" or non-objectifying) do not objectify. The paper needs an answer to the non-objectifying variant, not only to objectifying reflection. (b) Cohen & Dennett (2011): an unreportable floor risks being unfalsifiable unless the paper names its third-person signatures. (c) Morris (2025): training may bring preconscious items into access, so the paper must show why attending to the minimal self is a change of level rather than a shift of attention to an existing item. (d) Baggio & Gallagher (2026) are new and close; the FMT floor must be distinguished from their minimal self-sentience in one or two sentences.
