*Prediction 8: disrupting the self-model lowers pain unpleasantness more than pain intensity (new Section 8.9).* When the explicit self-model is disrupted, pain unpleasantness should fall more than pain intensity, and the size of that gap should scale with measured self-model disruption, not with drug dose or with expectancy. The prediction follows from qualia as the way the explicit self-model registers its own states (Section 3.4.2) and from the simulation as the place where hedonic valence is registered (Section 4.2.1): intensity is content generated from the nociceptive implicit models, unpleasantness is that content registered as bad for the self the model represents. The claim is relative, because self-model disruptions can also change the sensory dimension. Two designs are given: a psilocybin crossover with ego-dissolution, 5D-ASC and depersonalization measures and expectancy as a covariate, and a four-group comparison of contact and non-contact martial-art practitioners with and without formal mental training. The section states three falsification conditions and concedes that the dissociation of the two pain dimensions is established and that Gerrans (2020, 2024) argues the coarse version in active-inference terms. Twelve references were added: Gerrans (2020, 2024), Lee et al. (2013), Perlman et al. (2010), Rainville et al. (1997, 1999), Riegner et al. (2023), Röder et al. (2007), Studerus et al. (2010) and Thornton et al. (2017, 2019, 2024).
