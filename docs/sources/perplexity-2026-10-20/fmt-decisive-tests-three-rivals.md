# FMT: Decisive Tests and Potential Against Three Rivals

Assessment as of 20 October 2026. This evaluates FMT v23 as a consciousness research program, excluding team size, institutional standing, and the separate cosmological program. “Potential” means the explanatory and scientific payoff if its distinctive commitments succeed, not the probability that they will.

## Executive judgment

My assessment is that FMT has the potential to become a significant integrative theory of consciousness, rather than merely a useful descriptive vocabulary. Its strongest opportunity is to make a specific architecture constrain many phenomena with a small, independently measurable set of mechanisms.

That ceiling is not demonstrably higher than the ceilings of IIT or consciousness theories developed within active inference. Compared with GNW, I see more emphasis on the internal organization of selfhood and altered experience, but less completed formal and experimental work. These are comparative judgments about the reviewed programs, not a quantitative meta-analysis.

The main three comparison groups here are Integrated Information Theory (IIT), Global Neuronal Workspace (GNW), and predictive-processing/active-inference approaches. The last is a family, not one theory: the current minimal active-inference account and AI-C make consciousness-specific commitments, while Integrated World Modeling Theory (IWMT) is an especially close integrative comparator ([minimal active-inference theory](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.06633), [AI-C adversarial review](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.00555), [IWMT](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2022.642397/full)).

The main question is not whether FMT can explain familiar findings. It is whether its additional commitments **exclude outcomes that strong alternatives permit**, and whether those exclusions survive testing.

## Three claims that could most change the assessment

These are not three interchangeable experiments. They are, respectively, the highest-stakes constitutive claim, the most important formal vulnerability, and a concrete empirical opportunity.

### Self-referential closure is necessary for experience, not just for reporting it

**Current commitment.** FMT predicts that disrupting the self-modeling loop itself degrades experience, while disrupting metacognitive readout alone need not do so; it also acknowledges that no established measurement protocol directly tests closure ([FMT v23, §3.1.2 and Table 1b](https://zenodo.org/records/23492859)).

**Why this matters.** This is where FMT must become more than a theory of self-description, confidence, or autobiographical cognition. A first-person architecture is a strong proposal only if its role in experience can be distinguished from its role in producing judgments about experience.

Before attempting a decisive experiment, I would require the following. These conditions separate a test of closure from a test of general impairment.

- **Operational definition:** Identify the specific reciprocal dependencies constituting closure without using the presence of consciousness as the criterion for locating them. Do not substitute a named brain region or generic recurrence for the construct.
- **Selective intervention:** Specify a manipulation that disrupts those dependencies while preserving enough arousal, sensory processing, task comprehension, and response capability to interpret the result.
- **Independent assessment:** Use converging measures of experience, including appropriate report-minimizing measures, without declaring that the closure measure itself is the consciousness measure. No-report measures are evidential instruments, not direct access to another subject’s experience.
- **Adversarial predictions:** Establish in advance how a specified GNW or active-inference model responds to the same intervention. Merely predicting some impairment is not a discriminator.

**What would move FMT upward.** A selective, reproducible relationship between verified closure disruption and loss or restructuring of experience, beyond what the competing models and general impairment controls predict.

**What would move it downward.** Credible evidence of preserved experience after the operationally specified closure has genuinely been disrupted. A result confined to poorer confidence, narrative self-description, or reporting would not establish the constitutive claim.

**Scope of the conclusion.** This has the greatest potential impact on FMT’s core. A human intervention cannot currently be treated as a clean, readily available test until the operationalization problem is solved; a synthetic closure-on/closure-off experiment alone does not supply a consciousness ground truth.

### “Free modeling” is an independently identifiable requirement, not a flexible label

**Current commitment.** FMT identifies free modeling as open-ended computation autonomously recruited for the system’s own modeling, treats it as necessary but insufficient for consciousness, and distinguishes that requirement from near-criticality as a cortical signature ([FMT v23, Table 1b](https://zenodo.org/records/23492859)).

**Why this matters.** A crucial condition must be recognizable independently of whether the theory needs a system to count as conscious. Otherwise the condition can absorb counterexamples rather than constrain the theory.

**The immediate weakness to fix.** Table 1b proposes comparing novel-input behavior against a fixed lookup fitted to past behavior ([FMT v23](https://zenodo.org/records/23492859)). My assessment is that this is insufficient as the main discriminator: beating a memorization baseline can establish generalization, but not unrestricted computational universality, autonomous self-modeling, or a requirement for experience. Conversely, matching a finite set of behaviors does not establish that the actual system is implemented as a lookup table.

A stronger research target would have the following components. Their purpose is to turn a broad capacity claim into a specific, resource-sensitive prediction.

- **Definition:** Separate computational repertoire, endogenous recruitment, and self/world modeling instead of treating them as one informal capacity.
- **Resource-sensitive prediction:** Derive a scaling or transfer prediction across increasing task novelty, compositional depth, or intervention complexity.
- **Strong baselines:** Compare against trained predictive models, active-inference agents, recurrent controllers, and appropriate feedforward or unrolled alternatives, not just reactive or memorizing controls.
- **Ablations:** Preserve parameter count, training exposure, memory, and inference budget as far as possible while selectively altering the claimed mechanism.

**What would move FMT upward.** A formal characterization with a nontrivial empirical consequence, followed by an independently reproduced advantage or dissociation that the strongest alternatives do not predict.

**What would move it downward.** Inability to distinguish free modeling from ordinary generalization or recurrent inference, repeated dependence on weak baselines, or post hoc reclassification of every apparently conscious counterexample as secretly satisfying the condition.

**Scope of the conclusion.** A positive engineering result validates an architectural or resource claim, not consciousness by itself. A null result in one task also need not falsify the consciousness requirement; the tested scaling claim and its domain must be declared beforehand.

### Self-model redirection predicts the content of altered identity

**Current commitment.** FMT predicts that during psychedelic ego dissolution, the dominant sensory input systematically shapes what a participant reports “being” or “becoming”; §8.3 explicitly recognizes that predictive processing with relaxed self-priors can generate a related input-tracking prediction ([FMT v23, Prediction 2](https://zenodo.org/records/23492859)).

**Why I would prioritize it empirically.** It targets the content and organization of experience, rather than just whether neural complexity increases or a participant reports an altered state. It also creates an opportunity to predict new observations rather than accommodate another existing dataset.

A useful study would require the following controls and commitments. In particular, inducing an unusual experience is not the same as predicting its identity content.

- **Controlled contrast:** Manipulate sensory content or relative modality dominance in an ethically approved research setting, while accounting for attention, salience, expectancy, suggestibility, and the strength of ego dissolution.
- **Outcome distinction:** Separate identity attribution from imagery, perceptual incorporation, metaphor, and agreement with an experimenter’s suggestion.
- **Prospective prediction:** Specify the expected cross-modal pattern and meaningful effect size before data collection, including conditions under which the effect should disappear.
- **Real rival:** Obtain a quantitative prediction from an explicitly specified relaxed-prior predictive-processing model, ideally with a collaborator who endorses it.

**What would move FMT upward.** A held-out pattern of identity redirection predicted more accurately by the FMT mechanism than by the rival, without granting FMT additional unconstrained parameters.

**What would move it downward.** No meaningful input–identity relationship under conditions that independently meet the stated prerequisites, or a result explained equally well by the matched rival. The former challenges the redirection mechanism; the latter primarily reduces its claimed distinctiveness.

**An important correction to the comparison.** Modality-specific tracking is not automatically incompatible with predictive processing. A rival with modality-specific likelihoods and precisions could potentially predict it. Whether it actually does so must be worked out before the result is advertised as a discriminator.

## Potential against the three rivals

### IIT: a sharply different criterion, not just less mathematics

IIT 4.0 identifies experience with the intrinsic cause–effect structure of a maximal physical complex; phenomenal quality is associated with the full structure, not simply with a scalar \( \Phi \) ([IIT 4.0](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011465)). Its formalism is explicit, but exhaustive analysis of realistic systems is computationally infeasible, and its small-network demonstrations are not demonstrations that those networks are conscious ([IIT 4.0](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011465)).

**FMT’s opportunity:** Develop a tractable architectural criterion that explains why a system has an organized, embodied perspective and predicts changes to that perspective. Such a criterion could prove more useful for building and experimentally manipulating candidate systems.

**IIT’s advantage:** It already states a comparatively precise physical identity claim and provides a formal procedure for applying it to small, fully specified systems ([IIT 4.0](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011465)). That precision provides a clearer target for auditing internal consistency.

**FMT’s burden:** Specify architectures for which the two criteria genuinely disagree. Then establish what accessible evidence bears on the disagreement; neither an impressive agent nor a \( \Phi \) calculation independently establishes experience.

**Potential judgment:** Comparable ambition, different strengths. FMT could become more operationally useful, but tractability and intuitive appeal do not make its identity claim more likely to be true.

### GNW: a possible deeper architecture, not a theory that never addresses experience

GNW proponents describe conscious content as explicitly encoded in workspace activity, with commitments concerning ignition, distributed availability, and the decodability of consciously perceived attributes; they do not characterize their program as merely a theory of verbal reporting ([GNW proponents’ response to COGITATE](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2025/1/niaf037/8280147)).

**FMT’s opportunity:** Explain why particular contents are experienced as mine, how an explicit self/world organization changes, and why experience can diverge from metacognitive report. My assessment is that these are natural strengths of FMT’s chosen architecture.

**GNW’s advantage:** A more developed set of neuronal models and experimentally engaged predictions, rather than only a conceptual broadcast metaphor ([GNW response](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2025/1/niaf037/8280147)).

**FMT’s burden:** Produce a clean dissociation between closure-related experience and the workspace conditions a strong GNW model identifies as constitutive. Simply finding self-modeling within a workspace would be compatible with a synthesis.

**Potential judgment:** FMT has a plausible route to a more extensive account of selfhood and altered phenomenology, but it has not shown that GNW cannot accommodate those phenomena. GNW is a demanding experimental benchmark, not an obsolete foil.

### Predictive processing and active inference: the strongest direct competition

The current minimal active-inference account links conscious contents to inferred states and discusses access at the interface between continuous sensory processing and discrete counterfactual policy selection ([minimal theory](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.06633)). The cited AI-C study protocol is designed to test whether active inference is necessary for changes in conscious content, distinguishes that necessity claim from sufficient conditions for experience, and does not report completed experimental outcomes ([AI-C study protocol](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0328836)).

IWMT already proposes coherent, embodied self/world modeling as a condition for experience, integrating active inference with ideas from IIT and GNW ([IWMT](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2022.642397/full)).

**FMT’s opportunity:** Supply additional, restrictive architecture: the implicit/explicit distinction across self/world scope, a particular closure condition, and mechanisms whose measured parameters constrain predictions across phenomena.

**The rival family’s advantage:** Explicit inference and policy-selection machinery, existing computational models of multiple consciousness-related paradigms, and a consciousness-specific testing program ([minimal theory](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.06633), [AI-C protocol](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0328836)).

**FMT’s burden:** Demonstrate that its architecture contributes something beyond a redescription or particular implementation of an embodied generative model. If it is a special case, show why that case earns special status through prediction, explanatory economy, or causal evidence.

**Potential judgment:** FMT’s ceiling is high, but not clearly higher. This family, especially IWMT, is where I would demand the hardest novelty and discrimination tests.

## What the current empirical landscape does and does not imply

The 2025 COGITATE study tested preregistered IIT and GNWT predictions in 256 participants and found challenges for both, while testing selected biological implementations rather than directly adjudicating the entirety of either theory’s mathematical or computational core ([COGITATE study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12137136/)). GNW proponents dispute the centrality and interpretation of some tested predictions, including offset ignition ([GNW response](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2025/1/niaf037/8280147)).

This is not an automatic promotion for FMT. A theory with fewer demanding tests has had fewer opportunities to fail; that is not evidence that it performs better.

The same standard should apply to every program. IIT does not earn its phenomenal identity merely by formalizing it, GNW does not earn its identity merely by explaining information availability, and FMT does not earn its identity merely by constructing self-referential dynamics.

## Recommended order of work

- **First, secure a differentiating prediction:** Take the redirection claim to a strong predictive-processing or IWMT collaborator and determine whether the models actually disagree before launching a study.
- **In parallel, define the core constructs:** Make closure and free modeling identifiable without reference to the desired consciousness verdict. State the admissible implementation changes and compensation mechanisms in advance.
- **Then test under strong controls:** Use held-out prediction, explicit model complexity penalties where applicable, and independently reproducible analyses.
- **Keep the levels of success separate:** Architectural utility, an empirically preferred account of conscious phenomena, and an established cross-substrate criterion of experience are different achievements.

My bottom line is that FMT could become an important theory without replacing all three rivals. Its most credible high-value outcome is an empirically constrained architecture that explains which forms of modeling and recurrence matter, how they generate the organization of experience, and where the competing accounts make incorrect predictions.
