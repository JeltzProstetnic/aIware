## Weak points

1. **Whittenberger's epiphenomenalism undermines itself**: if experience causes nothing, his reports about experience aren't caused by it (Chalmers' paradox of phenomenal judgement).
2. **The pain argument fails empirically**: congenital insensitivity to pain usually means early death, because pain drives learning and avoidance. The fact that withdrawal comes first is a spinal reflex and says nothing about the function of pain.
3. **The brainscope yields correlates**, which is the easy problem. A perfect correlation map explains nothing; the gap is relabelled "brute fact".
4. **Shermer conflates two problems**: the access/other-minds problem ("I can't know what it's like to be a dolphin") and the hard problem (why there is experience at all). A is A only touches the first. He is also inconsistent: "neural activity = qualia" (identity) next to Pinker's "stipulate it".
5. **Both stop at the same wall**, brute fact or unknowable, from opposite sides.

## FMT relevance (MG 2026-10-18: "1-7 there is your answer")

1. **Both commit the category error FMT names**: they look for phenomenal properties at the substrate level. Whittenberger's "M ≠ B, B produces M" is a mechanism-free intuition of FMT's two-level ontology (substrate vs. virtual simulation). FMT replaces "produces, brute fact" with the spreadsheet/transistor relation: qualia are constitutive of the computational level and incoherent at the substrate level.
2. **FMT is not epiphenomenal**: the explicit models do causal work, because the self-model and the world-model each enter the other's update rule (closure). That answers Whittenberger's weakest point.
3. **"Machine vs. inside the machine"**: FMT gives a precise third answer. The machine doesn't feel; the simulation does, and the "I" is the explicit self-model within it. Each of the two is half right.
4. **Shermer's A is A / dolphin**: the FMT psychedelic prediction ("what you become tracks dominant sensory input") and the forking of the self-model show that the self-model is not a fixed identity. That gives no access to a real dolphin, but it undercuts the essentialist "I'd just be a dolphin" premise. Whittenberger's brain-transplant thought experiment is covered by the book's chapter on copying a mind.
5. **Whittenberger's own research agenda overlaps what FMT already accounts for**: psychedelics, anaesthesia, dreams and lucid dreams. FMT also covers split-brain, DID and animal consciousness. The other items on his list (blindsight, sleep, coma, hypnosis, brain stimulation, psychophysics) have not been checked against the paper.
6. **"Explanation stops here", stated honestly**: FMT also has one step that is argued for, not derived (that closure constitutes phenomenality, paper §3.4.3). But FMT puts that step at a specific architectural point that yields testable predictions, rather than as a global stipulation. The principled version of Shermer's "known unknowable" is in the cosmology work: a self-referential system cannot fully specify itself from within (Gödel). That is a limit on self-description, not on the hard problem.
7. **Hard evidence neither piece mentions**: independent convergence on criticality (Xu 2024; Hengen & Shew 2025; Algom & Shriki 2026), which bears on the computational requirement and is shared with other criticality frameworks.
