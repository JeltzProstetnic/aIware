# How To Make A Standard Model Of Consciousness

**The method, extracted from having done it once.** Companion to `docs/smoc-middle-layer-draft.md`, which is
the product. This file is the recipe — the thing MG proposes at Copenhagen as *the road to* a standard model,
as distinct from proposing a standard model.

Working draft, Session 285, 2026-08-05.

---

## Why the method is the better thing to propose

A field that is handed a standard model has to accept or reject it, and a pre-paradigmatic field will reject
it — there is no mechanism by which eleven rival theories adopt a twelfth. A field that is handed a **method
for building one** has something else available: it can *run* it, and the running is collaborative even
between people who disagree about everything else.

That is the whole argument for the sequencing. **The chart is the demonstration; the method is the proposal.**
Presenting the method first also removes the only real objection to an unaffiliated author supplying a field's
coordinate system, because a recipe is judged by whether it works when someone else follows it.

**It is deliberately field-agnostic.** Nothing below is specific to consciousness, and that is a feature: a
recipe that only works for one theory's subject matter is special pleading. It should be applicable to any
pre-paradigmatic field, and if it is not, that is a defect in the recipe.

---

## Step 0 — Diagnose whether the middle layer is actually missing

Do not skip this; some fields are fine and the work is wasted.

Two tests, both answerable in an afternoon:

1. **The landing test.** Can a curious outsider land *anywhere* between popular nonsense and the technical
   literature? If the only two available altitudes are horoscope and forty years of argument, layer 1 is
   missing.
2. **The talking-past test.** Take two specialists from adjacent disciplines who both study the subject. Can
   they determine whether they *disagree* or are merely *using different words*? If not, there is no shared
   coordinate system, which is the same finding from the inside.

Consciousness fails both, decisively. Chemistry passes both because of the periodic table; molecular biology
passes because of the central dogma.

## Step 1 — Convert the contested terms into axes

The generative move, and it is `docs/mg-thinking-tools.md` Tool 1: **every claim has a side on which it is
true and a side on which it is false, therefore it is not a point but a position on an axis, and the axis has
to be named.**

Procedure: list the field's central contested terms. For each, find the dimension it lives on. *"Is it
conscious?"* becomes a ladder. *"Is closure necessary?"* becomes advantage-as-a-function-of-task-fit. *"Are
you a dualist?"* becomes how-much-is-left-outside.

Two disciplines that make the difference between an axis and a decoration:

- **Reject any axis you cannot at least order.** Nameable is not enough; a pseudo-axis is worse than a term
  because it looks rigorous.
- **Check that the axis carries the variance.** This is the step everyone skips and it has cost this project
  twice — an advantage axis drawn on *recursion depth* when the signal was on *redeployed-model richness*, and
  a result certified by two careful controls that both varied *rank* while the governing confound was
  *redundancy*. Both controls were real. Both were blind.

## Step 2 — Separate what there is from what it does

Constituents and interactions get **different tables**. Elements and bonds. Fermions and bosons. Components
and mechanisms. A single undifferentiated inventory is the most common failure of homemade frameworks, and it
is why they cannot be reasoned with: if a thing and an operation occupy the same list, no composition rule can
exist.

## Step 3 — Build the ladder, because it is what buys placement power

Order the capabilities so that a position implies which of them are present. This is the periodic table's
*periods* and it is the single highest-value structure in the whole exercise, because it converts the chart
from a description into an instrument that answers the question the public, ethicists and regulators are
actually asking: **where does this system sit?**

Requirement: the ordering must be *forced*, not stylistic — each rung genuinely presupposing the one below.
If the rungs can be permuted, it is a list wearing a ladder's clothes.

## Step 4 — Choose motivated notation and refuse every lookup table

The periodic table's `Fe` works because print-era rote schooling pre-installed the symbol→meaning table in
every head at society's expense. **That subsidy is gone, and print's constraints are gone with it.** So:

- **Names are plain words that define themselves.** *Bottleneck*, *return*, *gate*, *aperture*.
- **Pictograms are motivated** — the form diagrams the operation. Test: *nobody was ever taught the wifi icon.*
- **New concepts are compositions of existing marks, never new symbols.** This is the property arbitrary
  symbols lack and it is worth more than the compression they buy: `Fe` + `O` does not draw rust, but the
  wired mark + an arrow + the running mark *is* explicitation.
- **Prefer a space to a table and an axis to a term** — a dimensional model lets the reader apply their
  spatial cortex to a semantic problem, which is redeployment of an already-rich model onto a non-native
  referent.

If a reader has to look something up, the notation has failed. If *you* invent a shorthand and the author of
the field has to ask what it means, that is the same failure caught early — it happened in this project and
the shorthand was withdrawn.

## Step 5 — Mark every cell for how settled it is

Five states: **agreed** (everyone signs) · **converging** (independent theories posit something equivalent in
different words) · **disputed** (they disagree, but the question is well-posed) · **blank** (nobody knows) ·
**theory-specific** (only this account proposes it).

Three rules that carry the whole method:

- **Blank cells stay blank.** The temptation is to fill them with something plausible. Mendeleev's blanks are
  the reason his chart predicted anything.
- **Mark your own theory's cells honestly as theory-specific.** A chart that quietly promotes its author's
  fillings to *agreed* is a manifesto, and the field will read it as one within seconds.
- **Credit convergence without claiming priority.** *Consistent with*, always.

## Step 6 — Name and box the residue

Every real science has an acknowledged unexplained remainder, and the mature ones **put it on the chart**.
Physics names ~95% of the mass-energy budget *dark matter* and prints the particle chart anyway.

- **Name it neutrally and attractively.** "Dark matter" describes the *evidence*, commits to no answer, and
  sounds like a frontier rather than a failure — which is why it drew talent instead of embarrassment. Reject
  any name that presupposes an answer or sounds like a mess to be cleaned up.
- **Make the neutrality structural, not asserted.** Rivals must be able to adopt the chart and fill the
  residue differently. If your chart requires your metaphysics as an entry fee, you have written a manifesto
  with a grid on it.
- **Give it an exclusion ledger.** Residue research is real research and most of its output is *elimination* —
  dead parameter windows, killed variants. Nobody calls that wasted.
- **Set a procedural admission bar, not a doctrinal one.** A speculative claim that states what would
  disconfirm it is admitted; one that does not, is not. Nobody is excluded for their metaphysics — only for
  declining to specify.
- **Keep the residue separate from the extensions.** Speculative *additions to the physics* (multiverse,
  simulation) are a different category from a claimed *non-physical remainder*, and a chart that heaps them
  together drags all of it into every argument. Most extensions turn out to be chart-invariant.
- **State the disanalogy where there is one.** Physics *quantifies* its residue. A qualitative residue cannot
  be a budget gap, and claiming a quantified gap you do not have is the fastest way to lose the room.

## Step 7 — State what the chart forbids

A chart with no forbidden sentences is a vocabulary. Selection rules are what let a reader detect an error
without re-deriving the theory, and the most valuable ones are those learned by an experimental programme
failing — those generalise past your own account, which is exactly why rivals will use your chart.

Rule of thumb: if a selection rule does not rule out a sentence someone respectable is currently writing, it
is decoration.

## Step 8 — Build the bridge, and read its empty cells as findings

One row per chart term, one column per adjacent discipline. Fill in each discipline's word for the same thing.

**The empty cells are the result.** Where three mature disciplines have no word for one of your terms, either
your term is confused or the field has a gap — and the table forces that choice into the open instead of
leaving it implicit. The bridge is also what makes the chart *worth adopting* by people who do not care about
your theory: it is immediately useful as a translation device.

## Step 9 — Read the consensus off the chart instead of arguing for it

This is the payoff, and it is mechanical:

| Read | Get |
|---|---|
| agreed ∪ converging | **the consensus core** — the paragraph nobody has written, because without a chart there was nothing to read it off |
| disputed | **the negotiation agenda** — each becomes a question answerable in ninety minutes, unlike "what is consciousness" |
| blank | **the research programme**, with owners |
| theory-specific | **your exposed surface** — publish it; volunteering where you can be attacked is what distinguishes infrastructure from doctrine |

## Step 10 — Publish the metric, not the verdict

Cells move **blank → disputed → converging → agreed** as the field advances. That is a measurable field-level
progress indicator, and a pre-paradigmatic field typically has none — it cannot tell whether it is converging
or merely producing.

**Offering the field its first convergence instrument is a larger contribution than any single filling of the
chart**, and unlike a theory it cannot be desk-rejected, because it is not a claim about consciousness.

## Step 11 — Loop

Forward-engineer: fill the cells, re-mark, re-measure, publish the trend. Each *blank* gets an owner
discipline and an experiment class; each *disputed* gets the minimal experiment that would move it. The
organised open-question set is an output of the method, not a byproduct — which is the part that no current
practice in the field produces at all.

## Invariants — hold these throughout or the method degrades

1. **The revisability licence.** Mendeleev got eight atomic weights wrong and did not know protons existed;
   the Bohr atom is *wrong* and was indispensable. **The chart does not have to be right to be useful — it has
   to be organised, falsifiable and revisable.** This is the licence to publish before you are certain, and
   the standing answer to "you are oversimplifying."
2. **The pre-fashion test.** If a cell cannot be stated without a result from the last few years, it is a
   technical detail wearing a chart's clothes. Doubles as an anti-overfitting check: a middle layer that
   depends on recent data is fitted to it.
3. **The chart is not the theory.** Say so, repeatedly, and mean it structurally — a rival must be able to
   fill it differently and lose nothing.
4. **Honest marks or it is a manifesto.** There is no third outcome.
5. **A component is a role, not a lump.** Never identify a functional role with a particular physical
   structure, or the chart becomes parochial to one implementation.

## The Copenhagen road — the method applied to MoC7

What to propose, in order, and what each step actually asks of the room:

1. **Propose the diagnosis, not the theory.** *The field has no middle layer, and here are the two tests that
   show it.* Nobody has to concede anything to agree with this, and most people in the room have felt it.
2. **Propose the method** (this file) as the road. It is collaborative by construction and field-agnostic by
   design, so it is not a bid for FMT.
3. **Put one discussion question to the ~Sept 12 vote.** Best candidates, FMT unnamed:
   - *"How much does your theory leave outside itself, and why?"* — the residue question. It separates every
     major position in one move, lets dualists participate without conceding anything, and is the rare
     question on which a room of rivals can each answer honestly with nobody losing.
   - *"What structural criterion distinguishes conscious from non-conscious computation?"*
   - *"Is a global workspace a primitive or a composite?"*
4. **Bring the chart as a worked example, not as a proposal.** *Here is what happens when you run the method
   once. Fill it differently and we can finally locate the disagreement.*
5. **Offer the convergence metric to the collective paper.** A field-consensus paper whose contribution is a
   measurable definition of consensus is a better paper than one that asserts a consensus, and it is the one
   thing on this list that cannot be desk-rejected.
6. **Ask the questions only a room can answer.** Kleiner on whether a monograph containing the full paper
   prejudices NoC/JAIC submission of derived slices (`AIW-149`); the residue-boundary question to Zahavi and
   Atmanspacher; the ladder's rung boundaries to Blum, who will engage a formal criterion rather than bounce
   off it.

**What to avoid, stated plainly:** do not present the chart as finished, do not present *agreed* marks that
are actually judgement calls (the citation audit is not done — `docs/smoc-middle-layer-draft.md` §6), and do
not let the poster become a theory pitch. The strongest position in that room is *the person who supplied the
coordinate system*, and it is available precisely because nobody is competing for it.
