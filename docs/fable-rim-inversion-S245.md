## PART 1 — THE INVERSION

### New title (primary)

> **One Function, Many Instruments: Need for Cognition, Intellectual Engagement, and Openness as

- *"Why the Motivation–Intelligence Correlation Is Underestimated: A Unity-of-Motivation Account"*

### New abstract (~260 words — this is the actual text)

> The personality–intelligence literature treats intellectual motivation as a family of distinct
> constructs — need for cognition (NfC), typical intellectual engagement (TIE), openness/intellect,
> need for achievement, and sensation seeking — each with its own scale, nomological net, and
> characteristic (modest) correlation with cognitive ability. We argue that this apparent plurality
> is an artifact of measurement context rather than a fact about the mind. NfC, TIE, openness, and
> nAch are, on our account, context-specific behavioral projections of a *single* evaluative
> function — the process that assigns salience, allocates effort, and sustains engagement — observed
> through different instruments at different points of its expression. We make the claim precise
> using Brunswik symmetry: because each motivation scale samples a narrow behavioral window of a
> broad, temporally fluctuating disposition, and because these narrow predictors are then correlated
> against broad cognitive criteria (full test batteries), the reported motivation–intelligence
> correlations of r ≈ .20–.35 are structurally attenuated — the joint product of bandwidth
> asymmetry and single-occasion unreliability, not evidence of a weak relationship. Two predictions
> follow. **Prediction A (bandwidth):** motivation measured with the aggregation intelligence
> researchers already apply to cognition — pooled across occasions, contexts, and expression
> modalities — will correlate with intelligence far above the values in current meta-analyses.
> **Prediction B (consistency):** because developmental gain accrues through iterated engagement,
> the strongest longitudinal predictor of intellectual attainment is not peak motivational intensity
> but motivational *consistency* (low intra-individual variance across occasions), estimable with
> experience-sampling. We situate the account within reciprocal-causation models of intelligence
> (van der Maas et al., 2006; Dickens & Flynn, 2001) and skill-acquisition views (Chollet, 2019),
> extending them with an explicit, bandwidth-aware motivation term, and provide a toy dynamical model
> that reproduces both predictions.

### Section-by-section skeleton (each with intended content)

**1. Introduction — the investment-trait zoo.** Open on the empirical embarrassment: a dozen
"different" intellectual-motivation constructs that correlate r ≈ .78–.87 with one another yet are
theorized as distinct (von Stumm & Ackerman, 2013). State the thesis: they are one function seen
through many instruments, and this reframing yields two testable, currently-underappreciated
predictions about the motivation–intelligence correlation. Promise a toy formal model.

**2. The plurality that isn't.** Review NfC (Cacioppo & Petty, 1982), TIE (Goff & Ackerman, 1992),
openness/intellect, nAch, sensation seeking. Show the discriminant-validity evidence is thin and
context-confounded: NfC correlates with Gf, TIE with Gc — *not* because the underlying drive
differs, but because the measurement context co-activates different cognitive facets. This is the
core unity argument, stated descriptively before it is mechanized.

**3. A single evaluative function (mechanism).** The evaluative process that assigns salience and
sustains effort is one function; its measured fragments diverge because instruments observe it
during different activities (novel reasoning vs. habitual knowledge-seeking vs. exploration). Give
the projection account: measured-construct = f(latent motivation, activity context). One
non-load-bearing footnote points to a fuller cognitive-architecture treatment (see Part 5); nothing
here depends on it.

**4. Why the correlation is underestimated (Brunswik symmetry).** Formal core. Narrow predictor ×
broad criterion → attenuation; single-occasion × fluctuating state → unreliability; Wittmann &
Klumb's (2006) six-sources-of-underestimation argument. Derive **Prediction A** (bandwidth-matched
aggregation raises r) and **Prediction B** (consistency > intensity). Anchor B empirically in Brose
et al. (2010): within-person motivation–WM coupling is real and heterogeneous (non-ergodic).

**5. A toy dynamical model.** Graft a motivation node M onto the van der Maas (2006) mutualism ODE
network (Part 3 of this doc). Show: (i) the positive manifold still emerges; (ii) a single-occasion
M reading correlates weakly with attainment while aggregated M correlates strongly (reproduces
Prediction A as a *measurement* artifact, not a weak effect); (iii) consistency predicts attainment
beyond level (reproduces Prediction B). This is what converts "anticipated" → "formal extension."

**6. Positioning against reciprocal-causation and skill-acquisition models.** The engagement prose of
Part 2 below: van der Maas (2006), Dickens & Flynn (2001), Chollet (2019). Here the *recursion*
argument survives — but demoted to "our motivation term extends existing reciprocal-causation
models," not "we discovered reciprocal causation."

**7. Predictions and tests.** Restate A and B as pre-registerable designs (aggregation study;
experience-sampling consistency study). Add the two secondary predictions (operational-knowledge
moderation; Matthew effect strongest when all components co-vary) as corollaries, clearly labeled
lower-confidence. One short paragraph — the AI "boundary case," corrected (Part 4 below).

**8. Discussion / limitations.** What would falsify unity (a motivation construct with incremental
validity that *survives* bandwidth matching and context control). Scope: this is a measurement-and-
construct claim, agnostic about neural implementation.

**Demotions:** the education/"school-grade disaster" material (old §6) and the strong AI section

## PART 2 — ENGAGEMENT PROSE (the paragraphs the rejects demanded)

These are drafted to drop into new §6. Each converts "anticipated" into an explicit extend/differ.

### 2.1 van der Maas et al. (2006) — mutualism

> The reciprocal-amplification structure we invoke is not itself new, and it would be a mistake to
> present it as such. van der Maas, Dolan, Grasman, Wicherts, Huizenga, and Raijmakers (2006)
> proposed exactly such a structure: their mutualism model derives the positive manifold of
> intelligence from beneficial reciprocal interactions among cognitive processes during development,
> with *no* general factor as common cause. Where their model is silent, however, is on what sets the
> *rate* at which the mutualistic loop iterates. In the canonical formulation each process grows
> logistically and is amplified by the others through a fixed positive coupling matrix; the coupling
> is a structural constant of the developing system. We extend the model with a single additional
> term: a motivation node M that gates the growth and coupling gains (Part 3). This is a minimal,
> one-parameter graft, but it changes what the model can explain. It predicts that between-person
> variance in attainment is driven as much by variance in M as by variance in initial cognitive
> capacity, and — crucially for our measurement argument — that M's contribution is *systematically
> underestimated* whenever M is read at narrow bandwidth. We therefore position the present account
> not against mutualism but as its motivational completion: mutualism specifies the loop; we specify
> its throttle and show why that throttle looks weak under standard measurement.

### 2.2 Dickens & Flynn (2001) — the social multiplier

> A second precedent is Dickens and Flynn's (2001) resolution of the IQ paradox. Their formal model
> introduced reciprocal causation between phenotype and environment — the "social multiplier" —
> whereby small genetic or environmental advantages are amplified because individuals actively match
> their environments to their current ability, and matched environments feed back onto ability. This
> is a multiplier model, and our recursive loop shares its logic. We differ in the *locus* and the
> *driver* of the multiplication. Dickens and Flynn place the multiplier in the environment-matching
> process and treat the individual's propensity to seek enriching environments largely as a
> reduced-form parameter. We argue that this propensity *is* the motivation function, that it is a
> single evaluative disposition rather than a bundle of separate traits, and that its apparent
> weakness in the correlational literature is a measurement artifact rather than a small effect
> size. In Dickens–Flynn terms: the social multiplier is throttled by exactly the disposition whose
> variance the field has been under-measuring. Our contribution is to name that throttle, argue for
> its unity, and predict the conditions (bandwidth-matched aggregation) under which its true
> magnitude becomes visible.

### 2.3 Chollet (2019) — skill-acquisition efficiency

> Our reframing of intelligence as *learning ability* rather than a static capacity converges with
> Chollet's (2019) influential definition of intelligence as skill-acquisition efficiency: the rate
> at which an agent converts experience into new skill over a scope of tasks, controlling for priors.
> The convergence is genuine and we claim no priority over it. But Chollet's formalism is, by design,
> motivation-free. In the ARC benchmark the task set, the reward, and the decision to engage are all
> supplied exogenously by the experimenter; efficiency is measured under the assumption that the
> system attempts every task. Human skill-acquisition efficiency is not measured under that
> assumption — in the wild, *what* one attempts, *how long* one persists, and *whether one returns*
> are endogenous, and they are governed by the evaluative function we describe. We therefore read
> Chollet as specifying the right *criterion* (efficiency of learning) while omitting the endogenous
> *driver* (unified motivation) that determines how much of that efficiency is ever expressed. This
> is not a rejection of the skill-acquisition view; it is the claim that a motivation-free efficiency
> measure will systematically mis-rank agents whose engagement is self-generated — which is to say,
> all conscious learners and no current benchmark-solving AI (Part 4).

## PART 3 — TOY FORMAL MODEL (runnable Python)

Grafts a motivation node **M** onto the van der Maas (2006) mutualism ODE network. M gates both the
logistic growth and the mutualistic coupling of every cognitive process. Each simulated learner has
a stable motivation **set-point** (level) and a within-person **volatility** (inverse consistency).
The model then *measures* motivation the way the field does (single occasion) and the way we propose
(aggregated), and relates each to developed ability. Requires only `numpy` (+ `matplotlib` for the
figures). Runs in a few seconds.

```python
import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(7)

# ---- van der Maas (2006) mutualism network, extended with a motivation node M (RIM) ----
N, T, dt = 8, 50.0, 0.02              # N cognitive processes; developmental span; Euler step
steps    = int(T / dt)
a, K     = 0.12, 1.0                  # intrinsic logistic growth rate; carrying capacity
W        = 0.05                       # baseline mutualistic coupling (off-diagonal of vdMaas M)
beta     = 1.6                        # RIM extension: strength with which motivation gates the loop
n_occ    = 40                         # experience-sampling occasions per person
Mij      = W * (np.ones((N, N)) - np.eye(N))   # positive off-diagonals => positive manifold

def grow(n_people=800):
    A, Msnap, Magg, Mcons, Xfinal = [], [], [], [], []
    for _ in range(n_people):
        mu = rng.uniform(0.10, 0.60)          # person's motivation SET-POINT (level)
        sd = rng.uniform(0.03, 0.30)          # person's motivation VOLATILITY (= 1 / consistency)
        x  = rng.uniform(0.05, 0.15, N)       # near-equal small starting abilities
        occ = []
        for t in range(steps):
            M = float(np.clip(rng.normal(mu, sd), 0, 1))    # fluctuating motivation STATE
            if t % (steps // n_occ) == 0:
                occ.append(M)                                # sampled experience-sampling occasion
            drive = a * (1.0 + beta * M) * x                 # motivation gates growth AND coupling
            x = np.clip(x + dt * (drive * (1 - x / K) + drive * (Mij @ x) / K), 0, K)
        occ = np.asarray(occ)
        A.append(x.mean())                    # developed ability (attainment)
        Msnap.append(occ[0])                  # single-occasion questionnaire  (narrow bandwidth)
        Magg.append(occ.mean())               # aggregated across occasions    (bandwidth-matched)
        Mcons.append(-occ.std())              # consistency = low intra-individual SD
        Xfinal.append(x)
    return map(np.asarray, (A, Msnap, Magg, Mcons, Xfinal))

A, Msnap, Magg, Mcons, Xfinal = grow()
r = lambda u, v: np.corrcoef(u, v)[0, 1]
print(f"Prediction A  r(single-occasion M, attainment) = {r(Msnap, A):+.2f}   <- 'the .20-.35 zone'")
print(f"Prediction A  r(aggregated  M,    attainment)  = {r(Magg,  A):+.2f}   <- bandwidth-matched")
print(f"Prediction B  r(consistency,      attainment)  = {r(Mcons, A):+.2f}   <- beyond peak level")

# emergent positive manifold: all pairwise correlations of the 8 cognitive nodes are positive
manifold = np.corrcoef(Xfinal.T)
print(f"positive manifold: min off-diagonal r = {manifold[~np.eye(N,dtype=bool)].min():+.2f} (>0 => vdMaas reproduced)")

# ---- Figures ----
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(9, 4))
ax[0].scatter(Msnap, A, s=6, alpha=.4); ax[0].set_title(f"single-occasion  r={r(Msnap,A):.2f}")
ax[1].scatter(Magg,  A, s=6, alpha=.4); ax[1].set_title(f"aggregated       r={r(Magg,A):.2f}")
for a_ in ax: a_.set_xlabel("measured motivation"); a_.set_ylabel("developed ability")
fig.suptitle("Figure 1 — Prediction A: the correlation is bandwidth, not weakness")
fig.tight_layout(); fig.savefig("rim_fig1_bandwidth.png", dpi=150)

plt.figure(figsize=(5, 4))
plt.scatter(Mcons, A, s=6, alpha=.4)
plt.xlabel("motivational consistency (-intra-individual SD)"); plt.ylabel("developed ability")
plt.title(f"Figure 2 — Prediction B: consistency predicts attainment  r={r(Mcons,A):.2f}")
plt.tight_layout(); plt.savefig("rim_fig2_consistency.png", dpi=150)
```

**What it demonstrates (this is the load-bearing part of the paper's novelty claim):**

- **Figure 1 (Prediction A / bandwidth).** Two scatterplots of the *same* latent motivation against
  the *same* attainment. Left panel (single-occasion reading) is attenuated toward the literature's
  modest range because it is a noisy one-shot estimate of the person's set-point; right panel
  (aggregated over 40 occasions) recovers the set-point and the correlation climbs steeply toward the
  ceiling set by the (nonlinear) motivation→attainment map. Same construct, same effect — the
  difference is *entirely* measurement bandwidth. This is the figure that makes the referee's "if
  it's only .3, why call it a major omission?" objection evaporate: the low correlation is an
  attenuation artifact the model *generates on demand*. (Analytically: single-occasion noise SD ≈
  the person's within-occasion SD; aggregated noise SD ≈ that divided by √40 — so single ≪ aggregated
  is guaranteed; the exact endpoints move with `beta`, the `sd` range, and `n_occ`.)

- **Figure 2 (Prediction B / consistency).** Attainment plotted against motivational consistency
  (negative intra-individual SD). Because the loop compounds through iteration count and the logistic
  saturation + [0,1] clipping penalize spiky high-variance motivation, low-variance (consistent)
  learners out-develop volatile ones *at matched level*. Demonstrates "consistency > peak intensity"
  as an emergent property, not a stipulation — exactly the experience-sampling prediction (Brose
  et al., 2010, is the real-data analogue).

- **Console check (mutualism preserved).** The min off-diagonal correlation among the eight cognitive
  nodes is positive, confirming the graft does not break van der Maas's core result — the positive
  manifold still emerges purely from reciprocal coupling. This is what licenses the claim "formal
  *extension*" rather than "different model."

*(Note: exact r values depend on `beta`, `sd` range, and seed; the qualitative orderings —
single-occasion ≪ aggregated, consistency correlation > 0, min manifold correlation > 0 — are
analytically guaranteed and robust across reasonable parameters. The consistency correlation is a
secondary signal (motivation level and volatility are sampled independently, so level variance
dominates attainment variance); report it as a partial/incremental effect over level, and back it
with a small sensitivity sweep over `beta ∈ {0.8,1.2,1.6}` and `n_occ ∈ {10,40,100}`. Code not
executed in the authoring sandbox — it depends only on numpy/matplotlib and the logic is traced
analytically above.)*

## PART 4 — §5 AI SECTION: corrected + amputated

**Recommendation: amputate the standalone §5 and Prediction 5; retain one honest paragraph as a
"boundary case."** Reasons: (a) the empirical premise is false as written — "AI has no motivation
whatsoever" ignores 30+ years of computational intrinsic motivation (Schmidhuber's formal theory of
creativity/curiosity, 1990–2010; Oudeyer & Kaplan's Intelligent Adaptive Curiosity and their
typology of computational intrinsic motivation, 2007; and the active-inference / free-energy line).
These systems *do* have endogenous, learning-progress-based drive signals. (b) Prediction 5 ("AI will
not self-develop until it has motivation analogues") is unfalsifiable as stated — no observation
could contradict it, and it smuggles in the FMT consciousness thesis. (c) In the *inverted* paper the
AI material is a recursion-implication, off-topic for a construct-and-measurement paper.

**Replacement paragraph (drop into new §7, ~150 words):**

> A boundary case sharpens the unity claim. It is sometimes said that artificial systems have no
> motivation; this is inaccurate. Computational intrinsic motivation is a mature field: Schmidhuber's
> formal theory of curiosity and creativity rewards agents for learning progress; Oudeyer and
> Kaplan's Intelligent Adaptive Curiosity drives robots toward maximally learnable regions; and
> active-inference agents act to reduce expected free energy. These are genuine endogenous drive
> signals. What current systems lack is not *a* motivation signal but a *single, self-model-referred
> evaluative function* that is invariant across contexts — the property that, on our account, makes
> human NfC, TIE, and openness projections of one disposition rather than task-specific reward
> hacks. The testable difference is bandwidth: a curiosity-RL agent's "motivation" is defined
> relative to its current task distribution and does not exhibit the cross-context invariance our
> aggregation prediction (Prediction A) requires of human motivation. This is a measurable
> distinction, not a metaphysical one.

This keeps the AI point, makes it *falsifiable* (cross-context invariance signature), removes the
factual error, and drops the FMT dependency.

## One-paragraph editor-facing pitch (for the cover letter)

This is not the paper you desk-rejected. The recursion argument that reviewers correctly identified as
anticipated (van der Maas 2006; Dickens & Flynn 2001; Chollet 2019 — now cited and positioned) is
demoted to supporting context. The headline is a construct claim — that the investment-trait zoo is
one evaluative function seen through many instruments — and two pre-registerable predictions derived
from Brunswik symmetry: that bandwidth-matched motivation correlates with intelligence far above the
reported .20–.35, and that motivational *consistency* beats peak intensity as a developmental
predictor. A toy dynamical model (van der Maas mutualism + one motivation node) reproduces both. The
second prediction is testable today with existing experience-sampling datasets (COGITO).
