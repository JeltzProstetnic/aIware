# AIW-357: misreadings of MG's own works in the Perplexity "Beginner's Guide to Thinking" dossier (FMT / Emergenz / Simulation scope)

Audit date: 2026-10-27. Scope: everything in the dossier that touches the Four-Model Theory, *Die Emergenz des Bewusstseins*, *The Simulation You Call "I"* and the internal registries. The logic material (thesis 5 proper, *No Outside*, patch error) is out of scope and is touched only where it overlaps.

**Abbreviations**
- **D** = dossier, `docs/sources/perplexity-2026-10-26-beginners-guide-thinking/beginners-guide-to-thinking-research-and-outline.md` (line numbers).
- **P00 / P03 / P04 / P05 / P06 / P08** = files in `~/simbook/thinking-guide/perplexity-package/` (line numbers). These are the files Perplexity received.
- **Emergenz PDF p.N** = the 2016 PDF `/mnt/c/Dropbox/DMS-Sync/Academic/book-consciousness-fmt/Die Emergenz des Bewusstseins 6x9 lit.pdf`. The Lulu page to cite is N+1 (`project-reference.md`).
- **Registry** = `.claude/knowledge/fmt-misconception-registry.md`; **Patterns** = `.claude/knowledge/didactic-patterns.md`.

**Verdict key**
- **MISREADING**: the dossier states MG's position wrongly although the supplied text was correct.
- **MISATTRIBUTION**: the dossier assigns a claim to the wrong source, or to a source that does not contain it.
- **BRIEF-INDUCED**: our own brief or excerpt caused the error.
- **FAIR**: no error.

## Findings

### 1. Thesis 3 and risk row 4: "the brain can implement every mechanism" is read as a claim of unlimited capacity
- **Dossier location.** D:127 restates the thesis as "it can model anything". D:133 says: "I reject 'the brain can implement every mechanism' as a literal established claim; the cited modelling literature does not demonstrate unlimited representational capacity, computational resources, learning access, or realizability in one finite biological system … Even a proof about an abstract universal computer would not establish feasible human implementation under finite memory, time, and training constraints". D:141 then grades it "speculative for … universal brain implementability".
- **What the source says.** The claim is about which mechanisms the substrate can realise. It says nothing about capacity. P08:18 (Bible Code, Pattern #17): "In a substrate of such high dimensionality, *every conceivable mechanism can be — and often is — implemented* … (b) support for FMT's architecture-agnosticism (any mechanism is realisable on a high-dim substrate)." The brief says the same at P00:27: "any mechanism can be implemented on a substrate of that dimensionality". In FMT the claim belongs to the free-compute / Class-4 (universal-computation) capability (Registry §3: "Class-4 (universal-computation) *capability*"). MG has also raised the finite-resource caveat himself. Emergenz PDF p.101 says a Turing machine, "im Gegensatz zu einem echten Computer, einen theoretisch unendlich großen Speicher" hat ("unlike a real computer, has a theoretically infinite memory"). Emergenz PDF p.102 adds that the halting problem means "wir können nicht von vorne herein entscheiden, ob wir erklären werden können, was wir denken" ("we cannot decide in advance whether we will be able to explain what we think"). So MG does not claim unlimited capacity, resources or learning access. The dossier refutes a position he does not hold, and it uses Clark 2013 (predictive processing) as evidence against it, which has no bearing on the question.
- **Verdict.** MISREADING. It is partly BRIEF-INDUCED, because the brief's thesis heading reads "The brain can model **everything**" (P00:27). That wording invites the capacity reading.
- **Severity.** High. This is the core of thesis 3. A repair that keeps MG's claim would read: "no mechanism *type* is excluded by the substrate; which ones a given brain actually runs is bounded by resources and learning history."

### 2. Risk row 4 and thesis 8: implementability is merged with "I can achieve anything", and the stance is treated as a claim of unlimited ability
- **Dossier location.** D:691 (risk 4) reads: "Every cognitive mechanism is implementable; 'I can achieve anything' works". It is refuted with self-efficacy and mindset meta-analyses (Stajkovic & Luthans; Macnamara & Burgoyne). D:293: "I disagree that adopting the stance establishes unlimited ability … not omnipotence". D:323: "speculative for the universal-mechanism claim".
- **What the source says.** The merge comes from our brief. P00:32 opens thesis 8 with "**Every cognitive mechanism can be implemented in the brain — use that.**" So the dossier did not invent the pairing. The misreading is what it does next. In the brief, thesis 8 claims that the *stance* "is a mechanism you can run on purpose, and it works" (P00:32). It is a claim about running a stance. It does not say the stance creates abilities. Implementability is the premise that lets the stance be run at all. The self-efficacy and mindset literature tests whether the stance improves outcomes. It says nothing about whether every mechanism can be realised. The dossier's own repair at D:691, "I do not decide in advance that I cannot learn this", is close to MG's actual meaning. Its disagreement at D:293 is aimed at a claim of omnipotence that MG never made.
- **Verdict.** BRIEF-INDUCED for the merge. MISREADING for "establishes unlimited ability" and for using the efficacy evidence against the implementability premise.
- **Severity.** High. This is risk row 4, and it would be the headline objection in a review.

### 3. The Bible Code pattern is used for only one of its two halves
- **Dossier location.** D:131 and TOC entry 19 (D:569): "A pattern can be manufactured — Use the Bible Code case to expose analytical flexibility." D:127 says the pattern "originates in the internal registry".
- **What the source says.** P08:18 (Pattern #17) says the pattern "**Cuts both ways:** (a) a caution against over-reading single-cell correlational neuroscience ('you'll always find a neuron'), and (b) support for FMT's architecture-agnosticism". The brief cited it for (b) (P00:27). The registry records the pattern but did not originate it: MG directed it on 2026-07-31.
- **Verdict.** MISREADING. It drops the half the brief asked about, and it also drops the neural application.
- **Severity.** Medium.

### 4. Thesis 1: the broadened amygdala claim is attributed jointly to the brief and to Emergenz
- **Dossier location.** D:75: "a safe, sedentary modern life leaves the amygdala under-stimulated, damaging learning, motivation, consolidation, and emotional regulation … The origin is the brief and Die Emergenz des Bewusstseins".
- **What the source says.** The 2015 box (P05:307; Emergenz PDF p.199, Lulu p.200) is much narrower. It names no learning, motivation, memory-consolidation or "sedentary" mechanism. Its comparison is between people exposed to danger and the "civilisation-spoiled" average, and its outcome is clinical: „Naturvölker, Kampfsportler, Extremkletterer, Extrembergsteiger und andere Menschen mit extremen oder gefährlichen Berufen und Hobbies haben aus diesem Grund oft ein sehr rationales Verhältnis zur ‚Angst' … im Vergleich zur ‚zivilisationsverwöhnten' Durchschnittsbevölkerung, welche häufig Opfer einer ‚unterbeschäftigten Amygdala' werden, und dann Angststörungen oder Phobien, aber auch aggressive Störungen entwickeln." Gloss: indigenous peoples, martial artists, extreme climbers and others with dangerous jobs or hobbies often have a very rational relation to fear, compared with the civilisation-spoiled average population, who often fall victim to an "under-occupied amygdala" and then develop anxiety disorders, phobias, but also aggressive disorders. The remedy the passage names is training: fear conditioning „durch (jahrelanges) Training ‚um-trainiert' werden kann" ("can be re-trained through (years of) training", P05:304). The broader list (learning, motivation, consolidation, regulation, sedentary life, physical training) comes from the 2026 brief (P00:25). The word "starved" is also the brief's.
- **Verdict.** BRIEF-INDUCED. The dossier at least names "the brief" as co-origin, so this is not pure misattribution. In the book, keep the 2015 claim and the 2026 extension separate.
- **Severity.** Medium.

### 5. Thesis 1: the actual 2015 prediction is never tested, and the amygdala critique attacks a "fear quota" MG did not propose
- **Dossier location.** The evidence in D:77–89 covers McGaugh (consolidation), BDNF, and two umbrella reviews of exercise and cognition (Ciria, Singh). D:77 adds: "this concerns pleasant as well as unpleasant arousal, not a biological requirement for danger". D:632 adds: "an isolated fear centre waiting for its daily fright".
- **What the source says.** The Emergenz claim compares a danger-exposed population with the general population on rates of anxiety disorders, phobias and aggressive disorders (see finding 4). No study in the dossier addresses that comparison. The exercise-cognition meta-analyses test a claim about cognitive gain from exercise, which appears in neither Emergenz nor, as stated, the brief. On the "fear quota" point, the passage itself already gives the amygdala a broader role: „Die Amygdala ist dabei wesentlich an Angst und Aggression beteiligt, und auch an anderen Affekt- oder Lusthandlungen, und wirkt sich auf Lern- und Konditionierungsvorgänge aus" ("involved in fear and aggression, and also in other affective or pleasure behaviours, and affects learning and conditioning", P05:300). It also says it is „Ereignisse mit Emotionen zu verknüpfen" ("linking events with emotions", P05:304). McGaugh's "pleasant or unpleasant" corrective therefore corrects a position MG did not take. The dossier's exposure and inhibitory-learning evidence (Craske 2014: new learning that competes with the old association without erasing it) is in fact compatible with MG's "years of re-training". The dossier presents it as counter-evidence.
- **Verdict.** MISREADING, by testing the wrong claim and by attacking a straw man.
- **Severity.** Medium. The "speculative" grade at D:97 rests on evidence that does not address the stated prediction.

### 6. Sample entry 04 puts a retraction in MG's mouth
- **Dossier location.** D:630: "In Die Emergenz des Bewusstseins I called the amygdala 'unterbeschäftigt': under-occupied. My stronger suspicion is…". D:636: "I do not know whether 'under-stimulation' is the right neural explanation … For now, 'starved amygdala' is my metaphor, not my diagnosis."
- **What the source says.** In 2015 MG asserted a causal, clinical claim and did not call it a metaphor (P05:307). In 2026 he restated it as a thesis to research, "do not water them down" (P00:21, P00:25). The brief does ask Perplexity to "Flag clearly where 'starved amygdala' is metaphor rather than measured" (P00:25). That licenses an *editor's* flag. It does not license a first-person retreat written as MG. The entry also leaves out the 2015 prediction itself (anxiety disorders, phobias, aggression), so the reader never learns what MG actually claimed. A smaller point: MG did not "call the amygdala" under-occupied. He said the population "falls victim to" an „unterbeschäftigte Amygdala", in scare quotes.
- **Verdict.** MISREADING. The voice puts words in MG's mouth, against the brief's explicit instruction not to soften.
- **Severity.** High. It is drafted as book prose in MG's voice and reverses his stated position.

### 7. "Dualistischer Bias" is framed as an ad-hominem against critics, and the research construct behind it is denied
- **Dossier location.** D:526: "'Dualistischer Bias' / 'dualistic bias,' the author's term — Preserve as an originating hypothesis about motivated resistance, not an established diagnostic category. A critic may reject an explanation for technical reasons; describing their alleged wish for mystery does not answer those reasons." See also D:698 (risk 11).
- **What the source says.** The label is MG's own coinage: „Ich nenne es den dualistischen Bias" (P05:103), so "the author's term" is correct for the name. The construct, however, is a population-wide motivated intuition. It is not a charge against critics: „Viele, wenn nicht die meisten Menschen wollen — bewusst oder unbewusst — dass ihre eigene Existenz, ihr Bewusstsein, ihr freier Wille, ihr Leben, ihr Gehirn, und vor allem ihr Tod etwas Geheimnisvolles bleibt. Die latente oder gegenwärtige Angst vor dem Tod ist sicher eine der Hauptursachen." Gloss: many if not most people want, consciously or not, their existence, consciousness, free will, life, brain and above all their death to stay mysterious; latent or present fear of death is surely a main cause (P05:105; Emergenz PDF p.115). The passage targets everyone, the reader included („Wenn Sie nicht akzeptieren können…", "if you cannot accept…", P05:107). It is not addressed to technical critics. The construct also has an established research literature, which the dossier did not look for. Verified via Crossref/OpenAlex 2026-10-27:
  - Forstmann, M. & Burgmer, P. (2015). Adults are intuitive mind-body dualists. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 144:222–235. doi:10.1037/xge0000045. Eight studies. Cognitive load and intuitive-thinking primes *increased* dualistic beliefs, which supports dualism as a default. The abstract concludes that "most adults remain 'essentialistic mind-body dualists' at heart" despite scientific knowledge.
  - Riekki, T., Lindeman, M. & Lipsanen, J. (2013). Conceptions about the mind-body problem and their relations to afterlife beliefs, paranormal beliefs, religiosity, and ontological confusions. *Advances in Cognitive Psychology* 9:112–120. doi:10.5709/acp-0138-5. N = 850 + 73. Reflective dualism and afterlife belief are strongly related.
  - Heflick, N. A., Goldenberg, J. L., Hart, J. & Kamp, S.-M. (2015). Death awareness and body–self dualism: A why and how of afterlife belief. *European Journal of Social Psychology* 45:267–275. doi:10.1002/ejsp.2075. Mortality salience raised afterlife belief when dualism was high or facilitated. This links fear of death to dualism, which is MG's proposed mechanism. Note the direction: the study shows dualism enabling death-driven afterlife belief, not death fear causing dualism.
  - Bloom, P. (2004). *Descartes' Baby*. Existence confirmed via OpenAlex/OpenLibrary: intuitive dualism as a developmental default. Publisher not verified here.

  "Not an established diagnostic category" is true only of the label. MG's two-part claim (a widespread intuitive dualism, motivated in part by fear of death) has direct empirical neighbours.
- **Verdict.** MISREADING. The novelty-penalty pattern applies: a new label is treated as an unsupported construct.
- **Severity.** Medium. The dossier's advice not to use the label in place of answering an objection (D:698) is still sound editorial advice.

### 8. "Monokausalität": "a default of every brain" and "emergence as non-causal" were both introduced by our own gloss
- **Dossier location.** D:530: "Reject 'a default of every brain' as untested universality and do not describe emergence as necessarily non-causal".
- **What the source says.**
  - **"Every brain".** Emergenz is graded, not universal: „Bewegung und Symmetrie zu suchen ist geradezu ein Zwang, dem **fast alle** Gehirne folgen. Und dass etwas nicht monokausal sein soll, können sich **viele** fast nicht vorstellen." Gloss: seeking movement and symmetry is almost a compulsion that *almost all* brains follow, and *many* can hardly imagine that something is not monocausal (P05:280; Emergenz PDF p.70–71). "A default of every brain" comes from our excerpt header (P05:10) and our gloss list (P05:342).
  - **Emergence as non-causal.** MG explicitly calls emergence *indirectly multicausal*. The sentence was cut from our excerpt at the "…" in P05:292. Emergenz PDF p.72: „Der Überbegriff ist ‚nicht-kausale Zusammenhänge' … Das folgende Kapitel beschreibt eine Klasse von ‚**indirekt-multikausalen**' Phänomenen, nämlich die Emergenz." Gloss: the umbrella term is "non-causal relations"; the following chapter describes a class of "indirectly multicausal" phenomena, namely emergence. Our gloss P05:342 wrote "'non-causal relations' (emergence)", which is what equated the two.
  - **"Untested universality".** A preference for fewer causes is researched. Lombrozo, T. (2007). Simplicity and probability in causal explanation. *Cognitive Psychology* 55:232–257. doi:10.1016/j.cogpsych.2006.09.006, verified via Crossref. Adults prefer explanations that invoke fewer causes and need disproportionate probabilistic evidence before favouring a more complex one. Bonawitz & Lombrozo (2007, CogSci proceedings) report the same preference in six-year-olds (79 % chose the single-cause explanation).
- **Verdict.** BRIEF-INDUCED for both overstatements. MISREADING for the "untested" label.
- **Severity.** Medium. Fix the excerpt gloss (P05:10, P05:342) before any re-run.

### 12. "Material that should not be carried over as science" (D:537) conflates sources and invents one claim
Each item is traced here to its actual source.
- **(a) "Numerical claims about universal perceptual or action frequencies".** Source: *Simulation* ch. 13 (P06:110), "roughly 40 Hz … roughly 20 Hz". It is not from the martial texts. The flag is fair, but D:537 files it under "the supplied martial or philosophical text". **MISATTRIBUTION, low.**
- **(b) "Curriculum arithmetic built from a fixed 'seven-item' capacity".** Source: Black Book ch. 8 (P03:105), "Seven happens to be a neurologically 'magic number'…". Correctly flagged. **FAIR.**

Libet appears only in *Simulation* ch. 13 (P06:112–124), where it shows that conscious access is *delayed*. ### 13. Thesis 5 cites the "Acht Wahrheiten" as the origin of "every statement can be true and false simultaneously"

Emergenz claims no dialetheism, so D:533 rebuts a claim Emergenz does not make. The link to thesis 5 comes from our brief (P00:29, "my 2015 'eight truths' in file 05").
- **Verdict.** BRIEF-INDUCED. This overlaps the logic agent's scope, so it is noted here only.
- **Severity.** Low to medium.

## Novelty penalty: places in scope where a claim was graded down because no external literature confirms it

MG's concern holds in several places in this scope. In each case the dossier treats the absence of outside confirmation as a mark against an original claim, or it never searched for the confirmation that exists.

- **Thesis 3** (D:135, D:141, D:714). FMT's architecture is graded "speculative", and the dossier asks MG to "publish exact propositions and comparative predictions". The FMT master paper already contains numbered predictions; Perplexity was not given them, so this part is brief-limited. The grade measures external confirmation, not argumentative strength.
- **Dualistic bias** (D:526). The coined label is "not an established diagnostic category", but the underlying construct is established (finding 7). No search was run.
- **Monocausality** (D:530). Labelled "untested universality" without a search (Lombrozo 2007 exists; finding 8).
- **Determinism means trainable** (D:537). A conceptual argument is put into the "needs direct studies" bin (finding 12e).
- **Thesis 8** (D:323). The proposed safeguard is graded "speculative". This grade is fair for the internal-only safeguard. It is unfair once chapter 14's external calibration is included (finding 10).
- **Counter-case, where the grade is fair.** The amygdala claim (thesis 1) is an empirical, population-level prediction. "Not yet measured" is a legitimate grade there. The problem is that the dossier tested a different claim (finding 5).
