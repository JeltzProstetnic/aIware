# AIW-357 — theses 4, 6, 7, 9: what the Perplexity dossier did with MG's ideas

Main-loop check, S362 (2026-10-27). Yardstick: MG's own wording in the brief
(`~/simbook/thinking-guide/perplexity-package/00-BRIEF.md`) and the package files; dossier at
`docs/sources/perplexity-2026-10-26-beginners-guide-thinking/beginners-guide-to-thinking-research-and-outline.md`.
Test (MG S362): an idea may be weakened or dropped only where evidence contradicts it. "No literature
found", a misreading, or an inflated restatement is not a valid reason.

## Thesis 4 — AI may cause brain atrophy

- **Brief:** "AI **may** cause brain atrophy" — a hypothesis. The later addition: using the brain as
  calculator/memory for things that can be offloaded *without loss of insight* wastes resources and
  costs creativity.
- **Dossier:** "I do not accept anatomical 'brain atrophy' as **established**" (l. 147); risk #3 (l. 690)
  repairs it to "I will not call those different outcomes a **demonstrated** loss of brain tissue".
- **Verdict: INFLATION → STRAWMAN (medium).** MG never claimed "established" or "demonstrated". The
  "may" survives the evidence the dossier lists: none of it rules out structural effects, and
  experience-dependent brain structure is itself well documented (the dossier cites Dahmani & Bohbot only
  for behaviour). The idea stays a legitimate open hypothesis; the honest label is "untested at the
  structural level", not "speculative/rejected".
- **Kept fairly:** the offloading addition — the "without loss of insight" test table (l. 176–182) and the
  learning-mode/execution-mode split are a faithful, useful development. The dossier's "fixed tank of brain
  resources" critique (l. 172) answers a cruder claim than MG's opportunity-cost wording, but its own
  conclusion ("plausible as an opportunity-cost claim", l. 174) concedes MG's actual claim.
- **Restore:** "AI may cause atrophy" stays at full strength as a hypothesis, with the functional and
  structural readings both open.

## Thesis 6 — discourse asymmetry

- **Brief:** with humans, **start** by assuming the other is right; with AI, assume everything is wrong
  **at first**.
- **Dossier:** "I disagree with treating the asymmetry as a literal rule about truth probabilities"
  (l. 241), then proposes "different first moves, same final evidential standard" (l. 245, risk #12).
- **Verdict: STRAWMAN (low).** MG's thesis is already about the starting move ("start by", "at first"),
  not truth probabilities. The dossier's "procedural" reformulation is MG's thesis, restated as a
  correction of it. Nothing suppressed in substance; just do not let the book present MG's own position as
  the critic's repair.
- **Kept fairly:** the AI-evidence table and the human/AI stage table (l. 247–253) are good material.

## Thesis 7 — always a little content and a little discontent

- **Brief:** "**Pure** happiness stops progress; **pure** discontent leads to depression." Source
  (package 03, l. 78, *Eight Pattern Wing Chun Kuen* ch. 6): "to really be able to think freely,
  contradictions have to be accepted … You should always be content … And you should always be
  discontent … Without progress there is no real happiness. And without happiness, progress is worthless."
- **Dossier:** refutes "simple happiness–performance claims" with evidence that positive affect precedes
  success (Lyubomirsky 2005, l. 263–267); risk #9 (l. 696) drops "pure" from the second half.
- **Verdict: MISREADING (medium).** The qualifier "pure" is the whole claim. Evidence that *ordinary*
  positive affect helps achievement does not touch *complete* satisfaction (no remaining discrepancy to act
  on). Its "two gauges" proposal (l. 281) is MG's thesis — both at once — presented as an alternative.
  It also dissolves the contradiction into "different objects" (l. 273). That is a legitimate reading,
  but the source deliberately says the contradiction must be *accepted* (thesis 5 applied), and the book
  may keep it as a paradox.
- **Restore:** keep "pure" in both halves and keep the paradox form as MG wrote it. The Yerkes–Dodson
  caution (l. 279) is fair.

## Thesis 9 — the universal expert is still possible

- **Brief:** the belief that it is impossible is "more false than true"; one can become "**a kind of**
  universal scientist" by focusing on the frontiers and basics, skipping useless technicalities, and
  holding one or two clear goals. Education produces workers, not scientists; science is partly paper
  mill, workforce generation and industry support.
- **Dossier:** "I reject … universal **frontier-level** expertise" (l. 329); "remove the exemption from
  learning" (l. 390); risk #5 "remove any sentence whose entire evidence is that the system failed to
  recognize the author" (l. 692); risk #6 "Perelman as shortcut mascot" (l. 693).
- **Verdict: INFLATION + INVENTED RISK (high for Perelman, medium otherwise).**
  - "A kind of universal scientist", argued as "more false than true", is weaker than the
    "frontier-level expertise in everything" the dossier rejects.
  - **Perelman appears nowhere in the brief or the package** (grep, all nine files). The dossier brings
    him in, makes him a central entry (no. 71), then warns MG against misusing him as a "patron saint of
    skipping the difficult material", in MG's voice. That is a risk the dossier created, attributed to MG.
    If MG raised Perelman to Perplexity outside the package, this verdict changes. Check.
  - **MG S362 (2026-10-27) — Perelman's intended role:** Perelman is MG's example (raised outside the
    package). Main use: *great achievements do not necessarily come from institutionalised academia, and
    in some cases cannot* (MG: "imagine perelman as a lecturer and on conferences"). The dossier used him
    for "achievement ≠ reward" (adjacent, keep as secondary) and then warned against a "shortcut mascot"
    use MG never intended (drop). **Accuracy guard for the book:** Perelman was formally employed, at
    the Steklov Institute in St Petersburg, after US postdocs, while he did the work. The defensible form
    is "outside academia's incentive machinery": three arXiv preprints (2002–03) and no journal
    submission, a withdrawal from teaching and conferences, prizes declined, and he left the institute
    afterwards. Literally "outside academia" is not accurate. Verify those biography facts against primary
    sources before drafting the entry.
  - **MG S362, second use, ties to thesis 7 and to living in the now:** Perelman's reported reply to a
    reporter, *"You are disturbing me. I am picking mushrooms."* MG: it shows he knows the priorities
    of life, which are living in the now. **Provenance (checked S362):** widely reported around the 2010
    Clay prize, second-hand (a reporter's phone call relayed in press accounts, e.g. Michael Wolff,
    HuffPost, https://www.huffpost.com/entry/you-are-disturbing-me-i-a_b_513607; Brett Forrest,
    "Shattered Genius", https://www.brettforrest.com/shattered-genius); several sources call it a legend.
    In the book: "as reported", never as a verified quotation. Pin the earliest press source before
    drafting.
  - The "grievance" framing was partly invited by the brief ("anything in thesis 9 that reads as
    grievance rather than analysis"), so it is **BRIEF-INDUCED**. But "the system failed to recognize the
    author" is an insinuation with no basis in the package.
  - "Useless technicalities": the dossier's rule (l. 386), "useless only if prospectively irrelevant to
    the question, predictions, error bounds, safety", is a fair operationalisation that MG may adopt.
- **Restore:** the "more false than true" claim and "a kind of universal scientist" at the stated
  strength. The institutional critique stays MG's, stated with the dossier's numbers, which are useful
  (paper-mill and retraction table, l. 371–382).

## Cross-cutting pattern (MG S362)

The dossier tests the **strongest restatement** ("preserve the strong versions for examination", l. 71)
rather than the stated one, and grades novelty as weakness: "thin" or "speculative" wherever no external
study exists. MG: *"if all my wisdom was already known, the book would not be needed."* Partly
**brief-induced**: our brief said "support or refute each thesis with external literature" and asked for
an evidence verdict per thesis, but it never asked for a fourth outcome, "novel: needs argument or test,
not contradicted". The consolidated report carries the revised-brief proposal.
