# Is criticality NECESSARY for efficient spiking or biological computation? Literature check

**Date:** 2026-10-07. **Scope:** builds on `~/crucible/docs/research/cru204-criticality-vs-universality-literature.md` (CRU-204, 32 sources), which settled that computational universality is proven off criticality (Siegelmann & Sontag 1995; Maass 1996 for spiking nets; the LSM theorem has no criticality term). The question here is narrower: does near-criticality (edge of chaos, avalanches, branching ratio m ≈ 1, λ ≈ 0) become *necessary*, or nearly unavoidable, once energy, noise, wiring, homeostasis, finite size or biological plausibility enter?
**Method:** every source below was checked against a Crossref, Europe PMC or arXiv record on 2026-10-07 (venue, year, volume, DOI). "Abstract" = abstract read; "record" = bibliographic record only. Sources marked "CRU-204" were verified there and are reused, not re-fetched.

---

## 1. Verdict

**No source proves, and none formally argues, that near-critical dynamics are necessary for energy-efficient spiking computation or for biological nervous systems.** Every positive result is an optimality or typicality result: dynamic range, information transmission and capacity are *maximal* at criticality (Kinouchi & Copelli 2006; Shew et al. 2009; Shew & Plenz 2013); metabolic cost per unit of propagated information is *minimal* at or near criticality in specific models (Yang et al. 2017; Aguilar-Velázquez 2021; Liang et al. 2021); and homeostatic regulation at low drive tends to *park* a recurrent network near m = 1 (Levina et al. 2007; Ma et al. 2019; Xu et al. 2024). Against this, the same homeostasis sets the network subcritical in proportion to input (Zierenberg et al. 2018), non-conserving self-organizing models are "not generically critical" (Bonachela et al. 2010), the one hardware test shows criticality *hurting* simple tasks (Cramer et al. 2020), and the same chip reaches its headline energy efficiency with a design that never invokes criticality (Göltz et al. 2021).

**The one defensible conditional necessity is about timescale, not computation or efficiency.** In a recurrent network whose activity propagates as a branching or AR(1)-like process, the intrinsic autocorrelation time is τ = −Δt / ln m, so holding an input trace over many synaptic or membrane time constants forces m within O(Δt/τ) of 1 (this is the quantity Wilting & Priesemann 2018 estimate; "reverberating for hundreds of milliseconds" is m a few percent below 1). The linear-integrator version is Seung 1996: persistent integration needs a fine-tuned marginal mode. The exact conditions are therefore: (i) the function requires reverberation or integration over timescales ≫ the elementary time constants, **and** (ii) the substrate has no slow cellular or architectural mechanism (adaptation, NMDA, short-term facilitation, neuromodulatory states, dedicated memory units) to supply them. Under (i) and (ii), near-unity recurrent gain is necessary. Condition (ii) is false for cortex and for modern spiking nets (Bellec et al. 2018 reach LSTM-level memory with adaptive thresholds), so even this reduces to "necessary for one way of implementing long memory in a plain recurrent substrate". That reading is consistent with the author's position: free modelling needs long reverberation; in tissue whose cheapest slow mechanism is recurrence, that requirement *leaves* near-criticality as its signature. It does not make the signature the requirement.

Two further points limit any stronger claim. Under continuous drive without timescale separation (cortex, sensor-driven chips) classical criticality is not a point but a band or Widom line (Williams-García et al. 2014; Fosque et al. 2021), the universality class changes and m and susceptibility stop certifying criticality (Korchinski et al. 2021). And the biological operating point is consistently *slightly subcritical* (Priesemann et al. 2014; Wilting & Priesemann 2018, 2019), the regime Wilting et al. 2018 argue is chosen for *tunability* plus a safety margin from instability, which is an argument for proximity, not for the critical point.

## 2. Evidence for (advantage, typicality, and the conditional timescale argument)

- **Functional optima at criticality.** Dynamic range is maximal at the critical branching ratio in excitable networks (Kinouchi & Copelli 2006, record) and in cortex slice cultures tuned through E/I (Shew et al. 2009, abstract). Shew & Plenz 2013 (abstract) review quantitative evidence that dynamic range, information transmission and information capacity are all optimized at criticality. Beggs & Plenz 2003 (CRU-204) showed σ ≈ 1 optimizes transmission in a *feedforward* model. Hidalgo et al. 2014 (abstract) derive that the optimal response to "broadly different changing environments" arises in systems that self-organize to the vicinity of a critical point: an optimality theorem for generalist systems, the closest thing to a formal argument found, and still "optimal", not "necessary".
- **Energy and wiring.** Yang, Zhou & Zhou 2017 (abstract): the co-emergence of irregular firing, oscillations and avalanches achieves "minimal energy cost as well as maximal energy efficiency on information capacity". Liang, Wang & Zhou 2021 (arXiv abstract): wiring-economical modular networks near a Hopf bifurcation support self-sustained avalanches at low firing cost with enhanced sensitivity. Aguilar-Velázquez 2021 (Crossref, abstract paraphrased): "critical and intermediate stability regimes show the best compromise between information propagation and reduced metabolic consumption". Simões et al. 2026 (abstract) derive that "any system governed by critical avalanches must exhibit sublinear activity–size relations", matching Kleiber-like brain allometry; note the direction: criticality ⇒ economy of scale, not economy ⇒ criticality.
- **Homeostasis makes near-criticality typical.** Levina, Herrmann & Geisel 2007 (arXiv abstract): spiking networks with realistic dynamical synapses show "robust self-organized criticality … for a wide range of interaction parameters". Ma et al. 2019 (abstract): in vivo visual cortex maintains criticality, loses it under monocular deprivation and restores it within 48 h, >30 h before firing rates recover; criticality "is consistent with a homeostatic set point". Xu et al. 2024 (abstract): waking experience progressively disrupts criticality and sleep restores it; distance to criticality predicts sleep/wake behaviour better than slow-wave activity. Hengen & Shew 2025 (CRU-204) propose criticality as a unifying setpoint across 140 datasets; Fosque et al. 2025 (bioRxiv, preprint) generalize to "critical bifurcations" and explicitly frame it as optimal, not necessary. Zeraati, Priesemann & Levina 2021 (full-text quotes) review the plasticity mechanisms.
- **Hardware and reservoirs.** Cramer et al. 2020 (CRU-204, full text): on BrainScaleS-2, long-memory and nonlinear tasks profit from approaching criticality. Hochstetter et al. 2021 (abstract): memristive nanowire networks show avalanche statistics and edge-of-chaos states optimize "complex learning tasks". Freddi et al. 2025 (abstract) assert spiking reservoirs are optimal at the edge of chaos and give a mean-field recipe to initialize there for low-power devices. Legenstein & Maass 2007 (CRU-204): the edge predicts the best parameters in spiking microcircuits.
- **The timescale argument** (§1): τ = −Δt / ln m; Seung 1996 (record, abstract paraphrased) for the integrator case; Wilting & Priesemann 2018 (abstract) for the in-vivo estimate of reverberation over hundreds of milliseconds.

## 3. Evidence against necessity

- **Task dependence, including harm.** Cramer et al. 2020 (CRU-204): "only the complex tasks profit from criticality, whereas simple tasks suffer"; "for every single task complexity, a different distance to criticality is optimal". Del Papa, Priesemann & Triesch 2017 (abstract): a self-organizing recurrent network develops criticality signatures spontaneously, but they diminish under structured input, and the noise needed for them reduces performance on simple learning tasks. Toyoizumi & Abbott 2011, Schuecker et al. 2018, Haruna & Nakajima 2019 (all CRU-204): memory optima off the edge.
- **Efficient hardware without criticality.** Göltz et al. 2021 (record + arXiv abstract): fast, energy-efficient deep learning on the same BrainScaleS-2 system via first-spike times, with no criticality or edge-of-chaos component. Merolla et al. 2014 (record): TrueNorth, a million-neuron chip running trained feedforward networks "using very little power". Bellec et al. 2018 (CRU-204): LSTM-level memory from adaptive thresholds, criticality not a design variable. Maass 1996 and the LSM theorem (CRU-204): capability by construction, no regime condition.
- **Homeostasis does not force criticality.** Zierenberg, Wilting & Priesemann 2018 (arXiv abstract): spike-rate homeostasis produces "bursting, close-to-critical, reverberating and irregular states" as input strength increases; with the in-vivo estimate that ~1 % of spikes are input-driven, the outcome is reverberating, i.e. subcritical. Bonachela et al. 2010 (abstract): networks with dissipative dynamics plus loading "are not generically critical; unless parameters are fine-tuned, their dynamics is either subcritical or supercritical, even if the pseudo-critical region is relatively broad". Zierenberg et al. 2020 (arXiv abstract): dynamic range is maximal at criticality, yet the discriminable interval "is very similar for any network tuning", so ensembles of off-critical networks can be tailored to the stimulus distribution.
- **The biological operating point is subcritical, chosen for tunability and safety.** Priesemann et al. 2014 (CRU-204): in-vivo spiking matches a driven, slightly subcritical model. Wilting & Priesemann 2018 (abstract), 2019 (arXiv abstract): "neither" asynchronous nor critical, a "narrow reverberating regime". Wilting et al. 2018 (abstract): the regime enables task-dependent tuning ("dynamic adaptive computation"); the review search also surfaced the safety-margin-from-instability framing, which is in the main texts and not re-read here. Dahmen et al. 2019 and Kanders et al. 2017 (CRU-204): different "criticalities" dissociate, so a single necessity claim has no single referent.
- **The signatures do not certify the state.** Touboul & Destexhe 2017 (CRU-204), Priesemann & Shriki 2018 (abstract: time-varying drive on Poisson units yields approximate power laws; only causal interventions distinguish true from apparent criticality), Destexhe & Touboul 2021 (abstract: non-critical systems pass the same tests), Korchinski et al. 2021 (arXiv abstract: without timescale separation, "the branching ratio and dynamic susceptibility fail to establish criticality"), Sipling, Zhang & Di Ventra 2026 (arXiv preprint: coupling to slow energetic resources alone may generate scale-invariant correlations). Beggs 2022 (abstract) answers the first two but concedes the objections "refine research questions". Hesse & Gross 2014 (abstract) and Muñoz 2018, O'Byrne & Jerbi 2022 (abstracts) all phrase the functional role as advantage or hypothesis, never as necessity.

## 4. Suggested sentence, and whether to state it

**Recommendation: do not state a necessity claim, in any form, for efficiency or for biology.** The literature does not carry it, and the author's own position (free modelling is the requirement, criticality the signature) is strictly better supported. What *can* be said, and would strengthen the "signature" framing by giving it a mechanism, is the conditional timescale remark. Suggested wording, for §3.7 or a footnote, not for the abstract:

> "Near-critical dynamics are not required for computation, and the available evidence does not show them to be required for efficient spiking computation or for nervous systems either; the functional results are optima that move with task (Cramer et al. 2020) and the biological operating point is slightly subcritical (Wilting & Priesemann 2018). What the literature does support is narrower: a recurrent network that must hold input traces over many elementary time constants, and that has no slower mechanism to do so, must keep its recurrent gain close to the point of instability, and homeostatic regulation under weak drive tends to place it there (Zierenberg et al. 2018; Ma et al. 2019). In tissue, that is one plausible reason the signature of free modelling appears as near-criticality; it is not a reason to treat the signature as the requirement."

If a shorter version is wanted: *"Criticality may be close to unavoidable for one implementation of long reverberation in a plain recurrent substrate (m ≈ 1 is the only way such a network holds a trace for τ ≫ Δt), which would explain why the biological signature takes this form; it is not a requirement of efficient spiking computation, where off-critical designs are both common and energy-efficient (Göltz et al. 2021), nor of computation in general."*
