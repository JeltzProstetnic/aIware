## Q1 — Where Toker et al. 2022 put waking
Toker D, Pappas I, Lendner JD, et al. 2022, PNAS 119(7):e2024455119, doi:10.1073/pnas.2024455119.
- Measure: modified 0-1 chaos test (K), signal low-passed at a FOOOF cutoff (1–6 Hz, mean 3.27 Hz); median over channels → trial → subject. Higher K = more chaotic.
- SI Fig. S6 (per-subject medians, read by eye): waking K ≈ 0.67–0.87 (median ≈ 0.77); LSD ≈ 0.61–0.78 (≈ 0.71); propofol ≈ 0.82–0.92 (≈ 0.89); generalised seizure 0.15, 0.24, 0.51. SI: *"little overlap across conditions, with the exception of partial overlap between normal waking and LSD states."*
- No empirical K value is identified as the edge; the edge (LLE = 0) is defined only in the model. "Chaotic side" for waking is inferred from the model and from the direction of the LSD shift: *"our primary empirical evidence that waking … lie[s] on the chaotic side … as it implies that LSD is tuning slow cortical dynamics toward edge-of-chaos criticality from the chaotic side."*
- The authors' own hedge: *"the question of what 'side' of this critical point normal waking brain dynamics operate near, and how this might vary across different parts of the brain, is far from settled."* K variance is largest near criticality (Fig. S12), with a stated risk of misclassifying waking as periodic or chaotic.
- No within-waking analysis (eyes open/closed, drowsiness). Macaques eyes-open only; ECoG baselines were pre-induction in surgical patients; the LSD placebo baseline was eyes-closed rest.
- Terminology: SI Note 1 calls chaos the *"ordered"* phase with respect to edge-of-chaos criticality (broken topological supersymmetry). ⇒ An axis labelled "ordered/periodic ← → chaotic" contradicts Toker; use "periodic/stable ← edge → chaotic/unstable".
- Unverified side note: Toker's main-text citation for the LSD data (ref. 100, Muthukumaraswamy 2013) is a psilocybin paper; the SI correctly cites Carhart-Harris 2016.

## Q2 — Waking slightly subcritical on another axis (branching ratio)
- Priesemann et al. 2014, Front Syst Neurosci 8:108, doi:10.3389/fnsys.2014.00108 — *"driven, slightly sub-critical"* (α ≈ 0.98).
- Wilting & Priesemann 2018, Nat Commun 9:2325, doi:10.1038/s41467-018-04725-4 — m̂ 0.963–0.998, median 0.984, *"reverberating state"*.
- Wilting & Priesemann 2019, Cereb Cortex 29(6):2759–2770, doi:10.1093/cercor/bhz049 — median m̂ = 0.98.
- Priesemann et al. 2013, PLoS Comput Biol 9:e1002985, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002985 — human intracranial: subcritical; **SWS closer to criticality than waking, REM farther** (on the branching axis this runs against a simple level-of-consciousness reading).
- Different axis: m is avalanche/branching criticality (m = 1); Toker's K is perturbation sensitivity of slow oscillations. No paper measures both on the same data. Kanders, Lorimer & Stoop 2017, Chaos 27:047408, doi:10.1063/1.4978998: avalanche criticality does not entail edge-of-chaos criticality.

## Q3 — High-dose LSD and unconsciousness
- Klock et al. 1974 West J Med 120:183–188 (PMID 4816396) / 1975 Clin Toxicol 8(2):191–203, doi:10.3109/15563657508988063 — massive intranasal LSD: coma with hyperthermia, respiratory arrest, bleeding; all recovered. **Abstract only.** Toxic/medical coma, not a 5-HT2A analogue of anaesthesia.
- Haden & Woods 2020, J Stud Alcohol Drugs 81(1):115–118, doi:10.15288/jsad.2020.81.115 — 55 mg case "blacked out" ~12 h while sitting, eyes open/closed, occasional words: reduced responsiveness + amnesia, not documented unconsciousness. Anecdotal, doses approximate.
- Research view: psychedelics *raise* signal diversity (Schartner et al. 2017, Sci Rep 7:46421, doi:10.1038/srep46421); extreme-dose unresponsiveness = possibly disconnected, not absent, consciousness (Sanders et al. 2012, Anesthesiology 116:946–959, doi:10.1097/ALN.0b013e318249d0a7; Timmermann et al. 2025, Neurosci Conscious niaf007, doi:10.1093/nc/niaf007). Blackburne et al. 2025 Cell Rep (doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2025.116040, abstract only): slow waves under 5-MeO-DMT with reported consciousness.
- Toker's LSD data: 75 µg IV, MEG ~4 h post-dose, eyes-closed rest, subjects responsive (in-scanner ratings) — Carhart-Harris et al. 2016, PNAS 113:4853–4858, doi:10.1073/pnas.1518377113 (details via page summary).

## Consequences for Fig 1a
1. Waking and LSD regions overlap (SI-confirmed partial overlap).
2. Waking may be drawn mostly on the chaotic side but reaching across the edge, matching the authors' "far from settled".
3. LSD label carries the dose (75 µg); high-dose LSD is not placeable on this axis — no measurement exists.
4. Axis end labels: "periodic / stable" and "chaotic / unstable", not "ordered".
