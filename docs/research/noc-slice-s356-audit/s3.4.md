Why, then, is cortex near-critical, and why does the set-point co-vary with conscious level? Optimisation explains the first: critical dynamics maximise dynamic range (Shew et al., 2009), susceptibility and correlation length (Chialvo, 2010), and homeostasis tunes toward them (Ma et al., 2019; Hengen & Shew, 2025). The four-model theory adds a reason for the second that leaves the optimisation account intact: free modelling, the enabling condition of Section 3.1, recruits in a human brain far more tissue than the modelling task needs and keeps survival work running alongside, and that recruitment is what shows as near-critical dynamics across the cortex. When the explicit models are abolished, as under propofol or in deep sleep, the recruitment ends and the dynamics usually settle toward the stable side; when they return, the signature returns with them. The correlation the literature has recorded is, on this reading, the trace free modelling leaves in one kind of substrate under loads of a certain size, which is why it announces no cognitive advantage of its own, and why the theory allows it to fail at both margins: a near-critical cortex running unconscious modelling, and a conscious system small or efficient enough to model freely below the signature's threshold.
