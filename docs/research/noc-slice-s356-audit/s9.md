Is there more to consciousness than base computation? Yes, and the more is made of computation: a second-order regime in which computation operates on computation, closed upon a self-model that takes itself for the being it occupies. The enabling claim is a necessity: this regime requires free modelling, open-ended computation that the system recruits for its own models, and a system without it has no consciousness whatever its dynamics. In cortex free modelling usually shows as near-critical dynamics, so the field's decades-old criticality signature reads as the trace of the enabling condition in one substrate, present where human conscious processing is and allowed to be absent in smaller or differently built systems that meet the same requirement. The constitutive claim says what experience is: the integration of implicit into explicit models and the self-model's conflation of itself with its bearer. The optimisation programme explained why criticality is useful; the four-model theory adds what the criticality literature was measuring, and puts the requirement itself at risk: find robust consciousness in a system that a matched feedforward model keeps pace with on novel input of growing compositional depth, and the necessity claim is wrong.
