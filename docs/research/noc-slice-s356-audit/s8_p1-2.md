The requirement is stated at the level that is substrate-neutral: open-ended computation recruited for autonomous self-modelling. A neural substrate meeting it usually exhibits near-critical dynamics, because of how much tissue a brain recruits and how it manages the energy of doing so; a digital realisation of the same second-order computation can meet it with none of the avalanche statistics of cortex, because its open-endedness is supplied architecturally; and a small nervous system meeting it may do so below the signature's threshold. A separate treatment develops the substrate-neutral side directly, testing closure and open-ended computation as enabling conditions for world-modelling in an artificial architecture (Gruber, 2026b). The two treatments describe one condition at two grains, and the neural signature is the local, contingent appearance of the condition in one of them.
