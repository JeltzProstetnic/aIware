# MG's Intelligence-Maximizing Memes — a collection, opened 2026-08-05

Matthias's own cognitive tools: the general-purpose thinking habits he has built and automated over
decades. **Started at his explicit instruction (S285, 2026-08-05): "this is one of the
intelligence maximizing memes i use btw, and we need to start collecting them when they come to my
mind."**

> ## ⚠ PERSONAL-MATERIAL CAUTION — binding, and it governs this whole file
>
> Tools 2, 6 and 7 originate in MG's childhood and involve depression, aggression and dissociation.
> MG himself noted of the age-10 conversion that *"many children do similar things with sometimes fatal
> consequences."* **Nothing from those entries goes into a poster, preprint, talk, handout, blog or
> social post without MG's explicit, specific consent for that artefact.** Flagged precisely *because*
> the origin story would make unusually strong MoC7 material — that is the pressure this caution exists
> to resist. The decision is MG's alone; it is not aIware's and not social's.

## Why this file has to exist, and why it will fill slowly

MG: *"they are deeply buried and automated, so it is a rare thing for one to resurface after so many
decades."*

**That is not an aside — it is a prediction his own framework makes, and it dictates the collection
method.** By his definition of knowledge (S285d.1, `docs/decisions.md`), knowledge *is* information
pulled close to the explicit self-model and then committed to automation and removal from
consciousness. His best thinking tools are therefore knowledge in the strict sense: **wired, not
running.** They cannot be introspected out on demand, because being un-introspectable is what
finished them. It is the same structure as the derived result that experts are frequently bad
teachers — their knowledge is no longer in a transmissible format — applied by MG to himself.

**So the protocol is behavioural, not introspective.** Do not ask him to enumerate his thinking
tools; the request cannot be answered by design. Instead:

1. **Catch them when they fire.** They surface as asides during real work — "this is one of the memes
   I use", "I took that from X", "you have to always...". Capture on the spot, in his words, verbatim.
2. **Verbatim first, unpacking second.** The phrasing carries the tool; a tidied paraphrase can lose
   the operative part. Quote, then analyse below the quote.
3. **Log where it fired.** A tool caught in the act comes with a worked example for free, and the
   example is what makes it teachable to anyone else.
4. **Name the failure mode.** A tool with no stated failure mode is a slogan. Every entry needs the
   conditions under which applying it makes things worse.
5. **State the precondition.** Added 2026-08-05 (see the cost principle below). Every entry declares
   what machinery the operator must already have, because without it the entry oversells.

**One working-method note, from crucible, and it generalises.** Crucible proposed two clean binaries
for MG's examples (direct/indirect, then structural/not) and MG corrected both. The reason is
structural: **MG is not reasoning from theory down to examples — the examples are the origin and the
theory is their compression.** Imposing a theory-shaped cut on the data that generated the theory keeps
producing wrong axes. Treat MG's worked examples as **primary data** and derive the axis from the set.

## The cost principle — read this before any entry

**MG:** *"also the cost of doing martial arts vs coffee or drug entzug … normally you have hunger or
fear and using the body intensely solves the problem. or you die. thats the subjectively easy path.
stopping a habit is way harder. long term i mean."*

**Subjective cost tracks NATIVE-PATH availability, not physical demand.** Martial arts is physically
brutal and subjectively *easy*, because fear/hunger → intense bodily action → resolution is machinery
the organism is built around, so the tool **rides** it. Not drinking coffee is physically trivial and
subjectively *brutal*, because **nothing in the machinery implements "stop doing X, repeatedly, over
time"** — there is no primitive for sustained non-action, so the tool must **oppose** the substrate.

**And it is INDIVIDUAL, not only species-level** (MG's correction, 2026-08-05 final addendum). The
sadness→anger conversion was cheap *for MG specifically* because that subsystem was already in surplus;
for an operator without that surplus the same tool is expensive or unavailable outright.

> **A thinking tool's cost is a function of the operator's existing machinery, not of the tool.**
> *"Here is a thing that worked for MG"* and *"here is a thing that will work for you"* are different
> claims, and the gap between them is exactly this. **Any presentation implying transferable technique
> without stating the precondition is overselling it** — which, for a file of intelligence-maximizing
> memes, is the failure mode most worth designing against.

**In FMT's own vocabulary this is variable permeability in the cost domain.** The explicit layer's
leverage over the substrate is not uniform: cheap where it recruits existing machinery, expensive where
it opposes it. A claim FMT already makes structurally (v14 §6.0), here with a human-scale demonstration.

## The shape of the collection

Two entries bracket the rest, and they are not peers of the instances:

- **Tool 2 is the root** — *treat yourself as a machine with parts, dynamics and entry conditions.* It
  is the generative move of which Tools 3–7 are instances, and by MG's own account it is **FMT's
  origin**. It opens the collection logically even though it is numbered second.
- **Tool 8 is the bracket that closes it** — *seek consequential external feedback.* Every other tool
  has the same single point of failure, the accuracy of the self-model, and none of them can supply it.

**And the collection is not a set of solo techniques.** Roughly half of MG's own examples route through
other people or the world; Tool 8's extension is explicitly *someone else's level-2 acting directly on
your level-1*. An entry that read as "things you do to yourself alone" would misrepresent the set.

**Cross-reference to crucible's numbering.** Crucible's `docs/decisions.md` (2026-08-05, §3c–3q) uses
letters. The mapping, so citations survive: **Tool 3 = crucible Tool A** (§3f) · **Tool 4 = Tool B1**
(§3c) · **Tool 5 = Tool B2 / "Tool D"** (§3k) · **Tool 6 = Tool C** (§3i, corrected §3q) · **Tool 7 =
the age-11 constructive rule** (§3m) · **Tool 2 = the meta-tool** (§3l) · **Tool 8 = the capstone**
(§3o, extended §3p).

## Tool 1 — Two sides, therefore dimensions

**In MG's words (2026-08-05):**

> i start with the idea that every fact or theory or claim or basically everything must have two
> sides, one on which it is true and one false, one good, one bad. took that from taoism. then
> follows that everything is dimensional and you can start developing a habit of forming dimensional
> models automatically for everything so you unbias yourself massively and start combining dimensions
> and geometry instead of single biased terms in your cognition.

### The move, unpacked

The chain is short and each link does work:

1. **The Taoist premise.** Every claim has a side on which it is true and a side on which it is
   false; a good side and a bad side.
2. **The inference that does the lifting.** If a claim has two poles, it is **not a point — it is a
   position on an axis**, and the axis has to be named. This converts *"X is true"* into *"X is true
   in region R of dimension D"* — a term becomes a geometry.
3. **The habit.** Build the axis automatically, for everything, until it is the default.
4. **The payoff.** You reason with **dimensions and geometry** instead of **single biased terms**.

### Why it debiases — four distinct mechanisms, not one

- **Terms smuggle valence; axes do not.** "Necessary" carries its own verdict. *"Advantage as a
  function of task-fit"* carries a curve you have to look at. The word decides for you; the axis makes
  you measure.
- **Dimensions compose; terms collide.** Two terms give you an argument. Two axes give you a plane —
  with four quadrants, and usually one that nobody occupies.
- **A space has empty regions; a term has none.** This is the whole reason a dimensional model can
  *predict*: Mendeleev's blank exists because he built a space rather than a list. Term-based thinking
  structurally cannot find gaps.
- **It recruits hardware you already have.** MG's own statement of the mechanism, same session:
  *"a clear dimensional model can help massively in understanding, it enables humans to apply their
  spatial processing cortex to a semantic problem."* Which is, in FMT's own vocabulary, **redeployment
  — re-pointing an already-rich model (spatial/visual) at a non-native referent** (didactic pattern
  #26/#20). *The tool is an instance of the mechanism FMT says intelligence runs on.* One model, K
  deployments.

**Rides or opposes:** rides — it recruits spatial cortex, which is why it is cheap.
**Precondition:** strong spatial/visual processing to redeploy. An operator without it gets the
bookkeeping and not the intuition.

### Where it fired — worked examples from this project

| Where | The term it replaced | The axis it built |
|---|---|---|
| FMT's core taxonomy | "four models" (reads as four modules) | the 2×2: **wired/running × world/self** |
| Graded consciousness | "is it conscious?" | the **ladder**, R0–R7, with placement |
| Criticality | "the brain is critical" | **two dials** — extent × complexity |
| The closure correction | "closure is *necessary*" | **efficiency in a fit window** — a curve, not a verdict |
| The philosophical orientation (S285) | "are you a dualist?" | **the argument space** — residue size × substrate constraint (`figures/smoc-philosophy-map.svg`) |
| The hard problem's residue (S285) | "the hard problem" | **the size of the Presence Term** — an axis every theory has a value on |

The pattern across all six: **the biased single term was the thing blocking progress, and naming the
axis it lived on unblocked it.** In the closure case this is measurable — the necessity framing cost
crucible a run of experiments that were scored on the wrong side of the question, and the dimensional
form (*cost at matched function*) is what corrected it.

### Failure modes — the tool has two, and both have already bitten this project

1. **Forcing an axis where there is a genuine kind-distinction.** Not everything is a continuum. Some
   contrasts are categorical, and stretching them into a dimension manufactures false middles. FMT's
   own 2×2 carries this in miniature: *wired/running* is a real orthogonal contrast, *world/self* is a
   **nesting** — and pretending the second is an axis like the first is an error the theory has to
   correct explicitly every time it is taught.
2. **Choosing the axis that does not carry the variance.** The tool tells you to build an axis; it does
   not tell you *which*, and the wrong axis is worse than no axis because it looks rigorous. Two
   instances on record: the closure-advantage axis was first drawn on **recursion depth** when the
   signal was on **redeployed-model richness** (corrected 2026-08-01 — depth was the confound); and a
   result was certified by two careful controls that both varied **rank** while the confound governing
   the effect was **redundancy** (crucible pattern 46). Both controls were real; both were blind.
   **Discipline that follows: having named an axis, ask whether it is the one carrying the variance —
   and prefer axes you can measure over axes that are merely nameable.**

## Tool 2 — The machine stance *(the root; ⚠ personal material)*

**MG, confirming it (2026-08-05):** *"indeed, that was the first realization right after the
depression->anger experiment and what later led to the first pre-FMT ideas when i turned 14."*

The tool: **treat yourself as a machine with parts, dynamics and entry conditions, and intervene at
whichever level gives you a lever.**

### Why it is the root and not the fourth entry

The sequence MG gives is a derivation, not a biography:

| age | | |
|---|---|---|
| **10** | the sadness→anger conversion (Tool 6) | a self-modification that **worked** |
| **immediately after** | *I am a machine with parts and dynamics, and I can intervene on it* | **the generalization** |
| **~11** | *invent a mechanism, believe it, run it, and it becomes real* (Tool 7) | **the rule** |
| **14** | the first pre-FMT ideas | **the theory** |

**⇒ FMT descends from the practice, not the other way around.** Its seed is a generalization from a
self-modification that succeeded — and the seed content is already the core claim: **the self is a
model of a machine, held by that machine, and usable as a CONTROL INTERFACE.** Not a passive
representation but a control surface, whose existence is what makes self-modification possible.

That is the same proposition as MG's *"i can do that because i know that i can change my self! that is
only possible with a self"* — **the biography and the theory are one claim at two scales.**

**Rides or opposes:** neither — it is the stance the others are executed from.
**Precondition:** a self-model with **parts**, not merely states. You cannot address "sub-machine x"
without a functional decomposition of yourself.
**Failure mode:** the decomposition can be wrong, and a wrong functional attribution propagates into
every tool built on it. This is exactly what Tool 8 exists to correct.

## Tool 3 — Give the part a different job *(crucible Tool A)*

**MG:** *"it would be like a game of life machine that has multiple sub machines and some day sudednly
decides to dial down sub-machine x. like i did as a child: give the amygdala something to do (martial
arts) and get rid of fears."*

**The move: indirect self-modification, routed through the world.** You do not edit the subsystem. You
select an *action in the world* whose downstream effect on a named subsystem you can predict, and
ordinary plasticity does the rest.

What makes it the strongest of the set structurally: it needs **no exotic machinery** — no
weight-editing operation to invent — while demanding *more* of the self-model, because a list of
internal patterns is not enough. The model must carry **parts with functions**.

**Rides or opposes:** **rides**, and this is the cleanest case. Fear → intense bodily action →
resolution is machinery the organism is built around, which is why something physically brutal is
subjectively easy.
**Precondition:** the target subsystem must have a native path that some available action recruits.
**Failure mode:** trains the wrong thing if the functional attribution is wrong — you can spend years
retraining a part that was not the one doing it.

## Tool 4 — Delete the antecedent *(crucible Tool B1)*

**MG:** *"i see that i drink too much coffe, i break my habit by focusing my attention on patterns that
precede my drinking coffe and can delete them."*

Not the behaviour — its **antecedents**. Identify the internal patterns that reliably precede the
action, and suppress those. It requires a represented **causal-antecedent relation** between internal
states and an action, which is compositional analytic work on the explicit layer.

**The sharp part, and it is worth keeping in view:** occupancy-driven plasticity *strengthens what
recurs* — that is what Hebbian learning is. This intervention **weakens exactly the pattern that
recurs, because it recurs.** The symbolic route is anti-correlated with the dumb route on the very
pattern it targets, which no occupancy-driven mechanism can produce.

**Rides or opposes:** **opposes** — nothing in the machinery implements sustained non-action. This is
the expensive one, and its expense is the cost principle's headline case.
**Precondition:** internal states available to the self-model **as objects**, and enough attentional
control to act on them repeatedly over a long horizon.
**Failure mode:** burns sustained effort on a misidentified antecedent, with no early signal that it is
the wrong one — the tool's cost profile hides its own failure.

## Tool 5 — Refuse this instance *(crucible Tool B2 / "Tool D")*

**MG:** *"short term: i am not drinking THIS coffee to demonstrate to myself that i have free will and
AM doing sth. is cheap so there are actually two different coffee cases."*

**This is not Tool 4 at a shorter horizon. It is a different tool with a different payoff.**

| | **Tool 4 — delete the habit** | **Tool 5 — refuse this instance** |
|---|---|---|
| horizon | long-term, sustained | a single act |
| cost | **high** — opposes with no native path | **cheap** |
| target | the antecedent pattern | ***nothing structural*** |
| **purpose** | change the behaviour | **generate evidence, for the self-model, that it can act** |

Its payoff is **epistemic and about the self**: perform an act whose only function is to *observe that
you can perform it*, and let the observation update the self-model. That is **the self-model running an
experiment on its own causal efficacy** — and it explains the cheapness, because the move has high
evidential value per unit cost. You cannot afford to test your agency by quitting for a month; you can
afford one refusal.

**Why crucible flags it as the most FMT-relevant of the set:** it is arguably the purest
compute-on-compute instance available — level-2 work whose entire payoff is information about level-2's
own efficacy, costing the substrate nothing it was not already spending. And it is the closest thing in
MG's collection to *reporting a self*: a system acting to verify its own agency, with nobody having
supplied the concept of self.

**Rides or opposes:** neither, and that is why it is cheap — it asks nothing of the substrate.
**Precondition:** a self-model that can hold a proposition about its own efficacy and update it.
**Failure mode — the important part.** The token act can **substitute** for the expensive work. The
demonstration becomes the point, the self-model updates *"I am someone who acts"*, the feeling of agency
is discharged, and the habit survives untouched. **Cheap self-evidence is cheap precisely because it is
not the change.** (Adjacent to the moral-licensing / symbolic-self-completion literature if an external
anchor is ever wanted.)

## Tool 6 — Reroute the state by authoring its entry condition *(crucible Tool C; ⚠ personal material)*

**MG (age 10):** *"i decided sadness is even worse than anger, so i learned to convert sadness into
anger by stipulating something or someone to be responsible for my sadness and then using the anger to
generate the energy to do sth about whatever was causing my sadness, knowing that sadness leads to
passivity and a death spiral."*

Nothing is deleted and nothing is retrained. A state that would run one way is **rerouted** to run
another.

**⇒ Why this is the strictly symbolic one.** Anger has a **representational entry condition** — to be
angry you need something to be angry *at*. Sadness does not. The conversion works by **authoring the
missing semantic argument**: stipulating a responsible party. That is not attention and not practice;
it is *constructing a proposition in order to satisfy the entry condition of a different processing
regime.* No amount of attentional reallocation or repetition can produce it, because what is required
is **content**.

**⇒ And it is model-based control over TRAJECTORIES, not over states.** *"knowing that sadness leads to
passivity and a death spiral"* — the model predicts where a state *goes*, and the intervention is
chosen against that prediction. Tools 3 and 4 target a part or a pattern; this targets a trajectory.

**⇒ It also unites the two senses of "free".** The stipulation need not be veridical, and MG knows it is
a stipulation. It is **free modelling** (anything can be integrated into a model) deployed
instrumentally on the self, to produce a **free-compute** effect. The freedom to model *anything* is
precisely what makes the conversion available.

**⚠ CORRECTION, MG 2026-08-05:** *"btw. obviously at first around age 9 or so aggression got almost to
the point they had to take me out of the volksschule LOL."* The anger machinery was **already running
hot a year before the conversion was invented**. So the tool did not manufacture a state — it
**repurposed an existing surplus**, which puts it structurally much closer to Tool 3 (*give the part a
different job*) than first recorded: directing something already overactive, not producing something
from nothing.

**Rides or opposes:** **rides — but only for this operator.** This is the entry that forced the cost
principle to become individual.
**Precondition:** an existing surplus in the target regime, plus a model of one's own state-transition
dynamics. Without the surplus the tool is expensive or unavailable.
**Failure mode:** the stipulation calcifies if never checked — an invented responsible party becomes a
standing belief about a real person. MG's own note that the mechanism has fatal variants in other
children is the strong form of this.

## Tool 7 — Invent it, run it, and it becomes structural *(the age-11 rule; ⚠ personal material)*

**MG, on how he put it at ~11:** *"everything you do with your thoughts makes an imprint and becomes
'true' after a while, so i can invent a mental mechanism, believe in it, keep 'doing' it and it will
start to actually work after a while. i can immediately develop a second person in my brain and become
a scizophrenic, ill not do that. fortunately. i didnt even try- it would have worked."*

**Two things make this the largest claim in the collection.**

First, *"everything you do with your thoughts makes an imprint and becomes true after a while"* is
**the imprint principle, stated at eleven** — the same object the crucible programme has spent months
on.

Second, and more important: **Tools 3, 4 and 6 are CORRECTIVE** — fix a part, delete an antecedent,
reroute a state. **This one is CONSTRUCTIVE:** install machinery that was not there, by running a
fiction until repetition makes it structural. That is a strictly larger claim about the substrate's
plasticity, and it is what a *modelling* account of selfhood predicts and a thing-based account does
not.

**The tulpa clause is an FMT prediction, and it has an accidental measurement.** Taken at face value,
*"i can immediately develop a second person in my brain … it would have worked"* claims that the
machinery for "a person" is instantiable by sustained deliberate practice — i.e. selfhood is a modelling
structure, not a thing, so running a second modelling loop consistently yields a second self-model with
its own closure. **That is the same claim as CRU-58**, where the "closure OFF" arm was shown at N=22,000
not to remove a self-loop but to decompose one closed system into **two** (ON = 1 strongly-connected
component, OFF = 2; `dominant_loop_mode` returns a cleanly localized real-positive self-loop on OFF's
ESM and VOID on ON). **The ablated condition has two selves, not zero** — the second-person condition
was built by accident and called an ablation. An eleven-year-old's intuition and the project's
generic-attractor thesis are the same proposition arrived at ~30 years apart from opposite directions.

**Rides or opposes:** rides the substrate's ordinary plasticity — which is exactly why it is dangerous.
**Precondition:** sustained, consistent repetition. The tool is cheap per unit and expensive in
aggregate.
**Failure mode, which MG demonstrated by declining:** the method installs **whatever you run**, not only
what you intended. *Everything you do with your thoughts makes an imprint* cuts both ways — rumination
installs rumination, an invented persecutor becomes a real one. **The tool has no built-in target
discrimination; the discrimination has to come from the operator.**

## Tool 8 — Seek consequential external feedback *(the bracket that closes the set)*

**MG:** *"actively seeking feedback at the workplace is the ultimate meta of this, just to complete
that end."*

**Every other tool has the same single point of failure: the accuracy of the self-model.** Tool 3
trains the wrong thing on a wrong functional attribution. Tool 4 burns effort on a misidentified
antecedent. Tool 6's stipulation calcifies if never checked. Tool 7 installs whatever you run and has
no target discrimination of its own. **The binding constraint on the entire apparatus is model accuracy
— and none of the tools can supply it.**

**⇒ Why it is the meta rather than a ninth peer: it does not operate on the substrate at all. It
operates on the model every other tool depends on.** It is the toolkit's calibration step.

**⇒ And it rests on a structural claim worth stating on its own: a closed loop cannot validate itself.**
The self-model's predictions are generated by the very thing under test, so **the error signal for a
self-model must come from outside the self.** Introspection is not merely unreliable here; it is the
wrong *kind* of channel. *"At the workplace"* is the operative detail — consequential, repeated feedback
from people who are not managing your feelings.

### ⚠ MG's extension, and it is stronger than the feedback framing

**MG:** *"exactly. your colleagues will touch your substrate for you. some more gentle, some less. or
the therapist."*

The entry above treats other people as an **external error signal on the self-model** — i.e.
information. MG's point is stronger: **the interaction IS the intervention.** Colleagues, therapists
and partners do not merely tell you what you are; they **change what you are**, intended or not.

**So the collection needs a row it did not have: someone else's level-2 acting directly on YOUR
level-1** — teaching, therapy, close relationships — distinct from both (a) your own tools acting on
your own substrate and (b) feedback correcting your model. **It is arguably the most powerful route in
the set**, because the other person's model of you sits *outside* your closed loop and therefore has
exactly the validation property this entry identifies: a closed loop cannot validate itself, but
another loop can.

**The gentleness axis is a real dimension and belongs here.** The operation varies in force, skill and
consent — deliberate and consented in therapy, neither in a hostile workplace, constant and high-access
in intimacy — **and cost/harm track those rather than the magnitude of the change.**

**Rides or opposes:** neither — it runs outside the operator entirely.
**Precondition:** access to a channel where the feedback is consequential and the giver is not managing
your feelings. Most available channels fail this.
**Failure modes (two).** Feedback is noisy, political and often self-serving, so seeking it
indiscriminately imports other people's models of you, which may be worse than your own. And it goes
**performative** — sought to *signal* openness rather than to update — at which point it costs social
capital and returns nothing. **The tool requires a filter it does not itself supply.**
