**v8 (2026-10-08)**

Supersedes v7 (2026-10-07).

*Class 5 and initial data (§2.2, §2.4).* Ontic randomness can enter through an inexpressible rule or through inexpressible initial data: a one-line shift rule carries random initial data forward unchanged (Brudno, 1978). In a bounded system initial data are finite, which closes the second route; in a quasi-infinite universe it stays open, and the abductive Class 5 elimination of §3.2 covers both routes.

*Class 3 elimination (§3.2).* New paragraph answering the objection that a reducible universe could look irreducible from inside. The elimination rests on universality: a universal system any cell of whose state at time t is computable in time polynomial in log t and in the size of the initial data would decide exponential-time problems in polynomial time, which the time hierarchy theorem excludes (Hartmanis & Stearns, 1965). For weaker shortcuts the step from universality to irreducibility is stated as a heuristic.

*Limits of self-description (§6.4).* Capacity, irreducibility and the distinction between a description and its instance are separated from Gödelian incompleteness. §2.5 and §9.5 now speak of cross-scale correspondence and shared boundary structure, matching §7.3.

*Scope of the cosmology–cognition correspondence (§7.3, §9.4).* The correspondence covers the implicit/explicit axis of the Four-Model Theory, a shared computational class and a shared closure structure. The world/self axis has no cosmological counterpart, since the theory draws the world/self line by the difference between direct and indirect feedback, which requires an outside.

Two references added: Brudno (1978); Hartmanis & Stearns (1965).
