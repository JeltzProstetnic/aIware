Computational theories of conscious experience face a dilemma. Without a counterfactual condition on implementation, triviality arguments in the style of Putnam and Searle make every open system implement every computation. With such a condition, Maudlin's Olympia makes idle machinery matter to what is experienced. This paper defends a third exit for a conditional claim: if a simulated organism has experience, a static record of its whole trajectory has the same experience. Three conditions are kept apart. A trajectory is realized when its states are present in some medium and can be recovered one by one by a decoder that never computes one state from another; a rule together with an initial state fixes a trajectory without realizing it. A realized trajectory has internal dependency structure when a rule much shorter than the trajectory relates its successive states. This is a structural relation among realized states, distinct from causal production, and it is defended against Barnes's causal-history condition. Whether a realized, dependent structure has an inside is decided by a candidate architecture, here the closure of self-model and world-model proposed by the Four-Model Theory. Triviality is answered without counterfactuals: a mapping that does any work is itself an object in the world, and algorithmic information records where a trajectory is stored, so the pixie a Putnam mapping finds in a rock is stored in the mapping. The proposal differs from Millhouse's simplicity criterion and Aaronson's reduction-cost argument in applying to records with no counterfactual structure and in reading complexity as location, and from Marchal's movie-graph argument and Tegmark's mathematical universe in requiring realization. The conditional has a definite denial. A record and a run hold the same states and relations, so if the record lacks experience, what it lacks is a physical property of actual unfolding and not a structural one. Which horn holds depends on whether physical time involves genuine becoming and causation is primitive production, and no experiment on a conscious system decides it.

Keywords: implementation; triviality arguments; counterfactuals; Olympia; recordings; algorithmic information; computational functionalism; self-model
