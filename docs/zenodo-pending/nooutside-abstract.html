Mathematics and logic are languages in which embedded observers serialize the regularities of their models of the world. Every observer that uses them is a proper part of what it describes. This book argues that the limits that matter in mathematics, logic and physics follow from that position, and that they hold for any embedded perspective, human, animal or machine, not only for human minds.

Two limits hold without conditions: no perspective holds its own current generation, as a whole, as content, and no part can certify its own description of the whole complete. Others hold wherever their conditions hold: capacity where the whole is finite and incompressible, irreducibility where no shortcut exists, incompleteness for consistent systems that can express arithmetic. Read from inside, these limits work as refutations of theories that require a view from outside the universe.

The book applies this to the asymmetry between establishing truth and establishing falsity, to the scope of quantum mechanics and the certification of randomness, to time, reversibility and recurrence, to infinity and observer counts in cosmology, and to the question whether a world laid out statically on a disk has an inside. On that last question it identifies a split that physics, not the theory of consciousness, would have to decide.
