Zheng et al. (2026) propose recurrency as the implementation-level feature shared by the major theories of consciousness, and leave open where along the continuum of causal closure it becomes sufficient, and by which measure. Central pattern generators time each next state from the circuit's own trajectory, so on the glossary's measure they are deeply closed; the article excludes them because they sustain no perceptual hypothesis and represent nothing. One formalisation the glossary names, non-trivial information closure, draws the same line by asking whether the closed dynamics encode the environment. The criterion therefore turns on what the loop represents. The Four-Model Theory adds one condition: recurrency becomes relevant to consciousness when the loop re-conditions a world-model that contains a model of the system itself, and the self-model's output changes the system's next state. Self-modelling can then be treated as a dimension that cuts across the four levels of recurrency, and tested with the article's methods by holding closure depth fixed and varying only whether a self-model's output is written back. Two further points concern the reply to the unfolding argument, where one sentence contradicts the premise it accepts and either of two rewrites restores consistency, and the claim that systems run on von Neumann hardware only simulate recurrence, which can disagree with the perturbational tests the framework proposes for artificial systems.
