*Accessible versus accessed content (Section 3.4.6; abstract, Table 1, Table 2, Figure 1 caption, Section 3.3).* Accessible content, poised for report without being in use, is not conscious. It may be stored on the implicit side or held in an explicit model without entering the self-model's closed loop, and it becomes conscious only when it enters that loop: through a permeability event in the first case, by being taken up by the self-model in the second. The paper previously treated every explicit model as phenomenal. The abstract, the explicit-model row of Table 1, Table 2, the Figure 1 caption, the virtual-side definition of Section 3.3 and the implicit/explicit pairings now say that explicit content is phenomenal when it enters the closed loop of self-model and world-model. No prediction, falsification criterion or reference changed.

*Body-first expectation (Section 3.7.3).* For a recurrent network coupled to a body, the body is the most persistent, most controllable and most informative regularity in its input, so the first stable attractor such a network forms is expected to model it. Nerve nets of the cnidarian kind coordinated a body without representing it, and the first representations brains formed would then have been of the body they coordinated. This is stated as an expectation, not yet measured in spiking networks. One reference was added: Keijzer, van Duijn, & Lyon (2013).

*Pattee's epistemic cut (Section 3.4.3).* A paragraph relates the implicit/explicit split to Pattee's epistemic cut between rate-independent memory and rate-dependent dynamics, with the stored implicit models on the side of memory and the running explicit models on the side of dynamics. Pattee holds the cut to be epistemic, so his argument supports the claim that an outside description does not exhaust what such a system does, and the constitutive reading remains the theory's own commitment. One reference was added: Pattee (2001).

*The master argument and the indexical gap (Section 4.2.5).* A paragraph answers Chalmers' master argument against the phenomenal concept strategy. FMT's analogue of phenomenal concepts is a closed self-model whose content is indexical and whose generating substrate is opaque to it; the truth of our phenomenal beliefs follows from that fact a posteriori, through the identity of closure with phenomenality. On FMT the phenomenal gap is the indexical gap of a closed self-modeling system, widened by opacity; the paper states that this claim is not proven. Three references were added: Chalmers (2007), Perry (1979) and Balog (2012).

*Sperling's partial report (Section 3.4.6).* In Sperling's displays all the letters are seen; identifying a letter as a specific, reportable letter requires holding it in short-term memory, which has limited capacity and decays during read-out. The partial-report advantage then measures short-term memory, not phenomenal content beyond access. One reference was added: Sperling (1960).
