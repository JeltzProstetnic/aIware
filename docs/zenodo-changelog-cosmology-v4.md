This version is a substantial correction and strengthening pass, prompted by an adversarial review that found five errors serious enough to affect the argument. All five are repaired, the whole paper was then re-reviewed, and every reference in the paper has since been resolved against an external source.

<strong>Five corrections to the physics</strong>
<ul>
<li><strong>Information impermeability (IB1) restated as a two-tier property.</strong> The earlier flat claim contradicted &sect;8.2 and contradicted Raju (2022), which the paper cites in its own support. Horizons are now semiclassically impermeable but non-perturbatively boundary-available (Bahiru et al. 2024; Raju 2022), and &sect;8.2 is cross-linked so the two sections agree. The tier structure <em>is</em> the substrate/simulation split, so the repair sharpens IB1 rather than weakening it.</li>
<li><strong>The Bekenstein bound was misattributed throughout, and it carried one of the three properties the unification rests on.</strong> &sect;5.2 Step 1 is rewritten to separate two distinct results: the Bekenstein bound (1981) is <em>S &le; 2&pi;kER/&hbar;c</em> &mdash; energy &times; radius, with the rigorous form a relative-entropy statement (Casini 2008) &mdash; while the area law is 't&nbsp;Hooft/Susskind, in covariant form Bousso (1999). The two coincide only at the collapse threshold, which is where horizons sit. The property is renamed <strong>holographic saturation</strong> throughout (26 sites). &sect;8.1 now uses the Bekenstein bound for what it actually says (energy &harr; information), which strengthens that section.</li>
<li><strong>&sect;5.6's particle-spectrum argument was self-refuting</strong> &mdash; an O(1)-bit boundary was asked to encode &gtrsim;60 Standard Model states. The bound <em>A &ge; 4&ell;<sub>P</sub>&sup2; ln N</em> is now stated transparently, one Planck area is conceded to be 0.36 bits, and "Planck-scale" is scoped to <em>of order</em>. The capacity argument now yields bounds rather than the multiplicity.</li>
<li><strong>The &sect;5.6/&sect;5.7 no-hair conflict is resolved in the direction physics points.</strong> Baryon and lepton number have no boundary home &mdash; and that is the model <em>agreeing</em> with sphaleron violation (Klinkhamer &amp; Manton 1984) and with no-global-symmetries-in-quantum-gravity (Harlow &amp; Ooguri 2021). The paper no longer derives B/L conservation.</li>

The interior <em>empties</em> instead, and the de Sitter horizon saturates by construction.</li>

<strong>Axiom 1 is now argued rather than asserted (&sect;3.1)</strong>
<p>The claim that nothingness is impossible had rested on calling it "a Platonic abstraction" &mdash; a bare assertion carrying a contested thesis. It now runs on an exhaustive dilemma. Any assertion that nothingness is possible must either locate it or not. <em>Located</em>: fixing it relative to what exists implies a separation, hence a dimension, hence a position, hence a property &mdash; which meets the Cambridge-property objection head-on, and of which vacuum decay and the empty possible world are instances. <em>Unlocated</em>: the claim becomes "nothing, everywhere and everywhen", refuted by the existence of whoever asserts it. No third form exists. An independent epistemic line is added (nothingness is unobservable in principle, since observing it requires standing in a spacetime relation to it), together with the deflationary last exit. Krauss and Albert are corrected &mdash; they reach <em>opposite</em> conclusions &mdash; and Albert's objection is granted as an instance of the first horn.</p>

<strong>A new conditional result in &sect;6.5</strong>
<p>The paper's stated open obligation was to derive the Tsirelson bound. &sect;6.5 no longer calls this a candidate direction: it states a <em>conditional</em> derivation. Information causality's inequality and the holographic bound on the shared locus turn out to be the same inequality once <em>m</em> is read as locus capacity rather than a transmitted-bit count, so Tsirelson follows via Pawłowski and Uffink &mdash; <em>given</em> a single-locus decoding postulate. That postulate is &sect;5.2's single-surface ontology, adopted for reasons having nothing to do with Bell. The Oughton &amp; Timpson measure-dependence objection is answered from the &alpha;&nbsp;=&nbsp;1 relative-entropy form of the Bekenstein bound. This is a conditional result with its condition named in the text, not an unconditional claim; the remaining gap is formalizing the decoding postulate in entanglement-wedge language.</p>
