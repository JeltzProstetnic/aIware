## Two figures were WRONG and are now corrected against the primary

- **Full Scale gain**: the manuscript said "about 18 points". Table 2 gives **16.83–17.63** over 54.25 years
  (midpoint 17.23). Corrected.
- **Performance subtest range**: "12 to 21" is now stated exactly as **11.70 to 21.50**, and the two values
  Flynn and Weiss **estimated rather than measured** (Object Assembly [17.35], Picture Arrangement [21.50] —
  bracketed in the source because the WISC-IV retained neither subtest) are flagged in the text. The argument
  is written so as not to rest on them.

## Must NOT be claimed (unchanged — these still bind)

- **Bratsberg & Rogeberg (2018) do NOT demonstrably report the reversal broken down by their three subtests.**
  Full text returned 403 on every route. §7.3 is deliberately written so that it does **not** depend on this:
  it uses Sundet et al. (2004) for the subtest structure of the same Norwegian archive and casts the
  differential *reversal* as the open test. **Do not "improve" that passage by attributing a subtest breakdown
  to Bratsberg & Rogeberg unless it is verified first.**
- **Flynn's explanation of the Similarities/Vocabulary split is a secular shift toward abstract and scientific
  habits of classification — not motivation and not simulation.** Now verified verbatim from the primary
  ("view the world through the spectacles provided by science"). §7.3 states this and uses "consistent with".
  Do not let a later edit quietly upgrade it.
- **Two limbs of §7.3 are predictions, not findings**: differential *trainability*, and differential *coupling
  to within-person motivational state*. No literature classifying subtests by trainability was located. They
  must not acquire citations they do not have.
- **Coding is excluded from the simulation/retrieval partition and must stay excluded.** It gained 18.00 points
  — third-largest in the record — but it is a speeded clerical task that is neither model-construction nor
  retrieval, and Flynn and Weiss attribute its gains to visual-media tempo. §7.3 declares the exclusion in
  advance and states the cost. Do not let it drift back in on the grounds that it is a Performance subtest.

## The lesson that generalizes (S290's, extended by S291)

S290: the brief that went to the reviewing agent asserted "Openness correlates with Gf at roughly .2–.3."
That was wrong — Ackerman & Heggestad (1997) put Openness–**Gf at .08**. Verifying a premise before building
on it is what saved prediction 9.

S291 adds the other half: **verify citations that are already in the manuscript, not only the ones being
added.** Two long-standing references were defective — one with a fabricated volume and article number and a
claim inverted relative to its own source (Hilger), one cited for the opposite of its conclusion (Jussim &
Harber). Both survived every prior pass because the citation gate checks that keys resolve to bibitems, not
that the bibitems are real or that the sentence matches the source. See `AIW-170`.
