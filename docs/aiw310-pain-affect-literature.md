# AIW-310 literature check: does pain affect track the self-model?

Research note, S342, 2026-10-19. Backlog: `AIW-310`. Status: literature check only; nothing here is in the master.

## Verdict

**Partly tested, and the cleanest existing test points the wrong way for one half of the claim.** The two pain dimensions dissociate, and many self-altering interventions lower unpleasantness more than intensity (THC, open-monitoring meditation, expert meditators, hypnotic affect suggestion, ketamine). That part is old and well-replicated. No published study has tested the specific scaling, i.e. whether the *affect-minus-intensity gap* tracks a measured self-model disruption (EDI, 5D-ASC, CADSS depersonalization, DMN integrity) over and above dose and expectancy. Three studies come close, and none supports the mapping as stated. Ketamine depersonalization does not covary with analgesia (Goldway et al., 2026), but only a single pain rating was taken. Mindfulness-induced decoupling of thalamus and precuneus tracks the drop in *intensity*, not in unpleasantness (Riegner et al., 2023). Hypnotic depersonalization lowered reported *intensity* and S1 activity (Röder et al., 2007, n = 7). The idea that pain suffering is anchored by the self-model is also already in print as an active-inference account (Gerrans, 2020, 2024). So the prediction has mixed novelty and must not be presented as unique to FMT. What FMT can still claim is the fine structure in §5.

Every claim is marked: **[V]** means verified against the abstract or full text (PubMed, PMC or the publisher), **[V-abs]** means verified from the abstract only, so details are not checked, and **[U]** means not verified.

## 1. Has the specific scaling been tested?

### Psychedelics

- **Ramaekers et al. (2021), LSD 5/10/20 µg, cold pressor, n = 24, within-subject, placebo-controlled.** [V, full text PMC8054163] They rated *painfulness* and *unpleasantness* separately on VAS, and measured tolerance. 20 µg raised tolerance, and the main Treatment effect was significant for unpleasantness (F3,157 = 2.6, p = 0.05, ηp² = .05) and near-significant for painfulness (p = 0.064). Both moved in the same direction with similar effect sizes. They measured dissociation with the CADSS (depersonalization, derealization, amnesia) only at 6 h, after the CPTs at 1.5 h and 5 h. A canonical correlation between the dissociation set and the pain set was significant but weak (canonical r = 0.25, about 6% of variance), and the authors note that dissociation was "almost negligible". No EDI or 5D-ASC was used, and no affect-minus-intensity contrast was computed. *Bearing:* this is analgesia with no ego dissolution, and affect and intensity fell together. That does not refute FMT, which predicts nothing special below the ego-dissolution threshold. It does show that a psychedelic produces analgesia by some route other than the self-model.
- **Cavarra et al. (2025), LSD 15 µg microdosing ×4, CPT.** [V-abs] Painfulness, unpleasantness and stress were rated. There was no analgesic effect in the whole sample. A post-hoc subsample showed higher tolerance and lower unpleasantness after the first dose only. Subjective drug experience did not correlate with tolerance, while blood pressure did.
- **Full-dose psilocybin, LSD or DMT with experimental pain plus EDI or 5D-ASC in humans: none found.** A PubMed search for psilocybin AND pain AND (mystical OR ego dissolution OR 5D-ASC OR oceanic boundlessness) AND correlat* returned zero hits (2026-10-19). Clinical work so far consists of feasibility pilots: Dean et al. (2026), 25 mg psilocybin for phantom limb pain, n = 9, weekly PLP intensity only [V-abs]; Zarrabi et al. (2026, medRxiv), open-label, pain intensity only [V-abs]. There is also a qualitative self-medication report (Bornemann et al., 2021) in which participants named "Positive Reframing" and "Somatic Presence" as the processes behind relief [V-abs]. Note that *more* bodily presence is the opposite direction from ego dissolution.
- **Historical:** Kast & Collins (1964), LSD as an analgesic in terminal patients [V-citation only; content not checked]. The often-repeated claim that Kast's patients "still felt the pain but were indifferent to it" is **[U]**. Do not cite it without reading the paper.
- **Review/mechanism:** Castellanos et al. (2020) propose a DMN or connectivity "reset" mechanism for psychedelic pain relief and mention ego dissolution [V-citation; summary via secondary source, **[U]** for the exact claims].

### Ketamine

- **Goldway et al. (2026), Neuropsychopharmacology, n = 37, IV ketamine 0.4 mg/kg + 0.4 mg/kg/h vs saline, fMRI, thermal pain.** [V, abstract + full text] Ketamine produced analgesia (session × intensity F(1,54) = 11.22, p = .001) and dissociation (CADSS total and subscales: depersonalization, derealization, amnesia). Dissociation correlated with *reduced DMN connectivity* (ρ = .49). Pain and dissociation markers did **not** covary (ρ −0.24 to 0.32, all p > .09). **Caveat that matters for AIW-310:** pain was a *single 10-point rating*, the figure caption calls it "subjective pain intensity", and unpleasantness was not collected. The design was fixed-order, not randomized. *Bearing:* this is the closest existing test. Its result agrees with FMT's *intensity* half: intensity is independent of self-model disruption. It cannot speak to the affect half.
- **Sprenger et al. (2006), S-ketamine 0.05–0.15 mg/kg/h, heat pain, fMRI.** [V-abs] Intensity and unpleasantness were rated separately. Unpleasantness fell 55.1% and intensity 23.1% at the top dose. The drop went with dose-dependent reductions in S2, insula and midcingulate activity. "Alterations in consciousness" were measured with a questionnaire. Whether these correlated with the affect gap is **[U]** (not in the abstract). *Bearing:* the affect-greater-than-intensity pattern is there, but it is confounded with dose, which is exactly what FMT says should not drive it.
- Rogers et al. (2004): ketamine at 200 ng/ml lowered pain scores along with insula and thalamus activity. There was no affect/intensity split in the abstract [V-abs].

### Meditation

- **Perlman et al. (2010):** in long-term meditators, Open Monitoring lowered *unpleasantness but not intensity*. Focused Attention had no effect [V-abs]. No self-model measure.
- **Lutz et al. (2013):** expert meditators (>10,000 h) reported *equal intensity, less unpleasantness*, with higher dorsal anterior insula and aMCC response and lower anticipatory baseline [V-abs]. No self-model measure.
- **Gard et al. (2012):** practitioners lowered unpleasantness by 22% and anticipatory anxiety by 29%. Lateral PFC activity fell and posterior insula activity rose [V-abs]. Intensity is not reported in the abstract [U].
- **Grant & Rainville (2009):** in Zen meditators, "changes in pain unpleasantness generally paralleled those found in pain intensity", partly explained by respiration rate [V-abs]. *This counts against the dissociation.*
- **Grant et al. (2011):** in Zen meditators, decoupling of executive and pain cortices predicted low pain sensitivity (thresholds) [V-abs].
- **Zeidan et al. (2011):** after 4 days of training, unpleasantness fell 57% and intensity 40%, and the two had different neural correlates [V-abs]. **Zeidan et al. (2015):** mindfulness, placebo cream and sham mindfulness *all* lowered both intensity and unpleasantness, and mindfulness lowered both more than sham did [V-abs].
- **Riegner et al. (2023), Pain 164:280–291, n = 40, preregistered (NCT03414138).** [V, full text PMC9823141] Intensity and unpleasantness were rated separately on VAS. Mindfulness lowered intensity by **−32%** and unpleasantness by **−33%**, which is *no affect advantage*. In the whole-brain PPI, unpleasantness was orthogonalized to intensity, and the greater thalamus–precuneus decoupling (the "self-referential" mechanism in the title) correlated with **intensity** reductions. The authors report "no other significant ... correlations". *Bearing:* this is the cleanest existing neural self-referential measure in a pain study, and it maps onto the dimension FMT says it should **not** map onto.
- **Bhatt et al. (2026), pooled RCTs, n = 480 chronic pain patients on opioids.** [V-abs] Gains in *nondual awareness* (NADA, a self-model-disruption measure) mediated MORE's effects on pain *severity* and *interference*. The analysis was between subjects, and it did not separate affect from intensity. *Bearing:* the self-model measure mediates relief, but relief of severity, which is closer to intensity.
- **In progress:** Childs et al. (2026), JMIR protocol, n = 173 chronic musculoskeletal pain. Self-transcendence is measured by self-report, EEG theta and fNIRS of the DMN, and the outcome is pain intensity. Data run to May 2027 [V-abs]. Unpleasantness is not listed among the outcomes in the abstract.

### Hypnosis

- **Rainville et al. (1997), Science:** hypnotic suggestion selectively changed unpleasantness without changing intensity. ACC activity changed and S1 did not [V-abs]. **Rainville et al. (1999), Pain:** suggestions aimed at affect changed unpleasantness largely independently of intensity, and heart rate tracked unpleasantness, not intensity. Suggestions aimed at sensation changed intensity, with secondary changes in unpleasantness. "The specific pain dimension on which hypnotic suggestions act depends on the content of the instructions and is not a characteristic of hypnosis itself" [V-abs]. **Hofbauer et al. (2001):** intensity suggestions changed S1, which forms a double dissociation with the 1997 ACC result [V-abs]. No self-model measure in any of the three.
- **Röder et al. (2007), hypnotically induced depersonalization, n = 7 highs, fMRI, median-nerve electrical pain.** [V-abs] Subjects reported a significant **decrease in pain intensity** from waking to hypnotic depersonalization. Activity fell in contralateral S1, BA40, BA9, putamen and amygdala. Unpleasantness is not reported in the abstract [U]. *Bearing:* a direct self-model manipulation lowered the sensory dimension.

### Depersonalization / dissociation (clinical)

- **Ludäscher et al. (2007), BPD, n = 12 + 12:** electrical **pain thresholds** were raised, and the rise correlated with state dissociation and aversive arousal. Detection thresholds did not differ [V-abs]. Thresholds are a sensory-dimension measure.
- **Medford (2012):** depersonalization disorder as de-affectualization [V-citation; content not re-read].
- No study was found that measured unpleasantness against intensity in depersonalization disorder, using CDS scores as the scaling variable.

### Bodily self-identification (Blanke group)

- **Hänsel et al. (2011):** synchronous stroking of a virtual body raised self-identification, and self-identification correlated with raised **pressure pain thresholds** [V-abs].
- **Romano et al. (2014):** self-identification with an avatar lowered skin conductance responses to pain, and the effect correlated negatively with self-identification ratings [V-abs].
- *Bearing:* manipulations of the bodily self change pain, but here the measure is thresholds or arousal, not the unpleasantness/intensity split.

### Pain asymbolia

- **Berthier, Starkstein & Leiguarda (1988), Ann Neurol 24:41–49:** asymbolia for pain as a "sensory-limbic disconnection syndrome" after insular lesions [V-citation; clinical details not re-read].
- **Grahek (2007), *Feeling Pain and Being in Pain*, 2nd ed., MIT Press** [V-citation]. The 2001 first edition is [U] as to the exact year and publisher.
- **Klein (2015), "What pain asymbolia really shows", Mind 124:493** [V-citation, Crossref DOI 10.1093/mind/fzu185]. Argued from memory: asymbolics lack concern for bodily integrity. That would be a self-related reading. [U] for the argument.
- **Ploner, Freund & Schnitzler (1999):** the reverse dissociation, pain affect without pain sensation after a postcentral lesion [V-citation].

### THC

- See §4.

**Summary for Q1:** the affect/intensity dissociation is tested many times over. The scaling of the *gap* with a *measured* self-model disruption, controlled for dose and expectancy, has not been tested. The three studies with a self-model or depersonalization measure next to pain (Goldway 2026, Riegner 2023, Röder 2007) either lacked an unpleasantness rating or found the self-related measure tracking intensity.

## 2. Evidence against

1. **The self-related neural measure tracks intensity, not affect** (Riegner et al., 2023). This contradicts the mapping as sketched in AIW-310.
2. **Hypnotic depersonalization lowered intensity** (Röder et al., 2007), and **dissociation tracks sensory thresholds** (Ludäscher et al., 2007; Hänsel et al., 2011). A self-model disruption reaches the sensory dimension, so "intensity tracks the nociceptive input" cannot be held strictly.
3. **Affect and intensity often fall together:** Grant & Rainville (2009), Ramaekers et al. (2021), Zeidan et al. (2015), and Riegner et al. (2023), with −32% against −33%.
4. **Affect can be lowered selectively by suggestion content alone, with no measured self-model change** (Rainville et al., 1997, 1999). This already partly meets AIW-310's second refutation clause ("affect drop tracks expectation/placebo ... without self-model change"), unless FMT argues that hypnotic affect suggestion works *through* the explicit self-model. That would need a self-model measure during hypnotic affect suggestion, and none exists. If no such measure is ever taken, the clause becomes unfalsifiable, so it has to be rewritten (see §5).
5. **Placebo lowers both dimensions and acts mostly on evaluation, not nociception**: placebo effects on the Neurologic Pain Signature were tiny (g = −0.08) against g = −0.66 on reports (Zunhammer et al., 2018) [V-abs]. An expectancy route that lowers report without touching nociception is therefore established. Any FMT design needs measured expectancy as a covariate and cannot assume it away.
6. **Ketamine's dissociation does not covary with its analgesia** (Goldway et al., 2026). This is against a general "dissociation causes analgesia" story, though it is silent on affect.
7. **Self-reported routes to psychedelic pain relief include increased "somatic presence"** (Bornemann et al., 2021), which runs opposite to ego dissolution. The evidence is anecdotal.

## 3. Predictive-processing and precision accounts, and where FMT differs

- **Büchel et al. (2014):** placebo analgesia as a prior expectation that is combined with nociceptive input, weighted by relative precision [V-citation]. **Ongaro & Kaptchuk (2019):** symptom perception as Bayesian inference, with placebo acting through priors [V-citation]. **Tabor et al. (2017):** pain as a statistical inference about bodily threat [V-citation]. **Kiverstein, Kirchhoff & Thacker (2022):** embodied predictive-processing theory of pain [V-citation]. **Wiech (2016):** cognitive modulation of pain [V-citation].
- **The rival closest to FMT is Gerrans (2020, 2024).** He holds that pain processing is anchored by a "multidimensional self-model". Pain asymbolia is "depersonalization for pain experience", and depersonalization and asymbolia arise "at different levels of hierarchical self modelling" (interoceptive and allostatic active inference) [V-abs]. **This already predicts, in PP terms, that suffering depends on the self-model.** The coarse version of AIW-310 is derivable from Gerrans in one step, exactly as Prediction 1's coarse version is derivable from REBUS + Fotopoulou (master §8.2). Letheby & Gerrans (2017) give the same framework for ego dissolution [V-citation].
- **What generic precision accounts predict:** any manipulation that raises the precision of a "no threat" prior, or lowers the precision of nociceptive prediction errors, should lower pain. Expectancy and self-model disruption are interchangeable channels into the same precision variable. REBUS (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019) [V-citation] has psychedelics *relaxing* high-level priors and raising the weight of bottom-up signal. Applied naively to pain, that predicts unchanged or even *raised* sensory intensity (vividness) under high doses, plus a loss of high-level threat appraisal.
- **Where FMT could differ testably** (to be confirmed against what Gerrans would say; not verified that he rules any of these out):
  1. **Mediation, not channel equivalence.** FMT puts valence in the explicit self-model (master §3.7.3: "at least one pain-pleasure axis" as a sufficient-condition conjunct; §3.4.2: an emotion is a quale in which the self-model registers the organism's own state). It therefore predicts that the affect gap is mediated by the self-model measure *after* expectancy is covaried out. A precision account with no self-model term predicts that matched expectancy produces a matched gap.
  2. **Intensity preserved or raised while affect falls**, both at high psychedelic doses. REBUS-style bottom-up gain plus ESM disruption should yield "vivid but not mine / not bad". A flat precision account, in which pain falls whenever priors win, predicts that both fall.
  3. **Threshold at ego dissolution, not dose.** The gap should appear only above the dose at which EDI rises, and its size should follow EDI rather than plasma level. Ramaekers 2021 (no gap at 20 µg) is consistent with this. Sprenger 2006 (gap grows with ketamine dose) has to be re-analysed against the consciousness questionnaire before it counts.
  4. **Implicit markers spared.** If FMT locates affect in the *explicit* self-model, then implicit nociceptive processing should be relatively spared when unpleasantness drops. Examples are the NPS, the nociceptive flexion reflex and withdrawal latency (the IWM/ISM). ⚠ Rainville et al. (1999) found heart rate tracking unpleasantness, so autonomic markers are *not* a clean implicit marker. Only spinal and reflex markers (NFR) would serve. This point needs theory review before use.

- **Lee et al. (2013).** "Amygdala activity contributes to the dissociative effect of cannabis on pain perception." *Pain* 154:124–134. doi:10.1016/j.pain.2012.09.017. [V, full text PMC3549497] **Confirmed:** "On average, THC reduced the reported unpleasantness, but not the intensity of ongoing pain and hyperalgesia". There was diminished aMCC activity for hyperalgesia. The individual reduction in hyperalgesia unpleasantness correlated with right amygdala activity. THC reduced amygdala–S1/sensorimotor functional connectivity in ongoing pain, and that reduction correlated with the *difference* between the drug's effects on unpleasantness and intensity. **Wording correction for the guide material (§4, line 54):** the phrase "altered amygdala coupling" is right, but the precise claim is *reduced amygdala–primary sensorimotor connectivity, correlated with the unpleasantness-minus-intensity difference*. Design: healthy cannabis-naive volunteers, randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover, capsaicin model. Sample size [U] (not read). Note also that Lee's "dissociative effect" is exactly the gap metric AIW-310 needs, |Δunpleasantness − Δintensity|. That makes it the template analysis, though Lee had no self-model measure.

## 5. Bottom line

**Classification: partly tested.** The dissociation is established. Self-model-to-pain links exist (Riegner, Röder, Hänsel, Romano, Bhatt), but they mostly hit intensity, thresholds or arousal. The specific claim, that the affect gap scales with self-model disruption independently of dose and expectancy, is untested. The theory behind it is shared with Gerrans' self-modelling active-inference account. Present it under the master's "distinguishing power" heading with the shared part conceded first, as §8.2 does with REBUS.

**The sketch has to change before it goes anywhere.** As written, AIW-310 says intensity "tracks the nociceptive input" and is unaffected by self-model disruption. Röder (2007), Ludäscher (2007), Hänsel (2011) and Riegner (2023) all show self-model manipulations moving the sensory dimension. The defensible version is *relative*: the affect drop is greater than the intensity drop, and the gap is what scales. The second refutation clause also has to be reworded, because Rainville 1997/1999 already show affect changing with suggestion content alone (§2, item 4).

**Cleanest existing dataset:** Maastricht's **NCT06368492** (psilocybin 5 mg and 10 mg vs placebo, fibromyalgia, n ≈ 35, within-subject, primary completion est. 2025-12, recruiting as of the registry read) [V, ClinicalTrials.gov API]. It records CPT tolerance, pressure pain threshold and painfulness VAS, *plus* **EDI, 5D-ASC, CADSS and two expectancy measures (CEQ, TEC)**. That is every covariate the test needs. The one gap: the registry lists only painfulness VAS. The same group's earlier CPT studies also collected **unpleasantness** VAS (Ramaekers 2021; Cavarra 2025), so it may be in the data unregistered **[U]**. The doses are low, so EDI variance may be small. A secondary re-analysis of **Ramaekers 2021** (painfulness, unpleasantness and CADSS depersonalization, n = 24) could compute the gap now, but CADSS was taken at 6 h, after the pain tests, and dissociation was near floor. For ketamine, **Sprenger 2006** has unpleasantness, intensity and a consciousness questionnaire across four dose levels, which is the closest existing dose-versus-state contrast. *(Contacting authors is MG's decision, not part of this note.)*

**Suggested operationalisation** (style of `.claude/knowledge/prediction-framing.md` and master §8.2: architectural source, rivals, disconfirmer):

> **Statement.** When the explicit self-model is disrupted, pain unpleasantness falls more than pain intensity, and the size of that gap scales with measured self-model disruption rather than with dose or expectancy. **Mechanism.** On the theory, valence belongs to the explicit self-model (Section 3.7.3, the pain–pleasure axis; Section 3.4.2, affect as the self-model registering the organism's own state), while intensity is carried by the content generated from the nociceptive implicit model. Disrupting the explicit self-model therefore removes the bearer the pain is bad *for* faster than it removes the sensation. **Design.** Randomized, double-blind, within-subject crossover in healthy volunteers: placebo, a sub-ego-dissolution dose and an ego-dissolving dose of psilocybin (or a ketamine dose series), with an active-placebo arm matched for expectancy. Tonic heat or cold-pressor pain at calibrated intensity is delivered at peak. Separate VAS for intensity and unpleasantness are taken after each stimulus (Price-style anchors), along with the nociceptive flexion reflex, and EDI, 5D-ASC (oceanic boundlessness, disembodiment) and CADSS depersonalization at the same time point. Expectancy is measured before dosing and rated after. Optional fMRI: PCC/precuneus–mPFC integrity and the NPS. **Contrast.** The dependent variable is the within-subject gap, Δunpleasantness − Δintensity (Lee et al., 2013 metric). It is regressed on EDI (or depersonalization) with plasma level and expectancy as covariates. The prediction is a positive EDI slope that survives both covariates, a near-zero gap at the sub-ego dose, and a reflex/NPS change smaller than the unpleasantness change. The sample size has to come from a power analysis on the slope. With n ≈ 40 and three sessions, a slope of standardized β ≈ 0.3 is detectable. That is an estimate, not a calculation. **Disconfirmation.** (a) The gap is absent at ego-dissolving doses, with affect and intensity falling together. (b) The gap is present but fully explained by dose or expectancy, with EDI adding nothing once both are covaried. (c) The EDI slope attaches to intensity rather than to the gap, which is the Riegner (2023) pattern in a psychedelic. **Distinguishing power.** Gerrans' (2020, 2024) self-modelling active-inference account predicts the coarse effect, so it is not unique to the theory. A precision account with no self-model term predicts matched effects for matched expectancy. FMT's differential content is the mediation surviving the expectancy control, the threshold at ego dissolution rather than at a dose, and preserved (or raised) intensity alongside lowered affect.

Two open questions are for MG, not this note. First, whether "intensity tracks the input" should be weakened to the relative claim. Second, whether the implicit-marker sub-prediction (§3 point 4) is FMT-faithful, given Rainville's heart-rate finding.

## 6. Martial artists and athletes (S343, 2026-10-20)

Research subagent, PubMed eutils + OpenAlex, abstracts only; every item **[V-abs]** unless marked. Prompted by MG S343: his first-person basis for AIW-310 is THC and martial arts training with the mental training built on it.

**Verdict.** No martial-arts study rates intensity and unpleasantness on separate scales or relates their gap to a self-model measure. Partial support only.

- **Thornton, Sheffield & Baird (2017)**, *Scand J Pain* 16:36–44, doi:10.1016/j.sjpain.2017.02.007, PMID 28850410. Longitudinal, n = 102 new contact athletes (martial arts, rugby, American football), 0/4/8 months. Ischaemic tolerance rose in those who stayed and fell in drop-outs; catastrophizing and the bothersomeness of contact pain fell; **VAS intensity did not change in either group.** Closest existing pattern to AIW-310, but "bothersome" is not unpleasantness and no self-model measure was taken.
- **Thornton et al. (2019)**, *Eur J Pain* 23:1020–1030, doi:10.1002/ejp.1370, PMID 30697875; same data in **Thornton et al. (2021)**, *J Pain* 22:68–75, PMID 32599156. 40 experienced (>3 y) vs 40 novice (<6 mo) contact athletes vs 40 non-contact; pressure pain; challenge vs threat instructions. All contact athletes found pain less psychologically bothersome; only the experienced group reported lower intensity and higher tolerance. Affective change early, intensity change only after years — fits the gap, but exposure is confounded with appraisal.
- **Leźnicka et al. (2017a)**, *Physiol Behav* 180:131–136, doi:10.1016/j.physbeh.2017.08.018, PMID 28844852. 198 combat athletes (MMA, karate, boxing) vs 86 students: higher cold-pressor and pressure tolerance; temperament differences. No affect ratings.
- **Leźnicka et al. (2017b)**, *Res Sports Med* 25:290–299, doi:10.1080/15438627.2017.1314288, PMID 28393630. 140 contact athletes vs 181 students: tolerance higher (median 120 vs 94 s), **threshold no different**.
- **Leźnicka et al. (2023)**, *Genes* 14:733, PMID 36981004. Group effect survives SCN9A genotype; attributed to exposure. Minor.
- **Choi et al. (2013)**, *Horm Behav* 64:618–623, PMID 24005187. n = 17 kumdo, pre-competition stress raised threshold and lowered ratings. State confound for any design.
- **Christakou et al. (2025)**, *Healthcare* 13:447, PMID 40077010. Clinical low-back pain SF-MPQ sensory and affective subscales across BJJ/Muay Thai/boxing; no controls.
- **Tesarz et al. (2012)**, *Pain* 153:1253–1262, doi:10.1016/j.pain.2012.03.005, PMID 22607985. Meta-analysis, 15 studies, n = 899: tolerance g = 0.87; threshold g = 0.69, not significant once high-risk-of-bias studies are excluded. No sensory/affective split.
- **Thornton, Baird & Sheffield (2024)**, *J Pain* 25:104450, doi:10.1016/j.jpain.2023.12.007, PMID 38154623. Meta-analysis, 36 studies, n = 2,492: tolerance g = 0.88, intensity g = −0.80, threshold g = 0.41; **unpleasantness (5 studies) g = −1.23, not significant** (CI as printed [−2.29, .18]). Point estimate larger than intensity, the AIW-310 direction, but underpowered. Full text needed to identify the 5 studies.
- **O'Farrell … Rainville, Pageaux (2022)**, *Sports Med* 52:2593–2604, PMID 35867332. Scoping review: lab stimuli unrepresentative of being hit. Rainville co-author (potential collaborator — MG's call).
- **Spantios et al. (2025)**, *Sci Rep* 16:2943, PMID 41423552. Kendo vs non-kendo, fMRI oddball: lower resting DMN–CEN, higher DMN–SN and SN–CEN connectivity. No pain measure; only DMN finding in practitioners.
- **Marusak et al. (2020)**, *J Pain Res* 13:3467, PMID 33402843. Martial-arts + breathing programme, children with cancer, n = 59, uncontrolled: distress fell in 89 %, pain in 50 %.
- Not read: Ryan & Kovacic (1966), *Percept Mot Skills* 22:383 [U].
- **Not found:** any study linking martial-arts mental training (mokuso, zazen, kiai, mental toughness) to experimental pain or to self-related measures with pain (queries recorded in the subagent report, S343).

**Practitioner design (draft).** Four cells matched on training years and hours: (1) contact art with formal mental training (traditional karate/kendo with mokuso or zazen); (2) contact art without (MMA/kickboxing gym); (3) non-contact practice with the same mental training (kata-only, iaido); (4) matched non-athletes. 1 vs 2 varies mental training at matched exposure; 1 vs 3 varies exposure at matched mental training. Calibrated heat (intensity equated) plus pressure at a hit site; separate intensity and unpleasantness ratings; gap = Δunpleasantness − Δintensity. Self-model: CDS, MAIA, a self-referential encoding task, optional resting DMN; plus PCS and FPQ. Controls: per-block expected intensity and unpleasantness; logged sparring hours; threshold, temporal summation, CPM; testing away from competition. Prediction: gap largest in cell 1 and tracking the self-model scores; threshold and intensity differences track exposure, not mental training. Stronger optional arm: 3-month randomised mokuso/zazen add-on inside contact gyms.

### 6a. Thornton, Baird & Sheffield (2024) — FULL TEXT READ (S343, 2026-10-20) [V]

Read via the publisher's open-access full text (jpain.org, hybrid OA; plain download is bot-blocked, rendered with headless Chrome). PROSPERO CRD42019119611; 36 studies, 2,492 participants.

- **Unpleasantness: 5 studies, 6 estimates, total n = 270 (range 14–53).** Primary pooled **g = −0.93 [95% CI −2.05, 0.19], not significant**, Q = 61.23, I² = 91.83 %. The often-quoted **g = −1.23 [−2.29, 0.18]** is the *sensitivity analysis* after excluding the one high-risk-of-bias study (Tajet-Foxell & Rose, 1995); I² 75.50 %. No subgroup analysis (too few studies).
- **The five studies (none on martial artists; none on contact athletes as a group):**
  1. Hall & Davies (1991), *Percept Mot Skills* 73:779–786 — varsity track, 14 v 14, cold pressor; athletes lower on BOTH intensity and affect.
  2. Manning & Fillingim (2002), *J Pain* 3:421–428 — mixed NCAA Div 1A (soccer, gymnastics, football, tennis, fencing, crew, wrestling) v active non-athletes; **no difference in intensity and none in unpleasantness**.
  3. Petersen et al. (2019), *Pain Med* 20:1822–1830 — endurance runners v active controls; athletes lower on BOTH (intensity p = .03, unpleasantness p = .009).
  4. Sternberg et al. (2001), *J Pain* 2:65–74 — varsity track; non-athletes higher on BOTH (p = .01 each).
  5. Tajet-Foxell & Rose (1995), *Br J Sports Med* 29:31–34 — ballet dancers; high risk of bias (excluded in the sensitivity analysis).
- **Intensity: 21 studies, g = −0.80 [−1.13, −0.47], significant.** A different, larger set of studies, so the unpleasantness-v-intensity magnitudes are NOT a like-for-like comparison.

**Bearing on AIW-310 (corrects §6 and master v22 §8.9).** In every one of the five studies that rated both, unpleasantness and intensity moved together (both lower, or neither). Athletes therefore show **no** gap between the two dimensions; the meta-analysis does not "put the unpleasantness effect above the intensity effect" in any meaningful sense, and its studies are endurance/track/mixed collegiate athletes, not contact athletes. This is *consistent* with Prediction 8, which ties the gap to self-model change, not to exposure: athletes without mental training should show co-movement. It is not support for a gap.
