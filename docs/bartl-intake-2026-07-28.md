## #3 — psypost "Is intelligence related to happiness?" — "Sift through this for rim material/references"
Core: **no individual-level correlation** between IQ and happiness; the relationship is context-dependent.
- National level: Veenhoven & Choi (2012) — positive assoc. of national cognitive scores × happiness (moderated by individualism).
- Individual: DeNeve & Cooper (1998, meta-analysis, 137 traits) — IQ only modest; personality (extraversion) dominates.
- Low cognition: Nordmo et al. (2025) — lower cognitive ability → higher mental-disorder risk; Ali et al. (2013) — low-IQ lowest happiness but mediated by income/health/disability, not direct.
- High cognition: Pollet & Schnell (2017) — Mensa members lower well-being than high achievers.
- **Type matters:** emotional intelligence (Furnham & Petrides 2003) explained >50% of happiness variance; verbal = *relative* vocabulary predicts; numeracy amplifies social comparison.
- **RIM relevance:** convergent with RIM's anti-monolithic-g stance — "intelligence" isn't one thing, and *g* specifically fails to buy wellbeing while EI/specific abilities matter. Also echoes the FMT/NoC point that the higher-order faculty **carries costs and doesn't optimize general outcomes** (gifted less happy). Candidate framing/citation cluster for RIM; verify each secondary cite before use.
