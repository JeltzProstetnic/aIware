**v2 (2026-10-19)**

Supersedes v1 (2026-10-19).

*Corrections from a cross-check of the author's publications.* No hypothesis, design element or refutation condition changed.
- Statistical power (Sections 6.4 and 7.1). The planned sample is powered to detect a hazard ratio of 0.6, not for refutation condition R1. Section 6.4 now states this: with no true effect, the R1 condition is met in roughly a quarter of samples at 30 to 40 events, and 80 % power for R1 takes about 160 events. Section 7.1 adds that at the planned sample size a null result will usually be inconclusive.
- The role of Palmer (1954). Kidd and Modlin (1998) is now cited for the role of Palmer's study in the rejection of gastric bacteria (Sections 1, 2 and 5.6); Thagard (1998a) remains the source for the assumption that acid keeps the stomach sterile. The unsourced statement that Palmer's method was later judged unsuited to detecting the organism was removed.
- Worked cases (Sections 5.6 and 9). Section 5.6 now says that three of the four cases of Section 1 are coded and that the meteorite case is left out for brevity; the conclusion refers to "the standard cases" in place of "the three standard cases".
- Dates and references. Wegener's 1915 book is cited and listed alongside the 1912 paper, and Bradley's letter on stellar aberration is dated 1728/9 throughout. The No Outside reference no longer carries a version number and cites the concept DOI.

References added: Kidd and Modlin (1998); Wegener (1915).
