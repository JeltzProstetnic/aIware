## His answers, and what checking them showed

**Q1 — Wittmann (1988), the Extraversion/Neuroticism mean-vs-intraindividual-SD demonstration.**
His answer: the whole theory-guided aggregation procedure is in **Kap. 4.4.1, pp. 541–545** of the
1988 handbook chapter, and also in **Wittmann (2002), pp. 10–12** (the attached paper).
⚠ **Not yet checked against the 1988 chapter itself** — we do not hold it. What we now hold is his
page-level answer plus a second source for the same procedure.

**Q2 — Wittmann & Hattrup (2004), risk-taking as mediator between intelligence and performance.**
His answer, and it settles the question against us: *"In Wittmann und Hattrup habe ich die Mediation
nicht direkt getestet, sondern nur als eine Vermutung geäußert um die Geschlechtsunterschiede zu
erklären."* ⇒ **The mediation was never tested — it was a conjecture offered to explain gender
differences.** ✅ **MG's v5 softening to "bears on" / "consistent with" was correct, and is now
confirmed by the author.** Do not restore any stronger wording.

**Q3 — the Motivation → variability path.** He points to **Fig. 11** of the Singapore paper.
✅ **Verified here against the PDF, p.15 (printed p.14).** Fig. 11 is titled *"Predicting performance
variability in TRACON"*; dependent variable **SDAPLANE** (the intra-individual SD of planes landed),
**N = 93**, multiple R = 0.674, **R² = 0.454** (adj. 0.416). In that model the motivation variable
**MOTSKIL** is a significant predictor: coefficient 0.001, **std. coef. 0.203**, t = 2.293,
**p = 0.024**, tolerance 0.806. Other predictors: TAPLANG (0.685, p < .001), SELG (−0.382, p = .005),
SDSEL (0.280, p = .002), ART (0.173, p = .043), SEXX (−0.144, p = .150, n.s.).

⚠ **Two things to get right before this is cited.**
1. **The sign is positive** — higher MOTSKIL predicts *more* variability in planes landed, not less.
   RIM's consistency-over-level prediction (Prediction 8, and pattern **#39**'s *"a good schedule shows
   as consistency of observation across a season, not intensity on one night"*) needs the direction
   stated explicitly and reconciled, not assumed. **Do not cite this as support without saying which
   way it points.**
2. **It is a regression coefficient in one model, not a test of mediation or of a causal path.**
   The same discipline that Q2 just forced on Wittmann & Hattrup applies here.

**What the paper's own title gives us, and it is the strongest part:** *"Work motivation and level of
performance: A disappointing relationship?"* — the paper exists because motivation fails to predict
*level*. That is RIM's thesis in the source's own voice: motivation is not a capacity measured on one
occasion. Fig. 11 is where it predicts something else instead.
