## 0. The thesis in one sentence, and the sharper version

**MG's thesis:** first-person methods, however refined, give access to the explicit self-model's *reconstruction* of experience, not to the minimal self. Every report passes through reflective self-modelling, then memory, then language, so microphenomenology is the wrong tool for the minimal self.

**Sharper, FMT-native version (proposed):** the minimal self is defined by being *pre-reflective*, meaning lived without being taken as an object. A report is a behavioural output, and producing one requires the system to take its own state as an object, which on FMT means at least one extra rung of recursive self-modelling (§3.5). **The act of reporting therefore changes the level the self is running at. It does not just make the description noisier.** What comes out is a faithful or unfaithful description of a *reflective model of* the minimal self, and never the minimal self itself. Better interviewing can make that model more detailed and more consistent. It can't stop it being a model at the wrong level.

**The one-line verdict a tired reader could repeat:** *you can't open the fridge to check whether the light is off, and the self is both the fridge and the person opening it.*

### ⚠ A mapping correction to settle with MG before drafting

The task framing places the minimal self at "implicit self model / basal sentience floor". **The FMT paper does not support putting it in the ISM.** §3.2 says the ISM "is never directly conscious … a structural feature of the substrate, not an experiential one", and 2015 says *"Das Selbstmodell an sich ist nie bewusst"*. A minimal *phenomenal* self can't sit in a model that is by definition non-phenomenal.

The mapping that is consistent with the paper:

| Phenomenological term | FMT locus | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Minimal / pre-reflective self, for-me-ness (Zahavi, Gallagher 2000) | **Basic consciousness:** EWM + *rudimentary, non-recursive* ESM, generated from the ISM. "Experiences *as* a subject" but does not model itself experiencing | §3.5 "Basic consciousness"; §6.3 lucid onset = ESM crossing from basic ("experiencing *as* an agent") to extended ("experiencing *that* one is an agent") |
| Possibly below that: subject-less felt floor | **Basal sentience** ("the fish"): felt, valenced, locked in the now, *no ESM* | Pattern #33; terminology file (use **sentience**, never "awareness") |
| Reflective self-awareness, the self that reports | **Simply/doubly extended consciousness:** ESM models the system's own states (1st/2nd-order recursion) | §3.5 |
| Narrative self (Gallagher 2000) | ESM self-narrative, confabulated continuity | §3.1 ("unified narrative … outright confabulations"); §4.2.2 personal identity |
| Source material for all of the above | ISM (body schema, proprioceptive calibration, autobiographical structures), never conscious | §3.2 |

So the paper should say: **the minimal self is the basic-level ESM, grounded in the ISM and possibly resting on a sentience floor beneath it. Reporting requires the extended-level ESM.** That puts the gap between the two layers into the architecture rather than leaving it a matter of method. It is also more defensible than the ISM placement, because FMT itself already distinguishes the two rungs, and the lucid-dreaming passage (§6.3) uses exactly this crossing. → Open question Q1.

## 1. Argument map

**P1 (report is output).** Every first-person datum (interview transcript, DES description, questionnaire item, button press with a confidence rating) is a behavioural output produced by the reporting system. *Uncontroversial; microphenomenologists accept it.*

**P2 (report needs objectification).** Reporting on one's *own* experience requires the system to represent its own state *as an object*, i.e. as content of a self-model operating on its own states. In FMT that is extended consciousness (§3.5: "the ESM includes a model of the system's own states and processes"). Patterns #21/#22: only content that is formatted both first- and third-person ("felt AND objectifiable") is sayable. The first-person-*only* subject has no third-person handle until the closure loop gives it one, and giving it one *is* objectifying it.

**P3 (the minimal self is defined by non-objectification).** Zahavi (2005, 2014) and Gallagher (2000) define the minimal self as pre-reflective, non-positional, *not* given as an object. For-me-ness is "the subjective character of experience", not one more item in experience (Zahavi & Kriegel 2016).

**C1 (from P2+P3).** Any report *about* the minimal self is generated from an objectified (extended-level) model of it. The report's subject matter has been converted. In Guillot's (2017) taxonomy, **reporting turns for-me-ness (a structural feature of experience) into me-ness (the self as content)**. This is the conceptual core of the paper and, as far as this search found, nobody has put it in exactly these terms. Guillot separates the notions but does not make the report-conversion argument.

**P4 (the live readout can't cause the report; only re-simulation can).** FMT-internal and recent (pattern #35, MG 2026-08-10): live quale-reading is a **dashboard** (#13): a non-causal readout, "a component of a causal-power system". Causal power over content begins only where the readout *triggers associated simulation* (MG calls this "dreaming": daydreaming, planning, delving into memories). A verbal report is a causal effect. So **whatever drives the report is a re-simulation, not the live frame.** ⚠ #35 is new theory, so check paper-assertability with MG (Q4).

**P5 (memory reconstructs).** Microphenomenological interviews are retrospective: they "evoke" a singular past episode, often minutes to hours afterwards. Evocation is re-enactment (pattern #16: retrieval of a consolidated trace *reconstitutes the loop*, so it is a new instantiation, not a playback). Bartlett (1932), Loftus & Palmer (1974): recall is reconstructive and sensitive to the wording of the question. The interviewer's "content-empty" questions reduce suggestion but can't make retrieval non-reconstructive.

**P6 (language compresses and back-writes).** Language serialises a high-dimensional state through a low-rank channel (§3.6.1: working memory = occupancy of a narrow read/write path). 2015 (S.86): the receiver reconstructs the model the sender intended. Verbalising can overwrite the non-verbal memory it describes (verbal overshadowing, Schooler & Engstler-Schooler 1990). The ESM fills gaps by confabulating, and does it fluently (split-brain interpreter, Gazzaniga 2000; FMT §4.2.2; choice blindness, Johansson et al. 2005).

**C2 (main conclusion).** First-person methods sample the extended-level ESM's re-simulated, verbally serialised model of an earlier frame. At best (high authenticity, high coherence, trained subject) they give an **accurate description of a reflective model of the minimal self**. That is real and useful data about the *reporting system*. It can't settle claims about the *structure* of the pre-reflective layer (whether for-me-ness is ubiquitous, whether there is selfless experience, whether minimal selfhood is agentive or passive) without independent third-person anchoring.

**C3 (constructive).** The right tools for the minimal self are those that don't route through the extended ESM: no-report and no-cognition paradigms, implicit bodily-self measures, perturbational complexity, serial awakening scored against physiology, lesion/dissociation logic, and in-silico ablation (§4). First-person reports keep a role as **one channel to be modelled** (a heterophenomenological text whose generator is known to be the ESM), not as the ground truth.

**What the thesis does NOT claim (state it in the paper):**
- Not that experience is illusory. FMT is not illusionist: experience is real at the virtual level (§3.4.5). This is the main point that separates the paper from Dennett's heterophenomenology.
- Not that reports are worthless or that microphenomenology is bad science. Its reports of *EWM content* (what was seen or heard, task strategy) can be accurate and even predict neural data (Allen, MoC7 #52; Lutz et al. 2002). The claim is scoped to **pre-reflective self-structure**.
- Not that the minimal self is unknowable. It is reachable by inference from data that don't pass through the explicit self-model.

**Why this is more than Schwitzgebel/Nisbett-Wilson restated (the paper's novelty claim):** those critiques are *empirical* (introspection turns out unreliable). This one is *architectural*: it says *why* the minimal self specifically can't be reported (reporting requires a level change), and it predicts *which* features will be distorted: those that consist in not being objectified (for-me-ness, perspectival origin, the absence of a subject/object split, temporal thickness) rather than EWM content. That differential is what makes it testable (§6).

## 2. Literature: the minimal self (all verified, §9)

- **Gallagher 2000** (*TiCS*): canonical split between the *minimal self* (immediate, pre-reflective; sense of ownership and agency, which dissociate in schizophrenic thought insertion) and the *narrative self* (extended in time, linguistic). FMT: minimal ≈ basic ESM; narrative ≈ extended ESM self-narrative.
- **Zahavi 2005** *Subjectivity and Selfhood*; **2014** *Self and Other*: pre-reflective self-awareness; for-me-ness as the "experiential dimension" of selfhood; Husserlian inner time-consciousness answers the Heidelberg regress (reflection presupposes prior pre-reflective self-acquaintance). **Zahavi & Kriegel 2016** "For-me-ness: what it is and what it is not": what the notion is and is not committed to. *Zahavi is on the MoC7 advisory board, and the paper will be read in Copenhagen.*
- **Legrand 2006** (*PCS*), **2007** (*Conscious Cogn*): bodily, sensorimotor roots of pre-reflective self-consciousness; "self-as-subject" vs self-as-object. Closest to FMT's ISM→basic-ESM grounding.
- **Christoff, Cosmelli, Legrand & Thompson 2011** (*TiCS*): "Specifying the self for cognitive neuroscience". Argues the self-related cortical-midline literature (Northoff & Bermpohl 2004) studies self-*as-object*/self-evaluation, not the pre-reflective self-as-subject, and proposes a sensorimotor self-specifying process instead. **Key ally:** a phenomenology-friendly paper (Thompson co-author) that already concedes the *paradigms* that rely on self-reference/report miss the minimal self. MG generalises the point from task paradigms to report methods.
- **Blanke & Metzinger 2009** (*TiCS*): minimal phenomenal selfhood (MPS) = self-identification, self-location, first-person perspective; full-body illusions. **Lenggenhager et al. 2007** (*Science*), **Ehrsson 2007** (*Science*): induced with visuotactile conflict and measured *implicitly* (drift in self-location, skin conductance to threat on the illusory body). **This is the model for FMT's alternative toolkit:** the minimal self manipulated and measured without trusting the report. **Park & Blanke 2019** (*TiCS*): coupling inner (interoceptive, heartbeat-evoked) and outer body for self-consciousness.
- **Metzinger 2003** *Being No One*; **Metzinger 2020** "Minimal phenomenal experience" (*PhiMiSci*); **Gamma & Metzinger 2021** MPE-92M questionnaire (*PLoS ONE*, 1,403 usable responses, 12 factors); **Metzinger 2024** *The Elephant and the Blind* (MIT Press; open-access status UNVERIFIED). ⚠ **The MPE project is built *entirely* on retrospective reports of "pure awareness"**, so it is the cleanest target for MG's argument, and also the cleanest case where an author has already worried in print about the report problem (check Metzinger's own discussion of retrospective-report reliability in *Elephant* before writing; not verified here). Note **Lopez-Sola (MoC7 P11)**: MPEs show *raised* neural entropy without phenomenal richness, a third-person handle on MPE.
- **Damasio 1999** *The Feeling of What Happens*; **2010** *Self Comes to Mind*: protoself (non-conscious body mapping), core self (transient, here-and-now, survives amnesia), autobiographical self. FMT maps protoself→ISM, core self→basic ESM, autobiographical→extended ESM. Publishers (Harcourt 1999; Pantheon 2010) UNVERIFIED; works verified.
- **Panksepp 1998** (*JCS* 5(5–6):566–582): "SELF" = Simple Ego-type Life Form in subcortical/midbrain emotional circuits, primary-process affective consciousness. **Panksepp & Northoff 2009** (*Conscious Cogn*): trans-species core SELF. ≈ FMT's sentience floor (#33, "there is still a fish in there"). Panksepp's evidence base is *animal and lesion data*, not human introspection, which is a useful precedent for MG's method.
- **Skeptics of for-me-ness (useful allies):** **Guillot 2017** (*Rev Phil Psych*, published online 2016) separates for-me-ness / me-ness / mineness and argues they get conflated. **Letheby 2020** "Being for no-one" (*PhiMiSci*) and **Letheby & Gerrans 2017** (*NoC*), **Millière 2017** (*Front Hum Neurosci*): psychedelic ego dissolution as a possible counterexample to ubiquitous minimal selfhood. ⚠ These anti-ubiquity arguments **also rest on retrospective reports**. MG's thesis cuts both ways: reports can neither establish nor refute ubiquitous for-me-ness. Saying so even-handedly is the paper's strongest rhetorical position.
- **Sass & Parnas 2003** (*Schizophr Bull*): ipseity disturbance in schizophrenia, detected by phenomenological interview (EASE). **This is a counterexample to watch** (§5, W4).
- **Windt, Nielsen & Thompson 2016** (*TiCS*): "Does consciousness disappear in dreamless sleep?" Proposes categories of dreamless-sleep experience (including selfless/contentless states) investigated with serial awakenings. **Thompson 2015** *Waking, Dreaming, Being* (Columbia UP). Direct bridge to pattern #33 and the MoC7 keynote speaker.

## 3. Literature: microphenomenology and its critics

### 3a. The method and its self-understanding (steelman material)
- **Varela 1996** (*JCS* 3(4):330–349): neurophenomenology. First-person and third-person data mutually constrain each other; disciplined first-person method in place of a "theoretical fix" for the hard problem.
- **Vermersch 1999** "Introspection as practice" (*JCS* 6(2–3):17–42): the explicitation interview; introspection as a practice that can be improved, without needing a full theory of it.
- **Depraz, Varela & Vermersch 2003** *On Becoming Aware* (Benjamins): three gestures of becoming aware (suspension, redirection, letting-come). The pre-reflective is "lived but not yet noticed", and **becoming aware is described as a *transformation* of experience.** ⚠ This is the concession MG can build on: the founders themselves say the reflective act changes the experience.
- **Petitmengin 2006** (*PCS*): the micro-phenomenological interview. Second person; stabilise attention on one singular past episode; *evocation* (re-living) verified by linguistic and nonverbal markers; questions on the *how* (not what/why); "content-empty" questions; aims at the pre-reflective, procedural dimension.
- **Petitmengin & Bitbol 2009** (*JCS* 16(10–12)): validity redefined as **authenticity and coherence** rather than correspondence; replies to the introspection critiques from Comte to Nisbett & Wilson. ⚠ Pages conflict (HAL: 252–284; other listing 363–404). UNVERIFIED pages.
- **Bitbol & Petitmengin 2013** (*Kairos*): "On the possibility and reality of introspection". **Bitbol & Petitmengin 2017** (Blackwell Companion to Consciousness): neurophenomenology and the micro-phenomenological interview.
- **Petitmengin, Remillieux & Valenzuela-Moguillansky 2019** (*PCS*; online 2018): micro-phenomenological analysis method, generic diachronic and synchronic structures.
- **Petitmengin, Remillieux, Cahour & Carter-Thomas 2013** (*Conscious Cogn* 22(2):654–669): "A gap in Nisbett and Wilson's findings?" Repeated the choice-blindness protocol. Unassisted participants missed the swap in ~79.6% of trials. **Participants guided by the interview detected it in 80% of cases.** Their conclusion: people are usually unaware of their decision processes but can reach them through specific mental acts. ⚠ **This is the strongest single empirical steelman. The paper must engage it head-on** (§5, W3, and the prediction in §6 is built to answer it).
- **Lutz, Lachaux, Martinerie & Varela 2002** (*PNAS*): trained first-person reports of preparation states sort trials into clusters with distinct EEG synchrony patterns. Reports *predict* neural dynamics.
- **Gallagher 2003** (*JCS* 10(9–10):85–99) and **Gallagher & Sørensen 2006** (*Conscious Cogn*): "front-loading" phenomenology into experimental design. Note that front-loading uses phenomenology for *hypotheses* and keeps third-person *measurement*, which is very close to what MG proposes. A natural ally.
- **Hurlburt & Heavey 2006** *Exploring Inner Experience*; **Hurlburt & Akhter 2006** (*PCS*): Descriptive Experience Sampling (DES). Random beep, describe the "pristine" experience at the moment of the beep, iterative training interviews. **Hurlburt 2011** *Investigating Pristine Inner Experience* (CUP).

### 3b. The critiques
- **Nisbett & Wilson 1977** (*Psych Rev*): people report causes of their behaviour by applying a priori causal theories, not by introspective access; observers' reports match subjects'.
- **Johansson, Hall, Sikström & Olsson 2005** (*Science*): choice blindness. **Johansson et al. 2006** (*Conscious Cogn* 15(4):673–692): reports from manipulated and non-manipulated trials are linguistically almost indistinguishable. **Hall, Johansson & Strandberg 2012** (*PLoS ONE*): moral attitude reversals.
- **Gazzaniga 2000** (*Brain*): the left-hemisphere interpreter. Earlier formulation: **Gazzaniga & LeDoux 1978** *The Integrated Mind* (Plenum). Book verified; the claim that the interpreter is formulated *there* is UNVERIFIED, so cite Gazzaniga 2000 for the interpreter. FMT already uses this (§4.2.2, §5.2).
- **Bartlett 1932** *Remembering* (CUP; reissued 1995); **Loftus & Palmer 1974** (*JVLVB*): reconstructive memory; question wording alters recall. **Schooler & Engstler-Schooler 1990** (*Cogn Psych*): verbal overshadowing, i.e. describing a face impairs recognising it. *The best evidence that putting an experience into words can damage the memory being described.*
- **Schwitzgebel 2008** "The unreliability of naive introspection" (*Phil Rev*): we're often wrong about our current conscious experience, even in the most favourable cases. ⚠ It targets *naive* introspection, which microphenomenologists will point out at once. MG's argument must work against *trained* introspection, which is why it has to be architectural.
- **Schwitzgebel 2007** (*JCS* 14(3):5–35) "Do you have constant tactile experience of your feet in your shoes?": the rich-vs-thin debate; introspective arguments beg the question. **Refrigerator light problem** (SEP *Introspection* links it to this paper, with Jaynes, Dennett, Noë and Block): checking whether experience is present creates the experience being checked. ⚠ Attribute the *phrase* through the SEP entry, not to a single originator; who coined it is UNVERIFIED.
- **Hurlburt & Schwitzgebel 2007** *Describing Inner Experience? Proponent Meets Skeptic* (MIT Press): DES proponent vs skeptic, worked through real samples. The best existing model of the debate MG wants to have.
- **Dennett 2003** "Who's on first? Heterophenomenology explained" (*JCS* 10(9–10), pp. 19–30 or 10–30, sources differ); **Dennett 2007** "Heterophenomenology reconsidered" (*PCS*). In the same JCS issue: **Jack & Roepstorff (eds.) 2003** *Trusting the Subject?* Vol. 1 (Imprint Academic). The issue/volume is verified; a separate Jack & Roepstorff editorial title "Why trust the subject?" is UNVERIFIED.
- **Froese, Gould & Barrett**, "Re-viewing from within: a commentary on first- and second-person methods in the science of consciousness". Title, authors and abstract verified (Sussex repository). Conclusion: **no convincing case in the literature where the specialised first/second-person techniques, combined with standard methods, produced results that simple introspective reporting could not have.** Venue/year (*Constructivist Foundations* 6(2), 2011) UNVERIFIED. An outsider-friendly critique from *within* enactivism, which makes it very useful.
- **Bayne & Spener 2010** "Introspective humility" (*Phil Issues*); **Spener 2015** "Calibrating introspection" (*Phil Issues*): introspection can be trusted only where it can be calibrated against independent measures. ⚠ **MG's position is a specific case of Spener's calibration view: the minimal self is the domain where calibration through report is structurally impossible.** Cite as convergence.
- **Irvine 2012** (*BJPS*): "Old problems with new measures": subjective measures of consciousness carry the same old problems.
- Pro-training evidence (to concede): **Fox et al. 2012** (*PLoS ONE*, Thompson & Christoff co-authors): meditation experience predicts introspective accuracy for body sensitivity. **Ramsøy & Overgaard 2004** (*PCS*): finer report scales reveal more (origin of the PAS).

## 4. FMT's alternative toolkit: concrete methods that don't route through the explicit self-model

The common principle is to **manipulate or measure the minimal self along a channel that does not require the subject to model themselves as an object**, and treat any report as one more channel whose generator (ESM + memory + language) is known.

1. **Implicit bodily-self measures under covert manipulation.** Full-body and rubber-hand illusions measured by proprioceptive/self-location drift, skin-conductance response to threat on the illusory body, and heartbeat-evoked potentials (Lenggenhager 2007; Ehrsson 2007; Park & Blanke 2019). These measures index self-identification and self-location *without* asking.
2. **No-report paradigms** (Tsuchiya et al. 2015; Frässle et al. 2014: optokinetic nystagmus and pupil track rivalry, and frontal activity tied to introspection/report drops out; Pitts et al. 2014; Kapoor et al. 2022, prefrontal decoding without report). Critiques to cite and absorb: **Overgaard & Fazekas 2016** (can no-report extract true NCC?) and **Block 2019** (you can't stop subjects *thinking*, so no-*cognition* is needed). Block's point helps MG: even silent cognition may engage the reflective ESM, so the strictest version is *no-cognition*, achievable for the minimal self only through implicit/physiological readouts.
3. **Perturbational complexity** (Massimini et al. 2005; Casali et al. 2013 PCI): state-level, report-independent. FMT Table 1 already uses it. Proposal: PCI or its equivalent under conditions that remove the *extended* ESM while sparing the basic level (lucid vs non-lucid REM; ketamine vs propofol; deep meditative MPE vs rest).
4. **Serial awakening scored against physiology** (Siclari et al. 2017; Windt, Nielsen & Thompson 2016): the three-way contrast (experience with recall / experience without recall of content / no experience) is already a partial dissociation between having experience and being able to report it. Pattern #33's prediction (no-experience reports concentrate at the highest arousal thresholds) is a re-analysis of existing data (AIW-190).
5. **Lesion and dissociation logic.** **Anosognosia** is FMT's own flagship (§3.6, Prediction 1): the ISM holds the information, the reporting ESM lacks it, and the sincere report is wrong *about the subject's own body*. It is the clinical proof that the reporting self can be blind to self-level facts. Also the split-brain interpreter (Gazzaniga 2000), thought insertion (ownership/agency dissociation; Gallagher 2000), depersonalisation. ⚠ Literature on *implicit* awareness of deficit in anosognosia is not verified here; find and verify it before use.
6. **Psychedelic and meditative perturbation measured physiologically.** MPE entropy/complexity markers (Lopez-Sola, MoC7 P11); ego-dissolution content controllability (FMT Prediction 2). Use reports only as a dependent variable whose relation to physiology is modelled.
7. **In-silico ablation** (FMT §8.9; crucible / mirror-box): in an artificial system with an explicit self-model and a separable "report head", you can read the basic-level self-model's state directly and compare it with what the report head says. This is the one setting where the minimal-self ground truth is *available*, so it can *quantify* report distortion as a function of recursion depth and serialisation bandwidth. Strong, novel, and MG's home ground. ⚠ Avoid any claim that the AC system is conscious; the point is about the *channel*.

## 5. Honest weaknesses: where a phenomenologist will catch MG out, and how to pre-empt

**W1. Performative contradiction / self-undermining.** FMT was itself built partly from MG's introspection (2015; pattern #33 is MG's intuition: *"an animal in your body you usually have not much to do with"*). Serial-awakening data are reports too. → *Pre-empt:* say it first. The thesis is not "reports are false" but "reports are outputs of a known generator". FMT uses them the way astronomers use a telescope with a known point-spread function: model the instrument, then infer. The claims in this paper about the minimal self are inferences from architecture + implicit/physiological data + dissociations, never from "it seems to me". Name the residual honestly: the minimal self's *existence* is still motivated partly by reflective intuition. Conceding that costs little.

**W2. Unfalsifiability.** "Every report is post-reflective", so no report could ever count against the view. → *Pre-empt:* the thesis makes a **differential** prediction (§6): an asymmetry between how well reports track EWM content and how well they track minimal-self structure. Say in advance what would refute it: trained reports that track covert minimal-self manipulations as well as they track content manipulations.

**W3. Petitmengin 2013 and Allen (MoC7) show that trained reports work.** → Concede for content (R4, R5). The argument stands or falls on the *content vs self-structure* scoping, so define that line carefully: self-*location* and body *content* may be partly reportable as EWM/ESM content. What is claimed unreportable is for-me-ness/perspectival origin as *structure*. Risk: a reviewer says the line is drawn so that whatever turns out reportable counts as "content". Fix that by committing to the operational list *before* the data (pre-registration language).

**W4. Clinical phenomenology detects minimal-self disturbance** (Sass & Parnas 2003; EASE). → Interviews detect that the *ESM's model* of the self has changed. A difference can be visible even when the structure behind it isn't. Analogy: you can tell a photo is blurred without being able to recover the scene. Not a full answer, so flag it as a limit.

**W5. "That's just Dennett/heterophenomenology."** → Two differences, both explicit: (i) FMT is realist about experience at the virtual level (§3.4.5), whereas Dennett is not; (ii) FMT says *where* the distortion enters (rung change, re-simulation, serialisation) and so predicts *which* features distort. Dennett treats the report text as the whole of the data.

**W6. "That's just Schwitzgebel/Nisbett-Wilson."** → They give empirical unreliability of *naive* reports. This paper gives an architectural impossibility for *one target* (pre-reflective self-structure) that survives training. It also *grants* that trained reports do well on content, which they don't.

**W7. FMT's own placement of the minimal self is unsettled** (§0 mapping; basic ESM vs sentience floor). A phenomenologist will press: "if you can't say where it is, how do you know reports miss it?" → Settle the mapping (Q1) before drafting. The argument needs only that the minimal self sits *below* the reflective rung, which both candidate loci satisfy.

**W8. Asserted-not-derived exposure.** P2 leans on patterns #21/#22, which the registry marks as **paper-assertability undecided** (they ride on the §3.4.3 asserted-not-derived cut). → Either state P2 using only §3.5's recursion ladder (safe), or get MG's ruling (Q4). The same applies to P4/#35 (new theory).

**W9. Tone.** "Inflated and garbled through language" will read as dismissive to a phenomenology audience and conflicts with the prose-register rules. → Use the neutral terms: *reconstructed, serialised, re-simulated*. Let the argument do the work.

**W10. Microphenomenology isn't one thing.** Petitmengin's analysis method, DES, Vermersch's explicitation and Metzinger's MPE survey differ a lot (DES is momentary, MP retrospective). → Treat them separately; the timing argument (P5) hits MP harder than DES, while P2/P3 hit all of them equally.

## 6. A testable prediction the paper could carry

**"Minimal-self blindness": the choice-blindness logic of Petitmengin et al. (2013) applied to the minimal self.**

*Design sketch.* A within-subject study with three arms, mirroring Petitmengin et al. 2013 so it answers their best result on their own terms:
- **Content manipulation (control):** covert change to an EWM feature of a stimulus during a task (as in choice blindness).
- **Minimal-self manipulation:** covert change to self-location/self-identification with a full-body or rubber-hand illusion (synchronous ↔ asynchronous visuotactile stroking switched mid-block, or a heartbeat-synchronous visual signal; Park & Blanke 2019). Implicit markers recorded throughout: self-location drift, SCR to threat, heartbeat-evoked potential.
- **Report conditions:** untrained retrospective report vs full micro-phenomenological interview (certified interviewer), and DES-style immediate probe as a third arm if resources allow.

*FMT prediction (differential).* Micro-phenomenological interviewing will raise detection/report accuracy for the **content manipulation** (replicating Petitmengin 2013) **substantially more than for the minimal-self manipulation**. For the minimal-self arm, interview reports will (a) track implicit markers trial by trial *worse* than they track the content change, and (b) converge on the subject's prior beliefs and the interview's own descriptive vocabulary (scored with the MOSAIC-style topic modelling presented at MoC7, Beauté #108). In plain terms: **training improves report accuracy for content far more than for the self.** The implicit markers will show the manipulation worked in both arms.

*Refutation.* If MP-trained reports track the covert minimal-self manipulation as well as the content manipulation (matched for implicit-marker effect size), and beyond what prior beliefs predict, the thesis is wrong for that facet of the minimal self.

*Why it's good.* It uses established paradigms only, it steelmans MP (a certified interviewer, their best protocol), and it is a natural collaboration with exactly the labs MoC7 showed are active (Allen at Durham; Petitmengin; the Blanke/Park line). ⚠ Effect-size matching between the two arms is the hard design problem: the implicit markers must show comparable manipulation strength, or the asymmetry is uninterpretable.

**Secondary, cheap prediction (re-analysis, already in FMT's pipeline):** pattern #33 / AIW-190: in serial-awakening datasets, "no experience" reports concentrate at the highest arousal thresholds, while "experience without recall" dominates elsewhere. This links the sentience floor to report failure using existing data.
