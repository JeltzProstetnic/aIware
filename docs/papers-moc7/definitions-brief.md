## 1. Theses

**Paper A, "The definitions are not settled."** The phenomenological formulations treated as the agreed definition of consciousness (Nagel, Block, Searle, Chalmers, the IIT axioms, Schwitzgebel) are by their own authors' admission reference-fixing devices (pointers, synonyms or lists of examples), not definitions. None is non-circular and none is operationalisable without importing a further theory. The best survey data show researchers disagree on the definiendum itself. That is consistent with the MoC7 participants asking for a better definition, or at least a map of the terms.

**Paper B, "Deriving the definition."** A definition of consciousness can be built step by step from consciousness-free primitives (data → information → interpretation → knowledge → meaning → model → self-model). The resulting definition, the capacity of an entity to generate a model of itself, relate it to itself and interact with it, is formally non-circular. The phenomenological pointers, unpacked with care, presuppose the same structure: a subject, a vantage point, memory and report. The one premise that is not derived is the identification of the pointers' referent with this capacity. The paper has to state that premise openly and defend it, because that is where the serious objection is (§5).

**3.2 Francken et al. 2022. [V+q] The strongest published evidence.**
Francken, Beerendonk, Molenaar, Fahrenfort, Kiverstein, Seth & van Gaal, "An academic survey on theoretical foundations, common assumptions and the current state of consciousness science", *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2022(1):niac011, DOI 10.1093/nc/niac011. Twenty-two questions; ASSC 2018 and 2019; **n = 166**.
- Phenomenal content beyond access: **60.6% yes**. "Consciousness requires higher-order states": **34.5% yes**. Sharp unconscious-to-conscious boundary: **33.5%**; seniors and juniors differ significantly (p = 0.009).
- Verbatim conclusion: *"The definition of 'what' needs to be explained by consciousness science is not shared between all respondents."* They recommend making one's definition explicit as *"a conceptual or computational definition"*, which supports FMT's demand directly.
- They **did not ask respondents to write down a definition** because *"we expected that many respondents would have difficulty formulating their definition."* For Paper A this is a striking data point: the survey designers expected the field to be unable to state its own definiendum.
- They note that operationalisation is theory-laden, so researchers "may not be investigating the same phenomenon even though the same overarching label is used" (citing Irvine 2013; Figdor 2013; Francken & Slors 2014; the last two **[UNVERIFIED]** independently).

**Premise list for Paper B (what a reader must grant):**
1. M0, the admissibility criteria. These are the only normative premises, and Paper A argues for them.
2. D1–D6 are stipulative definitions. They are consciousness-free and a reader may accept them without accepting FMT.
3. P-B: a reference system is a model. This is the conceptual hinge that joins epistemics to architecture (`fmt-2015-definitions.md` §"Bezugssystem is a model").
4. D5a: the regress has a non-assigned base layer. This is argued, not stipulated (§3.6), and it is what keeps the D4–D7 loop from being vicious.
5. S8–S10 compose D7 reflexively. No new primitive is needed.
6. **B, the identification.** This premise is not derived. It is argued by (a) semantic unpacking (§5.3), (b) explanatory payoff: the meta-problem (§3.8), qualia as level-specific properties, the Block note (§3.4), and (c) the empirical dissociations of §6.

**Plain-words verdict on "derived":** steps D1 through DC are a genuine derivation from consciousness-free primitives. Calling the result *consciousness* rests on premise B, which is an identification hypothesis, the same kind of claim as "water is H₂O" or "life is self-maintaining replication". The paper should say this in those words rather than claim that the derivation alone forces the identification.

## 5. Kleiner's objection: steelman, replies, honest weaknesses

### 5.1 The objection as heard (MG's report)
"FMT's definition is not a definition of consciousness at all; it is circular, and it confines FMT to talking about cognition at best."

### 5.2 Steelman, three versions in increasing strength

**K1, formal circularity.** "Self-model" presupposes a self; "relate to itself" presupposes a subject; so the definiens contains the definiendum.
*Reply:* this fails. "Itself" is the reflexive pronoun for the *entity* (D1's reading device), not for an experiential self. The ESM is an *output* of the process, not an input (S8). Each term reduces to D1–D7.

**K2, a hidden loop in "model".** D7 defines a model by read access = *interpretation*; interpretation (D4) is assignment to a reference system; and a reference system *is* a model (P-B). That is a circle.
*Reply:* it is a recursive definition with a base case (D5a), like an inductive definition of the natural numbers. It is not vicious **provided the base layer is specified without the word "model"**, i.e. physically, as learned connectivity that registers differences differentially (D1). The full paper's §3.6 makes the regress argument but **does not state that it also grounds the definition of "model"**. Paper B must make this explicit (W5). This may be the circle Kleiner actually saw. See Q2.

**K3, the strongest version: change of subject / question-begging.** "Grant the derivation. What you have defined is a *cognitive capacity*. Whether that capacity *is* consciousness is precisely what needed showing. To check that your definition hits consciousness, you need an antecedent grip on consciousness, and that grip is phenomenological. So either (a) your 'consciousness' is a new technical term, in which case you are no longer talking about what the field means, or (b) you are analysing the ordinary concept, in which case the phenomenological concept is presupposed and your claim that it is circular applies to you as well. Either way, the self-modelling definition reaches *access/cognition* and leaves phenomenality untouched: 'cognition at best'." This is the line of Smith (MoC7 P15) and the structural-turn programme (Kleiner 2024; K&L 2024 take experience as the given explanandum).

**Aggravating evidence against FMT's own text (fix before submitting either paper):**
- §3.1 *opens* "Consciousness — **the capacity for subjective experience** — is constituted by a specific kind of process". FMT's own first sentence uses the phenomenological gloss to fix the referent. K3 can quote it.
- Table 1 operationalises consciousness by PCI > 0.31, a threshold calibrated on clinical states classified by behaviour and report, so on the folk concept (W6).
- Table 1: "The ongoing capacity … A process, not a property". This conflates the 2015 split between **Bewusstsein (capacity)** and **Bewusstheit (process)** (W3).

### 5.3 Replies

**R1, reference-fixing is not definition (the main reply).** Use the Kripke/Putnam distinction (Kripke 1980, *Naming and Necessity*; Putnam 1975, "The meaning of 'meaning'": both **[UNVERIFIED]** bibliographic details, standard works). A reference-fixer picks out the phenomenon ("the clear liquid in rivers"); a theoretical definition says what it is (H₂O). **Searle uses exactly this water example** to say his common-sense definition only "identif[ies] the target" and that analytic definitions come at the end. Block and Schwitzgebel concede the same. So:
- Using the phenomenological pointer to fix the referent is legitimate and *not* circular, for FMT as for anyone.
- Calling the pointer the *definition* is the error Paper A diagnoses.
- FMT's sentence is a candidate *theoretical* definition. It is open to the same test as H₂O: does it pick out the same cases as the pointer, and does it explain more than the pointer?
This also corrects MG's framing, and the correction matters: **the phenomenological formulations are not "circular definitions" so much as non-definitions**, as their authors say. Attacking them as failed definitions invites the reply "we never claimed they were". Attack instead the claim that the matter is *settled*.

**R2, symmetry: the bridge is everyone's problem.** K&L 2024 make the concept of experience a free parameter ("one's chosen concept"). IIT needs its own bridge from axioms to postulates. Every theory, phenomenological starting point or not, needs a premise linking its definiens to the pointer. FMT states its bridge (B). The claim that FMT's definition is "not one of consciousness" holds against every theory or against none.

**R3, semantic hygiene: the pointers, unpacked, presuppose FMT structure.** For each, list what must be in place for the formula to apply. *Caution:* this establishes the pointers' **application conditions**, not that the referent *is* the self-model (see W2).
- *Nagel, "something it is like for the organism".* Both of Stoljar's readings help: the "resemblance" reading requires a comparator holding two representations and a respect of comparison; the reading that is not empty requires the subject to be *aware of being in* the state, a self-relational condition. "For the organism" requires a point of view: a represented distinction between the organism and the rest, i.e. a self/world partition. That is ESM ⊆ EWM.
- *Block, "experience".* In ordinary use, "I experienced X" has an indexical subject ("I": the self-model), a tense (memory) and a report (language). MG's point: phenomenology is accessed through the experience-report, which is the output of a self-model consulting memory. Block concedes that experience can only be pointed at, and pointing is a report act.
- *Searle, "states … that begin when we wake … until … 'unconscious'".* The definiens contains "unconscious". The boundaries are known to the subject only *retrospectively*, as a gap in episodic memory: one never experiences the onset of unconsciousness. So the anchor runs through autobiographical memory, and Windt et al. 2016 dispute it anyway.
- *IIT existence axiom.* "*My* experience here and now exists … the only fact *I* can be absolutely sure of … from its own intrinsic perspective." This is a first-person certainty claim, i.e. a self-model asserting its own existence. In FMT terms it is ESM content in the strict sense. IIT's starting point is an act of self-attribution.
- *Schwitzgebel.* The positive and negative examples are sorted by the subject's introspective classification. Masked stimuli are negative because they are unreportable. The classifier is the reporting self-model, so the concept is anchored on the access side.
**Result:** every accepted pointer is applied *through* self-reference, memory and report. That is the structure FMT defines. It is the core of Paper B's argument that "careful semantics forces the concession".

**R4, report is post hoc: the literature.** These sources show that introspective reports are constructed by the self-model, from memory and language, rather than read off experience:
- **Nisbett & Wilson 1977** [V]: "Telling more than we can know: Verbal reports on mental processes", *Psychol. Rev.* 84(3):231–259, DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.84.3.231. Reports on higher-order processes rest on implicit causal theories, not introspective access.
- **Schwitzgebel 2008** [V]: "The unreliability of naive introspection", *Phil. Rev.* 117(2):245–273. DOI **[UNVERIFIED]**; believed to be 10.1215/00318108-2007-037. Broad ineptitude about *current* experience (emotion, peripheral vision, thought).
- **Hurlburt & Schwitzgebel 2007** [V]: *Describing Inner Experience? Proponent Meets Skeptic*, MIT Press. Descriptive Experience Sampling with one subject: is careful, beeper-cued report reliable?
- **Dennett 1991** [V] (*Consciousness Explained*; in the FMT reference list) and **Dennett 2003** [V], "Who's on first? Heterophenomenology explained", *JCS* 10(9–10):19–30. Heterophenomenology: treat reports as data about what the subject *believes*.
- **Chalmers 2018** [V]: "The meta-problem of consciousness", *JCS* 25(9–10):6–61 (volume/issue from secondary sources). Explaining *why we report* a hard problem is an easy problem. FMT's §3.8 answers it.
- **Graziano & Webb 2015** [V]: a model of attention makes the brain *conclude* that it has experience.
- **Johansson et al. 2005**, choice blindness, *Science* 310:116–119 **[UNVERIFIED]**: confabulated reasons for choices never made.
- Use carefully: **Frankish 2016** [V]. FMT is *not* illusionist: on FMT, experience is genuine at the virtual level (§3.4 two-level ontology). Paper B must separate "reports are constructed" from "there is nothing reported" (Q6).

**R5, "cognition at best" assumes the partition FMT rejects.** The charge assumes that cognition/access and experience are two things. FMT's §3.4 Block note argues they are two descriptions of one process at different levels. This is a substantive thesis, so R5 alone gives a standoff. It needs R1 to R4, plus the *explanatory* reasons (the meta-problem; why the explanatory gap appears, from the level distinction and the regress) to break the tie.

**R6, the capacity/process split answers MG's sleep case.** On the 2015 split, a sleeping human retains **Bewusstsein** (the capacity; a stimulus restarts the process within seconds) while **Bewusstheit** (the ongoing interaction) is intermittent or reduced. The phenomenological pointer, by contrast, has no way to tell "capacity intact, process paused" from "capacity absent". That is a clean, checkable advantage of the derived definition over the pointer. **Do not use arousability as the evidence of consciousness**: a thermostat is arousable (W4).

### 5.4 Honest weaknesses (MG asked for the truth)

- **W1, the choice of self-modelling is not forced by D1–D7.** The epistemic chain yields "model" and allows "self-model", but nothing in it says the definiens of consciousness must be *self*-modelling rather than world-modelling, or modelling as such. That choice is premise B, and B needs independent support. The best counter-cases are Metzinger's selfless MPE ("pure awareness", 2020) and Windt's proposal that dreamless-sleep experience is a candidate MPE. FMT's reply ("the *report* of selflessness is itself produced by a self-model; phenomenal selflessness ≠ computational absence of ESM/ISM") is available, but see W2.
- **W2, the report argument cuts both ways.** If every datum about consciousness reaches us through self-model, memory and language, then the association between reported consciousness and self-modelling is *guaranteed by the method*. It cannot also count as evidence that consciousness *is* self-modelling. The report argument therefore supports **Paper A** fully (phenomenological definitions are not observation-neutral) but supports **Paper B** only negatively (no rival definition escapes the self-model). Without care, B reaches Kleiner's conclusion: FMT defines *reportable/accessible* consciousness. To reach more, FMT must argue that no phenomenal residue lies beyond what the self-model ladder supports. The paper's own claim that basic consciousness may exist without global broadcast (§3.4) shows FMT does take a position here. Paper B must state it in one place.
- **W3, capacity vs process.** Current English text ("ongoing capacity … a process, not a property") merges Bewusstsein and Bewusstheit. Kleiner's "not a definition" may partly target this blur. Adopt the 2015 split in Paper B, and in the master (Q4).
- **W4, MG's sleep and insect claims conflict with the paper as written.** (a) The full paper's state table gives deep NREM as "Mostly absent" consciousness, while MG now says one is "not unconscious while sleeping". R6 reconciles the two only if the claim becomes "the *capacity* persists". (b) The insect claim ("only a now, no how") is stronger than the paper's "insects (basic, if present)". (c) Arousability ("a touch wakes you") is a property of the arousal axis, which clinical neurology already separates from awareness. Used as evidence of consciousness it proves too much.
- **W5, the model/interpretation loop (K2)** is closed only if the base layer is physically specified. The paper never says this in the definitional context.
- **W6, the operational anchor borrows the folk concept.** PCI > 0.31 was validated against clinically classified states. Say so and present PCI as a *marker*, as footnote 1 of Table 1 already half does.
- **W7, "more scientific" must be comparative and criterion-based.** It must be scored against M0's criteria (non-circularity, physical grounding, substrate neutrality, gradedness, predictive yield), or it is rhetoric. Reichert (MoC7 P22) and Francken (call for "conceptual or computational" definitions) give external warrant for the criteria.
- **W8, provenance.** The claim "derived in 2015" needs the actual book passage. If the book states the p.57 definition without an explicit argument from ch. 3.1, Paper B should present the derivation as a *reconstruction that the 2015 definitions support*, not as a quotation (Q1).
- **W9, not a fight with Kleiner.** Paper A cannot cite a private conversation, and a paper framed as a rebuttal of an editor at NoC/JAIC (Kleiner sits on both boards and on the MoC7 committee; see `docs/pending-moc7-copenhagen.md`) carries reputational cost. A needs a *published* statement of the "settled" view as its foil (Q7).

## 6. Outlines, venues and the merge question

### Paper A, "Pointing is not defining: the state of the definition of consciousness" (working title)
1. **Introduction.** The claim that the definition is settled (cite a *published* statement, Q7). The field's own call for a map (Francken 2022; MoC7 consensus-project aims).
2. **Criteria for a scientific definition.** M0: non-circularity, grounding, operationalisability, substrate neutrality. Distinguish *reference-fixer* from *definition* (Kripke/Putnam; Searle's water example).
3. **Survey of the accepted formulations** (table §2, rows 1–12). For each: definiens, undefined terms, circularity, operationalisability.
4. **Authors' own concessions.** Block ("cannot define … non-circular"), Searle ("analytic definitions come at the end"), Schwitzgebel (synonymy rejected), Sutherland via Chalmers, K&L ("one's chosen concept").
5. **Empirical non-consensus.** Francken 2022 numbers; Vimal's ~40 meanings; the clinical/philosophical inversion of "awareness"; disputed sleep anchor (Windt et al. 2016); Irvine's eliminativism as the cost of doing nothing; MoC7 consensus-project result *if a written source exists*.
6. **Report dependence.** Every pointer is applied through report (Nisbett & Wilson; Schwitzgebel 2008; Hurlburt & Schwitzgebel; heterophenomenology), so none is observation-neutral.
7. **What a map of definitions needs.** A short constructive section: an orthogonal glossary (arousal / content / access / phenomenal pointer / self-reference / capacity vs process), each term grounded. End on criteria; FMT is named at most once.
- **Length:** 7,000–9,000 words.
- **Venues (from `docs/aiw07-journal-options.md`):** **JCS**, 9,000 words incl. references: the natural home for a conceptual critique, and the paper fits. It was excluded only for the full FMT paper's length. **PhiMiSci**: typically 4–10k, hard ceiling 20k, double-blind, diamond OA; strong fit. **Theory & Psychology**: ≤10,000 incl. references. **New Ideas in Psychology**: 7,500. **Synthese**: 15–30 printed pages. **Excluded:** NoC and C&C (both rejected us, S324; Kleiner is an AE at NoC).

### Paper B, "Deriving a definition of consciousness from the theory of information" (working title)
1. **The problem.** Pointers are not definitions (one paragraph, citing Paper A).
2. **The primitives.** D1–D3 (data, coding, information), physically grounded and consciousness-free.
3. **Interpretation, knowledge, meaning** (D4–D6). Knowledge without truth and without consciousness; the regress and its base case (D5a).
4. **Reference systems are models** (P-B, D7). The loop and its base case (K2/W5 answered explicitly).

7. **Semantic hygiene.** Nagel, Block, Searle and the IIT axioms (Schwitzgebel optional) unpacked into self-reference, memory and report (R3), with the post-hoc report literature (R4).
8. **Objections.** K1–K3; "cognition at best" (R5); the selfless-MPE and dreamless-sleep cases (W1); the method-guarantee problem (W2); illusionism contrasted.
9. **What the definition buys.** Sleep: capacity persists while the process pauses (R6); graded levels; substrate-neutral criteria; non-report predictions taken from the master's §8.
- **Length:** 9,000–12,000 words.
- **Venues:** **PhiMiSci** (first choice: theory-friendly, double-blind, €0); **Synthese** (the philosophy-of-science angle on definition and reference); **Philosophical Psychology**; **Mind and Matter**.

**Recommendation: two papers, A first.** Reasons:
