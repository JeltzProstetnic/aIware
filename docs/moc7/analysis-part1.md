## 1. Conference structure relevant to MG's goals

### 1.1 Transdisciplinary Consensus Project (pp. 22–23; plenary #20)
- **Format.** Small-group discussion sessions in classrooms A101–A112: Mon 15:00, Tue 10:00 and 14:00, Wed 10:00, Thu 10:00 and 14:00. These close with the Friday 15:00 plenary and closing remarks in Margrethe Bohr Salen (NBB). Signorelli introduced the project on Mon 14:30 (#20, "The MoC transdisciplinary consensus project").
- **What it produces.** A roadmap of "focus areas (i.e. research programs)", each with success criteria, deliverables, requirements, methodologies, breakthroughs and milestones, and a note of how the areas "conflict or complement each other". In the booklet's words, consensus means "agreeing on the map of possibilities, not on the final destination, theory or framework". The plenary abstract says it "may end (or not) with the first collective paper of its kind in the field".
- **For MG.** The project wants methods, not theories. The welcome text adds that the sessions aim to "clarify concepts, reach common methodological standards, and set future priorities". The guide's playbook (reporting standard, timestamped prediction registry, interaction predictions, structure-vs-absolute benchmark) fits this well. **New:** the roadmap figure ("focus areas, their milestones and deliverables, and how they relate") is itself a graph. Mudrik's prediction maps (#21) and Streicher's causal graph of C-tests (#3) both supply graph-structured inputs for it. If the collective paper happens, a "theory-claim registry / prediction map" focus area is the natural home for FMT.
- **Follow-up.** The booklet has a "Consensus project" QR link with more information about the groups. Find out how to contribute to the collective paper after the conference (authorship model, deadline) from moc7-organisers@amcs.science.

## 2. Abstract-by-abstract analysis

Scores: 0 = irrelevant, 1 = background, 2 = relevant (engage or cite), 3 = high priority (act on it).

### 2.1 Score ≥ 2

**#1 Megan Peters (University College London).** Keynote, Mon 9:00, Aud 1. *"A proximity-guided iterative natural kinds strategy: Let's do computational epistemology to discover what consciousness is and who has it"*
- **Summary.** Indicator, test and "vibes" approaches are inadequate because indicators cannot be assumed to "mean the same thing" in systems they were not validated on. She proposes extending the iterative natural-kinds strategy into a joint philosophical, empirical and computational-epistemology programme.
- **Score: 2.**
- **Relation to FMT.** Competes and offers a collaboration opportunity. FMT names a mechanism-level kind: self-simulation with self/world update closure. That is a candidate for the natural kind, not a surface indicator. The graded hierarchy (§3.5) gives the "proximity" metric. The indicator-transfer worry also bears on §8.7 (artificial substrates).
- **Follow-up.** FMT already cites Bayne et al. 2024 and Butlin et al. 2026 (Peters is a co-author on both). Cite Peters' natural-kinds extension once it is published. Ask whether architecture-level kinds escape the validation-transfer problem that indicator-level kinds face.

**#2 Niccolo Negro (Tel Aviv University).** Showcase, Mon 10:00, Aud 1. *"Value Encroachment in Consciousness Science"*
- **Summary.** Non-epistemic values encroach on hypothesis testing and on theory choice. Which theory is "best" becomes relative to the purposes of different epistemic agents.
- **Score: 2.**
- **Relation to FMT.** Competes with FMT §7.1 (eight-requirement scoring matrix) and §7.4. If theory choice is purpose-relative, a single scoring matrix needs a stated purpose. This supports the guide's "two rubrics, not one" move.
- **Follow-up.** Add one sentence to §7.1 stating the matrix's purpose (explanatory scope for philosophical requirements) as a value choice. Cite Negro once a paper exists. Tel Aviv is Mudrik's institution, so they may be connected (unverified).

**#3 Joaquim Streicher (Université de Montréal).** Mon 11:00, Aud 2. *"How do we test for consciousness in humans? Mapping the conceptual landscape of consciousness assessment"*
- **Summary.** An LLM-supported systematic review of consciousness tests (C-tests) in disorders of consciousness: 6,938 studies screened, 865 selected, and 419 topics labelled with MOSAIC. Planned next: LLM parameter extraction, an open collaborative C-test database, and a causal graph network linking C-tests, system capacities and phenomenal consciousness.
- **Score: 2.**
- **Relation to FMT.** Offers formalisation and collaboration opportunities. It is a structured, graph-based database open to contributions. FMT §6.2 (anaesthesia and clinical disorders) and §5.3 (states read through the criticality signature) give theory-derived C-test rationales, such as the perturbational-complexity rationale in Casali 2013, which FMT cites as pre-2015 consistency.
- **Follow-up.** Contact Streicher to ask whether the database records a "theoretical rationale" field per test that theories can register against. Offer the FMT rationale for PCI and criticality-based tests. This is also the best in-range lead for the "LLM tool" interest.

**#4 Emanuela Yeung (University of Copenhagen; organiser).** Mon 11:20, Aud 2. *"Beyond pass/fail: Partial recognisers in mirror self-recognition"*
- **Summary.** Micro-coded gaze in 66 toddlers. A third category, "partial recognisers", shows intermediate self-directed attention after marking (12% → 31% → 44%). This supports a graded emergence of self-recognition.
- **Score: 2.**
- **Relation to FMT.** Supports FMT §3.5 (graduated levels; "the levels are not discrete stages but points along a continuum"). Mirror self-recognition plausibly indexes the step from basic to simply-extended consciousness, where the explicit self-model starts to include a model of the system's own states. Self-directed versus body-directed dwell is a behavioural read-out of ESM content over EWM content.
- **Follow-up.** Cite Yeung once published, as developmental evidence for gradedness, in §3.5 or §6.4. She is an organiser, so this is a natural conversation.

**#5 Stephanie Wai (York University).** Mon 11:40, Aud 2. *"How Not to Aggregate Dimensions of Consciousness Across Species"*
- **Summary.** Multidimensional frameworks (Birch et al. 2020; Dung & Newen) face an "Aggregation Problem". Summation, weighting and threshold methods, and Euclidean, Manhattan and cosine distances, produce incompatible species rankings. Underdetermination is relocated, not resolved.
- **Score: 2.**
- **Relation to FMT.** A potential contradiction to watch. FMT §6.4 cites Birch et al. 2020. More importantly, §7.1's scoring matrix is itself an aggregation across eight requirements, so Wai's argument applies to it directly. FMT's partial answer: its hierarchy is ordinal by recursion depth (§3.5), a single structural axis rather than an aggregated profile.
- **Follow-up.** Pre-empt the objection in §7.1: report the per-requirement profile, not only a total. Argue in §6.4 that recursion depth is a principled single axis. Citing Wai is optional.

**#6 Johannes Kleiner (University of Bamberg; organiser).** Mon 12:00, Aud 2. *"Towards Direct Tests for Consciousness in Non-Human Animals, Infants, and AI"*
- **Summary.** Current C-tests are indirect: they need a known relation between the measured property and consciousness. He presents a method for direct tests that rely on covariates of the test instead, based on mathematical facts like those in psychological evaluation research. Worked example: infant consciousness.
- **Score: 3.**
- **Relation to FMT.** A formalisation and collaboration opportunity. Kleiner is an organiser, from MCMP/AMCS, and FMT already cites Kleiner & Ludwig 2024 for its formalisation roadmap. A theory-independent test method sidesteps the theory-laden validation that FMT §8.7 (artificial substrates) depends on. It could test FMT's graded-levels claim in infants without presupposing FMT.
- **Follow-up.** Contact Kleiner and ask for the preprint. Ask whether the covariate method can target a *level* (graded) rather than presence/absence. Raise the formalisation roadmap (Gruber 2026b), since he co-authored the structure paper FMT cites. Also ask about any "definitions" or "standard model" survey he runs, which is not in this range.

**#14 Krisztina Jedlovszky (University College London).** Mon 12:00, Aud 4. *"Computational Natural Language Analysis Reveals Psilocybin-Induced Changes to Meaning-Making"* **[guide]**

**#15 Montijn van Loo (Tilburg University).** Mon 11:00, Aud 5. *"A Measure-Theoretic Framework for Theories of Consciousness: Event Spaces, Information Channels, and Bridge Laws"*
- **Summary.** Separates experiential and physical event spaces, psychophysical information channels (conditional probabilities) and metaphysical bridge laws, and argues that a bridge law's learnability scales with the verifiability of its events. IIT is reconstructed as a deterministic full channel. "Sampled Consciousness Theory" (PP + RPT) is given as a probabilistic example, giving a formal route to overflow. It is pitched as a common formal language for comparing theories.
- **Score: 3.**
- **Relation to FMT.** A formalisation opportunity, and new relative to the guide. FMT's real/virtual split (§3.3) maps cleanly onto it. The physical event space is the substrate; the experiential event space is the computational (virtual) level; and FMT's bridge law is not substrate→experience but substrate→second computational level→experience. FMT's level-error diagnosis of the hard problem (abstract, §3.4) becomes a precise claim: the bridge law targets the wrong event space when phenomenal properties are sought in substrate events. FMT Open Question 6 ("decoding the virtual side") is a learnability claim in van Loo's sense.
- **Follow-up.** Contact van Loo and offer FMT as a third worked example (an architectural theory with a two-stage channel). This could be a co-authored formal note, cheap to produce, and it advances Open Question 2 (formalisation).
