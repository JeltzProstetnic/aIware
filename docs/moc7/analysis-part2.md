### #31 — Tue 11:20–11:40, Auditorium 5 (session "Consciousness in Social, Moral and Developmental Context")
- **Speaker:** Ulysse Klatzmann, Université de Montréal
- **Title:** "Socially Embedded Consciousness: Models, Mechanisms, and Implications"
- **Summary:** Reviews GNW, AST, IIT, HOT, Predictive Processing and Active Inference for whether social interaction sits in their core machinery. Distinguishes social interaction as *content* of consciousness, as *causal context* modulating it, or as *constitutive* of some forms of experience.
- **Score:** 2
- **Relation:** competes / formalisation opportunity. FMT is absent from his comparison set, and his three-way distinction maps cleanly onto FMT: others are modelled inside the EWM (content); the ESM is shaped by social learning (context); and "erweitert" levels (modelling oneself as modelled by others) arguably make sociality constitutive at higher rungs.
- **FMT touchpoints:** §3.2 (four models), §3.5 (graduated levels / erweitert ladder), §7 (comparative analysis).
- **Follow-up:** Contact; send the FMT paper with a short note placing FMT in his content/context/constitutive grid. Consider adding his distinction to §7 or §9 if he publishes.

### #34 — Tue 13:30–14:00, Auditorium 1 (HCØ), Plenary / Showcase

- **Title:** "From self-evidencing to consciousness"
- **Summary:** Presents self-evidencing as the framing for predictive-processing accounts of self-organisation, cognition and perception, draws philosophy-of-cognitive-science implications, then positions self-evidencing for consciousness science.

- **FMT touchpoints:** §7.2 PP paragraph (the "PP is not a theory of consciousness" claim); §3.4.3 self-referential closure; §9 ESM/EWM dissociation prediction, which is set against PP.
- **Follow-up:** Find the talk's paper or preprint and check whether §7.2 still describes PP's scope accurately. Update the PP paragraph to cite Hohwy's self-evidencing route. A good name to send a short note to on the closure-versus-self-evidencing difference.

### #39 — Tue 16:30–16:50, Auditorium 3 (session "Developing Theories of Consciousness")

### #41 — Tue 17:10–17:30, Auditorium 3
- **Speaker:** Vikas O'Reilly-Shah, University of Washington / Seattle Children's Hospital
- **Title:** "Delay Coordinate Embedding and Structuralist Quality Spaces: A Candidate Computational Mechanism"
- **Summary:** Proposes delay coordinate embedding (Takens) in recurrent networks as a mechanism that produces quality-space structure from temporal dynamics, so quality-space structure belongs to dynamics rather than snapshots. Gives proved embedding results, including a prediction–separation link (low prediction error entails approximately injective representations). Maps the gaps onto metameric collapse, categorical perception and discrimination thresholds, which yields predicted failure modes. Positions itself against Fleming & Shea, Kleiner & Hoel, and others.
- **Score:** 3
- **Relation:** formalisation opportunity / supports. FMT §3.4.4 says qualia character comes from the architecture of the implicit models ("implicit model echoes"), and §8.6 (Prediction 5) says qualia *structure* is shareable while absolute encoding is not. DCE supplies a mathematical mechanism for how recurrent implicit models acquire relational, not absolute, geometry, which is exactly the shareable-structure half of Prediction 5. FMT §7.3 already engages Fleming & Shea's quality-space computations.
- **FMT touchpoints:** §3.4.4 (qualia as implicit-model echoes); §8.6 Prediction 5; §7.3 (structural turn, Ellia & Tsuchiya).
- **Follow-up:** Read the O'Reilly-Shah & Selvitella (2026) preprint. Cite in §8.6 as a candidate mechanism. Contact about whether DCE's predicted distortions could serve as an FMT Prediction-5 test. Strong formalisation partner for someone who works by intuition rather than formal notation.

### #45 — Tue 17:10–17:30, Auditorium 4
- **Speaker:** Kristian Sandberg, Aarhus University
- **Title:** "Measuring consciousness: What do large-scale data reveal about metacognitive measures?"
- **Summary:** Uses a harmonised dataset of about 750 participants, 1,500 hours, 15 paradigms and three countries. Reliability of metacognitive measures varies widely. Regression-based measures depend on accuracy, which a linear transform largely fixes. **M-ratio is stable on average but shows strong performance-dependent heteroscedasticity**, which normalisation improves.
- **Score:** 3

Sandberg's heteroscedasticity finding means the per-subject M-ratio estimates FMT relies on are noisier at some performance levels. A near-zero correlation could partly reflect measurement noise rather than architectural separability.
- **FMT touchpoints:** §9 open question (ESM/EWM orthogonal dissociation) and its M-ratio reanalysis; §3.1.2 empirical handles (self-referential closure row).
- **Follow-up:** **Get the paper and normalisation method; re-run the FMT Confidence-Database reanalysis with his normalised M-ratio** before anyone cites the r = −0.03 result. Contact: his harmonised dataset would be the strongest test of the d′/M-ratio decoupling claim. A collaboration opportunity.

### #54 — Wed 11:40–12:00, Auditorium 2
- **Speaker:** Wataru Kawakami, The University of Tokyo
- **Title:** "Guarded Reflection: How Retention Dissolves the Regress Problem of Self-Consciousness"
- **Summary:** Maps Henrich's regress dilemma onto unguarded self-referential equations, which have no unique solution, and Zahavi's retention-based answer onto guarded recursion (Löb induction, a later modality ▷, the topos of trees). Under retention ↦ ▷ and self ↦ unique fixed point, proves that the guarded case has a unique, constructible self. Machine-checked in Agda; handles discreteness through perceptual cycles.
- **Score:** 3 (**flag: category theory / formal self-reference**)
- **Relation:** a **formalisation opportunity of the first order**. FMT's self-referential closure (§3.4.3) is exactly the regress-prone structure, and FMT's temporal echo mechanism (§3.4.4: discrete frames, each feeding the next, recursion smearing across frames) is in effect a *guarded* recursion with a one-frame delay. Kawakami's theorem would give FMT's closure claim an existence-and-uniqueness backbone: the self-model as the unique fixed point of a delayed self-modelling operator. That answers the objection that closure is circular. His "perceptual cycles" defence lines up with FMT's discrete computational frames.
- **FMT touchpoints:** §3.4.3 self-referential closure; §3.4.4 temporal echo / computational "now"; §3.5 erweitert ladder (iterated ▷ may index recursion depth); formal programme `paper/fmt_formal/`.
- **Follow-up:** **Contact.** Propose mapping FMT closure plus frame delay onto guarded recursion; ask for the Agda development. Cite in §3.4.3 and §3.4.4. Feed into the FMT formalisation roadmap. The single best formal collaborator in this range.
