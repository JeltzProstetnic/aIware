### #91 — Tonglin Yan (Paris-Saclay University) · Thu 15 Oct 17:10–17:30 · Auditorium 3 · Talk — **score 3**
**"Formalizing Consciousness: A Comparative Mathematical Analysis of Leading Theories"** (session: Comparing Theories of Consciousness)
Restates IIT, Active Inference, HOR/HOSS and the Projective Consciousness Model (PCM) in one probabilistic and geometric idiom (stochastic kernels, divergences, variational free energy, group-structured state spaces), sorted by Marr's three levels. Argues that many apparent conflicts disappear once the explanatory level is aligned (IIT = integrative property, AIF/HOR = access mechanisms, PCM = perspectival structure). Proposes a hierarchical POMDP testbed in which integration, higher-order access and perspectival control can be tested inside one model.
- **Relation:** formalisation opportunity, and also competition. It is the formal-comparison exercise FMT §7 does informally (scoring matrix §7.1, theory-by-theory §7.2). FMT is missing from it.
- **FMT touchpoints:** §7.1–7.2; §3.4.3 self-referential closure (the "self-model and world-model each enter the other's update rule" in the abstract maps naturally onto a coupled-POMDP formulation); §8.9 in-silico programme; Open Question 2 (quantitative operationalisation).
- **Follow-up:** contact Yan and ask whether the hierarchical POMDP testbed could take a fifth entry, FMT's four model kinds with ESM↔EWM mutual conditioning. Even a one-page FMT translation into that idiom (implicit = learned kernels on the substrate; explicit = transient posterior simulation; closure = ESM in the EWM update and vice versa) would answer Open Question 2 and give FMT a seat in comparative formal work. Cite in §7.3 once a paper or preprint exists.

### #92 — Krzysztof Dolega (Ruhr-Universität Bochum) · Thu 15 Oct 17:30–17:50 · Auditorium 3 · Talk — **score 3**
**"We Don't Need Another Model: Towards a Meta-Theoretic Framework for Comparing Theories of Consciousness"**
Theory proliferation (20 per Seth & Bayne 2022 to 200 per Kuhn 2024) with no standard for adjudication; adversarial collaborations are piecemeal (Melloni 2023; Lau 2023; Mudrik et al. 2025); method choice predicts which theory is being tested (Yaron 2022). Proposes a virtue-based meta-framework (predictive accuracy, simplicity, explanatory scope, …), formalised, weighted and traded off transparently, and argues for it over the Measure Centrality Index (Chis-Cuire et al. 2024) and Wiese's Minimal Unifying Model.
- **Relation:** competes rhetorically (the title argues against yet another theory such as FMT). The method is a collaboration opportunity, because FMT §2 (eight requirements) plus the §7.1 scoring matrix is already a virtue-weighted comparison instrument.
- **FMT touchpoints:** §1.2 "What would count as progress?"; §2 (eight requirements); §7.1 scoring matrix; §6.0 (consequences, not principles), which bears on simplicity and scope.
- **Follow-up:** contact Dolega and offer the §2/§7.1 matrix as a worked instance for his blueprint, including a candid account of where FMT scores low (untested predictions). Cite him in §1.2/§7.1 as the methodological frame. Answer the title's objection in one line: FMT also claims to *derive* PP-, GNW- and HOT-like features as consequences of one architecture, which reduces the theory count instead of adding to it.

### #89 — Sucharit Katyal (University of Copenhagen) · Thu 15 Oct 16:30–16:50 · Auditorium 3 · Talk — **score 2**
**"What Is Consciousness? And What Does a Theory of Consciousness Need to Explain?"**
Argues that disagreement over the explanandum comes from structuralism being restricted to qualia/content spaces. Proposes a structural model of the whole conscious episode with three components, *mental object*, *doer-I* and *existential-I* (Husserl plus P. R. Sarkar), and treats consciousness as a "phenomenal resource" distributed across them in varying degrees (perception, agency, dream, meditation, ego dissolution, nondual awareness, minimal experience). Offers this as a theory-neutral explanandum.
- **Relation:** a definitions-of-consciousness talk. It **supports** FMT's resource framing ("redirection of the modeling resource" in the abstract; §6.1 psychedelics, §6.3 dreams). It also offers an explanandum FMT can map onto: mental object ≈ EWM content, doer-I ≈ ESM in its agentive causal role (§4.2.3), existential-I ≈ the minimal self-model presence left when ESM content thins (ego dissolution).
- **FMT touchpoints:** §3.1 core definition / §3.1.1 operational definitions; §3.5 graduated levels; §6.1; Prediction 2 (§8.3, ego-dissolution content is controllable).
- **Follow-up:** cite as a convergent explanandum. Contact him (Copenhagen, local host institution) with the mapping above and ask whether his "distribution of resource" is meant to be measurable. FMT's redirection-of-modelling-resource claim gives a mechanistic answer to that question.

### #70 — Evan Thompson (University of British Columbia), Mind-Matter Prize · Wed 14 Oct 18:00–19:00 · Louisiana Museum · Keynote — **score 2**
**"Consciousness in Life"**
Organisms are the paradigm conscious beings, yet consciousness science is formulated without a theory of the organism. Sketches an "organism first" approach and draws out what it implies for consciousness and the brain, and for which systems are or could be conscious.
- **Relation:** **contradicts / competes** on substrate. If "organism first" becomes a necessity claim (life-mind continuity, autopoiesis), it runs into FMT §4.4 substrate independence and §8.7 artificial consciousness. It is in the same family as the Seth "beast machine" and Milinkovic–Aru biological computationalism positions FMT already engages.
- **FMT touchpoints:** §4.4 (the Seth and biological-computationalism paragraphs); §6.4 animal consciousness; §8.7–8.8.
- **Follow-up:** add Thompson (2007 *Mind in Life* plus whatever this keynote becomes) to §4.4 next to Seth. The rebuttal line: FMT accepts that organisms are where the architecture *evolved* and that interoceptive ISM content is central, but treats the organism as one realization, not a requirement. The olfaction and multiple-realizability arguments in §4.4 carry this. No contact; a high-profile keynote, so engage in print.

### #86 — Hanqi Wang (Carnegie Mellon University) · Thu 15 Oct 16:50–17:10 · Auditorium 2 · Talk — **score 2**
**"Higher-Order Representation as Cross-Timescale Integration in Time-Consciousness"**
Uses Singhal & Srinivasan's three timescale levels (30–50 ms cinematic, 300–500 ms extensional, 3–5 s retentional) and asks why level-2 content is conscious while level-1 content is not. Answer: Brown's HOROR. Level 2 integrates several level-1 slices, and that integration *is* the higher-order representation, supplying minimal self-attribution without a conceptual judgement. Predicts dissociations under CFS and the attentional blink.
- **Relation:** **supports / competes**. Very close to FMT §3.4.4 (temporal echo): recursion smears frames into a constructed "now" and supplies a perspective. Wang reaches a similar structure from HOT. FMT's distinguishing claim is that the integration must be *modelled as the recent past of a perspective* (self-model closure), not merely integrated.
- **FMT touchpoints:** §3.4.4; §3.6 implicit–explicit boundary; §7.2 (HOT comparison).
- **Follow-up:** cite in §3.4.4 as a convergent HOT-side mechanism. Contact him to compare predictions: FMT predicts that the level-2 window should depend on self-model integrity (e.g. it should shrink or disintegrate under depersonalisation or ego dissolution), which HOROR alone does not predict. That is a candidate joint prediction.

### #75 — Christoffer Lundbak Olesen (Aarhus University) · Thu 15 Oct 11:20–11:40 · Auditorium 3 · Talk — **score 2**
**"Towards Mapping the Space of Experience: Diversity as a Constraint on Theories of Consciousness"**
Individual differences (aphantasia to vivid imagery, inner voice or none, body and time experience) are latent and large. Each theorist's familiarity with their *own* consciousness biases their theory. Mapping the diversity of experience should constrain the space of admissible theories.
- **Relation:** a definitions/explanandum talk that **supports** FMT. Graded and variable explicit models are native to FMT (EWM/ESM content depends on the implicit models feeding them; §3.4.4 "qualia as implicit model echoes"). It also bears on Prediction 5 (§8.6, structure shareable, absolute encoding not).
- **FMT touchpoints:** §2.4 structure of experience; §3.5 graded levels; §8.6.
- **Follow-up:** cite in §2.4 or §8.6. FMT can say *which* diversity it predicts: variation in what the implicit models deliver into the explicit layer (aphantasia = weak implicit→EWM imagery channel; §3.6.1 channel occupancy). Low-effort contact.

### #76 — Wanja Wiese (University of Bonn) · Thu 15 Oct 11:40–12:00 · Auditorium 3 · Talk — **score 2**

- **FMT touchpoints:** §3.4.1–3.4.2; §8.6.

### #82 — William Seager (University of Toronto) · Thu 15 Oct 11:40–12:00 · Auditorium 5 · Talk — **score 2**
**"Is the Metaphysics of Predictive Processing Self-Refuting?"**
In PP, experience resides in the predictive model ("controlled hallucination"). But the brain is known only through perception and is therefore part of the hallucination. So "an hallucinated brain cannot explain the hallucination of a brain". Assesses what is right and wrong in that worry.
- **Relation:** **supports FMT by contrast**. FMT meets exactly this objection with its two-level ontology (§3.4.6): the brain-as-experienced is EWM content (virtual), while the brain-as-substrate is real, and the theory is stated at the substrate level about the virtual one. FMT's real/virtual split (§3.3, the bubble diagram) is a direct answer to Seager's paradox. Applies to PP in the §7.2 comparison.
- **FMT touchpoints:** §3.3; §3.4.6; §4.1 process physicalism; §7.2 (PP).
- **Follow-up:** cite in §3.4.6 or §7.2 and state the answer in one paragraph: the regress stops because the explicit model of the brain is not the thing doing the modelling. Possible short contact or commentary.

### #67 — Robert Prentner (ShanghaiTech University) · Wed 14 Oct 12:00–12:20 · Auditorium 5 · Talk — **score 2**
**"Unifying the Science of Consciousness: Mathematized Phenomenology and Trace Logic"**
An experience-first, non-materialist framework: phenomenologies as Markovian networks of transitions between experiential states, partitioned into accessible and inaccessible subsets ("trace chains"), from which objective processes emerge as representations. Proposed as a common formal language that reconciles IIT, PP and GNW.
- **Relation:** **competes** (a rival unifier from the idealist side, Hoffman's school) and offers a **formalisation idea**. The accessible/inaccessible partition maps onto FMT's explicit/implicit split (§3.6), but with the ontology inverted.
- **FMT touchpoints:** §3.6; §4.1 (process physicalism versus idealism); §7.3 emerging frameworks.
- **Follow-up:** mention in §7.3 as an idealist unifier. The Markov-chain-with-trace formalism could be borrowed for the implicit→explicit channel (§3.6.1). No priority contact.

### #60 — Maria Isabel Inzunza (KU Leuven) · Wed 14 Oct 12:20–12:40 · Auditorium 3 · Talk — **score 2**
**"When AI Explains Itself, Should We Believe It?: Toward a Formal Theory of Genuine and Simulated Explanation"**
Category-theoretic framework: an explanation is adequate when it is a *faithful functor* on a horizon-relative subcategory of model transformations, preserving the relational invariants the task needs. Identifies three failure modes, of which "simulation failure" is the most consequential. Motivated by the impasse in ascribing phenomenal properties to AI from behaviour alone.
- **Relation:** a category-theory talk and a **formalisation opportunity**. FMT's self-model is itself an "explanation" the system gives of itself, and FMT holds that the ESM is functionally adequate but not substrate-faithful (§4.2.3 two causal roles; confabulation, the meta-problem §3.8). "Functor faithfulness on a task-relative subcategory" is a candidate formal statement of "the self-model is useful but not true". It also bears on §8.8 (no behavioural test is conclusive).
- **FMT touchpoints:** §3.8 meta-problem; §4.2.3; §8.6 (structure-preserving maps, relational invariants); §8.8.
- **Follow-up:** contact. Her functor-faithfulness criterion could formalise Prediction 5 (structure preserved = a faithful functor between qualia categories; absolute encoding not preserved) and the ESM's partial faithfulness. A good collaboration fit for the formal-roadmap work (`paper/fmt_formal/`).
