**Verdict:** MG's memory most likely merges #110 (ETH, "theories as computational models") with #120

**What Waade's programme needs as input:** a theory stated as a *joint model* across three domains —
(a) experiential variables fitted to subjective-report data, (b) cognitive-process variables fitted to
behaviour, (c) physiological variables fitted to neuroimaging — plus the *bridge relations* that say which
neural/cognitive states are necessary for experience to be present.

**How FMT could be supplied (concrete deliverable — "FMT model card"):**
1. **State variables:** IWM, EWM, ISM, ESM (four model *kinds*); permeability of the implicit→explicit
   channel (§3.6, §3.6.1); self-referential closure flag (§3.4.3); free-modeling regime / Class-4 dynamics
   with criticality as a *contingent signature*, never a requirement (§3.7, S329 framing).
2. **Bridge law (experiential domain):** experience is present when implicit content is integrated into
   the explicit system and fed to a closed self-model that conflates itself with the system it occupies
   (project-reference "constitutive" framing); level of consciousness graded per §3.5.
3. **Cognitive domain:** ESM-mediated metacognitive report; redirectable ESM; dual evaluation (§4.2.1).
4. **Neural domain:** empirical handles from §3.1.2; criticality measures as a signature readout (§5.3).
5. **Manipulations → predicted trajectories:** psychedelics = global permeability↑ (§6.1); anosognosia =
   local permeability↓ (P1); ego dissolution content tracks dominant input (P2); DID alters (P3); lucid
   onset (P4); shared qualia structure vs. non-shared absolute encoding (P5).
6. Source material already exists: §3.1.1 operational definitions + §8.9 in-silico research programme.

**Follow-up:** email Waade (via ETH Zürich) offering FMT as a worked test case for joint modelling, with
the model card above as a 2-page attachment; ask what format their tool expects. High priority.

## 3. Detailed entries (score ≥ 2)

### #91 — Thu 17:10–17:30, Auditorium 3 — Tonglin Yan (Paris-Saclay University) — score 3
**"Formalizing Consciousness: A Comparative Mathematical Analysis of Leading Theories"**
Re-expresses IIT, Active Inference, Higher-Order Representation (HOR/HOSS) and the Projective
Consciousness Model in one probabilistic/geometric idiom (stochastic kernels, divergences, variational
free energy, group-structured state spaces), organised by Marr's three levels. Argues many conflicts
dissolve once explanatory levels are aligned (IIT = integrative property; AIF/HOR = access mechanisms;
PCM = perspectival structure), proposes a hierarchical POMDP as a shared computational testbed.
- **Relation:** formalisation opportunity + competes (FMT is not in the set). FMT's level distinction
  (§3.4.1, real/virtual) is exactly the "explanatory level" move Yan uses to dissolve conflicts.
- **Touches:** §7 comparative analysis, §8.9 in-silico programme.
- **Follow-up:** contact Yan; propose rendering FMT's four-model kinds + closure in the same kernel/POMDP
  idiom as a fifth theory. Yan also has poster P29 (Tue) — likely the same work.

### #92 — Thu 17:30–17:50, Auditorium 3 — Krzysztof Dolega (Ruhr-Universität Bochum) — score 2
**"We Don't Need Another Model: Towards a Meta-Theoretic Framework for Comparing Theories of Consciousness"**
Theory proliferation (20–200 theories) with no adjudication standard; proposes a virtue-based
meta-framework (predictive accuracy, simplicity, explanatory scope, …), formalised and weighted
transparently; argues against the Measure Centrality Index and Wiese's Minimal Unifying Model.
- **Relation:** competes/opportunity. The title is a direct jab at new theories like FMT; FMT's own
  scoring matrix (§7.1) is a home-made version of what Dolega wants standardised.
- **Follow-up:** cite in §7.1 as the methodological frame; offer FMT's scoring matrix as an input case;
  pre-empt the "another model" objection by framing FMT as a unification (MDM + SMT) not a 201st theory.

### #93 — Thu 16:30–16:50, Auditorium 4 — Jaya Caporusso (Jožef Stefan Institute) — score 2
**"Dissolution Experiences and the Experience of Self and World: An Empirical Phenomenological Investigation"**
29 phenomenological interviews (8 participants), 10 dissolution experiences vs 8 daily-life ones. In
dissolution the self loses coherence and most elements vanish — often only a *sense of mineness*
remains — while the world becomes open, unified, vivid; letting go of control and trust are central.
- **Relation:** supports. A residual "mineness" with a dissolved self-structure fits FMT's account of
  ego dissolution as a redirected/weakened ESM rather than absence of consciousness (§6.1, P2).
- **Follow-up:** cite in §6.1; ask Caporusso whether reported post-dissolution "unity" content tracks the
  dominant sensory input of the setting (nature/art/intimacy) — that is Prediction 2's discriminating test.

### #96 — Thu 17:30–17:50, Auditorium 4 — Andres Gomez Emilsson (Qualia Research Institute) — score 2
**"Field Lines Before Equations: Parameterized Dynamic Phenomenology and the HEART Retreat"**
PDP: represent a stimulus as data, apply a parameterised operation, render back, let participants tune
parameters to match their own state — parameters as stronger evidence than verbal report. Proposes
characterising states by oscillator coupling kernels; jhana vs 5-MeO-DMT comparison under way.
- **Relation:** formalisation opportunity. Parameter-matching is a route to P5 (shared qualia structure)
  and to quantifying FMT's permeability parameter in altered states.
- **Follow-up:** low-cost contact; suggest a permeability-style parameter in their replication tools.

### #97 — Thu 16:30–16:50, Auditorium 5 — Tobias Schlicht (Ruhr-University Bochum) — score 2
**"Modeling the Ghost in the Machine"**
Phenomenal consciousness (Block/Chalmers senses) is unfit as a modelling target in AI: markers are
absent, ambiguous or question-begging (computations); recommends illusionism for AI-consciousness work.
- **Relation:** partly supports, partly contradicts. FMT agrees the "intrinsic" phenomenal notion is
  unworkable but does not end in illusionism: qualia are real at the virtual level (§3.4.5, §3.4.6).
  FMT's claim that architecture-based markers are not question-begging (closure + four kinds) answers
  his "computations are question-begging" charge.
- **Follow-up:** rebut in §3.4.5 / §8.7 — one paragraph on why an architectural, level-relative account
  escapes Schlicht's trilemma.

### #98 — Thu 16:50–17:10, Auditorium 5 — Angela Johnson (Northeastern University) — score 3
**"From Indicators to Dynamics: Metastability, Recurrence, and Ignition in a Persistent Agent's Attention Stream"**
AURORA framework: treats a persistent-memory LLM agent's turn-by-turn attention selection as a state
stream and computes metastability (Kuramoto order variance), RQA, a perturbational "fracturability"
index (behavioural PCI analogue, Lempel-Ziv) and an ignition criterion; scoped to cognitive dynamics,
not phenomenality. Healthy vs pathological-collapse trajectories.
- **Relation:** formalisation + collaboration opportunity. This is a ready-made instrument for FMT's
  in-silico programme (§8.9) and for criticality-as-signature readouts (§3.7, §5.3) on artificial agents —
  and close to MG's own crucible/mirror-box work.
- **Follow-up:** contact Johnson; propose running AURORA metrics on an agent with an explicit self-model
  vs. ablated self-model (FMT-discriminating comparison).

### #100 — Thu 17:30–17:50, Auditorium 5 — Dezhi Luo (University College London) — score 2
**"(How) can we benchmark consciousness in foundation models?"**
Benchmarks should be theory-driven and antirealist (pragmatic, not truth-conferring); criticises
marker-based approaches lacking mechanism; recommends multimodal models, mechanistic interpretability +
behavioural paradigms, scaling analysis and causal ablation.
- **Relation:** opportunity. FMT yields theory-driven, mechanistic criteria (self-model closure,
  implicit→explicit integration) testable by causal ablation — exactly his recipe.
- **Follow-up:** contact; offer FMT-derived benchmark items (§8.7, §8.9).

### #101 — Fri 9:00–10:00, Auditorium 1 (HCØ) Keynote — Guillaume Dumas (Université de Montréal) — score 2
**"Formalizing Generative Neurophenomenology: From Coupled Brains to Metacognitive Agents"**
Extends Varela's neurophenomenology to reciprocal constraints among experiential, biological and
interpersonal dynamics; multi-brain recordings, dynamical/biophysical models, information integration
across agents, quantum computing for forecasting; artificial agents to manipulate attunement;
metacognitive self-monitoring as a candidate consciousness marker.
- **Relation:** partial support (self-monitoring as marker ≈ ESM operation) + competes (scale-spanning,
  interpersonal framing absent from FMT).
- **Follow-up:** cite as a formalisation peer; note that FMT's ESM gives "self-monitoring" a specific
  architecture.

### #102 — Fri 10:00–10:30, Auditorium 1 (HCØ) Showcase — Medha Shekhar (Université libre de Bruxelles) — score 2
**"Conscious awareness is necessarily valenced: evidence from performance-visibility dissociations"**
Two face-discrimination experiments: visibility and valence ratings co-vary and dissociate from
performance (both prioritise the affective feature; high staircase contrast → worst performance but
highest visibility/pleasantness). Concludes awareness and valence are intrinsically linked, probably via
a metacognitive/higher-order process.
- **Relation:** supports. Performance–awareness dissociation = implicit vs explicit processing (§3.6);
  automatic valence assignment fits the dual evaluation architecture (§4.2.1).
- **Follow-up:** cite in §3.6 / §4.2.1 as empirical support.

### #104 — Fri 11:20–11:40, Auditorium 2 — Chris Percy (Qualia Research Institute) — score 2
**"Topological segmentation: a mathematical toolkit for solutions to combination and boundary problems"**
Five-step method for modelling physical fields so they segment into separate subjects (topological
segmentation), applicable to panpsychist and non-panpsychist theories; critical review of quantum-holism.
- **Relation:** competes on FMT's own requirements list (§2.3 boundary, §2.6 combination). FMT answers
  boundary by self-model closure, not field topology.
- **Follow-up:** cite in §2.3; state FMT's boundary criterion in a form his framework can test.

### #105 — Fri 11:40–12:00, Auditorium 2 — Davide Aldé (Monash University) — score 3
**"Logical Q-space and the nature of mental qualities"**
Defines a logical Q-space (all possible mental qualities + phenomenal relations); experience is fully
captured by structure iff the space is asymmetric. Even if intrinsic character existed, introspection
could not detect it (symmetric qualities are indistinguishable), so we have no reason to posit it.
- **Relation:** strong support for virtual qualia (§3.4) and Prediction 5 (structure shareable, absolute
  encoding not) — Aldé gives a formal argument for the same structural/intrinsic split.
- **Follow-up:** cite in §3.4 and §8.6; contact Aldé (also poster P5, Tue) — P5 could be phrased in
  his Q-space vocabulary.

### #108 — Fri 11:20–11:40, Auditorium 3 — Romy Beauté (University of Sussex / UCL) — score 2
**"A practical guide to computational identification of experiential themes from phenomenological reports: A Wide-Angle approach for Consciousness Science"**
MOSAICapp: browser tool for the MOSAIC pipeline (sentence embeddings, density clustering, topic labels)
to find experiential themes bottom-up in large first-person corpora; workflow choices explained.
- **Relation:** tool opportunity for Prediction 2 — mine existing psychedelic/ego-dissolution report

### #110 — Fri 12:00–12:20, Auditorium 3 — Peter Thestrup Waade (ETH Zürich) — score 3
**"Towards joint computational modelling of experiential, cognitive and neural dynamics"**
Computational models in mind science live in two domains (neural, cognitive) and recently a third
(experience, fitted to reports); theories about how domains relate (which states are necessary for
experience) need *joint* models spanning all three; proposes extending joint cognitive–neural modelling
to phenomenology.
- **Relation:** collaboration + formalisation opportunity — see §1 above for the FMT model card.
- **Follow-up:** top priority contact.

### #111 — Fri 11:00–11:20, Auditorium 4 — Fernando Rodriguez (University of Sussex) — score 2
**"Is Cognition more real than Computation?"**
The mind–computer problem: anti-computationalist positions fall back into mechanistic computationalism;
two exits — non-computability, or computation as a natural phenomenon.
- **Relation:** supports FMT's second exit (process physicalism + Class-4 computation, §4.1, §4.4).
- **Follow-up:** optional cite in §4.4.

### #112 — Fri 11:20–11:40, Auditorium 4 — Anna Frammartino Wilks (Acadia University) — score 2
**"The "Natural" Path to Artificial Consciousness"**
Against Seth (2026)-style biological scepticism: on a gradualist enactivist view, "sense-making" is not
biology-exclusive; artificial consciousness achievable incrementally in appropriately embodied systems.
- **Relation:** supports gradualism (§3.5) and substrate independence (§4.4); competes on mechanism
  (sense-making vs self-model architecture).
- **Follow-up:** cite in §8.7/§8.8 as an ally against biological naturalism.

### #115 — Fri 11:00–11:20, Auditorium 5 — Antonia Antonova (California Institute for Machine Consciousness) — score 3
**"Self as Flow: Reification as a Control Parameter in Self-Modeling Systems"**
Selfhood is a mode of organisation of a self-world model, not a separate object; "reification" = precision
on self-referential vs world-referential states; high = sharp self/world partition, stable agency,
rigidity; low = fluid/flow/self-transcendent states. Predicts non-monotonic self-model-strength vs
performance and that agents modulating reification beat any fixed level.
- **Relation:** closest conceptual neighbour to FMT in this range. Her self/world partition = FMT's
  ESM/EWM split; reification ≈ FMT's permeability / ESM dominance; flow and ego dissolution map to §6.1.
  Competes (single self-world model vs four kinds) and offers testable AI predictions FMT can share.
- **Follow-up:** contact Antonova; compare reification with FMT's permeability parameter; propose a joint
  in-silico test (§8.9). Cite in §3.2/§3.6.

### #117 — Fri 11:40–12:00, Auditorium 5 — Michael Hegarty (University of Massachusetts Boston) — score 2
**"On the Natural Origins and Function of Self-Consciousness: Lessons from Studies of Animal Tool Use"**
Innovative bird tool construction is best explained by self-representational states that track the body
as locus of perception/action and bind present-moment states with merely possible ones; a naturalised
reading of pre-reflective self-consciousness.
- **Relation:** supports FMT's claim that consciousness requires a self-model (§3.1) and the animal
  account (§6.4); the "combine actual and possible" function matches FMT's simulation framing.
- **Follow-up:** cite in §6.4.

### P1 — Tue 15:00–16:30 poster — Paweł Sochacki (VIZJA University, Warsaw) — score 2
**"Formal Subjectivity and Locus: Distinguishing Givenness, Selfhood, and Experiential Content"**
Proposes "Locus" as the formal level of first-person givenness, explicitly *not* a self-model; separates
givenness, contents, the Self, and mechanisms; PP/active inference model content regulation, not
givenness itself; clinical symptoms as reorganisations of experience.
- **Relation:** contradicts. FMT says givenness *is* the self-model's conflation with the system it
  occupies (constitutive framing) — no extra "Locus" layer needed.
- **Follow-up:** rebuttal note for §3.3/§3.4.3 (the "who is it given to" objection).
