# `AIW-263` — where MG's second half of free will came from, what it claims, and the paragraph proposed for §4.2.2

Written S320 2026-09-23 on MG's *"263 explain the second half better, it is confusing, but i think it looks
like we need to add some info to 4.2.2 but i dont know what you would put in and where exactly it came from."*

## 1. Where it came from — and why it does not feel like it came from anywhere

It was **not** said in a theory discussion. It was a side remark inside an **experimental design ruling in
crucible**, on 2026-09-18, and it reached aIware as a peer note rather than as a task.

Crucible was settling `D3`: whether its *"having a will"* phenomenality marker should be *the creature
reporting options as standing open to it*. MG agreed — that reading is §4.2.3 `:586`, *"the option to stop and
dream … stands open in the simulation whether or not it is taken, and its standing availability is what the
freedom feels like"* — and then added a definition on top of the agreement. Verbatim, from
`~/crucible/docs/decisions.md:469`:

> *"d3 agree, free will is two things: 1 an illusion of having the initiative together with that illusion
> carving neuronal paths that lead to adaption towards more cognitive zufriedenheiut and abstract success."*

Crucible recorded it as binding for its own marker, flagged that *"Part (ii) goes beyond what §4.2.2 states in
words"*, and filed the peer note here. **So: a ruling about an instrument, carrying a theory claim the
instrument now depends on.** That is the whole provenance — there is no earlier aIware thread behind it.

## 2. Why the second half reads as confusing, and the resolution

On its face *"the illusion carves neuronal paths"* looks circular. The illusion is **produced by** the
substrate; saying it then **shapes** the substrate sounds like the output steering its own cause.

It is not circular, and the paper already contains both of the pieces that dissolve it — in different sections,
never joined:

| the paper already says | where |
|---|---|
| the explicit models' traffic reshapes the implicit ones — *"every episode of explicit evaluation — including bare conceptual exposure — leaves the substrate marginally different"* | §4.2.3, **maturing** |
| felt authorship is a **retrospective construction** — *"the felt authorship of retreating into a daydream is the same retrospective construction as the felt authorship of raising an arm"* | §4.2.3 `:586` |

⇒ The paper has authorship as an **output**, and explicit activity as an **input** to plasticity. **MG's part
(ii) says the authorship is also an input** — that the self-assignment *"I did this"* is part of what decides
**which** episodes leave a mark and **how deep** a one.

That is a claim about **selection for consolidation**, not about control. The substrate produces the tag; the
tag then weights what gets written back. It is the loop FMT already names in pattern **#14** (closure is the
consolidation channel) and pattern **#35**'s amplification loop (an informative value earns more grip over the
simulation) — applied to one specific tag rather than to qualia in general.

## 3. It is two claims, not one, and they are not equally safe

**(ii-a) The attribution weights consolidation.** Content assigned to the self is written back harder than the
same content merely observed. This is the half that joins cleanly to §4.2.3 and has a real empirical neighbour.

**(ii-b) The carving has a direction** — *"toward more cognitive Zufriedenheit and abstract success."*
§4.2.3's maturing is **undirected**: the substrate is shaped by what it is repeatedly used for, full stop. MG
adds a target. That is a separate and considerably stronger claim, and **nothing in the repo supports it**.

## 4. What §4.2.2 is actually missing

§4.2.2 establishes the illusion as **pure phenomenology** — the apparatus is unrepresentable, the decision is
assigned to the self, *"felt freedom is what an origin feels like from inside"* — and then never says the
illusion **does** anything. §4.2.3 assigns causal roles to the self-**simulation**, never to the authorship
tag. So there is a hole exactly where (ii) sits: **nowhere does the paper let the felt authorship do work.**

## 5. ⛔ SUPERSEDED — the first proposed paragraph, kept only so it is recognisable

**Do not draft from this section. The literature searches of 2026-09-23 replaced it; see §9.** It is retained
because it carries the placement argument, which still holds.

### 5a. The original text (superseded)

**Placement:** §4.2.2, as the closing paragraph of the *"unrepresentable apparatus"* block — after *"…never as
what produces the outcome of the weighing… volition is the case in which the omission becomes phenomenally
consequential rather than merely epistemically inconvenient."*, and **before** *"Two descriptions of the same
event follow."* That is the point at which the illusion has just been established and the inside/outside levels
have not yet been drawn, so the function can be stated without disturbing the scope guard that follows.

**Text, carrying (ii-a) only:**

> The attribution is not inert. Assigning an episode to the self is part of what determines how heavily it is
> written back: the explicit models reshape the implicit ones in proportion to what the explicit models take up
> (Section 4.2.3), and a deliberation undergone as something done is taken up more than the same deliberation
> observed as something happening. The illusion is therefore not only felt — it weights which contents of the
> simulation leave a residue. This returns no control to the self-model. What is assigned, and how heavily, is
> itself substrate-determined; the assignment is a step in the loop rather than a report on it.

## 6. ⚠ What must be checked before that paragraph ships — three debts, and the third is the familiar one

1. **The empirical leg is the self-reference effect** — self-referentially encoded material being better
   retained (Rogers, Kuiper & Kirker 1977; the meta-analysis usually cited is Symons & Johnson 1997).
   ⛔ **Unverified against primaries, and there is a real gap even if it verifies:** that literature is about
   **memory encoding in an experiment**, and the claim here is about **substrate plasticity over months**.
   Either the paragraph is scoped to what the literature supports, or the bridge is argued. Do not cite either
   paper from this file.
2. **(ii-b), the direction, has no support and should not enter the paper unflagged.** Its options are the ones
   §3.4.3 already models: state it in our own voice as **asserted, not derived** — the register the Bach reply
   used (*"the step from closure to phenomenality is asserted in my framework and not derived, and I would
   rather flag that myself than have you find it"*) — or keep it out of the paper and in the registry and the
   book. **My recommendation: keep it out for now.** It is the most contestable sentence in the definition and
   it is the one a reviewer reaches for first.
3. **Positioning risk, same shape as the one that killed pattern #35's proposed differentiator.** *Sense of
   agency modulating learning* is adjacent to a live literature — agency and outcome evaluation, and the
   free-choice / choice-induced-preference paradigm. Pattern #35 proposed a differentiator that the RL
   literature turned out to have already claimed (Dayan & Berridge 2014), verified at S300 and withdrawn.
   ⇒ **Check occupancy before drafting the final wording, not after.**

## 7. The consequence of doing nothing, which is what makes this a decision rather than a nicety

Crucible's *"having a will"* marker requires **both** halves — self-attribution of initiative **and** that
attribution feeding the learning signal. A §4.2.2 that states neither leaves the paper and the experiment built
to test it disagreeing about what is being tested. **Taking (ii-a) and leaving (ii-b) narrows that gap without
closing it**, and MG should know that is the trade rather than discover it later: the direction claim would
remain crucible-side only.

   asserted-not-derived, or stay out of the paper?
3. If (ii-b) stays out, is crucible's two-halved marker left as it is, or told that the paper carries one half?

# 9. ⭐ THE REVISED PROPOSAL — after the three literature searches

Record: `docs/aiw263-iib-literature-2026-09-23.md`. **What changed, in one line: neither half was safe as
written, and the searches handed back a better claim than either.**

## 9.1 What the searches settled

| | status after search |
|---|---|
| **(ii-a)** attribution weights consolidation | ⛔ **occupied** — Cockburn/Collins/Frank 2014, Luettgau 2020, Murty 2019, Chambon 2020, Ren 2026, Zhu 2026, Karsh & Eitam. Also faces a near-disconfirmation (Katzman & Hartley 2020), a null (Tsuji & Imaizumi 2022) and a salience reattribution (Hon & Teo 2024) |
| **(ii-b)** the direction | ⛔ **occupied** — Schmidhuber *defines* intrinsic reward as model improvement and calls it "fun" (1990–2010); plus Oudeyer/Kaplan, active inference, Hesp 2021, Murayama 2022 |
| **"abstract success" as a target** | ⛔ **contradicted.** Charpentier et al. 2018: the reward circuit values knowledge **only when the news is expected to be good**; people pay for ignorance. The circuit follows *Zufriedenheit*, not accuracy |
| **plasticity is gated, not coincidence-driven** | ✅ **established** — Pawlak & Kerr 2008 (necessity), Hong 2022 (in vivo), and the third factor is not reward-specific (Takeuchi 2016, Sharpe 2017) |
| **the gate responds to epistemic goods in humans** | ✅ **supported** — Guggenmos 2016 is the on-point case: learning with no external feedback, driven by a mesolimbic **confidence prediction error** whose size predicts individual learning success |
| **seconds → years** | ⛔ **unbridged, by both searches independently.** ~6 orders of magnitude, and accumulation is itself contested (Attardo 2015; Mongillo 2017) |

## 9.2 ⭐ The two things that are ours

**(1) The explanatory inversion.** Every prior claimant treats the agency signal as **tracking something real**
— a proxy for instrumental relevance. FMT says the self-model's **attribution decision** is the control
variable, so a **false** attribution should carry the same plasticity consequence as a true one. That is the
forward role §4.2.2/§4.2.3's *retrospective construction* has been missing, and nobody in that literature can
say it, because they all still treat the signal as tracking actual control.

**(2) Motivated cognition as a prediction rather than an embarrassment.** Charpentier's result looks like a
defeat for *"abstract success"* and is instead a gift: a substrate carved by a satisfaction-gated signal is
carved toward **what is satisfying to have concluded**. ⭐ **FMT already says this in MG's own words** — the
*maturing* material in `didactic-patterns.md`: *"usually you declare the ones you reached after the fact as
the ones you aimed for and try not to think about all the failed ideas."* **Charpentier supplies the mechanism
for an observation the theory already makes**, which converts a concession into a consequence.

## 9.3 ✅ THE PARAGRAPH AS SHIPPED — §4.2.2, closing the unrepresentable-apparatus block

**Written into `paper/full/four-model-theory-full.md` and mirrored into the hand-maintained
`paper/full/latex/paper.tex` on 2026-09-23, with four references added to `references.bib` and to the
markdown reference list. ⛔ The PDF has NOT been rebuilt — that is a separate gated step.**

⚠ A third `rather than` was cut from the opening sentence: the draft read *"gated rather than indiscriminate"*,
which would have put three of them in one paragraph against a file-wide baseline the S313 lesson says to
watch. `gated` carries it alone.

> The attribution is not inert. Which moments are written back into the substrate is gated (Pawlak & Kerr,
> 2008; Hong et al., 2022), and in humans the gating signal responds to epistemic goods — information,
> confidence, curiosity — and not only to primary reward (Guggenmos et al., 2016). Assigning an episode to the self is among the things that raise
> it above that threshold, so the attribution is a step in the loop rather than a report on it. Two
> consequences follow. A mistaken attribution should carry the same consequence as a correct one, because what
> does the work is the tag the self-model applies and not the control it tracks. And what the gating signal
> registers is satisfaction rather than accuracy (Charpentier et al., 2018), so a system carved by it is carved toward what is satisfying
> to have concluded: the same mechanism consolidates a hard-won correction and a congenial one, and the
> architecture therefore predicts motivated cognition. None of this returns control to the self-model. What
> is assigned, and how heavily, is itself substrate-determined.

**And the sentence that concedes the gap and names the falsifier:**

> The gating mechanism is measured on a scale of seconds; the structural persistence it is asked to explain
> runs over years; no study bridges them. The near end is testable. Encoding under an illusory sense of
> agency, with actual contingency held at zero, should show the same consolidation benefit as veridical
> agency if the attribution is the operative variable, and none if the agency signal merely tracks
> instrumental relevance.
