**DRAFT (insert after line 418):**

> #### 3.7.1 Two Orthogonal Dimensions of the Criticality Requirement
>
> The three signatures above characterize *whether* a system is at criticality, but two further questions can be asked of a system already in the Class-4 regime: how *much* of the substrate is recruited into the critical process, and how *complex* are the patterns that process computes. These are distinct, separately measurable properties — two orthogonal dimensions read off one edge-of-chaos substrate, not two competing definitions of criticality.
>
> The first dimension is **extent**: the spatial fraction of the substrate currently participating in Class-4 dynamics. Multimodal conscious processing should maximize extent, recruiting wide cortical territory into a single critical process; this is the integration dimension. The second is **complexity**: the differentiated richness of the Class-4 patterns themselves — the depth of computation sustained, maximized by demanding cognition rather than by mere breadth of recruitment; this is the differentiation dimension. A system can be high on one and low on the other: broad but simple recruitment (extent high, complexity low), or narrow but intricate computation (complexity high, extent low). Normal experience rides a frontier between them; the two are jointly maximized only rarely (Section 5.3).
>
> This extent/complexity distinction is the well-established **integration–differentiation tradeoff** of the consciousness-complexity tradition, and the Four-Model Theory claims no priority over it. Integration has been formalized as integrated information Φ (Tononi, 2004; Albantakis et al., 2023); differentiation is indexed by Lempel-Ziv complexity and perturbational complexity (Casali et al., 2013; Schartner et al., 2017); and the balance of the two is the explicit target of complexity-based measures of consciousness generally. The Four-Model Theory's contribution is not the tradeoff but its grounding: it places both dimensions on the same edge-of-chaos substrate (extent and complexity are two ways of measuring how much Class-4 dynamics a critical system is doing, not two substances), and it specifies extent in a way that discriminates conscious from merely co-active states (Section 3.7.2). On this reading, Φ and the differentiation measures are consistent with FMT's two dimensions and supply candidate operationalizations for them, rather than rival accounts. [CITE: integration–differentiation tradeoff — Tononi & Edelman 1998; Mediano et al. 2019 (Φ-decomposition) — verify before insertion.]
>

The two dimensions therefore require distinct observables, set out in Table 1 (criticality-extent and criticality-complexity rows).

### Pattern 4 — The seizure negative control

**DECISION: EXTEND-EXISTING — but this is an AUTHOR-DECISION because it requires editing line 410 and line 885 to remove a contradiction.**

**WHERE:** §3.7.2 (the new two-dimensions subsection above), as a worked example *immediately after* the Pattern-2 draft; AND a reconciling edit to §10.3 line 885 and §3.7 line 410.

**WHY:** Seizure is the negative control that forces the *extent* dial to be defined as **Class-4 involvement**, not raw co-activation: a generalized tonic-clonic seizure maximizes co-activation but is hypersynchronous, so it is Class-2/3, not Class-4 — extent (Class-4 involvement) is therefore LOW, predicting unconsciousness. This is a genuinely sharper definition than "lots of brain active." **But the paper currently says (line 410, line 885) that tonic-clonic seizures are a *supercritical cascade* (σ > 1, Class-3-like runaway).** Pattern 4 says *hypersynchronous* (Class-2/3, stereotyped). These are not the same dynamical claim, and the literature genuinely contains both: generalized tonic-clonic and absence seizures are dominated by pathological **hypersynchrony** (low entropy, low complexity), while some ictal recordings show **supercritical avalanche statistics** (σ > 1) at onset/spread. The paper must not assert both contradictory framings of the same event.

**Proposed rigorous reconciliation (do NOT let the paper claim both):** the unifying invariant is *loss of Class-4 dynamics*, reached from above (supercritical runaway — uniform maximal correlation) or via pathological synchrony (low-complexity, low-differentiation oscillation). Both collapse the *extent* dimension (Class-4 involvement) regardless of where σ sits, because both destroy the structured, differentiated dynamics Class 4 requires. Co-activation and synchrony are therefore explicitly distinguished from Class-4 involvement. Under this framing, line 410 and line 885 should drop the claim that tonic-clonic = supercritical *specifically* and instead say the seizure exits Class 4 (whether by hypersynchrony or supercritical runaway), with citations.

**DRAFT (worked-example paragraph, append to the §3.7.2 block):**

> The extent dimension must be defined as Class-4 *involvement*, not as raw co-activation, and the generalized seizure is the negative control that forces this distinction. A generalized tonic-clonic seizure drives a very large fraction of cortex into simultaneous activity — co-activation is near-maximal — yet the activity is pathologically synchronous and low in differentiation [CITE: hypersynchrony / low ictal complexity — Schindler et al. 2008; Meisel et al. 2012 ictal criticality]. Hypersynchronous, low-complexity dynamics are Class 2/3, not Class 4: the differentiated, structured regime that self-simulation requires is absent. Extent measured as Class-4 involvement is therefore *low* during a generalized seizure even though co-activation is high — and consciousness is lost, exactly as the theory predicts. The point generalizes: a system can have most of its units active while almost none of them are engaged in Class-4 dynamics. "How much brain is active" and "how much brain is conscious" are distinct, and only the latter — the Class-4 extent — is the integration dimension the theory invokes. (Some ictal recordings instead show *supercritical* avalanche statistics, σ > 1; Section 10.3 treats this. Either route — pathological synchrony or supercritical runaway — exits the Class-4 regime and collapses the extent dimension, so the prediction of unconsciousness is robust to which ictal dynamics obtain.)

> ### 8.6 Prediction 5: Both-Dials-Maximized States Produce a Joint Integration–Complexity Signature and Extreme Subjective Time Dilation
>
> **Statement.** States reported to involve extreme subjective time dilation — the near-death "life review," in which an entire autobiography is re-experienced in seconds, and high-dose *Salvia divinorum* (Salvinorin A) experiences, in which subjects report living through years or decades within minutes of clock time — should show *simultaneously* high integration-extent (broad Class-4 recruitment) and high pattern-complexity (differentiated computation), in contrast to ordinary conscious states, which trade the two dimensions off (Section 3.7.2). The two altered states should converge on the same joint signature despite arising from different pharmacology and physiology.
>
> **Mechanism.** *Derivation: Criticality's two dimensions (Section 3.7.2) combined with the temporal-echo construction of subjective time (Section 3.4.4) predict that subjective duration scales with processing volume per unit physical time, not with elapsed physical time.* The conscious "now" is a constructed computational frame, and subjective duration is built from the volume of self-simulation completed per clock-second, not measured against a physical clock (Section 3.4.4). Maximizing both criticality dimensions at once — broad recruitment (extent) and deep differentiated computation (complexity) — packs a very large processing volume into a short physical interval. If subjective duration tracks processing volume, the result is extreme time dilation: a life's worth of integrated, differentiated processing experienced as the felt passage of years, compressed into seconds of clock time. The same mechanism unifies the NDE life-review and high-dose salvia time dilation as two routes to the same both-maximized corner [CITE: salvia subjective time dilation — Addy et al. 2015; NDE life-review phenomenology — Greyson 1983 / van Lommel et al. 2001 — verify].
>
> **Why the corner is rare (governors).** Two governors normally forbid the joint maximum (Section 3.7.2 / Section 5.3): a metabolic-budget limit on simultaneous whole-substrate recruitment and maximal differentiated computation, and a reality-coupling limit treated in Section 4.2. The theory predicts that both NDE and salvia transiently disable the metabolic governor — NDE through the dying brain's metabolic surge and failing homeostatic brakes [CITE: terminal cortical surge — Borjigin et al. 2013; Xu et al. 2023 surge-EEG], salvia through κ-opioid receptor agonism disrupting normal regulatory control [CITE: salvinorin A κ-opioid mechanism — Roth et al. 2002] — which is why both can transiently enter a corner ordinary states cannot reach.
>
> **Testability.** The prediction is a *joint* signature: integration-extent (spatial extent of Class-4 dynamics / integration measures) and pattern-complexity (Lempel-Ziv / perturbational complexity, with the PCI caveat of Section 3.7) measured together, with self-reported subjective duration. Salvia is tractable in controlled settings with concurrent high-density EEG and validated time-perception and dissociation instruments; the NDE case is opportunistic (cardiac-arrest and terminal-care EEG cohorts). The signature is that integration and complexity rise *together* and correlate with reported time dilation, rather than trading off as in ordinary states.
>
> **Falsification.** If extreme subjective time dilation occurs without a joint rise in both criticality dimensions — for example, with high complexity but ordinary or low integration-extent, or vice versa — the processing-volume account of subjective duration is wrong. If integration and complexity continue to trade off in these states exactly as in ordinary states, the both-maximized claim fails.
>
> **Distinguishing power.** No competing framework predicts a joint integration–complexity signature tied to subjective time dilation. IIT predicts time-related changes via Φ alone (integration), with no complementary differentiation requirement; complexity-based measures (LZ, PCI) index differentiation without an integration-tied time-dilation claim; predictive-processing accounts of altered time perception operate through precision-weighting changes without predicting the specific joint signature. The conjunction — both dimensions maximized, tied to time dilation, unifying NDE life-review and salvia — is unique to the Four-Model Theory's two-dimension reading of criticality.

**DRAFT (new §4.2.3):**

> #### 4.2.3 The Two Causal Roles of Consciousness
>
> The dual-evaluation architecture (Section 4.2.1) establishes one causal grip the virtual self has on the world, but it is the slow and indirect one. The Four-Model Theory identifies a second, and the two together reframe the epiphenomenalism question.
>
> The **outward grip** is the familiar one: the virtual self shapes the substrate, the substrate shapes behaviour, and behaviour shapes the world — over months and years of learning and action. This is the grip the causal-role requirement (Section 2.7) usually has in mind, and it is exactly the grip the Libet results make look weak (Section 4.2.2): by the time the conscious simulation registers an intention, the substrate has already initiated the act, so the conscious self appears to be a delayed observer of its own behaviour rather than its author. On a single-grip view of consciousness, this is where the case for causal efficacy is fought, and it is contested ground (Section 10.3).
>
> The **inward grip** is immediate and total, and it is overlooked precisely because it is not directed at the external world. The conscious system can, at will, modulate the permeability and self-referential input of its own explicit models — redirecting attention, generating imagery, forking the simulation onto self-supplied content, and in the limit retreating into self-generated worlds (daydream, deliberate imagination, absorptive states). One cannot change the external room in the present instant; one *can* change what one is experiencing in the present instant. By the theory's own architecture this is not a weak or deniable power but the most immediate causal act available to a conscious system: the redirectable explicit self-model (Principle 3), simulation forking (Principle 5), and variable implicit-explicit permeability (Principle 4), described elsewhere as mechanisms, are here a single *causal lever* — the self setting how tightly its simulation is coupled to incoming reality.
>
> These two grips are one axis viewed from two ends. The same reality-coupling lever that the self can push voluntarily (graded: attention, imagination, daydream) can also be overwhelmed involuntarily. The brain's inner dimensionality vastly exceeds the dimensionality of its sensory and motor channels (Gruber, 2015): the thin input pipe normally servos the high-dimensional inner simulation against reality continuously. When both criticality dimensions are driven up (Section 3.7.2), the volume of inner dynamics outstrips what the narrow input channel can correct, and the simulation runs away from the world into its own attractor — a *lock-in* in which reality-contact is lost (the extreme involuntary end of the same axis whose voluntary end is ordinary imagination). The self-severing character of this is notable: it is the extent dimension — maximal internal recruitment — that swamps the input tether, so pushing into the jointly maximized corner cuts the very line that would keep the simulation grounded. This is the second governor on the rare corner of Prediction 5 (the first being the metabolic budget, Section 5.1): the corner is at once *peak consciousness and minimum reality-contact*, which is why the both-maximized states (NDE life-review, high-dose salvia, vivid dreams) are reliably reality-detached rather than hyper-veridical.
>
> The reframing for the causal-role requirement is direct. Classical epiphenomenalism asks whether consciousness moves the arm and finds the evidence equivocal. The Four-Model Theory's answer is that this is the wrong place to look: the conscious self's causal power is least visible where it is usually sought — slow, indirect influence on outward behaviour — and most visible where it is rarely sought — immediate, at-will control of its own reality-coupling. The position remains distinct from attributing independent causal power to qualia over the substrate (Section 4.2.1; Section 10.3): the inward grip is the substrate's own reality-coupling control exercised through the simulation, not a non-physical force. But it answers the "does consciousness do anything?" challenge with a capability that is hard to dismiss as idle accompaniment — a system that can re-aim its own experience at will is not a passive by-product of its substrate.

> The two-level ontology also supplies a mechanism for the transition the access/phenomenal distinction marks — from content that is merely *present* to content that is consciously *accessed*. What converts presence into access is not a separate broadcasting stage but added recursive depth: access begins when the explicit models (EWM, ESM) become rich enough to *self-interact* — when the self-model maps and operates on itself, so that the system can take its own representational content as an object. This is precisely the recursive-self-modeling ladder of Section 3.5: each rung (simply, doubly, triply extended consciousness) adds a degree of explicit-model self-interaction, and with it a degree of conscious access to content that was present but unmanipulable below. The "phenomenal overflow" cases (Block, 2007) are then content that is present in a thin simulation lacking the self-interactive depth required to access it. This is consistent with access-versus-phenomenal treatments in the literature; the Four-Model Theory's specific contribution is the access *mechanism* — explicit-model self-interaction (recursion depth), rather than a broadcast bottleneck — and its identification with the graduated hierarchy of Section 3.5. The capacity for such self-interaction is also what underwrites the inward causal grip of Section 4.2.3: a system whose explicit models can take their own content as an object is a system that can redirect, imagine, and fork its own simulation at will.
