# Paper 3 literature check: Maudlin, Olympia and their descendants (AIW-289)

Checked 2026-10-18 for `paper3-assessment.md`. Citations were verified against Crossref and OpenAlex
(DOI, venue, volume, pages) unless marked UNVERIFIED. "Read" means the full text or the abstract was
consulted. "Known" means the summary relies on standard secondary reports and was not re-read for
this check. The works citing Maudlin (1989) since 2010 were swept through OpenAlex (70 records), and
so were the works citing Gidon et al. (2022) (20 records).

**Paper 3's move, as tested here.** If a simulated organism has experience, a static, *realized* record of its
whole trajectory has it too. What carries the experience is the internal dependency structure of the
realized states, not host execution. Triviality (Putnam, dust) is answered without counterfactuals: the
dictionary a free reading needs is an object in the world, and its description length records *where*
the structure is stored. Rule + initial state is potential only, which separates the view from Tegmark.

## 1. The core: Maudlin and the direct responses

**Maudlin, T. (1989). Computation and consciousness. *Journal of Philosophy* 86(8), 407–432. doi:10.2307/2026650.** (Known; Klein 2018 reconstruction read.)
Olympia replays a fixed run by physically trivial activity. Inert "Klara" backup machinery restores the
counterfactual sensitivity that computationalism demands. Maudlin assumes that experience supervenes on
occurrent physical activity, and that idle parts cannot make a difference. So computationalism must either
count Olympia-without-Klara as conscious (which by its own lights does not compute) or let inert machinery
matter. He concludes that consciousness does not supervene on computation.
**Relation: the target.** Paper 3 takes the horn Maudlin treated as unacceptable. A counterfactual-free realization suffices. It then
goes further than Olympia, because Olympia still *moves* and paper 3 drops the activity premise as well.
That makes paper 3 a rejection of both Maudlin premises (the counterfactual condition and
supervenience on activity). It replaces them with "realized dependency structure". The paper must say
this explicitly. It is the paper's thesis, not a side remark.

**Barnes, E. (1991). The causal history of computational activity: Maudlin and Olympia. *Journal of Philosophy* 88(6), 304–316. doi:10.2307/2026687.** (Known.)
Olympia does not compute because her states are not *caused by* the preceding computational states
in the way the program specifies. Computation needs the right causal history, and counterfactuals
alone do not supply it.
**Relation: contradicts, and it is the sharpest test of paper 3's vocabulary.** A static record has
no causal production between its states. Paper 3 must therefore say whether "internal dependency"
means causal production (then the record fails, as Barnes says) or a structural/functional relation among
co-existing states (then it must answer Barnes's demand). Draft wording that blurs the two will be refereed against
Barnes.

**Klein, C. (2008). Dispositional implementation solves the superfluous structure problem. *Synthese* 165(1), 141–153. doi:10.1007/s11229-007-9244-z.** (Known.)
**Klein, C. (2015). Olympia and other O-machines. *Philosophia* 43(4), 925–931. doi:10.1007/s11406-015-9613-7.** (Abstract read.)
**Klein, C. (2018). Computation, consciousness, and "computation and consciousness". In M. Sprevak & M. Colombo (eds.), *The Routledge Handbook of the Computational Mind*, 297–309. Routledge. doi:10.4324/9781315643670-23.** (Full preprint read.)
In 2008, Klein argues that implementation is dispositional: idle parts count through their dispositions. In 2015,
he argues that a machine replaying a pre-programmed series is architecturally an O-machine (oracle) and not
a Turing machine, even if it would behave like a TM counterfactually. In 2018, he argues that consciousness should supervene on
*computational processes in an architecture* (sequences of primitive operations), not on the function
computed. A one-step primitive has no structure to carry the combinatorial structure of experience.
**Relation: contradicts.** On Klein's view a static record executes no primitive operations, so it has no
process for experience to supervene on. Klein's combinatorial-structure argument is also the strongest
ally paper 3 can borrow. Paper 3 can grant that experience needs combinatorial internal structure and
argue that the realized record *has* that structure spread across its states, rather than produced by operations. This is the
main exchange of the paper.

**Bartlett, G. (2012). Computational theories of conscious experience: between a rock and a hard place. *Erkenntnis* 76(2), 195–209. doi:10.1007/s10670-011-9325-8** (online 2011). (Abstract read.)
This is the dilemma in its cleanest form. Drop counterfactual sensitivity and you face Putnam/Searle triviality (the
rock). Keep it and you face Maudlin/Bishop (inert parts matter). Bartlett argues that Klein's dispositional escape fails.
**Relation: frames paper 3's contribution exactly.** Paper 3 claims a third way out: drop counterfactuals, and
block the rock by another means (in-world decoder cost). Bartlett is the natural reference for the
statement of the problem.

**Muhlestein, M. (2013). Counterfactuals, computation, and consciousness. *Cognitive Computation* 5(1), 99–105. doi:10.1007/s12559-012-9155-2** (online 2012). (Abstract read.)
He builds a cellular automaton that emulates a brain. In it the line between a computation and its recording
can be blurred arbitrarily while the physical implementation stays the same. He concludes that a purely computational account of
consciousness is unsatisfactory.
**Relation: anticipates the premise, reverses the conclusion.** It is the closest published
precedent for paper 3's "five operations" continuum. Paper 3 accepts the blur and concludes that the
recording side inherits experience. It should cite Muhlestein as the reductio it turns into a thesis. The CA setting
also matches paper 3's simulated-worm framing.

## 2. Triviality and its counterfactual cure

**Putnam, H. (1988). *Representation and Reality*. MIT Press. doi:10.7551/mitpress/5891.001.0001.**
**Searle, J. (1990). Is the brain a digital computer? *Proceedings and Addresses of the APA* 64(3), 21–37. doi:10.2307/3130074.** (Issue/page range from Klein's bibliography; DOI verified.)
**Chalmers, D. (1996). Does a rock implement every finite-state automaton? *Synthese* 108(3), 309–333. doi:10.1007/BF00413692.**
**Chalmers, D. (2011). A computational foundation for the study of cognition. *Journal of Cognitive Science* 12(4), 325–359. doi:10.17791/jcs.2011.12.4.325.**
Putnam and Searle argue that any open system implements any automaton under some mapping. Chalmers blocks this
with combinatorial-state automata whose transitions must support counterfactuals.
**Relation:** paper 3 explicitly answers triviality *without* Chalmers's counterfactual criterion, and Chalmers 1996 is
the reference it departs from. The book already cites these.

**Bishop, J. M. (2002). Dancing with pixies: strong artificial intelligence and panpsychism. In J. Preston & M. Bishop (eds.), *Views into the Chinese Room*, 360–378. Oxford UP. doi:10.1093/oso/9780198250579.003.0019.**
**Bishop, J. M. (2002). Counterfactuals cannot count: a rejoinder to David Chalmers. *Consciousness and Cognition* 11(4), 642–652. doi:10.1016/S1053-8100(02)00023-5.**
**Bishop, J. M. (2009). Why computers can't feel pain. *Minds and Machines* 19(4), 507–516. doi:10.1007/s11023-009-9173-3.** (All three: known.)
The Putnam mapping applied to a fixed-input run gives a phenomenal pixie in every open system
(panpsychism reductio). The counterfactual reply fails, Bishop argues, because never-entered states
cannot make a phenomenal difference.
**Relation: compatible with paper 3's rejection of counterfactuals. Paper 3 must answer the reductio itself.**
Bishop is the opponent the dictionary-cost argument has to defeat. If decoding a pixie from a rock costs a
dictionary as long as the trajectory, the pixie lives in the dictionary and not in the rock.

**Aaronson, S. (2013). Why philosophers should care about computational complexity. In B. J. Copeland, C. Posy & O. Shagrir (eds.), *Computability: Turing, Gödel, Church, and Beyond*, 261–328. MIT Press. doi:10.7551/mitpress/8009.003.0011.** (Waterfall section, known.)
**Millhouse, T. (2019). A simplicity criterion for physical computation. *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 70(1), 153–178. doi:10.1093/bjps/axx046.** (Abstract read.)
Aaronson argues that in the waterfall argument the reduction does all the computational work. Millhouse ranks rival computational
interpretations by the Kolmogorov complexity of the mapping. His mapping still includes counterfactual
intervention clauses.
**Relation: partly anticipated. This is the main priority risk for the triviality section.** "The decoder
carries the structure" is Aaronson's point, and "measure it by description length" is Millhouse's.
What paper 3 adds is narrower. (i) It applies the cost criterion to a case with *no* counterfactual clause, a static record. (ii) It reads
description length as bookkeeping for *where* in the world a structure is stored, not as a tie-breaker between
interpretations. (iii) It treats the dictionary as a physical object in the world. Claim only that.

**Schweizer, P. (2019). Triviality arguments reconsidered. *Minds and Machines* 29(2), 287–308. doi:10.1007/s11023-019-09501-x.** (Abstract read.)
Schweizer keeps a maximally liberal mapping account and rejects computational sufficiency, moving the
explanatory burden onto the target phenomenon.
**Relation: compatible, different route.** Paper 3 also lets the target (FMT architecture) carry the weight.
Cite it in one line.

## 3. Recordings, movies and replays

**Zuboff, A. (1981). The story of a brain. In D. Hofstadter & D. Dennett (eds.), *The Mind's I*. Basic Books.** Reprinted in T. O'Connor & D. Robb (eds.), *Philosophy of Mind: Contemporary Readings*, Routledge, 197–206, doi:10.4324/9780203987698-23. Original pages UNVERIFIED.
**Hofstadter, D. (1981). A conversation with Einstein's brain. In *The Mind's I*.** UNVERIFIED pages.
These are the classic intuition pumps. Zuboff's brain is fragmented and driven by recorded inputs until only
patterns remain. Einstein's brain is a book one can consult.
**Relation: background.** Hofstadter's book is paper 3's "reader vs inhabitant" case. One footnote is enough.

**Marchal, B. (2013). The computationalist reformulation of the mind-body problem. *Progress in Biophysics and Molecular Biology* 113(1), 127–140. doi:10.1016/j.pbiomolbio.2013.03.014.** (Known.) Marchal's earlier movie-graph texts (1988–2004 theses and conference papers) remain UNVERIFIED as citable publications.
**Standish, R. K. (2016). The movie graph argument revisited. arXiv:1608.07764** (preprint, no journal reference). (Abstract read.)
The movie-graph argument says that if a computation is conscious, a film of it must be too, by stepwise removal of
the parts that do the work. So consciousness cannot be tied to physical activity, and Marchal concludes that it lives in arithmetic.
Standish argues that the incompatibility only bites in single classical universes.
**Relation: anticipates "recording inherits experience; host activity is irrelevant".** This is the
strongest priority overlap, although in marginal venues. Paper 3 parts from Marchal where it parts from Tegmark:
mathematical existence is not enough, *realization* is (D1). The paper must cite Marchal and make that
difference its own sentence.

**Doerig, A., Schurger, A., Hess, K. & Herzog, M. (2019). The unfolding argument: why IIT and other causal structure theories cannot explain consciousness. *Consciousness and Cognition* 72, 49–59. doi:10.1016/j.concog.2019.04.002.** (Abstract read.)
Replies: Tsuchiya, Andrillon & Haun (2020), *Consciousness and Cognition* 79, 102877, doi:10.1016/j.concog.2020.102877. Herzog, Schurger & Doerig (2022), *Consciousness and Cognition* 98, 103261, doi:10.1016/j.concog.2021.103261.
Any recurrent network can be unfolded into a feedforward one with the same input-output function. So theories that tie consciousness to
causal structure are either false or unfalsifiable.
**Relation: a constraint, not a rival.** A static record is the limit case of unfolding. Paper 3's
claim is metaphysical and conditional ("if the simulation has experience …"), so it cannot rest on
third-person testability. It should say so, or reviewers will raise the unfolding argument against the
"dependency structure" criterion.

**Gidon, A., Aru, J. & Larkum, M. E. (2022). Does brain activity cause consciousness? A thought experiment. *PLoS Biology* 20(6), e3001651. doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.3001651.**
**Gidon, A., Aru, J. & Larkum, M. E. (2025). Does neural computation feel like something? *Frontiers in Neuroscience* 19, 1511972. doi:10.3389/fnins.2025.1511972.** (Both abstracts read.)
In the first paper, all action potentials are recorded and played back into the same neurons. In the second, a simulated network is
replayed so that activity is unchanged but counterfactuals are erased. They conclude that this paradox
"challenges the notion that consciousness arises from computation".
**Relation: same scenario, opposite conclusion. This is the most recent mainstream engagement, and the paper
should be positioned against it.** Paper 3 resolves their paradox by biting the bullet: the replay realizes the
same dependency structure, so the counterfactual erasure is irrelevant. The neuroscience readership
makes this the best hook for *JCS* or *Neuroscience of Consciousness*.

**Kanai, R. & Ma, S. (2026). Intrinsic computational functionalism and simulated consciousness. arXiv:2606.15348** (preprint, June 2026). (Full text scanned.)
The authors require counterfactual and intervention structure carried by components that are active in the
actual dynamics. They "explicitly exclude movies, static descriptions, arbitrary semantic mappings, and
trajectory replays". They place trace-replay triviality and Olympia under one condition.
**Relation: directly contradicts paper 3, and it is current.** It is the freshest statement of the view paper 3
rejects. Cite it as the representative contemporary opponent. Check whether it has been published by the time of submission.

## 4. Counting and integrity (secondary)

**Bostrom, N. (2006). Quantity of experience: brain-duplication and degrees of consciousness. *Minds and Machines* 16(2), 185–200. doi:10.1007/s11023-006-9036-0.**
**Gómez-Emilsson, A. & Percy, C. (2022). The "slicing problem" for computational theories of consciousness. *Open Philosophy* 5(1), 718–736. doi:10.1515/opphil-2022-0225.**
Bostrom argues that two identical runs give twice the experience. Gómez-Emilsson and Percy show that cleanly halving a computer forces
token/type choices about how many subjects there are. **Relation: the "second copy" question Ch 14 leaves
open.** If paper 3 mentions it, Bostrom is the citation. Otherwise flag it as out of scope.

**Shiller, D. (2024). Functionalism, integrity, and digital consciousness. *Synthese* 203, 47. doi:10.1007/s11229-023-04473-z.**
**Shiller, D. (2026). Bomb threats for functionalists. *Erkenntnis* 91(3), 1251–1270. doi:10.1007/s10670-025-00948-2** (online 2025). (Both abstracts read.)
The first paper argues that functionalism needs integrity constraints on realizers. The second argues that pattern-disrupting influences that intuitively leave
consciousness intact are a problem for counterfactual-loaded functionalism.
**Relation:** the integrity constraints are a possible objection to a record whose "parts" persist
only as stored states. The bomb-threat cases support paper 3's dismissal of counterfactuals. One sentence each.

**Not found / excluded.** No peer-reviewed paper was found that develops Egan's dust theory. Greg Egan,
*Permutation City* (1994), stays a fiction citation. Tegmark (2008), *Foundations of Physics* 38, 101–150,
doi:10.1007/s10701-007-9186-9, and Bennett (1973), *IBM J. Res. Dev.* 17(6), 525–532, doi:10.1147/rd.176.0525, are verified.
Fekete & Edelman (2011), *Consciousness and Cognition* 20(3), 807–827, doi:10.1016/j.concog.2011.02.010, treat
experience as trajectories in activity space. They were not read for this check. Klein (2018) reads them as
treating Olympia as a funny-implementation case, so check before citing.

## Verdict

**Gap: yes, but narrower than "realization, not execution is new".** Three pieces of the move are
already published. Recordings inherit experience (Marchal's movie graph). The record/computation line blurs (Muhlestein). The decoder
carries the structure (Aaronson, Millhouse). What no work found here does is combine three things. Paper 3 takes the counterfactual-free horn of
Maudlin's dilemma all the way to a *static* record. It blocks Putnam/Bishop triviality by an in-world decoder-cost
criterion rather than by counterfactuals. And it stops short of Marchal/Tegmark Platonism by requiring a realized
trajectory with a specific self/world-model architecture.

**One sentence:** a static, realized record of a conscious trajectory is itself conscious. The triviality
that counterfactual criteria were introduced to block is instead blocked by counting the decoder as an object in the world, so
counterfactuals can be dropped without accepting either pixies or Platonism.

**Must engage (five):** Maudlin (1989); Bartlett (2012), as the statement of the dilemma; Klein (2015/2018), as the
architecture/process objection; Millhouse (2019) with Aaronson (2013), as priority for the cost criterion;
Gidon, Aru & Larkum (2022/2025) or Kanai & Ma (2026), as the current replay opponents. Next tier: Barnes (1991),
because the causal-history versus dependency-structure distinction must be explicit; Marchal, for priority; Muhlestein; Bishop (2002).

**Framing consequence for the outline:** open on the Bartlett dilemma and present paper 3 as a third exit.
Do not present "host execution does not matter" as the novelty. Marchal and Gidon et al. make that point
too, from opposite sides.
