# Venue Research — Paper 1 (Epistemology / Philosophy of Logic & Mathematics)

**Paper topic:** limits of embedded description (incompleteness, Kolmogorov complexity,
computational irreducibility) treated as refutations; a falsity-without-truth asymmetry;
self-application of limitative claims.
**Author:** independent researcher, no institutional philosophy affiliation. AI-assisted
drafting must be declared wherever the venue requires it.
**Target length:** ~8,000–10,000 words.

**Research date:** 2026-10-18. **Method:** each journal's own current author-guidelines page
was fetched directly (curl, WebFetch, or an `r.jina.ai` reader-mode proxy where Springer's
Akamai/Fastly "Client Challenge" bot-wall blocked plain requests). Taylor & Francis's
Inquiry page was **unreachable by every method tried** (curl with multiple user agents,
WebFetch, the jina.ai proxy, Wayback Machine) and is flagged accordingly. Every field below
is either a **verbatim quote + source URL**, or explicitly marked **UNVERIFIED**. Where two
independent fetches of the same journal disagreed (this happened twice — Erkenntnis's word
limit and its decision time), both figures are reported rather than silently picking one.
Nothing is invented.

### 1. Synthese (Springer, journal id 11229)

Full title: *"Synthese: An International Journal for Epistemology, Methodology and Philosophy
of Science."*

- **Source:** https://link.springer.com/journal/11229/submission-guidelines ·
  https://link.springer.com/journal/11229/aims-and-scope ·
  https://link.springer.com/journal/11229/how-to-publish-with-us
- **Scope/fit:** Strong. The journal divides its interests into four groups; group (2) is a
  near-literal match: *"The foundations of logic and mathematics, where 'logic', 'mathematics',
  and 'foundations' are all broadly understood."* Group (1) covers *"epistemology,
  methodology, and philosophy of science."* Caveat stated on the page: *"manuscripts need to
  engage in detail with and contribute substantially to the relevant current philosophical
  literature. Authors should make clear how their work goes beyond what has already been
  done."*
- **Word limit:** No strict cap. *"Synthese does not prescribe a word or page limit, but as a
  rule of thumb, papers are typically between 15 and 30 printed journal pages in length."*
  Whether references/abstract count toward that page range is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Abstract limit:** *"Please provide an abstract of 150 to 250 words. The abstract should
  not contain any undefined abbreviations or unspecified references."*
- **Anonymisation:** Double-blind. *"This journal follows a double-blind reviewing procedure.
  This means that the author will remain anonymous to the reviewers throughout peer
  review."* A separate title page carries author identity; *"Authors should avoid citing
  their own work in a way that could reveal their identity."*
- **Reference style:** *"official APA version 7 guidelines"*; author-date in text; manuscripts
  may be submitted in LaTeX (Springer Nature template) or Word.
- **AI-use policy:** Springer-wide boilerplate, present on the page: *"Large Language Models
  (LLMs), such as ChatGPT, do not currently satisfy our authorship criteria... Use of an LLM
  should be properly documented in the Methods section."* Exempt: *"The use of an LLM (or
  other AI-tool) for 'AI assisted copy editing' purposes does not need to be declared."*
- **OA/APC:** Hybrid. *"The current APC for Synthese is £2390.00 GBP / $3390.00 USD /
  €2790.00 EUR."* Free route confirmed: *"Authors can choose to publish under the
  subscription publishing model where no APC charges apply; both options are offered after
  the paper has been accepted."*
- **Green OA/self-archiving:** **UNVERIFIED** on the pages checked.
- **Decision time:** **UNVERIFIED** on the pages checked. (General reputation — not sourced
  to an official page — is that Synthese is slow; treat as informal background only.)
- **Preprints/self-published work:** Springer Nature group-wide policy applies: *"Posting of
  preprints of research manuscripts is not considered prior publication and will not
  jeopardize consideration at Springer Nature journals."* Authors may use *"any legal
  preprint sharing platform"*, including Creative-Commons-licensed ones — Zenodo should

### 9. Philosophy of Science (Cambridge University Press, for the Philosophy of Science
   Association / PSA)

- **Source:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/philosophy-of-science/information/author-instructions ·
  /preparing-your-materials · /submitting-your-materials · /fees-and-pricing · /publishing-ethics
- **Scope/fit:** *"Philosophy of Science aims to publish the best original work in philosophy
  of science, broadly construed,"* requiring work to *"explicitly acknowledge and respond to
  existing work in the area"* and *"take into account the existing literature on the
  topic."* Decent fit if framed as a philosophy-of-science argument about the limits of
  formal/scientific self-description, but the core readership skews toward general
  philosophy of science rather than logic/mathematics specifically.
- **Word limit:** *"Articles are limited to 9000 words, inclusive of abstract, footnotes,
  in-text citations, figures and tables, and appendices."* References and online-only
  appendices are excluded. **This is a hard, almost-fully-inclusive cap** — an
  8,000–10,000-word draft would need real trimming to fit under 9,000 net of references, and
  the top of the target range would not fit at all. Discussion Notes are capped at *"4000
  words inclusive of abstract, footnotes, figures and tables, and in-text citations"*
  (references excluded).
- **Abstract limit:** *"an abstract of 100 words."*
- **Anonymisation:** Strict and detailed. Manuscripts must be *"prepared for anonymous
  review"*: *"Your name should not appear,"* self-citations in third person, no placeholders
  like "[Redacted]"/"[Author]", acknowledgements omitted from the initial submission, name
  stripped from headers/footers. Generic first-person pronouns are allowed unless
  identifying. **Submission-frequency constraints:** at most one peer-reviewed submission
  under review at PoS at a time; at most one submission per 6 months; a 12-month
  post-publication embargo also applies.
- **Reference style:** Chicago Manual of Style, author-date, e.g. *"(Jones 1974, 25)."*
- **AI-use policy:** AI-generated text *"should be accompanied by a full description of the
  process used... and be declared in the manuscript's Acknowledgements"*; AI-generated
  images must be declared in captions; *"generative AI tools such as ChatGPT... should not be
  listed as an author."* Disclosure must include tool name/version, how it was accessed, what
  modifications were made, and the dates used. (Note the apparent tension with "omit
  acknowledgements from the initial submission" above — likely resolved at the revision/
  acceptance stage, not fully clarified on the pages checked.)
- **OA/APC:** Hybrid. Gold OA: *"£2610"* standard / *"£2090"* CUP-member rate;
  *"$3655"* / *"$2925"* member rate. Non-Gold/subscription route confirmed free of APC.
  Colour-print surcharge *"£200/$320"* (irrelevant for online-only).
- **Decision time:** **NOT STATED** on any page checked — **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Preprints/self-published work:** Explicit and favourable — *"deposition of a preprint on
  the author's personal website, in an institutional repository, or in a preprint archive
  shall not be viewed as prior or duplicate publication."* This directly covers a Zenodo
  deposit of the author's own prior work. **The only journal among the required 9 with an
  unambiguous, explicit statement on this point.**

  **philosophy of mathematics**, philosophy of mind, philosophy of religion, **philosophy of
  science**, political philosophy.* Three of the five named fields are a direct hit for this
  paper's topic.
- **Word limit:** *"A contribution (research article) including references and notes should
  normally be within the limits of 20 pages or 8,000 words. Longer contributions may be
  considered, but additional length must be earned by the calibre and merits of the
  submission."* **Explicitly inclusive of references and notes** — the tightest default cap
  among all journals researched that state one at all; an 8,000–10,000-word draft would need
  trimming (or would need to argue for the "exceptional merit" exception) to fit comfortably.
- **Abstract limit:** *"Give a concise summary of your article in an abstract of approx.
  100-150 words in a single paragraph."*
- **Anonymisation:** Double-blind. *"KRITERION – The Journal of Philosophy is a double-blind
  journal. Manuscripts are reviewed anonymously by at least two independent reviewers
  selected by the editors."* The editors reserve the right to desk-reject without review for
  insufficient novelty/interest.
- **Reference style:** Chicago Manual of Style, author-date — *"Please adhere strictly to the
  reference style of KRITERION... as outlined in the CMS author date Reference Style
  Sheet."* Detailed conventions are given for classical-source citation (Stephanus,
  Bekker, Kant's A/B, etc.) — directly useful if the paper engages historical figures.
- **AI-use policy:** Explicit. *"We do not accept submissions that name Artificial
  Intelligence (AI) and/or Machine Learning Tools as credited author(s) on a manuscript
  because such tools cannot take responsibility for the submitted work and therefore cannot
  be considered as eligible authors. Where such tools or technologies are used as part of
  the design or methodology of a research study, their use should be clearly described in
  the Acknowledgments section."* This is phrased around "design or methodology of a research
  study" rather than drafting specifically, but the natural reading extends to AI-assisted
  drafting disclosed in the Acknowledgments.
- **OA/APC: the one real cost-related catch.** The journal is **Gold-OA-only** — *"4 issues
  per year, Open Access Online only"*, no subscription/free route was found. *"Authors who
  publish in open access journals must pay Article Processing Charges (APCs)."* The exact fee
  was **not** in the official PDF; a search-index extract of the publisher's own pricing page
  (degruyterbrill.com/publishing/for-authors/journal-authors/open-access/pricing-funding)
  gives **EUR 1,500.00 (subject to change)** — **this specific figure is UNVERIFIED at the
  raw-page level** (could not be independently re-confirmed by direct fetch) but is
  plausible and specific, not a vague estimate. A waiver route is explicit: *"authors whose
  affiliation or research grants do not cover APCs can receive a waiver by emailing
  kriterion@degruyterbrill.com after submitting their article"* — directly relevant for an
  independent author.
- **Decision time: the only journal in this entire research pass with an explicit, published
  commitment.** *"KRITERION – The Journal of Philosophy aspires to inform authors of the
  peer review decision within 6–8 weeks from submission date. Revised manuscripts should be
  returned within 6–8 weeks. Accepted articles will be published online within 2–4 weeks of
  acceptance."*
- **Preprints/self-published work:** Explicit and favourable. *"De Gruyter does not consider
  the following purposes of a paper as pre-publication: publication in the form of a
  congress abstract, publication as an academic thesis, publication as an electronic
  preprint on recognized servers such as arXiv, RePEc, bioRxiv, ResearchSquare, etc."*
  Zenodo is not named explicitly, but the "etc." after a list of "recognized servers"
  suggests it would likely qualify. Previously published material (including on such
  servers) must still be referenced appropriately in the manuscript.
- **Realism for an independent author:** Submission is via ScholarOne
  (mc.manuscriptcentral.com/kriterion); institutional e-mail is merely *recommended*, not
  required. Current editors: Christian J. Feldbacher-Escamilla and Alexander Gebharter
  (kriterion@degruyter.com). Founded 1991, published by De Gruyter since 2021 — an
  established, specialist, but not hyper-competitive venue.

## Ranked shortlist of 3

1. **Philosophia Mathematica** — the best raw scope match of every journal checked: it
   exists specifically for *"the philosophy of pure and applied mathematics including
   computing,"* which is exactly where incompleteness, Kolmogorov complexity and
   computational irreducibility as refutation-arguments belong. No hard word-limit was
   found, so the 8,000–10,000-word target is not at risk; double-blind review judges the
   paper on technical merit rather than institutional pedigree; and its preprint policy
   explicitly preserves the right to an "Author's Original Version" made available
   beforehand, compatible with an existing Zenodo deposit. The only real friction is the
   unusually tight 100-word abstract cap, which just needs a dedicated compression pass.

2. **Logique et Analyse** — the most precise scope match found anywhere in this research
   (*"Logic and Philosophy of Logic, Philosophy of Mathematics, Philosophy of Language,
   Formal Philosophy"*), the most Zenodo-friendly self-archiving policy of any journal
   checked (no-embargo green OA naming PhilArchive/PhilSci-Archive explicitly), and a
   smaller, specialist submission pool that plausibly raises acceptance odds for a rigorous
   independent-author paper relative to the hyper-competitive generalist Springer titles
   (Synthese/Erkenntnis). The main risk is that several fields (AI policy, exact APC,
   decision time) simply are not published anywhere findable — some terms will only become
   clear after direct contact with the editors.

3. **Kriterion – Journal of Philosophy** — the best-documented process of any journal
   checked, with the only explicit, published decision-time commitment found anywhere (6–8
   weeks to first decision) — a real practical advantage for an independent author who wants
   predictability rather than an open-ended wait. Scope fit is strong and explicit (logic,
   epistemology, philosophy of mathematics and philosophy of science are all named welcome
   fields), and the AI and preprint policies are both clear and workable. The two costs: the
   word cap (8,000 words, inclusive of references and notes) will require real compression
   from an 8,000–10,000-word draft or an explicit appeal to the "exceptional merit" exception
   for extra length, and the journal is Gold-OA-only with a (lightly verified) EUR 1,500 APC
   — though an explicit no-questions-asked-sounding waiver route exists for authors without
   institutional or grant funding.

**Honorable mentions, not in the top 3 but worth keeping as fallbacks:**
- **Journal for General Philosophy of Science** — a 55-year-old dedicated forum for exactly
  this kind of foundational/methodological argument, with standard, well-documented Springer
  process (APA 7, codified AI-disclosure policy, ~10,000-word soft cap) and a less saturated,
  less narrowly generalist readership than Synthese/Erkenntnis.
- **Erkenntnis** — outstanding scope fit (philosophy of mathematics and logic named
  explicitly as core fields) but carries the tightest, fully-inclusive-of-references
  word cap found among the Springer group (10,000 words, with a conflicting 12,000-word
  figure also surfaced — verify directly) plus a reputation for being highly competitive and
  slow (5–10 months to a substantive first decision per third-party data, versus a
  misleadingly fast 13-day "first decision" metric that almost certainly measures desk
  screening only). Worth a second-round submission.
- **Synthese** — strong scope fit (explicit "foundations of logic and mathematics" area), no
  hard word cap, a free subscription route, and a favourable Springer-wide preprint policy —
  a solid generalist option, held back mainly by its prestige/competitiveness.
- **Foundations of Science** — flexible length and good foundational fit, but **demoted from
  "close alternate" status** on new evidence: review is explicitly **not blind**, and
  submission requires nominating 5 potential reviewers across two continents with detailed
  justifications, plus a published author CV — a real practical barrier for an independent
  researcher with a thin institutional network.
- **Grazer Philosophische Studien** — triple-blind review is procedurally the fairest process
  found for an unaffiliated author anywhere in this research, and its scope (general analytic
  philosophy) is broad enough to accommodate this paper, but it offers no particular thematic
  advantage over the generalist journals above.

## Cross-cutting notes and method caveats

- **Access difficulty by publisher, worst to best:** Taylor & Francis (Inquiry) — totally
  blocked, every method failed. OUP (Philosophia Mathematica) and Wiley (Ratio) —
  Cloudflare-blocked on direct fetch, recovered via WebFetch or search-index extraction
  respectively (Ratio only partially). Springer (Synthese, Erkenntnis, EJPS, Philosophical
  Studies, JGPS, Foundations of Science, Axiomathes/Global Philosophy) — blocked by an
  Akamai/Fastly "Client Challenge" on plain curl, but reliably recovered via a 2–3-step
  auth-cookie redirect chain or an `r.jina.ai` reader-mode proxy. Cambridge (Philosophy of
  Science), Brill/De Gruyter Brill (Grazer Philosophische Studien, Kriterion), and Peeters
  (Logique et Analyse) — all directly reachable. **If any shortlisted journal's figures above
  are marked UNVERIFIED, a direct, logged-in-browser check before submission is warranted** —
  this is not exotic data, it was simply unreachable by automated fetch at research time.
- **AI-disclosure policies converge almost everywhere checked:** substantive AI/LLM use in
  drafting must be disclosed (typically in a Methods or Acknowledgements section, naming the
  tool and how it was used); AI-assisted copy-editing/proofreading alone is exempt from
  disclosure; an AI tool can never be listed as an author. This pattern held at every
  publisher where a policy was found (Springer, Taylor & Francis, Cambridge, Brill/De
  Gruyter, Kriterion/De Gruyter) — the specific disclosure mechanism (Methods vs.
  Acknowledgements) is the only real variable.
  **Practical implication for this submission: plan to disclose AI-assisted drafting in
  whichever section the target journal specifies, framed as drafting/editorial assistance
  under full human authorship and responsibility — this is satisfiable at every journal in
  this list.**
- **Abstract limits cluster around 150–250 words** at the Springer titles (with EJPS and
  Axiomathes/Global Philosophy allowing up to 450 for topics that need it), while OUP, CUP and
  Kriterion/De Gruyter run tighter (100, 100, and 100–150 words respectively) — the abstract
  will likely need two versions: a ~200-word "Springer" version and a ~100-word compressed
  version.
- **Decision/review times were essentially never published** on official guidelines pages —
  Kriterion is the sole exception with an explicit 6–8-week commitment. Where times appear
  elsewhere (Springer's own "submission to first decision (median)" metrics boxes, or
  third-party aggregators), the Springer figures read as desk-screening-only (measured in
  days) and are not a reliable estimate of a substantive first decision (which third-party
  aggregator data for Erkenntnis puts at 5–10 months).
- **Preprint/self-published-monograph citation policy — directly relevant to this author's
  circumstance:** only two journals in the whole list state an explicit, unambiguous policy:
  **Philosophy of Science** (CUP/PSA — preprint deposit "shall not be viewed as prior or
  duplicate publication") and **Kriterion** (De Gruyter — electronic preprints on "recognized
  servers... etc." are not pre-publication). The Springer group-wide policy (Synthese,
  Erkenntnis, EJPS, Philosophical Studies, JGPS, Foundations of Science, Global Philosophy)
  is also explicit but applies specifically to *the same paper's own preprint*, not
  necessarily to citing a separate self-published monograph — this distinction was not
  resolved by any journal's guidelines and is worth a direct editorial query before
  submission regardless of venue chosen.
