**v2 (2026-08-27) — the paper priced the architecture in its title and enabled it in its body**

v1 was titled *"Closure and Criticality as Enabling Conditions for World-Modelling."* No result in it shows closure enabling anything. A finite closed loop unrolled over a finite horizon is feedforward plus memory, so any input–output capability is feedforward-approximable and a necessity claim about closure is refutable by construction. What the results actually establish is a sample-efficiency advantage at a fixed budget, and a price at a fixed criterion. The paper is retitled accordingly, and — this is the larger half of the change — four passages of the body that still argued enablement have been rewritten, so the title and the Discussion now say the same thing. Section 7 states the prohibition in its own sentence.

*Three architectural-cost results are added as a new Section 5.* The parent paper banks eight results; the companion previously explained the first five and none of the last three. Section 5.1 reports the cost of preventing closure: an exhaustive minimum-synapse block search finds that opening the global loop costs 14.0% of the model connectome in its dense configuration and 12.0% in its modular one, leaving two and three smaller closed systems standing, where making the network acyclic costs 74.0% and 71.5% — a 5.3× and 6.0× multiplier. The cheap edit relocates closure; it does not abolish it. Section 5.2 reports that closure's advantage is conditional on the driven subsystem carrying change the rest of the system cannot derive for itself: downstream *R*² moves from −0.001 to 0.640 on that manipulation alone, and the advantage falls from 0.398 to 0.089 as the disturbance becomes derivable, without reaching zero. Section 5.3 reports an argmin over 72 enumerated architectures which selects a bottleneck exactly where content is low-dimensional relative to its substrate, spans otherwise-separate subsystems, must be delivered near the achievable ceiling, and reads back over a short path. For content spanning a world model and a body, the cheapest such bottleneck reads the body's own representation — a loop over the system's own state, with the word "self" nowhere in the derivation.

*One realization fact is now stated wherever it bears.* Every loop arm in this program was simulated as the materialised dense product of its factors rather than as a wired bottleneck. The factored bill is the right quantity for a storage or wiring-cost claim, which is what Section 5 makes; it is the wrong quantity for any dynamical or metabolic sentence. The synaptic-filter scope statement in Section 5.3 is imported verbatim from the source record rather than paraphrased.

*The open test is restated, because the old statement of it could not come out positive.* v1 named the theory's strongest single claim as whether a scaled, closure-driven modeller loses its modelling capability when the loop is cut. That is a capability-loss binary, and the same unrolling result forbids it: a programme running that test would be scoring its own strongest claim against a standard the mathematics rules out in advance. Section 8 now states the open test as cost at matched function on an open-ended workload — how much more it costs a cut system to reach the same behaviour, and whether that gap grows with the openness of the task.

*Smaller corrections.* Section 4.3 no longer describes the manipulation as ablating a model: the explicit models are generated activity rather than stored structure, so what an experiment cuts is the projection that generates them. A readout label carried over from the experimental record, `P1`, is dropped because the parent paper uses P1–P3 for its principles.

No number was altered. Section 6's null and Sections 4.1–4.6 report the same measurements as v1.
