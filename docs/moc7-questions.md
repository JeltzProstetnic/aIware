# MoC7 Questions — a running collection

**Opened S285 (2026-08-05) at MG's instruction: "i have more questions for moc7, we need to collect them."**

MoC7 Copenhagen, **Oct 12–16 2026**. Two hard dates:
- **~Sept 12** — discussion-session topics are *proposed and voted on by registered participants*, one month
  prior (MoC6 used slido). This is the deadline that matters, because the winning topics feed the
  **collective paper** on methodological and conceptual consensus.
- **Oct 12–16** — the conference itself, for the Class-B questions that need a human in front of you.

**Why this file exists separately from the prep file.** `docs/pending-moc7-copenhagen.md` holds logistics,
poster spec and the editorial map. Questions are a different kind of asset: they accumulate unpredictably,
they need ranking against each other, and the ~Sept 12 vote forces a *choice* among them. Keep them together
so the choice is made from the full set rather than from whatever was most recent.

## Class A — candidates for the ~Sept 12 discussion-session vote

The constraint that shapes all of these: **frame the field in FMT-compatible terms without naming FMT**, and
be answerable in a ninety-minute session. A question only earns its slot if a room of rivals can each engage
it honestly.

**Ranking note, and it is the thing to think about before September:** *value if it runs* and *probability it
wins the vote* are different axes, and the best question on one is not the best on the other. A question every
camp can answer produces a **taxonomy of existing positions** and is likely to win. A question most theories
*cannot* answer surfaces a **gap** — worth more if it runs, less likely to be voted in, and carries some risk
of reading as a trap. Decide deliberately which of those two we are buying.

### A1 — What is the contribution of consciousness to human intelligence, and how does this contribution happen?
*(MG, 2026-08-05)*

**Assessment: the highest-value question in the set if it runs, and the one most likely to expose a
field-wide gap.** Its structure is the reason — the two halves do different work:

- *"What is the contribution"* — most theories can gesture at something here.
- *"**How** does this contribution happen"* — **most of the field has no mechanism to offer at all.** IIT says
  nothing about intelligence. GNWT has broadcast-availability but no account of what it buys. Active inference
  has a story about inference in general rather than about what consciousness specifically *adds*. This is
  where the room goes quiet, and it is precisely where FMT has a concrete answer.

**FMT's answer, and it is a mechanism rather than a slogan: redeployment.** Re-pointing an already-rich
self-model at a non-self referent yields a rich model of the other *for free*, where a system without one must
relearn it — one model, K deployments (didactic patterns #20/#26/#27). The consciousness→intelligence link is
therefore a **sample-efficiency** claim about model reuse.

**⚠ Discipline that must hold in the room — CORRECTED by MG 2026-08-06 (S288); the earlier version of this
note was wrong and would have cost us the question.** It said "phrase it as efficiency, never as capability"
and that *"consciousness enables intelligence" is false and must not be said.* **That over-applied a result
from the ML lane to the human one.** MG: *"no free lunch is about ML not about human intelligence… human
intelligence includes social and emotional intelligence and other stuff that is consciousness altogether (not
the closure) IS a capability that lifts intelligence from insect to mammal level."*

**What actually holds:**
- The unrolling result constrains claims about **fixed tasks at unbounded budget** — the in-silico lane. There,
  and only there, keep to sample efficiency.
- For **biological intelligence the capability claim is licensed**, because the unrolled equivalent must be
  built inside a genome, a lifetime and a metabolic ceiling. An advantage unbounded in the limit **is** a
  capability boundary for every physically realizable organism.
- The task class is wrong too: the theorem assumes **episode-stationary** tasks; open-ended cognition is the
  non-stationary case where novelty outruns the amortizer.
- And "intelligence" here is **not** the *g*-loaded ML sense. Social and emotional intelligence *are*
  redeployment, and they are where human intelligence actually pays (`AIW-132`).

**So in the room:** the answer to A1's *how* is redeployment, and it may be stated as a **capability of
consciousness as a whole architecture** — not of closure as an isolated mechanism — **at realizable budget.**
If a formalist raises unrolling, concede it precisely and immediately: *yes, in function space at unbounded
budget; no organism has that budget, and the theorem assumes a stationary task.* That concession is a strength
— it shows we know exactly where our own claim stops.

**Also note:** this is RIM's home territory, and RIM is parked with 3 desk rejections. That is an argument for
raising it as a *question* rather than a position — the question is free, the position has a track record.

### A2 — What structural criterion distinguishes conscious from non-conscious computation?
*(drafted S281, the vehicle for the collective-paper slot)*

**The standing candidate, and the most likely to win a vote**, because every camp can answer it and wants to.
It produces a taxonomy of existing positions, which is exactly the raw material a consensus paper needs, and
it maps onto the chart's **R4** boundary without ever mentioning FMT.

Provenance, stated precisely: what was rated *highest possible value* was the **collective-paper slot** itself
(`docs/pending-moc7-copenhagen.md` §5 — "the highest-value item, and it isn't the poster"), on the grounds
that a named contributor slot on a field-consensus paper convened by the people who decide what NoC and JAIC
publish **cannot be desk-rejected**. A2 is the question drafted to occupy that slot.

### A3 — How much does your theory leave outside itself, and why?
*(S285, from the residue work)*

**The one question on which a room of rivals can each answer honestly with nobody losing.** It separates every
major position in a single move — dualists put all of phenomenality outside, IIT almost nothing, GNWT leaves
it unaddressed, FMT puts *presence* outside and everything else inside — and it lets dualists participate
without conceding anything. Ties to the **Presence Term** and its exclusion ledger
(`docs/smoc-middle-layer-draft.md` §2.7).

Strategic property worth noting: it is the only Class-A question that is *safer* for us the more hostile the
room, because our answer is unusually specific and unusually modest.

### A4 — Does the workspace do constitutive work, or is it only the channel?
*(S285, reframed 2026-08-05 after MG identified the Arbeitsmodell as the workspace)*

**Sharper than the version first drafted** ("is a global workspace a primitive or a composite?"), because the
structural half turned out to be closed: **FMT has had a workspace since 2015** — the *Arbeitsmodell*, i.e.
short-term memory plus scraps — so both theories have one, and the disagreement is about its **role**. GNWT
makes global broadcast constitutive of consciousness; FMT makes the self-model's closure constitutive and
leaves the workspace as the channel content passes through.

**Why this is good for the room:** it invites the largest camp to engage on its own terms, concedes them the
structure honestly, and puts a well-posed empirical question on the table — *what would dissociate broadcast
from closure?* Content globally available but not entering the self-model's update rule would decide it. High
engagement probability, and it reads as good faith rather than a takedown.

### A5 — What would a shared coordinate system for consciousness theories have to contain?
*(S285, the meta-question)*

Puts the chart and the method on the table without requiring anyone to endorse a theory. **This is the
question that most directly serves the actual goal** — supplying the field its middle layer — but it is
meta rather than substantive, so it may read as procedural to a room that wants to argue about consciousness.
Best used as a *second* proposal alongside a substantive one, or as the framing sentence for whichever of
A1–A4 goes forward.
