*Free modeling and criticality.* The requirement is free modeling: open-ended computation recruited by the system for its own modeling. Free modeling is necessary and not sufficient; near-criticality is its usual signature in cortex and is neither necessary nor sufficient. Every passage that made criticality a condition of consciousness is restated accordingly (glossary, §3.7, §3.7.3, §5.1, §5.3, §6, §7, §8.1, §8.5, §8.7, §10.3), and a conscious system with subcritical dynamics is now accommodated by the theory. Table 1b replaces the criticality row with a free-modeling row, whose falsification criterion is robust consciousness where a fixed lookup reproduces the system's behavior on novel input. §3.7.3 states that universal computation does not depend on the edge of chaos (Siegelmann & Sontag, 1995; Maass, 1996). Whether free modeling at depth needs a near-critical operating point in any substrate is stated as open.

*In-silico evidence (§8.9).* The rate-reservoir accuracy peak previously reported from a single run has been replicated on thirty fresh reservoirs with the edge measured by the driven Lyapunov exponent: the demand-gated shape replicates, and the peak lies in the ordered regime, not at the edge, as a memory-capacity account predicts (Schuecker et al., 2018; Haruna & Nakajima, 2019). The decodability of maintained operands at spiking scale is now reported from eight seeds with the membrane voltage bounded below by a biophysical floor (0.61–0.76 against chance 0.50, delays 20–45); the loop-off arm and the scrambled control stay at chance. That re-run awaits independent re-analysis, and the paper says so.
