# AIW-194 — Does the "recall tag" survive primary verification?

**Verification pass run 2026-08-24. Independent re-verification, not a re-read of the S298 record.**
Scope: MG's question — *"memories just like 3pp modeling must be somehow discernable from real
experience, and also doesn't the human brain even use different chemistry?"*
Gates: member (e) of the FMT v15 release epic.

---

## 1. VERDICT — **PARTLY. Cut member (e), but only in the requirement-plus-prediction form.**

The **requirement** survives primary verification and is in fact strengthened: the field states in its own
words that *"no single factor unambiguously signals whether an experience is internally or externally
generated"* (Dijkstra, Kok & Fleming 2022, verbatim from the abstract), so FMT is entitled to name the gap
and is not inventing a problem. The **tag** does not survive, and it fails harder than expected. A discrete,
positively-affixed source marker is not merely unevidenced — **it is the position the field explicitly
defined itself against thirty years ago**: Johnson, Hashtroudi & Lindsay (1993, p.3), the founding statement
of source monitoring, reads *"people do not typically directly retrieve an abstract tag or label that
specifies a memory's source, rather, activated memory records are evaluated and attributed to particular
sources through decision processes performed during remembering."* And the closest modern candidate was
**tested and rejected** in a primary study: Dijkstra
& Fleming (2023, *Nat Commun*, N = 272 + N = 339) set the intention-to-imagine account (the efference-copy-
shaped candidate) against a strength account and found for strength, reporting that imagined and perceived
signals are *"in fact intermixed, with judgments of reality being determined by whether this intermixed
signal is strong enough to cross a reality threshold."* MG's own reformulation — *the source properties
**are** the tag*, and the design requirement is **negative** (do not destroy source distinguishability) —
comes off far better than the original wording, and gains an empirical anchor it did not have: imagery and
perception **do** differ mechanistically and not merely in content, in that bottom-up drive is present in
perception and absent in imagery (Dijkstra et al. 2017, *Sci Rep*, open access). **But the sting, and it is
the most valuable thing this pass found: the brain's own reality judgement demonstrably does not read that
diagnostic property.** The difference exists in the signal; the reader ignores it and reads combined
strength in fusiform instead (Dijkstra et al. 2025, *Neuron*). So the honest position is neither "there is a
tag" nor "there is no tag" but the sharper third thing — **a source-diagnostic property exists and is not
what the system consults** — and that is a claim FMT can make, that no rival currently makes, and that is
checkable. What must **not** be written is any sentence asserting a mechanism that marks recalled states as
recalled.

## 2. THE CHEMISTRY CLAIM — established / contested / unsupported

MG's intuition is **partly right and partly wrong, and the split is clean.** Stated precisely:

### ✅ ESTABLISHED — and this is the real kernel of MG's intuition
- **Acetylcholine differentially suppresses intrinsic/feedback versus afferent pathways.** Clean, dose–
  response, pharmacologically controlled slice measurement: carbachol at 100 µM suppresses CA1 stratum
  radiatum (intrinsic) **89.1 ± 3.0%** against stratum lacunosum-moleculare (afferent) **40.1 ± 4.1%**;
  IC50 7.33 vs 89.89 µM (Hasselmo & Schnell 1994). ⚠ Note this is a graded **~2:1 pathway asymmetry, not a
  switch**, and the authors themselves note *"there was some suppression… in **both** layers."*
- **ACh causally gates which regime obtains** — driving septal cholinergic neurons stops ripples and
  produces theta (Vandecasteele et al. 2014, *PNAS*).
- **Encoding and retrieval occupy separate theta phases** — with in vivo confirmation of the model's phase
  predictions (Douchamps et al. 2013) and human intracranial support (Rizzuto 2006; Yoo 2021).

### ⚠ CONTESTED / MIS-SPECIFIED
- **The encoding/retrieval "mode" is MODELLED, not measured — at every link in the chain.** Hasselmo &
  Schnell 1994 measured the laminar suppression; the mode appears only *"**within the simulation**."*
  Hasselmo, Bodelón & Wyble 2002 is titled *"A **Proposed** Function"* and says *"This model **predicts**…"*
  Douchamps et al. 2013 confirmed the phase predictions but **never measured ACh**, and state outright:
  *"we have **inferred, rather than directly measured, encoding and retrieval states**."* ⇒ **No primary in
  this chain measures a chemical difference between two cognitive states.**
- **⚠ CORRECTION TO MY OWN EARLIER WORDING AND TO THE S298 RECORD: the tonic framing was NOT retired.**
  Hasselmo & Sarter 2011 propose a **two-component model that explicitly integrates both**: *"**In addition
  to** the second-based cholinergic transients… more slowly changing (**tonic**…) levels… are also
  present."* Write *"revised into a two-component (phasic + tonic) model"*, **never "retired."** ⭐ The
  genuinely load-bearing sentence is a different one: tonic and phasic measures *"are **not necessarily
  correlated** and… **may in fact be dissociated**"* ⇒ **there is no single scalar "ACh level" for any
  downstream reader to consult.** That kills the tag idea more cleanly than the vocabulary point does.
- **SPEAR's universality is not settled** — Guth et al. 2025 is the newest and best-powered human
  single-neuron test and is largely negative (**most** neurons did not shift phase; no relation to memory
  success).

### ⛔ UNSUPPORTED — and this is the half of MG's intuition that fails
- **Nothing in this literature distinguishes RECALL from PERCEPTION.** Every result above distinguishes
  **encoding from retrieval/consolidation** — *both endpoints inside memory*. That is a different contrast
  from the one AIW-194 needs, and the substitution is the single easiest error to make here.
- **⭐ The concrete null: the word "perception" occurs ZERO times across all six Hasselmo/Sarter papers**
  (and zero in the 2002 model). The contrast is simply not in this literature.
- **⭐⭐ Worse for the claim: ACh puts perception and encoding on the SAME side.** High ACh is the state where
  afferent sensory drive dominates ⇒ **perception and encoding are chemically co-located**, and *retrieval*
  is what gets suppressed. And Hasselmo (2006) opens by noting scopolamine *"impairs the encoding of new
  memories, **but not the retrieval** of previously stored memories."* ⇒ **Retrieval is the ACh-insensitive
  operation.** A marker that does not move with retrieval cannot mark retrieval.
- **Wrong direction of causation, and this is the structural objection.** ACh is an **input** that biases
  which pathway dominates, not an **output** reporting which pathway won. A reader seeing "ACh high" learns
  the circuit was configured for afferent dominance — not that any particular representation is perceptual.
  It is also a **regional state variable**, and a regional variable cannot label individual representations.
- **A targeted Europe PMC search for a neurochemical signature distinguishing memory retrieval from
  perception returns nothing that addresses the contrast.** Reported as a clean, documented null.
- **⚠ The REM fact runs the wrong way, and it is fatal to ACh-as-recall-marker. Verified from two primaries
  with numbers, this pass:**
  - **Marrosu et al. (1995)**, *Brain Res* 671(2):329–332 (`10.1016/0006-8993(94)01399-3`), cats: ACh rises
    ~100% in quiet waking and ~175% in active waking over slow-wave-sleep baseline; **during REM the
    hippocampus reaches ~4× SWS — twice quiet waking** — while cortex sits at roughly quiet-waking level.
  - **Kametani & Kawamura (1990)**, *Life Sci* 47(5):421–426 (`10.1016/0024-3205(90)90300-g`), rats: waking
    ~300% of SWS, and **"the rate of ACh release during paradoxical sleep was as high as during waking and
    appeared to be even higher."**
  - **Vazquez & Baghdoyan (2001)**, *Am J Physiol* 280(2):R598–R601 (`10.1152/ajpregu.2001.280.2.R598`), cat
    basal forebrain, 22 experiments / 4 animals: **REM 0.77 ± 0.07 > waking 0.58 ± 0.03 > NREM 0.34 ± 0.01
    pmol/10 min**, REM significantly above waking.
  ⇒ **Three independent primaries, three structures, two species, same direction.** If ACh marked offline or
  internally-generated content, REM would be the ACh **floor**. It is the **ceiling**.
  **⚠ Minor correction to the project's own wording:** *"comparable to or exceeding active waking"* is right
  for **hippocampus** but overstates **cortex**, which in Marrosu sits at quiet-waking level. Say *"REM is a
  high-ACh state, exceeding waking in hippocampus and basal forebrain, and around quiet-waking level in
  cortex."*
  **⭐⭐ Best move: quote Hasselmo against the claim rather than arguing.** Hasselmo & Sarter (2011) state
  that cholinergic enhancement of encoding *"might be coupled with a reduction in the retrieval… during
  behavioral states associated with **higher levels of acetylcholine such as REM sleep**."* ⇒ **Within the
  theory itself, REM is high-ACh, encoding-dominant, retrieval-SUPPRESSED.** Anyone using ACh as a recall
  marker gets the sign inverted on the one state where recall-like content most obviously runs.
  **⚠ Honest caveat that cuts both ways — state it if the REM numbers are used.** These are minute-scale
  microdialysis measurements, exactly what Sarter et al. (2009) argue is recovered from *"scarred tissue"*
  and is statistically reproducible by summing second-scale transients. That weakens the *measure*, but it
  weakens the tag claim further still: if there is no reliable tonic state variable, there is nothing for a
  reader to consult in the first place.
- The claim also **conflates two independent neuromodulatory axes and names the wrong one**: what withdraws
  in REM is **aminergic** tone. ⚠ And on what deactivates — see row 16g: **"dorsolateral prefrontal" is
  correct; "anterior prefrontal" is not** (Maquet reports anterior cingulate *up*).
- **The stated FUNCTION of the ACh work is write-protection, not reference.** Hasselmo's own framing is
  reduction of proactive interference — stopping the network's read-out being re-encoded as fresh input. **A
  firewall is not a label.** It protects the store; it tells no downstream system that the content is not
  current.
- **⚠ Do not describe replay as "offline" or "unconscious."** Human hippocampal ripples rise **1–2 s before**
  conscious recall and are content-selective (Norman et al. 2019, *Science*, verified abstract).

**One-line answer for MG:** *the brain does use different chemistry — but it separates **encoding** from
**retrieval**, not recall from perception; on that axis perception and encoding sit on the **same** side and
retrieval is the operation acetylcholine barely touches; and the one state where it could be checked
cleanly (REM) runs the wrong way, which Hasselmo himself concedes in print.*

## 3. TAG versus THRESHOLD — what the evidence actually supports

**The evidence supports a THRESHOLD, decisively, within a modality — and no tag anywhere.**

**⭐ The decisive citation, and it is older and blunter than anything the project had.** The source-monitoring
framework — the founding framework of this entire literature — **defines itself against the tag account**.
Johnson, Hashtroudi & Lindsay (1993), p.3, verbatim:

> *"A central claim of the source-monitoring approach is that people **do not typically directly retrieve an
> abstract tag or label that specifies a memory's source**, rather, activated memory records are evaluated
> and attributed to particular sources **through decision processes performed during remembering**."*

Restated by Simons, Garrison & Johnson (2017): *"memories do not contain propositional tags that directly
specify their source."* ⇒ **A paper asserting a source tag is not filling a gap in the literature; it is
reasserting the position the field rejected in 1993 and has not returned to.** This is the sentence that
settles member (e), and it must be in front of MG before the passage is written.

| | what it would commit the paper to | status |
|---|---|---|
| **Discrete TAG** | a positively-affixed, readable source marker on the state | **Falsified in the tested case.** Dijkstra & Fleming 2023 set the intention/self-generation account against strength and found for strength; congruent imagery *increased* the likelihood of judging a stimulus real, the opposite of a tag-and-discount mechanism. Claiming this earns a desk-level reviewer objection from the field's own primary literature. |
| **Graded THRESHOLD** | reality judgement = a criterion on combined signal strength | **Well supported** (2023 behavioural + computational; 2025 fusiform fMRI). **But it is the field's current default, so FMT adds nothing by asserting it** — and it is a *rival* to the architectural story, not a version of it. |
| **Requirement + ordering prediction** | source distinguishability must not be destroyed; and under perturbation **source attribution degrades before phenomenal experience does** | **The only defensible ground, and it is FMT's own.** Not occupied by anyone; instantiated nightly in dreaming; same discriminating shape as `R6`. |

**Three further primary results sharpen this, and two of them are new to the project:**

1. **The diagnostic property exists but is not consulted.** Imagery lacks the bottom-up drive that perception
   has (Dijkstra et al. 2017, *Sci Rep*; review Dijkstra, Bosch & van Gerven 2019 — *"the most prominent
   difference is the absence of bottom-up processing during imagery"*). Yet the reality judgement reads
   **combined** fusiform strength (Dijkstra et al. 2025). ⇒ **The system is not blind for want of a signal;
   it declines to use the signal it has.** This is the strongest available support for "the reality tag is a
   property of the READER, not of the signal" (pattern #34's conclusion) — and it upgrades that conclusion
   from an inference over four dissociations to a directly measured mismatch.
2. **⭐ NEW — metacognition does not rescue it, and the failure has a two-level structure.** Dijkstra, Mazor
   & Fleming (2024, *J Vis*, open access): confidence criteria **move in tandem** with the decision
   criterion, so reality monitoring fails at the metacognitive level too and the failure has a *perceptual
   rather than decisional* origin. **But offline, task-level insight was present and was UNCORRELATED with
   local trial-level insight.** ⇒ A directly measured dissociation between a **local** reality judgement
   (threshold-governed, fails) and a **global** cognitive insight (intact). That is the two-layer
   architecture MG's ordering claim requires, measured in waking humans, and the project did not have it.
   It is also the exact structure of lucid dreaming, which the authors themselves name in the abstract.
3. **⭐ NEW — a separate, better-evidenced filter exists, but it answers a different question.** Schnider's
   **orbitofrontal reality filtering**: *"an on-line mechanism which verifies whether upcoming thoughts
   relate to ongoing reality or not"* — a frontal positivity at **200–300 ms**, sourced to posterior medial
   OFC (area 13), with lesion evidence in confabulation/disorientation and causal tDCS modulation. **⚠ Read
   the scope carefully before anyone reaches for it: it distinguishes memories that pertain to NOW from
   memories that do not — temporal currency — NOT internally- from externally-generated content.** In
   Schnider's paradigm both classes are externally presented; the difference is temporal context. Schnider
   **explicitly** separates this from Johnson-style source monitoring, on both task logic and anatomy
   (posterior medial OFC vs DLPFC). ⇒ **Relevant to pattern #34's level-1 "currency" formulation, and it
   partially mechanises it; irrelevant to the recall-versus-perception contrast.** Do not let the two blur.

## 5. WHAT THE PAPER CAN HONESTLY SAY

It survives in the **requirement + prediction** form only. Draft below.

### 5a. Draft passage (300 words, paper register)

> **Source distinguishability as a design constraint.** Forking the self-model raises a question the
> preceding derivation does not answer. A forked run and a live one are computed by the same models in the
> same format; nothing stated so far makes the two discernible from within. Without such a distinction,
> recall is not memory but hallucination, and redirecting the modelling resource onto another agent would be
> experienced as *being* them rather than modelling them.
>
> The theory's requirement is negative rather than positive. It does not posit a marker affixed to
> internally generated states — an account the source-monitoring literature has set itself against since
> Johnson, Hashtroudi, and Lindsay (1993), for whom source is attributed by decision processes at retrieval
> rather than read off a tag. It requires only that composing the explicit
> models must not *destroy* the distinguishability the source pathways already carry. Any stage that
> normalises or homogenises its inputs deletes that information silently, and no later stage recovers it.
>
> This is a constraint, not a mechanism claim — a distinction that matters, because the empirical literature
> supplies no mechanism. Imagery and perception differ in more than content — perception
> carries bottom-up drive that imagery lacks (Dijkstra et al., 2017) — yet reality judgements do not appear
> to consult that difference, resting instead on the combined strength of sensory activity, whether
> internally or externally generated (Dijkstra & Fleming, 2023; Dijkstra et al., 2025). Reviewing the
> candidate cues, Dijkstra, Kok, and Fleming (2022) conclude that no single factor unambiguously signals an
> experience's origin.
>
> The theory therefore predicts an ordering rather than a mechanism: under perturbation of the substrate or
> of communication between its levels, **source attribution should degrade before phenomenal
> experience does**. Dreaming is the everyday instance — experience fully present, source attribution
> absent — and lucid dreaming the recovery case.
