2. NEW PROBLEMS INTRODUCED BY THE REVISION

N1 (must fix — misrepresentation of Searle, created by the revision). §2.1: "He would therefore reject any demand that membership be decidable from outside, which is condition C3 below. The disagreement with Searle is located there." Table row: "Searle rejects C3 in principle (ontological subjectivity)". Verified text says the opposite. Searle 1998 p.381: "the fact that the consciousness of others is 'unobservable' does not by itself prevent us from getting a scientific account of consciousness. Electrons, black holes and the Big Bang are not observable by anybody, but that does not prevent their scientific investigation." p.382: "the requirement that science be objective does not prevent us from getting an epistemically objective science of a domain that is ontologically subjective." The current C3 permits theory and asks only for third-person decidability-with-a-stated-theory, which Searle plainly accepts. Worse for the paper, it is missing a gift: p.381, immediately after the definition: "We do not at present know how far down the phylogenetic scale it goes, and until we get an adequate scientific account of consciousness it is not useful to worry about whether, e.g. snails are conscious." That is the paper's thesis in Searle's own words — the contested case waits on the theory.
Fix (§2.1, replace the two sentences): "Searle also holds that conscious states are 'ontologically subjective': they exist only as experienced by a subject. He insists that this does not prevent 'an epistemically objective science of a domain that is ontologically subjective' (Searle, 1998, p. 382), so he does not reject C3. He goes further than the present paper requires: immediately after giving his definition he writes that 'until we get an adequate scientific account of consciousness it is not useful to worry about whether, e.g. snails are conscious' (p. 381). The contested case is, on his account too, decided by the theory and not by the common-sense definition. The disagreement with Searle is only over whether the cases he treats as uncontested (humans and 'the higher mammals', p. 381) are reached by the definition or by an analogy that already carries a theory."
Fix (Table 1, Searle C3 cell): "Sleep and wake are measurable but mark arousal; no stated procedure for other systems, which Searle defers to 'an adequate scientific account'". Also drop or re-point §5.4's "it is the condition the realist may decline (Section 2.2)" so it no longer reads as a reference to Searle; Chalmers is the realist who might decline it, and even he supplies psychophysical laws.

N2 (internal inconsistency — new). §2.1 defines sense (iii) as "in terms independent of the description that picked it out", then the Kripke paragraph says "If it holds, the characterisation defines in sense (iii)". On the paper's own definition it cannot: the characterisation IS the description that picked it out. Fix: amend (iii) to "A *theoretical identification* states what the phenomenon essentially is; in the natural-kind cases these terms are independent of the description that picked it out ('water is H₂O')." Then the Kripke sentence reads: "If it holds, the characterisation already states the essence, so there is no further identification of kind (iii) to be had; but the characterisation can be applied directly only by a subject to its own states..."

N3 (inconsistency with the fixed sense of "settled" — new). §3.4: "If he is right, the concept is primitive and, as a primitive, settled." The paper has just stipulated (§2.1) that "settled" means shared AND decisive for the investigated cases, and spends G1/G2 insisting the sense be kept fixed; here it slips back to "settled = needs no analysis". Fix: "If he is right, the concept is primitive and needs no further analysis. It is not thereby settled in the sense of Section 2.1: what an experimenter facing a contested case then needs is the psychophysical laws..."

N4 (empirical over-claim now load-bearing). The reframed thesis rests on "that theory is rarely stated" (§1, §8 "That theory is usually not stated"). No evidence is given, and the most visible recent extrapolations do state it: Butlin et al. 2023 (arXiv 2308.08708, indicator properties derived from named theories for AI), Birch 2024 *The Edge of Sentience* (explicit precautionary premises), Bayne et al. 2024 (already cited; explicit about validation and theory-dependence), Casali et al. 2013 PCI (explicit IIT-derived index). A referee will list these. Fix: replace "rarely stated" with "stated in a few recent programmes and absent from most practice", cite Butlin et al. 2023 and Casali et al. 2013 as the practice the paper recommends, and reword §8: "That theory is stated in a handful of recent programmes (Casali et al., 2013; Bayne et al., 2024; Butlin et al., 2023) and left implicit in most clinical, developmental and comparative claims." This turns the paper from a complaint into a standard that existing work can be measured against — and the C-conditions become the rubric for those programmes.

N5 (minor, new). Table 1 Nagel C3 cell: "extended to other organisms by inference from reporters" — the bat is not a reporter; write "by inference from similarity to reporters" (the §6 wording).
N6 (minor). §4 "Block, Searle and Chalmers also hold that this is no defect" — Schwitzgebel holds it too (innocence as the point); add him or say "and Schwitzgebel presents it as the point".
N7 (minor, citation). Irvine's Springer monograph is dated 2013 by the publisher (© 2013; Francken et al. also cite 2013); manuscript has 2012. Check and align.
N8 (minor). Kripke 1980 cited as "Lecture III" only; JCS/Synthese will want the page (p. 152 in the 1980 Harvard edition — verify against the copy used).
N9 (minor). Abstract sentence "I distinguish four senses of 'definition' and define 'settled' as shared and decisive..." — a referee may read the stipulation as built to make the thesis true. One clause pre-empts it: "...decisive for the cases a science investigates, which is the work the field's own tests programme asks a definition to do (Bayne et al., 2024)."

3. VERDICT

JCS: yes, after N1–N3 (one afternoon's edits). The headline objections of round one are met, the criteria are now realist-neutral, the scholarship on Block, Chalmers, Kleiner, Schwitzgebel, Frankish and Francken is accurate as checked, the MoC7 material is footnoted and §7.3 is gone. The remaining risk is the Searle misreading (N1), which a referee who opens the 1998 paper will catch on p.381–382 and which costs credibility out of proportion to its size.
Synthese: borderline — probably major revision rather than reject. The paper is now sound but modest; Synthese referees will ask what is philosophically new beyond Carnapian explication plus Francken's recommendation, and N4 is where the answer has to live: a demonstration that the C6–C9 rubric discriminates among the stated-theory extrapolations that already exist (PCI, Butlin et al., Bayne et al.). Without that the paper is a well-argued position piece; with it, it is a contribution.
Single most valuable remaining change: N4 — replace "rarely stated" with named instances where the theory is stated, and run C6–C9 over one of them (Casali et al. 2013 PCI is the cleanest: identity premise from IIT, agreement on uncontested cases by construction, no meta-problem test, creature-level). That converts the conclusion from an exhortation into a measurable standard and also closes the Searle correction (N1) into an asset: Searle, Crick & Koch and Francken all say the theory decides the contested cases; the paper shows what stating it requires.
