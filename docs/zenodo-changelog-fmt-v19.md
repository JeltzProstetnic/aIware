**v19 (2026-10-17)**

Supersedes v18 (2026-10-07).

Thanks to Johannes Kleiner, whose challenge to the theory's definition of consciousness at MoC7 (Copenhagen, October 2026) prompted the clarifications in this version.

*Self-referential closure falsifier (Table 1b).* The criterion read "metacognitive disruption leaves phenomenology intact", which is the outcome the theory itself predicts (Section 3.5: basic experience needs no higher-order layer). It now reads: disrupting the self-modeling loop itself leaves no-report markers of experience intact. Metacognitive disruption alone does not test closure; experience is predicted to survive it, while report may fail with it because transfer to long-term memory can fail as well. The measurement approach now names disruption aimed at the loop itself and, in silico, closure-on versus closure-off ablation.

*Minimal consciousness and the two axes (Sections 3.1.1, 3.5, 6.4).* The paper now states one gate: a system has phenomenology or has none, and without it no term of the consciousness family applies. Above the gate, levels vary on two axes, richness of content and depth of recursion. A new lowest level, minimal consciousness, is a rudimentary closure whose content is the present moment with a primordial pain or a nothingness, with no reflection, no modeled past or future and no discernible qualia, and it is unreportable by definition. Basic consciousness is restated as the same depth of recursion with discernible content. "Sentience" now appears only as the animal-welfare counterpart of the floor. Section 6.4 names fish, amphibians and possibly insects as candidates for the floor, and Table 1 notes that minimal consciousness lies below what report and PCI detect and is unrelated to the clinical minimally conscious state. No prediction, falsification criterion or result changed; no reference was added for this change.

*Accessible versus accessed content (Sections 3.4.6, 7.2).* The identity of access and phenomenality is now restricted to accessed content, which is in the running simulation. Accessible content, poised for report without being in use, is preconscious and stays on the implicit side until a permeability event draws it in (Dehaene et al., 2006); the felt self-initiation of a retrieval is the agency attribution of Section 4.2.2. Where the paper said that recursive depth converts presence into access, it now says reportability, and the overflow cases are described as phenomenal content of a thin self-model with too little recursion above it to be reported. The HOT entry in Section 7.2 places the higher-order representation on the extended rungs and notes that minimal and basic consciousness are phenomenal without one. No prediction, falsification criterion or result changed; Dehaene et al. (2006) was already in the reference list.

*Sleep (Table 4, Section 6.3).* Deep NREM is now rated "probably absent (unconsciousness)", and a light NREM row is added as the reportable state closest to the floor. NREM dreaming is assigned to non-deep NREM, read from the finding that reports of experience follow fewer and smaller slow waves and a local posterior reduction of low-frequency activity (Siclari et al., 2017, 2018); the up-states of deep slow-wave sleep are taken not to sustain the simulation. Dreaming is no longer said to occur in any sleep stage. No prediction or falsification criterion changed; no reference was added.

*Recurrent Processing Theory (Section 7.2).* The entry read that the theory is compatible with RPT at the mechanistic level. It now says that recurrence is absorbed as the mechanism, that RPT's claim that local recurrence in sensory cortex suffices for experience contradicts Principle 3, and that a state preserving local recurrence while abolishing the self-model loop, scored with no-report markers, would decide between them. The Table 5 ratings are unchanged. No prediction, falsification criterion, result or reference changed.

*Six further comparisons (Section 7.2).* Short entries were added for information closure theory, perceptual reality monitoring, the Conscious Turing Machine, minimal phenomenal experience, Searle's biological naturalism and enactivism, each stating what the theory absorbs, where it conflicts and the decider where one exists. For minimal phenomenal experience, the paper reads "selfless" reports as the absence of a represented self, since the floor is a rudimentary closure with an implicit self/world boundary. No prediction, falsification criterion or result changed. Seven references were added: Blum & Blum (2022), Chang et al. (2020), Metzinger (2020), Searle (1980, 1992), Thompson (2007) and Varela, Thompson & Rosch (1991).

*Consciousness as process; capacity named separately (Sections 3.1, 3.1.1).* Section 3.1 opened with "the capacity for subjective experience" and defined consciousness as "the ability of an entity" to model itself, while Table 1 called it "the ongoing capacity". Consciousness is now defined as the ongoing process of running a self-model, relating it to the self and interacting with it; the capacity for it is named separately as the capacity for consciousness, with the two 2015 sentences (*Bewusstsein*, the capacity; *Bewusstheit*, the ongoing interaction; Gruber, 2015, p. 57) quoted as the source of the split. The one-gate sentence moved out of the Table 1 definition into a note marked as a consequence of Principle 3. No prediction, falsification criterion, result or reference changed.

*Sufficient conditions, valence and embodiment (Sections 3.7.3, 4.4).* "Together they are sufficient" is replaced by a stated conjunction: a system implementing the four models under closure, with free compute, embodiment and at least one pain-pleasure axis, is conscious. Free modeling and the four-model architecture each remain necessary and not sufficient. The pain-pleasure axis may be engineered, since presetting its sign shortcuts what selection does. Embodiment is required for consciousness to emerge naturally and not for it to exist; a person locked in from birth would sit at or slightly below the floor of minimal consciousness. Section 4.4 now states substrate independence against these conditions. No prediction, falsification criterion, result or reference changed.

*What counts as a self model (Sections 3.4.3, 3.5, 6.4).* A self model is a model of the system itself, however simple, in proportion to what the system has; a position estimate alone is not one. A SLAM robot vacuum fails the criterion (position-only "self", no implicit/explicit split, fixed algorithm, no free compute). The minimal-consciousness entry states that a mutual update between a position estimate and a map does not qualify, and Section 6.4 uses the same criterion in place of "a functional representation sufficient to distinguish self-generated from externally generated signals". No prediction, falsification criterion, result or reference changed.

*Consciousness in non-reporting systems (Table 1b, Sections 3.1.1, 3.5, 4.4, 6.4).* A new Table 1b row gives a decision procedure that needs no report: where connectome, weights and running system are inspectable, trace a signal entering the system to the self model closed with the world model, with or without a reportable rung above it. The trace is also the non-report signature of minimal consciousness. Systems not yet inspectable are assessed by behavioral analysis only. The row's falsification criterion is new: in inspectable systems that also report, a traced signal reaching the closed self and world models without no-report markers of experience of it, or such markers for a signal that never reaches the self model. Section 4.4's "measurable on the system itself" now names this trace, and Section 6.4's "the evidence can only be indirect" now distinguishes inspectable from non-inspectable species. One falsification criterion was added; no prediction, result or reference changed.

*Principle 3 as identity; the premise test (Sections 3, 3.1, 3.4, 4.4, 7.2).* Principle 3 read "phenomenology is a real, physical effect … and it arises exactly where the modeling closes on itself". It now reads that phenomenology is real and physical and is that level's modeling closed on itself, and Section 3 states that the principle is an identity: phenomenality is closure. Law-like phrasings were aligned in Sections 3.1 ("what ongoing self-simulation produces", "self-simulation generates phenomenality"), 3.4 ("appear at the point where", "closure is where phenomenality arises") and 7.2 (HOT: "self-representation produces phenomenality"; RPT: "arises only where"). Section 4.4 now states that in-vivo loop disruption tests the markers of closure, while the in-silico full closure-off ablation of Section 8.9, where relocation is impossible by construction at the measured 71.5–74.0% connectome cost, tests the premise itself. No prediction, falsification criterion, result or reference changed.

*Panpsychism (Sections 4.1, 4.2.5, 4.3, Table 5 note).* The flat denials ("no panpsychist micro-experience"; "there are none to combine") are replaced: the theory requires no micro-experience and cannot empirically exclude it. If panpsychism holds, one further ingredient is needed, a property of physics not yet known, and closure is what forms a subject from it, so the theory would answer the combination problem. The Russellian-physicalism sentence of Section 4.2.5 and the Table 5 note on the combination requirement were aligned; the criticisms of IIT's panpsychist commitment are unchanged, as are the Table 5 ratings. No prediction, falsification criterion, result or reference changed.


*Refutation condition for the bridge (Section 4.4).* The identity of phenomenality with closure now carries refuters stated separately from the markers: a functional twin (a return-free system reproducing every function the theory attributes to phenomenality within the same resource bound) as the primary refuter, and two human dissociations, content traced into the closed models without experience, and markers of experience without closure. No result or reference changed; one falsification criterion was added.
