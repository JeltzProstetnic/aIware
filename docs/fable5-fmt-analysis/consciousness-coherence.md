# FMT — Internal Logical Coherence Review

*Reviewer lens: argumentative soundness of the core theory. Candid, not deferential.*

The manuscript is unusually self-aware: it flags its own equivocation risks (three senses of "virtual," the "model kind" minimum reading, anthropomorphic projection), states falsification criteria, and concedes provenance and shared credit honestly. This raises its internal-coherence floor above most consciousness papers. But the core inferential chain — self-modeling → phenomenality, and "category error" → dissolution of the Hard Problem — does not hold as argued. The theory repeatedly presents a *posit* as a *discovery* and presents success on the Meta-Problem as if it discharged the Hard Problem.

## 1. The 2×2 and "model kind"

The taxonomy slides between three incompatible senses of "model." Table 1 defines a model functionally (write access, read access, boundary recognition). But the implicit models are then glossed as *stored structure* ("the substrate's total accumulated knowledge... stored in synaptic weights"), while the explicit models are *running processes* ("generated electrochemical activity patterns"). So one word spans (a) a functional reference system, (b) a static structure, and (c) a transient process — and the structure/process gap is precisely the real/virtual divide the theory treats as ontologically fundamental elsewhere. "Model kind" therefore papers over the very distinction the theory's metaphysics depends on.

Worse, the theory concedes (3.2, "principled minimum") that real neural models "do not respect the world/self boundary — they blend across it" and that the brain runs "an effectively uncountable number" of them. Once that is admitted, the 2×2 is a theorist's idealization imposed on a continuum, not a discovered architecture. The "four is the floor" defense ("Why four, not three?") establishes only a *generative* dependency (an explicit model needs an implicit source) — it does not establish the *scope* axis. The argument that the implicit level needs *both* world and self reduces to one functional requirement (distinguishing self-generated from external signals), which is a single discriminative operation, not two separate "models." The scope axis is thus under-motivated, and most of the theory's explanatory work (Section 5) never uses it.

Note also that being a "model" is explicitly insufficient for consciousness (IWM/ISM are models and non-conscious). The load-bearing notions are "explicit/generated," "relate to," and "interact with" — i.e., self-referential closure. The 2×2 is largely decorative relative to that core.

## 2. Self-referential closure: posited, not derived — and partly question-begging

This is the crux, and the theory is candid that it is a "foundational commitment... not deducible from physics alone." That candor is the correct verdict: the inference from self-modeling to phenomenality is *asserted*, not valid.

The equivocation is between *representational* self-reference (the system contains a token referring to the system) and *phenomenal* inside-perspective (there being something it is like). The decisive sentences — "the self-model... generates an ongoing first-person perspective... that exists only from within the closed loop" — simply install "first-person perspective" as an output of closure. But a first-person perspective *is* the explanandum. What the argument actually demonstrates is a structural/information-theoretic asymmetry (in a closed loop, the system's self-description is part of the system, so no external description occupies it). That is real but it is an *epistemic/architectural* fact. Relabeling it "a perspective with phenomenal character" is the smuggle: the structural asymmetry is renamed as subjectivity.

Two pressures expose this. (i) Closure is cheap: an ordinary reflective interpreter maintaining a model of its own execution state achieves "a model of the modeling." The theory must either bite the bullet (it is conscious — which it refuses) or offload exclusion onto criticality and four-model structure — in which case closure does *not* "explain why a class of computations has an inside," contradicting its stated role. The explanatory burden shuttles between closure (too cheap to exclude) and criticality (a dynamical regime with no evident tie to subjectivity), and neither bears it alone. (ii) The genuinely successful part of the closure story — the ESM cannot see past the EWM to its own substrate ("observability constraint") — explains *opacity*, i.e., the **Meta-Problem**. The text then treats this as progress on the **Hard Problem**. Throughout, Meta-Problem success is cashed as Hard-Problem payment.

## 3. Virtual qualia: redescription, not dissolution

The spreadsheet analogy defeats itself. "Sum of column B" is incoherent in transistor vocabulary, yes — but we possess a *transparent functional definition* of "sum," so there is no residual. The paper admits this: "We know why a spreadsheet sum is a sum — we wrote the program, the mapping is transparent." The entire force of the Hard Problem is that qualia have *no* such transparent functional cash-out — that is the explanatory gap. The analogy therefore imports exactly the feature whose absence constitutes the problem, begging the question against the qualia realist the theory says it respects.

The "category error / level confusion" reframing is a relocation, not an answer. Sophisticated formulations of the Hard Problem never claimed qualia are properties of single neurons; they locate the explanandum at the level of the conscious system. Telling the inquirer "look at Level 5, not Level 2" does not address "why is *any* level — substrate or computational — accompanied by experience rather than none?" Moving qualia from Level 2 to Level 5 and declaring them "not mysterious but constitutive" renames the mystery. Declaring a property constitutive is not explaining its presence. This is the clearest case of the conclusion outrunning the premises.

The temporal-echo mechanism (3.4.4) supplies real *structure* (why flow, why modality differences) but each step explains the *organization* of experience while presupposing *that* there is experience to organize. The "information singularity" black-hole analogy is evocative metaphor doing no argumentative work, and it risks reifying the virtual/physical boundary into something more than the supervenience thesis licenses.

## 4. The central unresolved knot: (A) deflationism vs (B) realism

The theory oscillates between two incompatible self-presentations and needs both:

- **(A) Deflationary:** full supervenience, "nothing missing from the physical picture," qualia = how the process registers itself, Hard Problem = level confusion. Respectable, but then "category error" is the whole theory and phenomenal realism is hollow.
- **(B) Realist:** "reading the code is not having the experience"; the property "exists only in the running process and cannot be extracted from any description, however complete"; accessible "only by instantiation."

(B) is doing genuine anti-functionalist work — and it is either trivial or dualist. If "instantiation exceeds description" means only that running a program differs from describing it, that is true but yields no phenomenal realism. If it means there is a fact (the experience) not fixed by the complete physical description, that *is* property dualism / strong emergence — which the theory spends Section 4.3 denying. You cannot have full metaphysical supervenience (the description fixes everything) and an experience that "cannot be extracted from any description." The theory wants (A)'s physicalist respectability and (B)'s anti-reductive payoff simultaneously. This contradiction sits at the foundation.

This propagates to causation. The "dual evaluation architecture" holds that qualia "lack independent causal power" yet are *constitutive* of the simulation that is "the substrate's own evaluation mechanism" with feedback that reshapes the implicit models. Constitutive-of-a-causally-efficacious-process and causally-inert-qua-quale cannot both hold; the evolutionary argument needs the former, the epiphenomenalism-avoidance needs to deny the latter. Acknowledging the theory "draws fire from both sides" (10.3) labels the tension without resolving it.

The zombie rebuttal (4.2.4) is openly circular: "to conceive a zombie is to conceive a self-referential loop that is not self-referential — a contradiction" holds *only if* phenomenality is identical to closure, which is the unproven foundational commitment. Against a dualist who denies that identity, closure-without-phenomenality is not contradictory. The rebuttal presupposes its conclusion.

## 5. Explanatory work vs redescription

The genuine explanatory yield (anesthesia, psychedelics, anosognosia, split-brain, dreams) flows almost entirely from **two** ideas: criticality (shared with the whole criticality program — the theory concedes this) and the single implicit/explicit (substrate/simulation) **permeability** axis. The *self/world* axis and the four-fold structure do little here; permeability needs only one axis. So the four-model apparatus is largely redescriptive; the working engine is essentially Metzinger/Seth self-modeling + the criticality literature, as the comparative section half-concedes (REBUS "derivable," PP "overlaps," convergence "shared").

The criticality *necessity* argument is a non sequitur: "self-referential simulation is universal computation, and universal computation requires Class 4 dynamics." Turing-universality is a computability property satisfied by orderly digital hardware nowhere near dynamical criticality (a laptop; Rule 110 is a fixed rule, not a tuned edge-of-chaos system). The theory conflates "capable of universal computation" with "operating at self-organized criticality." Its own finite-size concession (Game of Life is "technically Class 2" but behaviorally Class 4) dissolves the necessity claim rather than saving it, and it makes the "two independent thresholds" (criticality + architecture) non-independent.

Of the four "novel" predictions, only DID self-referential gradient (P3) is both distinctive and cleanly operationalized; P2 is conceded partly derivable from PP, P4 depends on non-existent measurement tech and competes with gamma-power accounts, and P1's derivation (why a *global* permeability rise should compensate a *local*, same-channel block) is loose with post-hoc boundary conditions. The architecture does some predictive work — less than claimed, and mostly via permeability+criticality rather than the 2×2.

## Verdict

Internally, FMT is a sophisticated, honestly-hedged self-modeling theory whose **Meta-Problem** account is well-integrated and whose implicit/explicit dissociation is a real structural commitment. But its signature claims fail on their own terms: self-referential closure is posited (and partly question-begging) as the source of phenomenality; the "virtual qualia / category error" move redescribes and relocates the Hard Problem rather than dissolving it; and the theory cannot simultaneously hold full supervenience and "experience cannot be extracted from any description." The framing (abstract: "dissolves," "category error") consistently overstates what the fine print concedes ("transformed, not dissolved"; "foundational commitment"; "rests on judgment"). The honest in-text caveats are correct; the headline claims are not licensed by them.
