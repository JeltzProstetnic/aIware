## Synthesis

**Which theory has the widest structured-content (self-disorder) reach?** Two theories, not one: **FMT and PP** are the only frameworks that reach across the structured-content rows. FMT carries specific accounts of anosognosia, DID, ego-dissolution, blindsight, split-brain, conversion disorder, plus Anton's/Cotard's (counting FULLs, FMT leads on raw breadth, ~9 FULL cells). But this lead is narrower than it looks, because PP matches FMT's breadth with a *more mechanistically specified* single principle — precision-weighting of prediction error — and **beats FMT outright on three syndromes**: schizophrenia (Fletcher-Frith / Frith's efference-copy account is the field-standard; FMT relegates schizophrenia to an *open question*), functional/conversion disorder (Edwards-Friston is the canonical account; FMT's "EWM models a deficit" is the same idea in different vocabulary), and depersonalization (Seth's interoceptive-inference account is the leading one; FMT's ESM/EWM-split framing is a redescription). HOT and AST have narrower but genuine self-disorder accounts — both own anosognosia, AST owns neglect (an attention disorder by definition), HOT owns the metacognitive face of source-monitoring and lucidity.

**Which owns the level axis, and is there a deployed instrument?** **IIT owns the level axis, and uniquely holds a validated clinical instrument: the Perturbational Complexity Index** (Casali 2013; Casarotto 2016; ~95% sensitivity / ~91% specificity across sleep, anesthesia, and disorders of consciousness, in bedside use). GNW is also strong on level via ignition and command-following paradigms. FMT's level account is genuinely good — the propofol-vs-ketamine dissociation falls out cleanly from "subcritical push vs. preserved-criticality-with-disconnected-input," which not every theory handles — but **FMT has no instrument of its own; it explicitly borrows PCI as a proxy** while conceding PCI is not constitutive for it. On the level axis FMT rents IIT's tool.

**Does any single rival span BOTH axes with ONE mechanism, the way FMT claims to?** Yes — and this is the finding that most threatens FMT's distinctiveness. **PP does.** Precision-weighting reaches the structured-content axis (psychedelics, DPDR, schizophrenia, FND, neglect, anosognosia) *and* the level axis (loss of hierarchical message-passing under anesthesia/DOC). IIT, by contrast, spans the level axis superbly but its content reach is thin and gestural (it has the qualia-space formalism in principle but no deployed account of anosognosia, DID, or source-monitoring). So the dual-axis, single-mechanism property FMT advertises is **not unique**: PP already has it, with deeper neural specification and a longer clinical track record. FMT and PP are the two genuine broad-spectrum theories; the others are specialists.

**FMT's honest gaps.**
- *No validated clinical instrument.* The single largest deficit versus IIT. FMT's criticality signatures (σ, DFA α, avalanche scaling) are measurable but not packaged into a deployed diagnostic; the one instrument it cites (PCI) belongs to a rival program.
- *Post-hoc redescription vs. genuine prediction.* Much of FMT's breadth is re-labeling. "Anosognosia = local permeability decrease," "DPDR = ESM/EWM dissociation," "conversion disorder = EWM models a deficit the substrate lacks," and split-brain confabulation are *consistency* claims that rename phenomena in FMT vocabulary without adding mechanism a clinician could not already state. They are architecturally coherent but not load-bearing.
- *The genuinely predictive core is small but real.* Three claims are risky, novel, and not redescriptions: **Prediction 1** (sub-threshold psychedelics should partially lift anosognosia by compensating a local block with a global permeability rise) — a cross-domain surprise that *no rival generates* and that is testable today with safety-profiled psilocybin; **Prediction 2** (ego-dissolution content tracks the dominant sensory channel, modality-specifically — discriminable from PP's general prior-relaxation); **Prediction 3** (DID alter differences follow a self-referential representational gradient — discriminating the neural-architecture model from the sociocognitive role-play model). Plus the **ESM/EWM independent-disruptability double dissociation** (Sec. 9), which is the sharpest test separating FMT from PP's unified generative model.
- *Where rivals simply win:* schizophrenia and FND (PP), DPDR (PP), the DOC level instrument (IIT/PCI), neglect (AST), and the metacognitive reading of anosognosia/source-monitoring (HOT). FMT is not best-in-class on any of these.

**Bottom line.** As it currently stands, FMT's clinical breadth reads **closer to an "explains-everything" liability than to a defensible differentiator** — because most of the breadth is post-hoc redescription, because PP already achieves comparable both-axis reach with a more specified mechanism and beats FMT on several named syndromes, and because FMT owns no clinical instrument while IIT does. What converts the breadth from liability to asset is *not* the number of cells FMT can fill — it is the **handful of cross-cell predictions that connect syndromes no other theory connects**. Psychedelics × anosognosia (Prediction 1) is the standout: it ties psychopharmacology to a neurological deficit through one mechanism, is falsifiable, and is uniquely FMT's. If that prediction (and the ESM/EWM double dissociation) survives an actual trial, FMT's breadth becomes a genuine differentiator — a single architecture earning its keep across domains. Until then, the breadth is a coverage table, and coverage is cheap. The defensible core is three predictions and one dissociation test; the rest is vocabulary.
