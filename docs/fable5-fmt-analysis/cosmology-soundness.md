# SB-HC4A — Soundness and Internal Consistency Review

**Reviewer lens:** internal soundness only. Novelty is assessed elsewhere.
**Manuscript:** `paper/cosmology/sb-hc4a.md` (Gruber, "Emergent Spacetime from Self-Referential Computation")

---

## 1. What the paper actually claims

Stripped of framing, SB-HC4A asserts: (1) dynamical systems fall into five classes (static, periodic, fractal, complex/edge-of-chaos, genuinely random), refining Wolfram; (2) by elimination, the universe's fundamental dynamics are Class 4; (3) all "singularities" — Planck regime, particle interiors, black hole horizons, cosmological horizons, temporal endpoints — are *one* phenomenon, the information-impermeable boundary of the computational domain, an identity argued via Leibniz's Identity of Indiscernibles (PII); (4) the universe is a holographic Class 4 automaton whose boundary encodes its interior, closed under a fixed point Φ(U) = U; (5) every cosmological endgame drives the domain to Bekenstein saturation and triggers cyclic (or, for the Big Rip, branching) renewal, possibly with CPT alternation; (6) particles are Planck-scale singularity boundaries ("computational atoms"), and entanglement is the James-Stein inadmissibility of independent state descriptions on a shared substrate; (7) self-modeling minds instantiate the same architecture locally.

To its credit, the paper labels itself speculative and enumerates six weak points, including a self-undermining epistemological one. That honesty is real and unusual. It does not, however, immunize the argument chain, which contains errors and internal contradictions beyond the flagged weak points.

## 2. Internal consistency — specific failures

**(a) The taxonomy's key inference is a non-sequitur.** §2.3 argues correctly (Kolmogorov/Chaitin) that a deterministic CA's output is compressible. But the step "the only way to generate infinite output from finite information is to reuse structure at different scales — exact reuse is periodicity, patterned reuse is fractal" is a false trichotomy. Pseudorandom deterministic sequences (Rule 30) are neither periodic nor self-similar in any geometric or statistical sense; calling them "high-complexity fractals" redefines "fractal" as "compressible," which empties Class 3 of its distinguishing content. The five-class gradient, the paper's foundation, rests on this equivocation.

**(b) The elimination argument is not exhaustive over the relevant possibility space.** Class 5 is defined as dynamics from inexpressible rules. But "expressible stochastic law" is a third option the taxonomy has no slot for: standard quantum mechanics has compactly expressible *probabilistic* laws whose outcomes are algorithmically incompressible. Such a universe produces Class-5-looking output while physics works perfectly well — directly refuting the claim that genuine randomness makes physics "fundamentally impossible." The elimination of Class 5 conflates *lawless* with *stochastic-but-lawful*. Since the whole architecture follows from Proposition 1, this is a load-bearing crack. (The 't Hooft determinism the paper inherits is a possible escape, but it is assumed, not argued, and it drags in the superdeterminism/Bell problem — see (f).)

**(c) Category error: rule class vs. operating point.** Wolfram classes are properties of *rules*; criticality (σ ≈ 1, λ_max ≈ 0) is a property of a *state/regime* of a driven system. §3.2 and §6.2 slide between "the universe's rule is Class 4" and "the universe operates at criticality" as if these were the same claim. SOC (Bak) is a mechanism by which certain driven dissipative systems sit near a critical point; it is not evidence about the universality class of the universe's update rule. The paper partially concedes this in §9.2 but continues to use the conflation everywhere else.

**(d) PII is used inconsistently — and in opposite directions.** §4.1 invokes PII to claim that what is beyond the horizon is "indistinguishable from nonexistence." §5.7 asserts that beyond the horizon is "more of the same universe — quarks, protons, stars, galaxies." These cannot both be held: the first says causal inaccessibility licenses ontological erasure; the second confidently populates the inaccessible region. The cyclic cosmology (§5.4) likewise depends on information *beyond* the boundary seeding the next cycle — information §4.1's own principle declared nonexistent. This is the sharpest internal contradiction in the paper.

Independently, the PII move in §5.2/Step 4 is a misapplication. Leibniz's principle requires sharing *all* properties; the paper restricts to interior properties and reclassifies mass, charge, and angular momentum as "boundary presentation." But by any operational standard those are properties of the singularities, and they differ. More fundamentally, "no observer can in principle distinguish X and Y from here" yields *epistemic* indiscernibility, not identity — the inference is verificationist, not Leibnizian. Reversing the burden of proof ("the critic must say what distinguishes them") does not repair this: GR already distinguishes them structurally — a black hole event horizon is a global, teleological null surface; a cosmological event horizon is *observer-dependent* (every observer has a different one); the Big Bang is a spacelike curvature singularity and not a horizon at all. These are mathematically distinct objects, discernible in the theory we actually use.

**(e) §5.2 and §5.3 use the same fact in opposite ways.** §5.2 dissolves singularity diversity by noting the horizon "surface is a coordinate artifact" (true: the infaller crosses in finite proper time). §5.3 then grounds "asymptotic unreachability" in exactly the exterior coordinate-time asymptotics just dismissed as artifactual. One cannot have it both ways. Worse, §5.3 contains a factual error: "in conformal time, the Big Bang maps to t = −∞" is false in standard cosmology — for radiation domination (a ∝ t^1/2), conformal time *converges* at t → 0; the finiteness of conformal lookback is precisely the horizon problem. The claim holds only in special continuations (past-eternal inflation, CCC, Boyle–Turok), which the paper does not establish. The Big Crunch case is also wrong as stated: geodesic incompleteness means observers *reach* the crunch in finite proper time; nothing recedes.

**(f) The James-Stein/entanglement section is physically backwards.** §6.5 honestly flags the CAT(0) formalization gap, but the deeper problem is conceptual and fatal as stated. (i) Inadmissibility is a property of *estimators* — a statistician's procedure under a risk function — not of physical states; the entangled state is not an "estimate" of anything. (ii) More seriously: correlations induced by a shared classical prior/common cause are exactly the local-hidden-variable structure Bell's theorem *excludes* as an account of entanglement. Shrinkage toward a common mean can generate classical correlations; it cannot generate Bell-inequality violation. Citing Afik & de Nova's LHC Bell violations as *support* therefore points in the wrong direction — that result rules out the proposed mechanism's natural reading. The only escape is 't Hooft-style superdeterminism, which the paper neither invokes nor defends. The three qualitative "matches" (decoherence, semiclassical limit) are generic to any noise model and carry no evidential weight.

**(g) The electron-as-Kerr-Newman flagship undercuts the unification criterion.** §5.7 correctly reports that the Kerr-Newman solution with electron parameters is super-extremal: a *naked* ring singularity, no horizon — and at the Compton scale (~10⁻¹³ m), not the Planck scale claimed in §5.5. A naked singularity is precisely *not* an information-impermeable boundary. The paper's strongest piece of evidence for "particles are singularities" thus violates the defining property (impermeability) on which the entire unification rests. This goes unnoticed in the text. Relatedly, "particle interiors inaccessible" is empirically shaky — electron substructure is constrained to ≲10⁻¹⁹ m by scattering, which probes exactly the regime declared inaccessible.

**(h) Heat-death "saturation" has no mechanism.** §5.4 concedes dilute-gas heat death is far from the holographic bound, then bridges the gap by noting the de Sitter horizon entropy eventually dominates. But that horizon entropy is constant (~10¹²² k_B) and dominated by the horizon from early on; nothing *happens* at the moment matter entropy becomes negligible. Calling this "Bekenstein saturation... by definition" and then asserting it "triggers a phase transition" is a definitional sleight followed by pure assertion: no dynamics, no instability, no decompression mechanism is given. The same applies to the Big Rip "fragments are Bekenstein-saturated" claim — no calculation supports it.

**(i) Φ(U) = U and the §10 "derivation" are decorative.** Φ is never defined as an operator on a specified space; no fixed-point theorem applies. The axiom chain in §10.1 is a concatenation, not an entailment: A3 *asserts* Class 4's uniqueness (the conclusion of the contested §3 argument), A5 asserts holography. §10.2's "uniqueness" argument shows only that each axiom is necessary for the conclusion — it nowhere shows the architecture is the *unique* model of the axioms (dimensionality, the singularity inventory, cyclicity, and CPT alternation are all undetermined by A1–A5, as §9.6 itself half-admits). The dimensional incoherence of E = I (bits are dimensionless; Landauer's conversion is temperature-dependent; Bekenstein's involves R) is also worth flagging beyond the paper's own hedge.

## 3. Load-bearing assumptions — defensible vs. not

**Defensible (mainstream or proven):** the Bekenstein bound and holographic principle; information conservation in black hole evaporation (Page-curve era results, correctly cited); Wetterich's CA↔fermionic-QFT equivalences and the Levin–Wen/Quantum Graphity results (real, and the paper correctly limits them to existence proofs, with a genuinely good simulation-vs-native-dynamics distinction in §5.6); the g = 2 Kerr-Newman coincidence (real, per Carter).

**Unjustified or broken:** exhaustiveness of the five-class taxonomy over physical ontologies (excludes lawful stochasticity); determinism (assumed, with Bell unaddressed); PII as a bridge from epistemic inaccessibility to ontological identity; the scale-invariance step in §5.2 ("Class 4 contains Class 3, therefore its boundary is scale-invariant" — a non-sequitur); the saturation-triggers-restart mechanism; the JS-entanglement identification; asymptotic unreachability as a universal singularity property.

## 4. The symmetry approach

The methodological instinct — same structure across scales as a symmetry claim — is legitimate, and the paper deserves credit for confronting it with its own strongest counterargument (§9.5, the cognitive ceiling: symmetry-seeking brains will find symmetry). But the symmetry is asserted by table (§7.1), not derived: no group, no invariance, no transformation under which the structure is shown invariant. As used, "symmetry" means "suggestive structural analogy," and §7.2's "not analogy but structural identity" overclaims relative to what is demonstrated.

## 5. Verdict

As a piece of disciplined speculation, the paper is unusually honest: it flags real weaknesses, distinguishes conjecture from derivation in several places (§5.6 generations, §6.5 gap, §9 throughout), and its survey of adjacent programs is accurate. As an *internally sound argument*, it fails: the taxonomy equivocates on "fractal"; the elimination argument omits lawful stochasticity; PII is applied in mutually contradictory directions (§4.1 vs §5.7); §5.2/§5.3 use the coordinate-artifact status of horizons in opposite ways; the conformal-time and Big Crunch claims are factually wrong in standard cosmology; the Kerr-Newman electron contradicts the impermeability criterion it is meant to support; the JS-entanglement mechanism is Bell-excluded in its natural reading; and the necessity/uniqueness claims of §10 are not derivations. The defensible core — "the universe may be a computational system at criticality, bounded by information horizons, with conserved information across them" — survives as a coherent speculative frame. The chain "therefore one boundary, therefore cyclic renewal, therefore CPT alternation, therefore baryon asymmetry resolved" is speculation in derivation's clothing, and the paper's own necessity language ("must," "unique," "guaranteed") repeatedly outruns what the argument licenses. Repair priorities, in order: (1) the §4.1/§5.7 PII contradiction, (2) the stochastic-law gap in the elimination argument, (3) the conformal-time error, (4) the naked-singularity problem, (5) reframe §6.5 to confront Bell explicitly or invoke superdeterminism openly.

— *Independent soundness review, 2026-06-10*
