## The clinical-breadth question, answered honestly

**Q: can any other theory explain the range of clinical phenomena FMT explains?**
**A (fair, evidence-rated): yes — predictive processing can, and that's the uncomfortable part.** PP/active inference (precision-weighting) spans both the *level* axis and the *structured-content* axis with one mechanism, and beats FMT outright on schizophrenia, FND, and depersonalization. IIT owns the *level* axis with a deployed clinical instrument (PCI) that FMT lacks and must borrow/cite. The narrow theories (GNW, HOT, RPT, AST) each own fragments — AST/HOT have genuine anosognosia accounts; RPT/GNW/HOT handle blindsight.

**So FMT is NOT uniquely broad.** Coverage is cheap, and much of FMT's is relabeling. What is *not* cheap, and what no rival generates, is the discriminating predictive core (P1, P2, P3, ESM/EWM double dissociation). **Breadth converts from "explains-everything liability" to "genuine differentiator" exactly when one of those predictions is tested and confirmed.** Same conclusion as F2 — everything routes back to the first experiment.
