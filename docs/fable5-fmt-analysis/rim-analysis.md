# Independent Expert Review: "Why Intelligence Models Must Include Motivation: A Recursive Framework" (RIM)

**Reviewer stance:** independent, critical, cognitive science / intelligence research. Manuscript read in full (paper/intelligence/paper.md, ~11,500 words).

---

## 1. Core Thesis (in my words)

RIM claims that intelligence is best construed not as a capacity but as a *developmental process* — a learning ability constituted by a closed positive-feedback loop among three components: Knowledge (factual + operational), Performance (processing capacity, roughly Gf), and Motivation (a unified evaluative function). Three subsidiary claims: (a) the family of motivation constructs (NFC, TIE, openness, need for achievement) are measurement-context projections of one underlying function, and reported r ≈ .20–.35 motivation–intelligence correlations are Brunswik-symmetry artifacts; (b) operational knowledge is the loop's multiplier, with sweeping educational consequences (grades suppress intelligence); (c) AI systems with superhuman K and P but zero M cannot self-develop, and genuine machine motivation requires an explicit self-model (consciousness, per the author's companion FMT preprint).

## 2. Theoretical Coherence

The argument is internally readable but rests on three load-bearing weaknesses.

**The constitutive/correlated distinction is largely verbal.** The paper's central charge — that motivation is "constitutive" of intelligence rather than a moderator of its development — is never cashed out in predictions that differ from PPIK-plus-reciprocal-effects models. The paper concedes PPIK "comes closest" and that the difference "changes the formal structure of the model rather than merely adding predictors," but no formal structure is given (§7.3 admits formalization is deferred to a future paper). Until the functional forms exist, "constitutive component" vs. "powerful moderator" is a labeling dispute. Psychometricians exclude motivation from the *construct* deliberately, on construct-validity grounds, while modeling it as a determinant of development — which is exactly what investment theory, PPIK, and the reciprocal-effects literature (the paper's own Huang 2024 citation) already do. The "systematic blind spot" framing partially dissolves into a definitional substitution: redefine intelligence as trajectory, then accuse capacity models of missing the trajectory.

**The unity-of-motivation claim is underdetermined by its evidence.** The differential NFC→Gf / TIE→Gc correlations are equally consistent with the standard distinct-constructs interpretation; no discriminating evidence is offered. Worse, the AI "support" is circular: AI lacks *all* motivation constructs simultaneously, which cannot distinguish "one missing function" from "many missing traits." Absence of everything is evidence for neither. There is also an internal tension: §3.1 argues motivation is a fluctuating state with low single-occasion reliability (to explain attenuated correlations), while the loop treats M as a stable trait-like input compounding over decades. State-vs-trait needs explicit handling.

**Immunization patterns.** The Macnamara & Burgoyne null (d = 0.05 for mindset) is interpreted as *confirming* the model ("must engage the full loop"), while positive findings (Heckman, Murayama) also confirm it. When both outcomes confirm, neither tests. Similarly, the Gignac & Zajenkowski IQ-up/g-down dissociation is claimed to be "directly predicted" — it is accommodated post hoc; ability differentiation and cohort-composition accounts fit it equally. And the Jussim & Harber citation is risky: that paper's actual conclusion is that self-fulfilling prophecy effects are *small* (~5–10%) and teacher expectations are largely accurate — it undercuts §6.3 more than it supports it. A hostile reviewer who knows that literature will flag this as miscitation. Likewise Hilger et al. (2020) is described as showing "dynamic ability to reconfigure" networks, but the cited paper concerns *static* modular structure of intrinsic networks — content/citation mismatch.

## 3. Novelty vs. the Field

**Fatal omission: formal recursive models of intelligence already exist and are not cited.** Van der Maas et al. (2006, *Psych Review*) — the mutualism model — formally derives the positive manifold from mutually reinforcing growth among cognitive processes during development; later versions explicitly include investment and environment. Dickens & Flynn (2001, *Psych Review*) formalized reciprocal IQ–environment causation with multiplier effects, explaining exactly the "compound interest" dynamics and the Flynn effect RIM invokes. Savi et al. (2019) extended this into a network theory of g. These are the direct competitors; RIM is, as written, a *verbal* version of dynamics they formalized 20 years ago, with motivation swapped in as a node. Reviewers at *Journal of Intelligence* will know these papers cold. Not engaging them is disqualifying for the novelty claim and easily fixed — RIM's distinct move (motivation as a node *inside* the mutualism network, plus the unity claim) could be positioned against them.

**Predictive processing / active inference:** unaddressed, and it preempts the headline thesis. In the free-energy framework, epistemic value (curiosity) is *constitutive* of inference — exploration drops out of expected free energy minimization (Friston; Schwartenbeck et al.). A PP theorist reads "motivation must be inside the model of cognition" as already standard. Similarly, **curiosity-driven / intrinsically-motivated RL** (Schmidhuber's artificial curiosity since 1991; Pathak et al. 2017; Oudeyer & Kaplan) directly engineers the thing §5 declares absent and §5.3 declares impossible without consciousness. §5 reads as if this literature does not exist. The escape — "those are surrogates, not genuine motivation" — makes the AI prediction unfalsifiable by stipulation (any engineered motivation is dismissed as not the real thing because it lacks a self-model).

**vs. Sternberg:** the paper candidly admits meta-intelligence is "structurally identical" to its loop. **vs. CHC/g:** the critique via Canivez & Youngstrom is borrowed, fine. **Genuinely novel content:** (i) the Brunswik-symmetry reanalysis of motivation–intelligence correlations (Prediction 7) — though intellectually this is Wittmann's, the application is fresh; (ii) the motivational-consistency-over-intensity prediction (8), which is specific, mechanistic, and ESM-testable; (iii) operational-knowledge-as-multiplier is a nice framing but is metacognition/SRL (Flavell, Zimmerman, Dignath & Büttner) relabeled with a stronger structural role asserted, not demonstrated.

## 4. Operationalization & Testability

K, P, M have no measurement models. The abstract writes "K × P × M" (multiplicative), but no functional form is defended — and multiplicativity contradicts §6.2's claim that P differences barely matter (in a multiplicative system, every factor's variance propagates). Predictions 1–4 are retrodictions of existing findings (von Stumm, Murayama, Stanovich, Dignath & Büttner) that PPIK/investment accounts predict equally. Prediction 5 (AI) is unfalsifiable on any practical horizon and stipulatively protected. Prediction 6 (compounding grade damage) is conceptually good but confounded — though quasi-experiments exist (grade-free vs. graded systems, Montessori cohorts, countries delaying grading) and should be named. Predictions 7 and 8 are the paper's real empirical content: aggregate motivation across contexts/occasions Brunswik-symmetrically and the correlation with intelligence should rise substantially; motivational *consistency* (low intraindividual SD) should outpredict peak motivation longitudinally. A decisive design: multi-occasion ESM motivation battery + cognitive battery in a longitudinal sample; if symmetric aggregation leaves r ≈ .3, the unity/centrality claim is in serious trouble. The author should state that concession criterion explicitly — currently the Brunswik argument doubles as an all-purpose excuse for any weak correlation.

## 5. Weaknesses & Referee Risks

What a hostile reviewer attacks first, in order: (1) missing mutualism/Dickens-Flynn — instant "this already exists, formalized" rejection; (2) telling the *Journal of Intelligence* community (Ackerman publishes there) it has a "systematic blind spot" while citing the very literature that addresses it — rhetorically self-defeating; (3) the consciousness dependency: the claim that cognitive learning requires explicit self-modeling rests entirely on a self-published 2015 German book and the author's own unreviewed Zenodo preprint — importing a contested consciousness theory as a premise makes the paper hostage to it and signals theory-of-everything overreach; (4) §5's ignorance of intrinsically-motivated RL; (5) §6.3's "school grade disaster" — policy advocacy with causal language ("actively suppressing intelligence") far exceeding the cited evidence, plus the Jussim & Harber problem; (6) no formal model in a paper whose central claim is about *system dynamics*. The three desk rejections are consistent with this profile: scope sprawl (psychometrics + measurement theory + AI + consciousness + education reform in one theoretical paper with no data and no math) reads as a manifesto, and editors triage manifestos out in 24 hours regardless of merit.

## 6. Verdict

**An interesting synthesis containing one genuinely publishable idea, currently wrapped in overreach.** The defensible core: motivation belongs inside dynamic models of intellectual development; the field's modest motivation–intelligence correlations plausibly reflect Brunswik asymmetry; consistency-not-intensity is a sharp, novel, testable prediction. That is a real contribution — roughly a strong *Journal of Intelligence* theoretical/measurement paper — *if* it is repositioned against the mutualism and Dickens-Flynn models (as adding a motivational node and a measurement program, not discovering recursion), stripped of the consciousness premise (one neutral paragraph, not a load-bearing claim), the AI section reduced to a boundary-condition discussion that honestly engages curiosity-driven RL, and the grading section cut to a calibrated implications paragraph. As it stands, the paper claims priority for dynamics the field formalized two decades ago, defends its strongest claims with evidence that confirms under all outcomes, and bundles in commitments (consciousness-requires-FMT, grades-destroy-intelligence) that any referee can reject independently of the thesis. Genuine contribution: ~30% of the manuscript. The rest is the reason it keeps getting desk-rejected.
