# FMT vs. the Established Field: A Candid Benchmark

*Independent senior-scholar assessment. Primary focus: the consciousness contribution (FMT), benchmarked against IIT, GNW, HOT, RPT, AST, Predictive Processing/active inference, Metzinger's SMT, and Dennett's MDM. RIM and SB-HC4A assessed for the cross-domain claim.*

## 1. The real, defensible contribution

Strip away the prose and the genuinely original content of FMT reduces to three things, in descending order of strength.

**(a) Variable implicit–explicit permeability as a single altered-states mechanism, and the predictions it generates.** This is the strongest claim to distinctiveness. The idea that one boundary parameter — modulated globally (psychedelics, dreams, sleep onset) or locally (anosognosia) — unifies a wide phenomenological range, and that global and local modulation should *interact*, yields the psychedelic→anosognosia prediction (Prediction 1). That is a real cross-domain surprise prediction: cheap to test, falsifiable, and not generated by IIT, GNW, HOT, RPT, or AST. Whatever else is true of FMT, this is a legitimate, novel, testable contribution that the leading theories do not already have.

**(b) Self-referential closure as the demarcation between conscious and non-conscious computation.** The Stage 1→3 weather-simulation argument (a model that includes a model of itself modelling the world) is the conceptual core. It is well-articulated and does real work — but it is a member of a *converging cluster*, not a singular insight: Hofstadter's strange loops, higher-order representation, Bach's "perception of perception," and especially Laukkonen/Friston/Chandaria's 2025 "Beautiful Loop" reach the same place. FMT's version is distinguished only by being bolted to criticality and the 2×2 taxonomy.

**(c) The criticality requirement derived from computational theory.** Deriving an edge-of-chaos (Wolfram Class 4) requirement for consciousness from computational universality, rather than from neuroimaging, is intellectually clean and the convergence with ConCrit (Algom & Shriki, 2026) and Hengen & Shew (2025) is the corpus's best "prediction-then-confirmation" story. But the distinctiveness is modest: the empirical criticality program was already running (Beggs & Plenz 2003 and Carhart-Harris 2014 both *predate* the 2015 source), the requirement is shared with multiple frameworks, and — as the paper honestly concedes — the priority rests on a self-published German book, not a peer-reviewed record.

The two-level "virtual qualia" treatment of the Hard Problem is *not* on this list, and that matters (see §2).

## 2. Head-to-head against the leading theories

**vs. IIT (Tononi).** FMT is weaker where IIT is strongest — mathematical formalism. IIT has Φ, a qualia-space geometry, an exclusion-postulate boundary criterion, and two decades of apparatus. FMT has none of its own and concedes the absence of formalization as its "primary disadvantage." FMT is stronger in avoiding panpsychism, Φ's intractability, and the recently surfaced non-Markovian undefinedness problem. Crucially, *both bottom out in an axiomatic primitive* — IIT identifies experience with integrated information; FMT asserts that closure-at-criticality constitutes phenomenality. FMT relocates the primitive; it does not dissolve it.

**vs. GNW (Baars, Dehaene).** GNW owns the empirical track record FMT cannot match: ignition, P3b, masking, the COGITATE collaboration. FMT's "becomes conscious = implicit→explicit transfer" is essentially ignition with an ontological gloss. FMT's advantage is that it *attempts* the Hard Problem GNW deliberately brackets — but attempting is not solving. Net: FMT adds philosophy to GNW-style mechanics without new empirical traction of its own.

**vs. HOT (Rosenthal, Lau).** Self-referential closure is a richer, more architecturally specific cousin of higher-order representation, and adds criticality and the virtual ontology. But HOT has a clinical/metacognition literature (PFC, confidence, masking) that FMT lacks entirely. FMT is the better *idea*; HOT is the better *grounded* idea.

**vs. RPT (Lamme).** No contest on grounding — RPT is empirically tight but narrow (vision). FMT subsumes recurrent processing as a candidate implementation and claims the breadth RPT disclaims. Different virtues; FMT is broader and far less tested.

**vs. AST (Graziano).** AST has the best Meta-Problem account in the field; FMT borrows it (and says so) and adds virtual qualia. AST is deflationary; FMT claims realism about qualia. AST is more parsimonious. FMT's selling point — explaining *why* phenomenality exists, not merely why we believe it does — is exactly the part it cannot cash out, because it reduces to the closure→phenomenality commitment.

**vs. Predictive Processing / active inference (Clark, Friston, Seth).** This is FMT's most dangerous competitor and where differentiation is thinnest. Seth's controlled hallucination and interoceptive inference already model the self as prediction; REBUS already unifies altered states *mechanistically* (relaxed precision on high-level priors) — more concretely than FMT's qualitative permeability. The paper repeatedly concedes that predictions are "partially derivable" from PP (explicitly for Prediction 2). FMT's claim that permeability is "architecturally prior" to REBUS, and REBUS derivable from it, is asserted, not demonstrated. FMT's one clean differentiator is the real/virtual level distinction — a philosophical commitment, not a mechanism.

**vs. Metzinger's SMT and Dennett's MDM.** FMT is explicitly a *descendant* of SMT + MDM, not a rival that supersedes them. Metzinger's transparency thesis and phenomenal self-model are the direct ancestors of the ESM; SMT remains far more philosophically developed. Against Dennett, FMT draws its one crisp philosophical line — qualia are *real at the computational level*, contra illusionism — but the paper itself admits this may be merely verbal relative to Frankish's weak illusionism and "cannot be settled by empirical observation." That is an honest admission that the headline Hard-Problem move is contestable.

**Bottom line on the Hard Problem:** FMT reframes rather than solves, and says so. Its rating of "addresses" in its own Table 5 reflects depth of engagement, not a settled result. On the eight-requirement rubric the paper is strong, but that rubric is self-selected and explicitly excludes the dimensions (formalism, quantitative prediction, replication) on which IIT/GNW/PP win.

## 3. The cross-domain ambition: strengthens or overreaches?

**RIM (intelligence)** is a competent integrative critique — the motivation-omission point is fair and has real precedent (Ackerman's PPIK, Wittmann's Brunswik symmetry, Sternberg's meta-intelligence, von Stumm's "hungry minds"). The K×P×M recursive loop is a sensible synthesis, and the Brunswik-symmetry argument (that motivation–intelligence correlations are attenuation artifacts) is its most testable original-feeling claim — though it is borrowed from Wittmann. RIM is the weakest of the three as *original* work (it is a theory-critique essay, thrice desk-rejected), and its reliance on FMT to ground "cognitive learning" imports a speculative consciousness theory to prop an intelligence claim — a structural liability.

**SB-HC4A (cosmology)** is the highest-risk item. It is natural philosophy, not physics: no derivations, no novel quantitative predictions, and its one empirical probe (CMB multifractal analysis) returned a null. Several moves — singularity unification via Leibniz's Identity of Indiscernibles, energy=information, particles-as-Planck-singularities — range from suggestive reframing to unfalsifiable speculation. To its credit it is unusually self-aware (it lists six weak points, including the devastating "cognitive ceiling" objection, and concedes the James-Stein/entanglement bridge's math does not transfer).

**Does spanning three domains help?** Intellectually, the criticality/self-reference thread is a genuine connective tissue, and for a sympathetic reader the cross-scale coherence is aesthetically compelling. **Sociologically, it is a serious credibility liability.** "One architecture explains mind, intelligence, *and* the cosmos" is the precise pattern reviewers use to triage theory-of-everything/crank submissions — and the Section-7 claim that brains and the universe are *literally the same architecture at different scales* is the single largest reputational risk in the corpus, flagged by the author's own Weak Point 5 (we may see Class-4 everywhere because we *are* Class-4 pattern-matchers). For professional reception, the breadth undermines the parts that could otherwise stand.

## 4. Honest verdict

FMT is an **interesting, unusually well-crafted synthesis within the self-modeling tradition** — it sits in the same contemporary cluster as Metzinger, Seth, Safron's IWMT, Graziano, Bach, and the Beautiful Loop, distinguished mainly by the permeability mechanism and its specific predictions. It is **not** a paradigm-level advance and it does **not** solve the Hard Problem (it relabels the explanatory gap as its "foundational commitment"). It is materially better than the median solo-theorist output: bibliographically current (2024–2026), honest about limitations, and internally coherent.

Realistic standing relative to the field *today*: a respectable but **currently unrecognized** independent contribution with no peer-reviewed footprint, no formalism, no empirical program of its own, and no institutional backing. It is best described as "interesting synthesis carrying one or two genuinely novel testable predictions," not "genuine theoretical breakthrough." The permeability predictions are the asset that could move it from synthesis toward advance.

## 5. What recognition would actually require

1. **Get FMT through peer review** at a serious venue (*Neuroscience of Consciousness*, *Phil. Studies*, *JOCS*). The thrice-desk-rejected RIM shows the gate is real and unforgiving of solo, cross-disciplinary theory.
2. **Quarantine the cosmology and the "theory of everything" framing** from every consciousness submission. Never let SB-HC4A or cross-scale identity touch the FMT cover letter. The breadth that excites the author is what gets the package dismissed.
3. **Get ONE novel prediction tested by an independent lab** — the psychedelic→anosognosia or controlled-ego-dissolution prediction. A single confirmed surprise prediction outweighs hundreds of pages of argument and is the *only* realistic route to genuine recognition.
4. **Deliver the mathematical formalization, not a roadmap.** A quantitative permeability model tied to Lempel-Ziv dose-response would be the highest-value deliverable; "formalization roadmap exists" is not formalization.
5. **Co-author with an established lab.** The papers themselves note that independent-scholar status without affiliation is a structural barrier; collaboration is the standard solvent.
6. **Position as a complementary mechanism, not a rival total theory.** Criticality + permeability are absorbable into existing frameworks; "extend where they are incomplete" (the paper's own stated stance) is a far more achievable goal than displacement — and is the honest description of what the work actually offers.
