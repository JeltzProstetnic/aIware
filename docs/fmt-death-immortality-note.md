# FMT and Death: Individual vs General Immortality

*Session 216 note (2026-06-10). Matthias's framing + the philosophical lineage, for evaluation — candidate material for the book's closing chapter and/or a paper "implications" section.*

## The position (Matthias, stated as a creed)
- **No individual immortality.**
- **Yes general immortality.**
- **Individualism is the actual illusion.**

## Why this is FMT's eschatology, not an add-on
If the "I" is a model the substrate runs (the explicit self-model, ESM), then:

- **Individual immortality is a category error.** There is no individual *substance* to persist — only a simulation that ran for a while. The self can't be immortal because it was never a *thing*; it was a process the brain enacted. The question "will *I* survive?" presupposes the very entity FMT says is constructed.
- **What persists is impersonal:** the substrate, the pattern, the general capacity to self-simulate. "General immortality" = the impersonal continuity of substrate / process / information, not the personal continuity of an ego.
- **Individualism is the *specific* illusion** the explicit self-model manufactures. Not merely "false" — it is the predicted artifact of a transparent self-model that presents itself as a substantial subject rather than as a model (cf. the transparency claim).

## Lineage — where this sits
- **Schopenhauer (closest parallel).** The individual (*principium individuationis* — individuation in space and time) is Maya, the veil; what does not die is the *Will* (Wille zum Leben), which was never personal. Individuals perish; the thing-in-itself does not. "No individual immortality, yes general immortality, individuation is the veil" is almost verbatim *Die Welt als Wille und Vorstellung* (Bk IV; the essay "Über den Tod"). **FMT's move:** replace the metaphysical Will with a computational/dynamical substrate — same structure, naturalized. (Worth flagging: this is also the risk — see evaluation Q1.)
- **Parfit, *Reasons and Persons*.** Reductionism about personal identity: no deep further fact; the self is a bundle/series, not an ego. Consequence: "my" death matters less than it phenomenally feels, because there was no persisting subject whose annihilation it is. The analytic route to the same consolation.
- **Metzinger, *Being No One* / *The Ego Tunnel*.** The phenomenal self-model is transparent; in the substantial sense "no one was ever born and no one dies," because the "one" was always a model. FMT's ESM ≈ Metzinger's PSM with the implicit/explicit × self/world structure added.
- **Buddhist anattā.** No-self; clinging to the individual self is the root illusion (and root of dukkha). The soteriological version.
- **Spinoza, *Ethics* V.** The part of the mind that is eternal is impersonal, *sub specie aeternitatis* — explicitly not personal survival.

## The seizure / unconsciousness corollary (same conversation)
A brain that loses consciousness *before* a runaway cascade (seizure) is the substrate **protecting the simulation by killing the stream** rather than letting it tear. This is the same architectural move as propofol leaving the hippocampus doing implicit-world-model work in the dark (Katlowitz 2026): explicit model suppressed, substrate intact. Loss of consciousness as a *protective decoupling* of the explicit simulation from a substrate under stress — graceful degradation, not failure. (A small, vivid FMT illustration; possibly a footnote, not load-bearing.)

## Evaluation prompts
1. **Does "general immortality" do real work, or is it "the substrate persists" dressed up?** Schopenhauer's Will was a metaphysical posit that genuinely doesn't disperse; a physical substrate (your brain) *does* disperse at death. So what is the general thing that's "immortal" — the type/pattern (which is repeatable, not continuous), information (which is lost), or the universe's general capacity to host self-simulation (true but cold)? The word "immortality" may overclaim for a physical pattern.
2. **The consolation is only real if what-matters-in-survival is the impersonal continuity (Parfit), not the personal.** Does FMT offer a *positive* account of what general thing continues, or only the negative (no individual to die)? Schopenhauer/Buddhism give a positive object (Will / Buddha-nature); FMT currently gives mostly the negative.
3. **Book angle.** This is the natural closing chapter of *The Simulation You Call "I"* — the death chapter writes itself from the architecture (the title already promises it). The danger is sliding from a defensible deflation ("no ego to lose") into an indefensible consolation ("you live on") — Q1/Q2 are exactly where a careful reader (or reviewer) will press.

## The indiscernibility argument (Matthias, Session 216) — candidate future piece
*Flagged as worth "an article, comment, or blog post — post-fame." This is the positive answer to Q2 above.*

**The argument.** Remove all memories (Matthias has *experienced* this — "strange feeling, but the feeling of selfness in the here-and-now is unchanged"). Then remove all sensory input. What remains — bare contentless awareness — has **no qualitative feature left to individuate it**: there is nothing to distinguish "my" stripped consciousness from "yours." By identity of indiscernibles, it is therefore **numerically one**. The individuation does not live in the experience; it lives in the **substrate**, and only on *activation* (re-adding memory/sensation/substrate-specific dynamics) do differences arise — and those differences are **optional to the experience**. So: one experiencer, many substrates; the many are contingent, the one is essential. → "no individual immortality (the optional differences die), yes general immortality (the one bare consciousness is undying), individualism is illusion (the differences are inessential)."

**Why it's load-bearing, not armchair:** the key premise — selfness survives total memory loss — is a *first-person datum* Matthias has, not an inference. Selfness ≠ autobiography; the bare "I am" is content-independent.

**Lineage.** Advaita Vedānta (Ātman = Brahman; the contentless witness is identical in all; the *jīva* is illusory) — the closest ally, this is its analytic reconstruction. Kolak, *I Am You* (open individualism: numerically one subject). Metzinger's "minimal phenomenal experience" / pure-consciousness program — the empirical handle. Schopenhauer (one Will), Parfit (no further fact).

**FMT mapping.** Bare consciousness = the universal *form* of self-referential simulation with content stripped — structurally the same wherever a substrate self-simulates. The individuating content (which memories, which ESM/EWM specifics) is substrate-level and optional. This is FMT's *positive* account of "what generally continues": not merely "the substrate persists" (Q1's weak reading) but "the one form of bare self-simulation, instantiated everywhere, individuated nowhere essentially."

**The two pressure points (where the piece must do work):**
1. **Indiscernible ⇏ numerically one.** Max Black's symmetric universe (two qualitatively identical spheres, still two) breaks strict identity-of-indiscernibles. The defense — locate numerical distinctness in the substrate, treat experience as having "no thisness beyond content" — is a *premise to argue*, not a theorem. The no-further-fact (Parfit) view supplies it, but it must be defended, not assumed.
2. **Is the stripped state truly contentless?** Or is there residual structure (bare temporality, the "thereness," a minimal self-model floor) that re-individuates it? This is exactly the Metzinger MPE debate; the argument needs a position on it.

**Why post-fame:** open individualism reads as mysticism until the architecture makes it cheap. It wants the credibility cushion first. But as the *closing chapter of the book* — with FMT's architecture already laid — it is earned rather than asserted, and the title ("The Simulation You Call 'I'") has been promising exactly this payoff all along.
