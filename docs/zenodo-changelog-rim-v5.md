**v5 (2026-08-27) — the Platinum edition: a re-typing, and a new title**

This is a partial rewrite rather than a revision. The paper's thesis has changed, and with it the title.

*What the argument now says.* Previous versions argued that the field had **omitted** a component: motivation is missing from the formal structure of every major model of intelligence, and the repair is to add it. That reading survives here only as the symptom. The claim now is that the field **mis-typed the constituents it already has**. The psychometric tradition assumed its constituents were all the same kind of thing and typed each of them as a capacity, because a capacity is what its instruments were built to measure. On the account developed here they are not the same kind of thing. Performance — processing capacity — is the only one of the three that is a capacity in the psychometric sense. Knowledge is not one constituent but two, divided by content into the store of what is the case and the store of how to learn. And motivation is a constituent of a third type: it sits in the loop, and the loop's outcomes revise it, but it is not a capacity. It is the policy that allocates the loop's time — a schedule, not a substance.

*Two distinctions that are kept apart, because the paper uses both.* The content split within Knowledge is one thing. The physical boundary between what is stored in structure and what runs as process is another, and this version states explicitly that the second **cuts across all three constituents and divides none of them**: every constituent has a stored mode and a running mode. That matters because no constituent then owns the running generative process, which earlier drafts had assigned in three places at once, and because the subtest partition of Section 7.3 is anchored on the mode distinction rather than on a claim about which constituent an item taxes.

*What the re-typing does and does not deny about motivation's place.* Motivation is a **node** of the loop, not an input to it: the Matthew effect the model rests on requires that success in learning revise the schedule, so Knowledge and Performance have edges into Motivation and the loop is closed only if they do. What is denied is the *type*, not the position — a node can be updated without being a magnitude, and a schedule that a season's results revise is still a schedule. Where the paper speaks of motivation being raised, reduced or low, that is shorthand for a change in the policy and never for a change in a level; the shorthand is declared as such in Section 3.1 and used deliberately thereafter.

*Why the re-typing is not a relabelling.* It changes what should be expected of measurement, and four results the field currently reports without an account follow from it. Motivation's long resistance to trait measurement is what an instrument finds when it looks for the magnitude of something that has none. The attenuation of motivation-intelligence correlations under narrow single-occasion sampling becomes a consequence of what is being sampled and not an excuse for a disappointing number. The contrast between the durable effect of schooling on measured intelligence and the absence of far transfer from working-memory training is what follows if education re-points the schedule while training drills the capacity measure. And motivation should predict the *consistency* of intellectual engagement rather than its peak intensity, because a loop compounds through iteration count. None of the four is new as an observation; they stop being four separate embarrassments.

*A correction to how the schooling result is reported, and it is the most consequential change in this version.* Earlier drafts of this edition claimed a durable effect of schooling on **fluid** ability. Checked against Ritchie and Tucker-Drob (2018) directly, that claim does not hold in the design the paper quotes: under control of prior intelligence the fluid-test estimate is 0.836 points per year at *p* = .152, which is not significant, and the significant signal in that design sits on composite tests at 1.876. The policy-change design does return a significant fluid effect, 2.272 points per year at *p* = .003. **The claim is therefore now made about measured intelligence and not specifically about fluid ability**, in the Abstract, in Section 3.3, in Section 6.1 and in the Conclusion; the body states both designs' estimates, the non-significance, and the outcome-age moderation that runs the effect from 2.154 points per year at age 18 down to 0.485 at 83.

*New material.* A section on the field's own critique of its construct-typing, argued entirely through psychology's own authorities — Meehl on non-cumulativeness, Borsboom and colleagues on validity as existence and causation, Eronen and Bringmann on constructs of unknown validity, and the mutualism model as the demonstration that the field's strongest predictor could predict everything it predicts while referring to nothing of the kind its name suggests. It closes on age-normed IQ, where the exchange between Salthouse, Schaie and the Betula group is presented as the exhibit: a field whose foremost measurement scholars cannot agree what its central instrument measures across age has a typing problem, acknowledged from within. The paper takes no side in that exchange.

*A concession the paper makes before a referee can.* Motivation-as-allocation is occupied ground, three times over, and one occupant is inside the ability literature itself. A new subsection names Kanfer and Ackerman (1989), Shenhav, Botvinick and Cohen (2013), and Kurzban and colleagues (2013) and states exactly what remains separable: all three allocate attention or control during engagement with a task at hand, whereas the resource allocated here is **iterations of the loop, across developmental time** — how often the cycle is run and at what it is pointed, over years. That separation is deliberately stated without leaning on the architectural conjecture the paper elsewhere invites the reader to reject, so that a reader who declines the conjecture still holds it. The subsection also declines a separation that was available and is not true — these accounts do derive psychometric consequences, Kanfer and Ackerman's paper being an aptitude-treatment-interaction study whose deliverable is predictions about ability-performance correlations. What is vacant is narrower: consequences for the measurement of the allocated disposition itself.

*The education and working-memory-training contrast is argued rather than asserted*, because the naive form of it does not survive scrutiny. The working-memory null holds against **treated** control groups; education has no placebo arm, and on the untreated-control ruler working-memory training would beat a year of schooling. What survives the design difference is what the treated-control comparison isolates, and durability: far transfer is negative at follow-up, while education's effect is estimated on adult outcomes years downstream.

*Extensions.* The polythetic character of the intelligence construct is derived rather than observed: a procedure built for commensurable magnitudes, applied to one capacity, one stock and one policy, must return something that behaves like a family resemblance. The subtest partition that the paper uses to test secular change is shown to be the stored/running boundary applied to test content, which supplies a principled reason for that cut rather than leaving it a free-standing device. And grading is re-described as a repeated institutional signal that re-points the allocation policy, which is what distinguishes it from one-shot motivational manipulations whose null the paper reports.

*What is unchanged.* The nine predictions and their disconfirmation criteria, the model survey, the subtest analysis with its arithmetic, and the verified citation base. The recursive structure itself is still not claimed as novel — mutualism and multiplier accounts formalized reciprocal causation two decades ago, and the paper says so. What is claimed is that one of the three things being reciprocally caused has been the wrong kind of thing all along.

*Exposure.* Two results would defeat the account, and both are stated in the text. A symmetric aggregation of motivation measures that still returned the currently reported average correlation would defeat the measurement claim — this version names prediction 7 as the risk-bearer for the unity claim, correcting an earlier pointer to prediction 9, which carries no motivation instrument. A demonstration that peak engagement predicts long-term intellectual development better than consistency does would defeat the policy reading.

*Where the evidence is thinner than the sentence, and the paper says so.* The Wittmann and Hattrup (2004) risk-taking result is reported as bearing on the intelligence-performance relationship and as consistent with the exploration-exploitation dynamic, not as empirical evidence for it: the primary is unreachable from here and the secondary literature confirms the intelligence-to-performance paths without confirming the mediation. The claim that motivation resisted trait measurement is dated to four decades of instrument-building from need for cognition (1982) onward, rather than to a century, because that is the instrument record the paper can point at.

*Not in this version.* Three citations remain unverified against their primaries and are flagged in the project's own records: Wittmann (1988)'s Extraversion/Neuroticism demonstration, Dignath and Büttner's long-versus-short follow-up comparison, and one cohort-replication characterization. The Abstract runs longer than the target journal's guidance; it is kept at length deliberately, because shortening it would mean dropping the disconfirmation criteria.

This version carries 110 references. Every one resolves against Crossref, arXiv, DataCite or a named catalogue record, or carries a hand-verified note naming the source that confirms it.
