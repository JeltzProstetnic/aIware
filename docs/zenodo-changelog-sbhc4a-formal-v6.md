**v6 (2026-10-19)**

Supersedes v5 (2026-10-08). Corrections from a cross-check of all FMT publications against the parent cosmology paper (Gruber, 2026a), the FMT master and each other. No module was added or removed.

*Necessity claim (opening summary, Sections 1.1, 7.1, 7.3, 7.4).* The Uniqueness theorem is replaced by a Proposition (Class Selection): given a deterministic substrate, the five axioms select the SB-HC4A architecture class. They do not select a model within that class; dimension, the singularity inventory and cyclic versus branching temporal structure remain open. The paper no longer claims that any possible physical universe must be SB-HC4A.

*Class 4 axiom (Sections 2, 7.1).* Axiom A3 is now universality, computational irreducibility and containment, with a new irreducibility predicate. Self-organized criticality is no longer a condition of the axiom; it is stated as what Class 4 typically shows in self-organizing physical substrates. The five-class split is described as a split by reducibility: Rule 30 belongs to Class 4, and Class 5 is reserved for ontic randomness, a stronger condition than irreducibility.

*Holographic saturation (Sections 3.1, 3.2, 3.4, abstract).* Condition IB2 is renamed holographic (area-law) saturation and kept distinct from the Bekenstein bound, which is set by energy and radius. The heat-death argument follows the parent paper: a dilute equilibrium gas stays far below the area bound, and saturation arrives only once the interior is emptied and the cosmological horizon is the only entropy carrier left.

*Particles as boundaries (Section 3.7).* "O(1) bits" per boundary is replaced by about 0.36 bits per Planck area, so encoding the particle spectrum needs a boundary of order 10–20 Planck areas. The conservation proposition now covers electric charge and angular momentum only; colour and weak isospin are not boundary labels, and baryon and lepton number are predicted to be approximate.

*Entanglement module (Section 4.6).* "Admissible" is corrected to "dominating" throughout; the James-Stein estimator is itself dominated by its positive-part variant. The consequences for Bell nonlocality and decoherence are restated as what a discrete Stein-type theorem would have to supply, not as results that follow. The unsourced reference to a "Diaconis-Holmes framework" is removed.

*Cyclic closure (Sections 5.6–5.8).* The theorem on irreducible cycle sequences is relabelled a Conjecture and its missing premise is stated. CPT alternation is called a testable consequence, not a strong prediction.

*Cross-scale correspondence (Sections 5.4, 6.2).* The cognitive fixed point is written Ψ(m*) = m*, acting on the joint state of self-model and world-model as in the FMT formalization (Gruber, 2026c, Section 6.4), and kept apart from the cosmological operator Φ. Explicit models are described as generated selectively from the substrate.

*Energy and information (Section 8).* The Planck energy is corrected to about 1.96 × 10⁹ J (it read 1.22 × 10⁹ J); one bit at that scale is about 1.36 × 10⁹ J and is called a temperature-dependent rate, not a universal conversion. Black-hole information scales with the square of the mass.

*Cognitive ceiling (Section 9).* Gödel incompleteness is derived from universality plus embedding, not from the fixed point alone.

*References.* The parent paper is cited by its concept DOI (10.5281/zenodo.18698605). Dong (2016) is corrected to Dong, Harlow & Wall (2016), and Boyle, Finn & Turok (2018) is cited with all three authors. Bourgain (1985) is added.
