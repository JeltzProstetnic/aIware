*Correction to Prediction 8 (Section 8.9).* v22 stated that a meta-analysis (Thornton, Baird, & Sheffield, 2024) puts athletes' pain-unpleasantness effect above their pain-intensity effect. That misreads the source. Its five unpleasantness studies examined endurance, track and mixed collegiate athletes, and in each of them unpleasantness and intensity fell together or not at all; the pooled unpleasantness estimate was not significant, and the intensity estimate came from a different set of studies, so the two cannot be compared in size. The sentence now reports this co-movement and notes that it is what the theory expects from exposure without a change in the self-model. The prediction, its designs and its falsification conditions are unchanged.
