## Search C — epistemic reward and intrinsic motivation

**Verdict: the first level is solidly supported; the directional formulation is heavily OCCUPIED; and the part
that would be ours has been shown by nobody.**

### C1. Is cognitive success itself rewarding? — YES, and this is not in dispute

Established in humans and monkeys, behaviourally and neurally. The cleanest single result is
**Bromberg-Martin & Hikosaka 2009** (*Neuron* 63(1):119–126, `10.1016/j.neuron.2009.06.009`): midbrain
dopamine neurons signal a preference for advance information that **cannot change the outcome** — i.e.
non-instrumental information has reward value. Supporting: **Gruber, Gelman & Ranganath 2014**
(*Neuron* 84(2):486–496, `10.1016/j.neuron.2014.08.060`) — curiosity states engage SN/VTA and hippocampus and
enhance learning, including of incidental material; **Kobayashi & Hsu 2019** (*PNAS* 116(26):13061–13066,
`10.1073/pnas.1820145116`) — information value and reward value share a neural code; **Kang et al. 2009**
(*Psychol Sci* 20(8):963–973, `10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02402.x`) — curiosity predicts caudate activity, real
expenditure to learn answers, and better recall 1–2 weeks later. On the insight side, **Tik et al. 2018**
(*Hum Brain Mapp* 39(8):3241–3252, `10.1002/hbm.24073`) and **Oh et al. 2020** (*NeuroImage* 214:116757,
`10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.116757`) identify reward signals specific to the *Aha!* moment.

⚠ Two bibliographic flags to carry: the Kang et al. **subtitle** (*"Epistemic Curiosity Activates Reward
Circuitry and Enhances Memory"*) is **not** in Crossref or OpenAlex and is UNVERIFIED — cite the short title;
and Crossref returns **no page range** for Charpentier, Bromberg-Martin & Sharot 2018
(`10.1073/pnas.1800547115`), so the commonly quoted E7255–E7264 is UNVERIFIED.

### C2. ⛔ OCCUPANCY — this is the finding, and it is worse than "adjacent"

> *"The current intrinsic reward or creativity reward or curiosity reward or aesthetic reward or **fun**
> r_int(t) of the action selector is the current surprise or novelty measured by the improvements of the world
> model p at time t."*

and states the goal as *"finding or creating data that can be modeled better or faster than before"*,
formalised as maximising the first derivative of model quality.

⇒ **His "fun" is our Zufriedenheit; his "improvements of the world model" is our "abstract success"; his
reward-shaped controller is our "adapts toward."** What he does **not** claim: that any of it reshapes neural

### C3. The trait / longitudinal half is WEAKER than the claim needs, and one study cuts against it

**Lavrijsen et al. 2024** (*J Intelligence* 12(10):103, `10.3390/jintelligence12100103`) is the key test: six
waves, **n = 3,409**, grades 7–12. Need for cognition shows rank-order stability **r = .50** from G7 to G11 —
**comparable to Big Five traits (~.51)** — and the three trajectory classes differ **mainly by initial level,
not by slope**. ⇒ **That is a stability result, not a "worn toward" result.**

The strongest *causal* evidence that intellectual activity changes cognition is **Ritchie & Tucker-Drob 2018**
(*Psychol Sci* 29(8):1358–1369, `10.1177/0956797618774253`) — but it is about **education**, i.e. externally
imposed schooling, not internally generated cognitive satisfaction. And **Salthouse 2006**
(*Perspect Psychol Sci* 1(1):68–87, `10.1111/j.1745-6916.2006.00005.x`) is the standing sceptical review of
"use it or lose it", which a reviewer will reach for.

Use→structure *does* exist experimentally: **Draganski et al. 2004** (*Nature* 427(6972):311–312,
`10.1038/427311a`, juggling) and **Woollett & Maguire 2011** (*Curr Biol* 21(24):2109–2114,
`10.1016/j.cub.2011.11.018`, London taxi trainees — qualifiers gained posterior hippocampal grey matter,
failers and controls did not). ⚠ **But note what those changes were**: a more detailed *concrete* map and a
visual-motion area. **Neither is a move up an abstraction hierarchy** — arguably the opposite.

### C4. The closest thing to the missing link, and it still is not it

**Becker & Cabeza 2025** (*Trends Cogn Sci* 29(3):255–268, `10.1016/j.tics.2025.01.001`) — "insight as
prediction error": insight → PE → dopaminergic/hippocampal LTP plus noradrenergic arousal → durable memory.
**That is a satisfaction→durability chain, published, and it is the nearest existing thing.** It does not carry
structural change over years, and it is not directed at abstraction.

### C5. ⛔ THE GAP — state it in our own voice before a reviewer does

1. **Nobody has shown that a felt satisfaction signal drives long-term structural change directed at
   abstraction.** Searched for directly; it does not exist. Every component exists separately; no study chains
   them.
2. **Abstraction-directedness is the specifically missing piece.** Granting reward→plasticity and
   use→structure, nothing says the reshaping yields *more abstract* representations rather than merely more
   efficient specific ones — and the two best use→structure studies point the other way.
3. **"Satisfaction" is doing unlicensed work.** The evidence is in *reward-signal* terms; the claim is in
   *felt* terms. Nothing establishes that the conscious feeling is the causal variable rather than an
   accompaniment of the dopaminergic signal. This is a real gap, not a wording quibble — and it is the same
   shape as the §3.4.3 asserted-not-derived cut.
4. **Timescale mismatch.** Reward-for-cognition evidence runs minutes to weeks (Kang's 1–2 weeks is the
   longest); structural evidence runs months (Draganski) to ~4 years (Woollett & Maguire); the claim is about
   lifelong shaping. Nothing bridges.

### B2. ⭐ WHAT IS GENUINELY UNOCCUPIED — and it is better than what we were going to write

**Every one of the seven treats the agency signal as tracking something REAL.** Cockburn's credit assignment
and Katzman & Hartley's framing both read it as a proxy for instrumental relevance: *information I obtained by
acting is likely to be useful again.*

**FMT's claim is different and stronger: the self-model's ATTRIBUTION DECISION is itself the control variable —
so a FALSE attribution should have the same plasticity consequence as a true one.** Nobody in this literature
can say that, because they all still treat the agency signal as tracking actual control. And it is exactly the
inversion FMT already needs elsewhere: §4.2.2/§4.2.3 make felt authorship a *retrospective construction*, and
this gives that construction a forward causal role. The loop closes.

⭐ **It yields a clean, cheap, falsifiable experiment, and the search could not find it run at the consolidation
timescale:** illusory-agency encoding with **actual contingency held at zero**, tested at 24 h or beyond. The
instrumental-utility account predicts the benefit **vanishes** when the attribution is false; FMT predicts it
**survives**. ⇒ **That is where the contribution is, and it is a prediction rather than a restatement.**

### B3. ⚠ THE THREATS — three results that cut against (ii-a) as stated, and one that guts a supporting paradigm

- **Katzman & Hartley 2020**, *Cognition* **199:104239**, `10.1016/j.cognition.2020.104239` (n = 96, ages 8–25),
  ✅ re-verified: *"In contrast to previous studies, we did not find that simply having the ability to make a
  choice enhanced memory."* The benefit appeared **only where choice had high instrumental utility**. ⇒
  **Attribution was present in the low-utility condition and bought nothing.** This is the nearest thing to a
  direct disconfirmation of attribution-per-se, and it is also the study that makes B2's experiment the right
  one to propose.
- **Tsuji & Imaizumi 2022**, *Sci Rep*, `10.1038/s41598-022-26210-1` — a clean **null**: SoA successfully
  manipulated, no effect on recollection or familiarity, intentional or incidental. (Crossref returns no
  article number; do not invent one.)
- **Hon & Teo 2024**, *Psychological Research* 89(1):11, `10.1007/s00426-024-02032-w` — the same lab's own
  deflation of Hon & Yeo 2021: a **weak** sense of agency also improves memory when made salient by
  infrequency, so the original effect is **confounded with salience**.
- **Chen & Risen 2010**, *JPSP* 99(4):573–594, `10.1037/a0020217` — proves a theorem that the free-choice
  paradigm yields spreading of alternatives as a **mathematical artefact** of noisy preference measurement,
  **with the flaw extending to every moderator and mediator variant**. ⇒ Do not lean on choice-induced
  preference change. And **Sharot, Velasquez & Dolan 2010**, *Psychol Sci* 21(9):1231–1235,
  `10.1177/0956797610379235`, report preference change after choices participants **did not know they had
  made** — which argues the felt authorship is not the operative variable at all.

### B4. The empirical base does not reach the claim — the numbers

- **Self-reference effect:** real but ordinary. **Symons & Johnson 1997** (*Psychol Bull* 121(3):371–394,
  `10.1037/0033-2909.121.3.371`) is the meta-analysis; **d ≈ 0.65** self-vs-semantic across 60 studies.
  ⚠ **That figure is UNVERIFIED against the primary** — the paper is closed-access and the number is confirmed
  only as a verbatim quotation inside **Bentley, Greenaway & Haslam 2017** (*PLOS ONE* 12(5):e0176611,
  `10.1371/journal.pone.0176611`). Applied meta-analysis: **Hedges' g = 0.40** (Liu et al. 2024,
  `10.1111/bjep.12636`). ⛔ **And its own explanation undercuts a privileged-self reading:** Symons & Johnson
  attribute the effect to the self being a *well-developed, frequently used construct promoting elaboration and
  organisation* — ordinary depth-of-processing — which is why it is **smaller** against other-reference than
  against semantic encoding.
- ⛔ **Timescale, and this is the decisive gap.** Verified maxima anywhere in this literature: **one week**
  (Pruss, Karni & Prior 2025, *Psychon Bull Rev* 32(5):2104–2113, `10.3758/s13423-025-02674-w`) and **24 h**
  (Murty et al. 2019; Ding et al. 2021). Every neural result is **single-session**. The step from *"forgetting
  rate reduced at 24 h"* to *"paths carved over a lifetime"* is **three orders of magnitude of time with no
  intermediate data point.** State it ourselves or a reviewer will.
- **Felt agency vs objective control is not cleanly separated.** Most of the strong work (Murty, Voss, Houser,
  Markant, Chambon, Cockburn) manipulates **actual** choice or volitional control. The studies isolating
  *felt* agency with the motor act held constant are the small, recent, mixed corner — and they include the
  null and the salience reattribution above. ⇒ **The specific FMT version, that the ILLUSION does the work, is
  precisely the version with the thinnest support.**
- **The effects are selective, not global.** Houser et al. 2022: temporal order yes, item and spatial no.
  Ren et al. 2026: spatial yes, item-recognition sensitivity no. Tsuji & Imaizumi: both no. A general
  weighting term has to explain why the weighting lands on binding/order/spatial structure and not on item
  strength.
- **The only cortical-reorganisation citations are about voluntary MOTOR drive, not felt authorship** —
  Held & Hein 1963 (`10.1037/h0040546`, the kitten carousel), Lotze et al. 2003 (*Brain* 126(4):866–872,
  `10.1093/brain/awg079`), Kaelin-Lang, Sawaki & Cohen 2005 (`10.1152/jn.00143.2004`). They are evidence for
  **efference copy**, not for narrative self-attribution. Do not cite them as if they were the latter.

## Search A — reward / neuromodulator-gated plasticity

✅ **Six load-bearing records re-verified against Crossref in the main session** (Charpentier 2018,
Guggenmos 2016, Hong 2022, Pawlak & Kerr 2008, Attardo 2015, Tse 2007) — all exact.
⚠ **One inter-agent discrepancy resolved:** search A gave Charpentier's pages as E7255–E7264; **Crossref
returns no page range**, which is what search C independently reported. Cite issue-only.

### A1. ✅ "Gated, not coincidence-driven" is established and safe to assert

- **Pawlak & Kerr 2008**, *J Neurosci* 28(10):2435–2446, `10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4402-07.2008` — the **necessity**
  result: blocking D1/D5 abolishes both corticostriatal LTD and LTP. Their own conclusion: *"spike timing
  alone is not enough… ongoing activity does not affect synaptic strength unless dopamine receptors are
  activated."* **This is the citation for "Hebbian coincidence is insufficient."**
- **Hong et al. 2022**, *Nat Commun* 13:3202, `10.1038/s41467-022-30827-1` — eligibility traces induced and
  converted **in vivo in mouse V1** (NE→LTP, 5-HT→LTD), with the process required for **ocular dominance
  plasticity**. Moves three-factor rules from slice curiosity to in-vivo necessity.
- The molecular clock is measured: **Yagishita et al. 2014** (*Science* 345(6204):1616–1620,
  `10.1126/science.1255514`) — dopamine enlarges spines **only 0.3–2 s after** glutamate; **Shindou et al.
  2019** (`10.1111/ejn.13921`) — potentiation only at synapses active **~2 s before** DA.
- ⭐ **And the third factor is NOT reward-specific**, which matters for us: **Takeuchi et al. 2016**
  (*Nature* 537(7620):357–362, `10.1038/nature19325`) makes it **novelty** — locus coeruleus, blocked by
  hippocampal D1/D5; **Sharpe et al. 2017** (*Nat Neurosci* 20(5):735–742, `10.1038/nn.4538`) shows dopamine
  transients are sufficient and necessary for **stimulus–stimulus** learning between *non-rewarding* events.

### A2. ⭐ The single most on-point paper for our claim

**Guggenmos, Wilbertz, Hebart & Sterzer 2016**, *eLife* 5:e13388, `10.7554/eLife.13388` — humans learn **with
no external feedback**; learning is driven by **confidence-based** reinforcement; mesolimbic areas encode
confidence anticipation and a **confidence prediction error** *"in remarkable similarity to previous findings
for external reward-based feedback"*; and the size of that modulation **predicts individual learning
success**. ⇒ **"Satisfaction with one's own performance drives learning", measured.** ⚠ fMRI plus a model,
correlational, perceptual rather than abstract-conceptual, and **no plasticity is measured**.

### A3. ⛔⛔ THE FINDING THAT CHANGES THE CLAIM'S CONTENT — the two halves of MG's direction come apart

**Charpentier, Bromberg-Martin & Sharot 2018**, *PNAS* 115(31), `10.1073/pnas.1800547115`: mesolimbic reward
circuitry assigns knowledge positive utility **only when the expected news is favourable** — people will
**pay to remain ignorant** of unfavourable outcomes. Orbitofrontal cortex responds to knowledge-over-ignorance
regardless of valence; **the reward circuit does not.**

⇒ **A substrate carved by that signal is carved toward COMFORT, not toward accuracy or abstraction.**
MG's formulation names two targets — *"more cognitive zufriedenheiut and abstract success"* — and **the
neuroscience says they are separable and sometimes opposed, and that the measured gating signal follows the
first.** The direction word that survives is **Zufriedenheit**; **"abstract success" is the half the evidence
contradicts.**

⭐ **This is a theory result, not merely a blocker.** Taken seriously, FMT's own mechanism **predicts motivated
cognition** — and FMT already half-states it. MG's *maturing* material in the pattern registry says exactly
this in his own words: *"usually you declare the ones you reached after the fact as the ones you aimed for and
try not to think about all the failed ideas."* **Charpentier supplies the mechanism for an observation the
theory already makes.** That is a better position than the original claim, because it converts an awkward
concession into a prediction the architecture generates.

### A4. ⛔ The gate is a PERMISSION signal, not a STEERING signal

**Kilgard & Merzenich 1998**, *Science* 279(5357):1714–1718, `10.1126/science.279.5357.1714`: adult A1 is
massively reorganised by **stimulating nucleus basalis** paired with a tone — **no reward, no task, no
satisfaction, no behaviour**, and the receptive-field changes follow the acoustic parameters. ⇒ The third
factor says *"this moment matters"*; **what makes a moment matter is decided outside the plasticity rule.**
A directed-carving claim needs the **criterion**, and the criterion is not in these papers.

### A5. ⭐ The one result that does gate on ABSTRACTION

**Tse et al. 2007**, *Science* 316(5821):76–82, `10.1126/science.1135935` — *Schemas and Memory
Consolidation*: once a neocortical **schema** exists, one-trial learning becomes hippocampus-independent
*"extremely quickly"*. **The gating variable is fit with an existing abstraction**, which is far closer to
"abstract success" than juice is. ⚠ But the mechanism is schema-dependent immediate-early-gene activation
(**Tse et al. 2011**, `10.1126/science.1205274`), **not** a neuromodulatory reward gate — so it supports the
*shape* of the claim while belonging to a different mechanism.

### A6. ⛔ THE GAPS

1. **No measurement of plasticity under an epistemic criterion exists.** The confidence/curiosity/insight work
   measures BOLD, EEG, spikes and behaviour — never synaptic or structural change. The sentence has **no
   direct empirical referent**.
2. **Epistemic value is measured as instrumental.** In both monkey studies (Bromberg-Martin & Hikosaka 2009;
   **Lak et al. 2017**, *Curr Biol* 27(6):821–832, `10.1016/j.cub.2017.02.026`) the epistemic quantity is
   information about, or confidence in, a **primary reward**. Guggenmos is the only genuinely internal case.
3. **Timescale chasm.** Seconds (eligibility traces, 0.3–10 s) → hours (synaptic tagging and capture;
   Froemke et al. 2007, `10.1038/nature06289`) → **nothing** → weeks-to-lifetime (**Yang, Pan & Gan 2009**,
   *Nature* 462(7275):920–924, `10.1038/nature08577`, where a fraction of new spines persist for life) → four
   years (Woollett & Maguire). **The middle is empty**, and the two ends are studied by different communities
   with no shared mechanism. Roughly **six orders of magnitude**.
4. ⚠ **Accumulation itself is contested.** **Attardo, Fitzgerald & Schnitzer 2015**, *Nature*
   523(7562):592–596, `10.1038/nature14467`: adult CA1 basal spines have a mean lifetime of **~1–2 weeks**,
   implying near-total turnover in weeks — *"a near full erasure of the synaptic connectivity pattern."*
