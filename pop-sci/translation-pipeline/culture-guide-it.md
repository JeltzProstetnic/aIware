# Culture & Tone Guide — Italian (standard) · AIW-108-IT

**Every translation agent receives this verbatim, alongside the glossary.**

## Variant (LOCKED)
- **Standard Italian** (mercato amazon.it). No strong regionalisms.
- **Reader-address: "tu" (informal singular).** Italian popular science addresses the reader warmly with "tu"
  ("Immagina di giocare a un videogioco."). This is natural and intimate, NOT disrespectful. Keep "tu" consistent.

## Register & voice
- **Educated general reader.** Warm, vivid, confident — narrative popular science, not a textbook, not hype.
- **Grandeur calibration = the WARM MIDDLE.** EN runs high/grand; DE was dialed DOWN for DACH reserve. Italian
  nonfiction accepts elegance and narrative sweep, but recoils from US-style self-promotion. If a sentence sounds
  like marketing, soften toward the DE register. When unsure whether a grand claim survives, keep the meaning and
  flag the tone — do not silently flatten OR inflate.
- **Sentence rhythm:** Italian tolerates elegant subordination but not German-length chains; keep the author's punch.
  Avoid calqued English word order.

## Sources — how to combine EN + DE (per chunk you get both)
- **Translate the EN** (fuller "grandeur" voice = primary source).
- **Consult the DE** for meaning (DE = source of truth for meaning), native-idiom solutions, grandeur calibration.
- Where EN and DE differ in *content*, DE wins on meaning; if they genuinely conflict, translate the EN but note it.

## Typography (Italian)
- **Quotation marks: «virgolette caporali» (guillemets) as PRIMARY** — the literary Italian standard, NO inner spaces
  («testo»). Nested = "virgolette alte". Innermost = 'apici'.
- **Em-dash / lineetta:** use — for parenthetical asides as in EN.
- **Numbers:** keep as the EN has them — do NOT reformat digits. Spell small numbers in prose as EN does.
- **Capitalization:** Italian titles capitalize only the first word + proper nouns ("Capitolo 2: I quattro modelli"),
  EXCEPT the fixed term "Teoria dei Quattro Modelli" and the model names, which stay capitalized (glossary).
- No ¿ ¡ (that is Spanish only).

## Do-not-localize list
- The author's personal anecdotes, biography, and place references (keep his life as lived).
- Proper names, citations, DOIs, reference entries (glossary §4).
- Culturally-specific examples STAY; if an idiom won't land, translate the *meaning* naturally (culture pass's job
  in Phase 4 — in Phase 1 prefer a faithful natural rendering and flag doubtful ones).

## Output contract (STRICT)
- Return **only the Italian translation of the EN span** — same paragraph/heading structure, same Markdown
  (##, **bold**, *italic*, lists, blockquotes) in the same places.
- Do NOT add, drop, merge, or reorder paragraphs. No translator notes. One EN paragraph → one IT paragraph.
- Preserve Markdown headings exactly (translate heading TEXT, keep the `##` level).
- If a span is a reference list or pure citations, return it **verbatim**.
