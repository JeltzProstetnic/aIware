# Culture & Tone Guide — Japanese · AIW-108-JA

**Every translation agent receives this verbatim, alongside the glossary.**

## Variant & register (LOCKED)
- **Standard Japanese, general-audience nonfiction.** Register = **です・ます体（丁寧体）** — warm, accessible, the
  standard for popular science addressed to a general reader. Consistent throughout (do not mix with である体).
- **Second person:** Japanese normally OMITS explicit "you". Avoid overusing あなた — prefer implied subjects and
  natural impersonal phrasing ("〜してみてください" etc.). Use あなた only where the EN's direct address truly needs it.

## Voice & grandeur
- **Warm, vivid, confident narrative science.** Grandeur calibration = the **WARM MIDDLE**: EN runs grand, DE was
  dialed down for DACH reserve; Japanese nonfiction favors understatement and precision — avoid boastful/promotional
  tone, but keep wonder and momentum. If a claim sounds like marketing, soften. Keep the meaning; flag borderline
  self-promotion rather than flattening or inflating silently.
- Break English sentence rhythm into natural Japanese flow; do not calque English word order. Keep the author's punch.

## Sources (CJK-specific)
- **Translate from the EN** (primary). Use the DE **only** to disambiguate MEANING (DE = source of truth for meaning).
  Do NOT try to inherit German idiom — it does not transfer to Japanese.

## Typography
- **Quotation marks: 「…」primary, 『…』for nested quotes / titles.** Do NOT use «guillemets» or "curly quotes".
- Full-width punctuation: 、。「」（）. No spaces between words.
- **Numbers:** keep the EN digits as-is (do NOT reformat or convert to 漢数字).
- Preserve Markdown structure exactly (##, **bold**, *italic*, lists) — translate heading TEXT, keep the `##` level.

## Do-not-localize
- Author's personal anecdotes, biography, place references (keep as lived). Proper names, citations, DOIs, references.
- Idioms that won't land → translate the MEANING naturally (culture pass refines later); flag doubtful ones.

## Output contract (STRICT)
- Return ONLY the Japanese translation of the EN span — same paragraph/heading structure, same Markdown, SAME
  paragraph count (one EN paragraph → one JA paragraph). No added notes, no code fences.
- Pure reference/citation lists → return verbatim.
