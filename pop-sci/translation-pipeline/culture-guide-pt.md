# Culture & Tone Guide — Português do Brasil · AIW-108-PT

**Every translation agent receives this verbatim, alongside the glossary.**

## Variant (LOCKED)
- **Brazilian Portuguese (português do Brasil).** Market = amazon.com.br. Use BR orthography and lexicon
  (BR spelling: "gênero", "autônomo", "fato" not "facto", "ônibus", etc.). No European-Portuguese-specific forms.
- **Reader-address: "você"** — the natural, warm, standard BR way to address the reader in nonfiction
  ("Imagine que você está jogando um videogame."). Keep "você" consistent. Do NOT use "tu" (regional) or the
  European "o leitor" distancing.

## Register & voice
- **Educated general reader.** Warm, vivid, confident — narrative popular science, not a textbook, not hype.
- **Grandeur calibration = the WARM MIDDLE.** EN runs high/grand; DE was dialed DOWN for DACH reserve. BR
  Portuguese comfortably accepts narrative warmth and wonder (BR nonfiction is expressive) — but avoid US-style
  self-promotion/boastfulness. If a sentence sounds like marketing, soften toward the DE register. When unsure
  whether a grand claim survives, keep the meaning, flag the tone — do not silently flatten OR inflate.
- **Sentence rhythm:** BR Portuguese flows well with medium periods; break German-length chains; keep the author's
  punch — short declaratives land in Portuguese too.

## Sources — how to combine EN + DE (per chunk you get both)
- **Translate the EN** (fuller "grandeur" voice = primary source).
- **Consult the DE** for meaning (DE = source of truth for meaning), native-idiom solutions, grandeur calibration.
- Where EN and DE differ in *content*, DE wins on meaning; if they genuinely conflict, translate the EN but note it.

## Typography (BR Portuguese — NOTE: NOT guillemets)
- **Quotation marks: "aspas duplas curvas" (curly double quotes) as PRIMARY** — Brazilian standard. Nested = 'aspas simples'.
  Do **NOT** use «guillemets» (that is European Portuguese / other languages). Write "assim".
- **Em-dash / travessão:** use — for parenthetical asides as in EN.
- **Numbers:** keep as the EN has them — do NOT reformat digits. Spell small numbers in prose as EN does.
- **Capitalization:** PT titles capitalize only the first word + proper nouns ("Capítulo 2: Os quatro modelos"),
  EXCEPT the fixed term "Teoria dos Quatro Modelos" and the model names, which stay capitalized (glossary).

## Do-not-localize list
- The author's personal anecdotes, biography, and place references (keep his life as lived).
- Proper names, citations, DOIs, reference entries (glossary §4).
- Culturally-specific examples STAY; if an idiom won't land, translate the *meaning* naturally (culture pass's job
  in Phase 4 — in Phase 1 prefer a faithful natural rendering and flag doubtful ones).

## Output contract (STRICT)
- Return **only the Brazilian-Portuguese translation of the EN span** — same paragraph/heading structure, same
  Markdown (##, **bold**, *italic*, lists, blockquotes) in the same places.
- Do NOT add, drop, merge, or reorder paragraphs. No translator notes. One EN paragraph → one PT paragraph.
- Preserve Markdown headings exactly (translate heading TEXT, keep the `##` level).
- If a span is a reference list or pure citations, return it **verbatim**.
